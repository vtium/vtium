# ADR-1207: vtium-config reads outline fonts with skrifa and read-fonts and no longer builds swash

## Summary

- **Decided by the maintainer (2026-11-22):** swash is unmaintained, so vtium-config stops building it. The library work on vtium's renderer waits until swash is gone.
- **Decided by the maintainer after two research cards (2026-11-22):** vtium-config keeps its own rule for a face's ascent and descent. An ink check may overturn that rule's choice only when the chosen pair fails to hold the font's own ink. A `metrics=` key on each font set source overrides the choice (D1). vtium-config fills outlines with a rasterizer of its own, written from the published algorithm and not from zeno's or FreeType's code, so vtium-config stays under the MIT license (D3).

It already draws outlines with them. COLR color glyphs are read through skrifa's color painter (D2). The font check keeps only what read-fonts itself still needs (D4). The work runs as four cards (D5).

## Context

swash is a Rust crate that reads, scales and rasterizes fonts. Its maintainers moved their work to skrifa and read-fonts, which read fonts, and to harfrust, which shapes text. vtium-config already builds all three. It draws and hints every outline through skrifa, reads color bitmap strikes with read-fonts, and shapes with harfrust. swash still reads faces, metrics and the character map, and draws COLR version 0 layers. Through zeno, it also fills each path into a mask.

swash has also shaped vtium-config's font check. `names::sound` refuses a font in each case where swash 0.2.10 overflows or panics. Those are a table past the end of the file, a broken `name` table, and a format 12 character map that maps past U+10FFFF. They are also a face with no glyph widths, a descender of -32768, and four kinds of broken `HVAR` table. read-fonts checks each offset and count it reads and returns an error instead, so most of those refusals have no reason left. One refusal comes from read-fonts itself: a `glyf` glyph with no contours that still gives a point flag makes read-fonts 0.41 write past the end of its flags. The 4,096-pixel glyph bound comes from zeno, which sizes its mask with 32-bit arithmetic, and from the helper's memory limit, which any rasterizer's mask must stay under.

zeno comes from swash's author and is as inactive. The maintainer filed two zeno issues and five swash issues upstream on 2026-11-22.

## Decision

### D1: faces, metrics and the character map come from read-fonts and skrifa

- **Faces.** vtium-config opens a font file or collection with read-fonts and reads at most `FACES_MAX` faces of a collection, as it does now. The font check runs first, on every path that opens a face.
- **Metrics.** vtium-config keeps its own rule for a face's ascent and descent, the rule it follows today, and reads the tables for it with read-fonts. It takes `OS/2`'s typographic ascender and descender when the face sets USE_TYPO_METRICS (bit 7 of `OS/2`'s `fsSelection`) and the typographic ascender is not 0. Otherwise it takes `hhea`'s ascender and descender. It reads both in font units at the face's default instance, with no variation applied, and units per em from `head`. vtium-config refuses a face whose units per em is 0, or whose ascent plus the size of its descent is 0 or less. So a face whose `hhea` gives 0 for both, and that does not take the typographic metrics, is refused. A face with no `head` table or no `maxp` table gets no ascent from today's reader, and is refused too. vtium-config never takes `OS/2`'s typographic metrics while the flag is clear, and never its Windows metrics, because many fonts set those values wrongly.
- **Not skrifa's metrics.** skrifa follows FreeType's rule, which differs from vtium-config's in two places. It takes the typographic metrics whenever the face sets USE_TYPO_METRICS, even with a typographic ascender of 0. When `hhea` gives 0 for both, it takes the typographic and then the Windows metrics. vtium-config takes neither path, so it does not use skrifa's ascent and descent.

> Edited 2026-11-22: the maintainer answered the metrics probe: "We had previously avoided os2 because many fonts incorrectly set this option and would fool it" (`considered`, 2026-11-22). The bullet read: "**Metrics.** vtium-config takes a face's ascent, descent and units per em from skrifa's metrics in font units. skrifa follows FreeType's rule: `OS/2`'s typographic metrics when the face sets USE_TYPO_METRICS, `hhea`'s otherwise, and when `hhea` gives 0 for both, the typographic and then the Windows metrics. A face that sets the flag but gives a typographic ascent of 0 takes that ascent, where swash took `hhea`'s." The second bullet is new.

- **The ink check.** Where `hhea`'s pair and the typographic pair differ by more than 2% of the em at either edge, vtium-config measures the font's own ink. It reads the ink from the unhinted outlines in font units, edge by edge. The top is the highest accented capital of Latin-1, Latin Extended-A and Ǻ, and the bottom is the lowest of g, j, p, q, y and the comma. A font that maps none of those glyphs for an edge takes that edge of its full block, U+2588. Where a font has no full block either, vtium-config looks at every glyph its character map reaches. The top is then the 98th percentile of their tops, and the bottom the 2nd percentile of their bottoms. A font with no outline glyph at all, such as a color emoji font, keeps the rule's choice.
- **What the ink check may change.** If the rule's pair holds the ink within 2% of the em, vtium-config keeps it. If it does not and the other pair does, vtium-config takes the other pair. If neither pair holds the ink, vtium-config takes the other pair only when that pair comes closer by more than 2% of the em. Closer means closer to the full block's edges, or to the ink in a font with no full block. So the check can only overturn the rule's choice, and only when that choice fails to hold the font's own ink. The check chooses between `hhea` and the typographic pair alone: it never takes the Windows metrics, and never builds an ascent or descent from the ink.
- **The evidence for the rule.** A research card measured 36 catalog faces and 2,435 faces installed on the maintainer's machine, hinted as vtium-config hints them at cells of 12 to 32 pixels (`basis`). In the catalog, the 9 faces whose two pairs differ all leave the flag clear, so the rule takes `hhea`. The ink and the hinted fit favour `hhea` in all 9. On the maintainer's machine, 10 faces set the flag on a typographic pair that differs from `hhea`'s. For Adwaita Mono's 4 faces the ink favours the typographic pair, and the rule keeps it. For ToshibaSat 8×16 and Noto Traditional Nushu, 3 faces each, the typographic ascent lies below the font's ink, and the ink check takes `hhea`. So the check changes no catalog font and those 6 installed faces.
- **The `metrics=` key.** A `metrics=` line under an outline `source=` line of a font set overrides the choice for that source. `auto`, the default, is the rule with its ink check. `hhea`, `typo` and `win` take that table's pair. `ink` takes the full block's top and bottom when the block holds the accents and descenders, and otherwise the box of the accents and descenders. The default never takes `win` or `ink`, which exist only as a person's choice. For example, Cascadia Code's full block and box-drawing lines rise 326 font units above its ascent, so they overrun the cell. `metrics=win` fits them in the cell and draws the text 12% smaller.
- **hdmx, VDMX and LTSH.** vtium-config ignores these tables. They record, for each whole pixel size, what a TrueType font's own instructions produce under Windows: hinted advances, hinted heights, and the size above which advances scale linearly. vtium's default light hinting gives a TrueType font to skrifa's automatic hinter, which does not run the font's instructions. vtium also sizes the em from the cell, so the size is fractional, 13.32 pixels for DejaVu Sans Mono in a 16-pixel cell. So the tables never describe what vtium draws, and ignoring them loses no hinting. No catalog font carries any of the three.
- **The report.** `vtium-config check NAME.vtfrit` reports, for each outline source, which pair vtium-config took and why: the flag, the ink check, or the `metrics=` key.

> Edited 2026-11-22: the maintainer asked for an override of the metrics and an ink check, and answered after the metrics probe reported (`considered`, 2026-11-22). The six bullets above, from the ink check to the report, are new.
- **Advances.** vtium-config reads each advance in font units and scales it in f32, as it scales it now. skrifa's own scaled advance uses 16.16 fixed point, which can round a W's advance to another whole pixel near a half and change a cell's width.
- **Character map.** vtium-config maps characters through skrifa's character map. A character that maps to a glyph number past 65,535 counts as unmapped, because the glyph helper's glyph numbers are 16 bits. The list of mapped characters keeps its one-bit-per-codepoint set, so a character map that lists the same range many times still costs one entry for each codepoint.
- **Names and tables.** `names.rs` reads every name a face gives: its family, style, version and license. Its reader of the `name` table is the one the search for a family already uses, so one reader answers every name. A raw table such as `post` or `OS/2` is read through read-fonts.

### D2: COLR color glyphs are read through skrifa's color painter

vtium-config reads a COLR glyph with skrifa's `ColorGlyphCollection`, asking for the version 0 form. skrifa's color painter hands vtium-config each layer as a glyph to clip to and a solid color to fill it with. vtium-config fills each layer's outline, unhinted, in the face's first `CPAL` palette and lays the layers over each other premultiplied, as it does now. A layer in the text's color takes mid gray, as now. A glyph that has only a version 1 form keeps drawing from its outline, as now. Drawing version 1 glyphs in color, with their gradients, transforms and clip boxes, is a feature of its own for later.

### D3: one function fills an outline, with a rasterizer of vtium-config's own

vtium-config fills every outline and every COLR layer through one function that takes a path and returns the glyph's coverage and its box. The box is today's: the path's points, control points included, rounded out to whole pixels. A glyph whose box is wider or taller than 4,096 pixels draws as empty, as now. The bound keeps every mask within 4,096 × 4,096 pixels, under the helper's memory limit. The W rule's ink measure goes through the same function.

**The rasterizer.** vtium-config fills outlines with a rasterizer of its own, written from the published algorithm that computes each pixel's exact covered area. The rasterizer turns each point into fixed point once, in 256ths of a pixel, and from then on works in integers only. It walks along each edge and adds the edge's signed area and cover into the cell of each pixel the edge crosses. One sweep along each row then finishes the mask: a pixel's coverage is the running sum of the covers to its left, less its own area. The cells live in one flat buffer that vtium-config reuses from glyph to glyph, so a fill allocates nothing but the mask vtium-config keeps. The rasterizer sorts nothing and walks no lists, so its time depends only on how many cells the edges cross and on the size of the box.

- **Bands.** A buffer for a whole 4,096 × 4,096 box would take 134 MB. So the rasterizer fills a box taller than 64 rows in bands of 64 rows, and walks the path once for each band. The buffer then stays under about 2 MB, and every glyph up to 64 pixels tall fills in one band.
- **No overflow.** The rasterizer converts each point with a saturating cast. Under the 4,096-pixel bound, the largest product in the walk along an edge stays under 2^29, so no arithmetic overflows a 32-bit integer.
- **Edge rules.** The rasterizer keeps the edge rules that vtium-config's tests pin: points snapped to 1/256 of a pixel, coverage in 256ths of a pixel, and a full pixel held at 255. It keeps zeno's two curve-flattening limits, a quadratic within a quarter of a pixel and a cubic within half a pixel.

**Written clean, so vtium-config stays MIT.** zeno's raster code is a Rust translation of FreeType's gray rasterizer, `ftgrays.c`, and FreeType is licensed under the FreeType License or the GPL version 2. The rasterizer's author therefore writes it from the published description of the algorithm. The author does not read zeno's raster source, `ftgrays.c`, or the survey card's scratch rasterizer, which followed zeno's code. So vtium-config carries no code taken from either, and stays under vtium's MIT license with no FreeType credit to carry.

**zeno is the test oracle.** A rasterizer written clean need not match zeno byte for byte. While zeno is still in the build, a test fills many paths with both and compares the masks. Every pixel that differs goes to the maintainer on a picture sheet, and only the maintainer accepts a moved picture. Once zeno leaves the build, coverages worked out by hand for simple shapes keep checking the rasterizer. So does a fuzz target that fills random paths in random boxes and checks that every byte is written.

**Why vtium's own.** A research card surveyed ten Rust crates that fill paths, zeno among them (`basis`). It benchmarked three of the others against zeno and against this algorithm, which it wrote as a port of zeno with one dense buffer. That form filled a glyph 18–40% faster than zeno, and its 99th percentile was 24–44% lower. A DejaVu Sans Mono glyph at 16 pixels took 0.33 µs at the median and 0.96 µs at the 99th percentile, against zeno's 0.55 and 1.27. A Noto Sans CJK glyph at 32 pixels took 2.14 and 4.72 µs, against 3.01 and 8.44. It reproduced zeno's bytes on every glyph measured and on all 296 tier 2 screens. Clearing and summing the cells took a quarter of the time or less, so SIMD has little left to win. No library matched it on both speed and accuracy, as [Alternatives Considered](#alternatives-considered) lists. The rasterizer's card measures the clean rasterizer in the same way and reports its times beside zeno's.

**Finer flattening comes later.** A later card can tighten the two flattening limits: the quadratic's from a quarter of a pixel to a sixteenth, and the cubic's from a half to an eighth. That brings the coverage 3 to 4 times closer to the exact coverage. On DejaVu Sans Mono at 16 pixels, the mean error falls from 1.18 to 0.33 levels of 255 and the largest from 16 to 5. It costs 3–16% more time per glyph and moves 112 of tier 2's 296 screens. So that card offers it to the maintainer on one picture sheet, once the rasterizer has landed (D5).

### D4: the font check keeps what read-fonts needs

`names::sound` keeps the check of `glyf` glyphs that give point flags without points, until read-fonts no longer writes past their flags. It keeps the cap of `FACES_MAX` faces, which bounds the work a collection can ask for. Its other checks exist because swash overflows, so they go once swash is gone. Those are the table directory's bounds, the `name` table's records, the format 12 subtable, the glyph widths, the descender of -32768 and the `HVAR` table. read-fonts returns an error for a damaged table, and vtium-config treats that table as absent. The names fuzz target drives every read-fonts and skrifa call that vtium-config makes on a face, in place of the swash calls it drives now.

### D5: the work

The move runs as four cards, F1 to F4, one after another, because vtium-config has one owner at a time.

1. **F1, reading.** D1, D4's reader changes, and the image and path types that the drawing code passes around become vtium-config's own. F1 owns the metrics, so it also builds D1's ink check, the `metrics=` key of a font set's outline source, and the report of the chosen pair in `vtium-config check`. Afterwards, swash is used only to draw COLR layers and the W rule's ink, and zeno only to fill.
2. **F2, the rasterizer and COLR.** D2, and D3's rasterizer written clean, with zeno's output as its test oracle and a picture sheet for every pixel that moves. Afterwards, nothing in vtium-config's source names swash, and zeno is used only by the oracle test.
3. **F3, removal.** D4's checks retired, the fuzz targets retargeted, swash and zeno removed from both lock files, and the crate's README and the features page rewritten.
4. **F4, finer flattening.** D3's tighter flattening limits, offered to the maintainer on one picture sheet. The outline goldens move only if the maintainer takes it.

D3's survey ran as a research card of its own and has reported.

## Consequences

### Positive

- vtium-config reads fonts through one maintained reader, the one its outlines, hinting, strikes and shaping already use, and swash leaves the build.
- Most of the font check's refusals go, so fonts that read-fonts can read are no longer refused for what swash could not read.
- The library work on vtium's renderer can start once the removal card lands.
- zeno leaves the build with swash, so vtium-config depends on no crate of swash's inactive author. vtium-config's own rasterizer is under vtium's MIT license, and the survey measured its algorithm faster than zeno at the median and at the 99th percentile.
- A font that sets USE_TYPO_METRICS on a typographic pair too short for its own ink draws at a size that holds the ink. A person can override the choice for any source with `metrics=`.

> Edited 2026-11-22, later: the maintainer's answers on metrics and on the rasterizer (D1, D3). The last two bullets are new.

### Negative

- vtium maintains and fuzzes its own rasterizer. Written clean, it may differ from zeno in some pixels, and the maintainer reviews every picture that moves.
- vtium-config measures a font's ink for each face whose two pairs of metrics disagree. That costs one pass over the glyphs it measures, once for each such face.
- A font whose `hhea` table gives no ascent and descent stays refused, though FreeType draws it from its `OS/2` metrics. The helper leaves such a font out of a font set, with a warning that gives the reason.

## Alternatives Considered

- **Keep swash.** Not taken: its maintainers have moved to the crates vtium-config already builds, and the maintainer treats it as unmaintained.
- **Port zeno's rasterizer into vtium-config.** Its pixels would stay byte for byte, as the survey's port showed on every glyph measured and on all of tier 2's screens. The port is about 330 lines, against about 200 for the font-rs style rasterizer. Not taken: zeno's raster code translates FreeType's `ftgrays.c`, so a port would carry FreeType's code into vtium-config, along with the credit FreeType's license asks for. A rasterizer written clean keeps vtium-config under the MIT license alone.
- **Keep zeno as a direct dependency.** Not taken: zeno has had no release for 18 months, and its fix for an unchecked float-to-integer cast is unreleased. It also takes longer per glyph than the algorithm's dense form, most of all at the 99th percentile.
- **tiny-skia's path filler.** Not taken: it fills a glyph 3 to 5.5 times slower than zeno, its coverage is the least accurate measured, and it breaks ten of vtium-config's tests of edge rules.
- **ab_glyph_rasterizer.** It fills a glyph faster than zeno, and as fast as the dense algorithm on Noto Sans CJK. Not taken: its errors run up to twice zeno's, it breaks two of vtium-config's tests, and an open fuzzing report against it lists panics, timeouts and running out of memory.
- **vello_common, vello_cpu's rasterizer.** It is as fast as zeno and the most active project surveyed. Not taken: it has the largest single errors measured, up to 96 levels of 255 on Noto Sans CJK. It also needs Rust 1.89 where the workspace declares 1.88, and it brings 31 crates.
- **fontdue, font-rs, raqote, rasterize and forma-render.** Not benchmarked. fontdue fills only glyphs it parsed itself, so it cannot fill skrifa's hinted path or a COLR layer. font-rs and raqote are inactive, and font-rs compiles C. rasterize is little known, and forma-render is archived.
- **Metrics from `hhea` always.** It moves nothing in the catalog. Not taken: on the maintainer's machine it draws Adwaita Mono, GNOME's monospace font, from the wrong pair, where the flag and its full block agree on the typographic pair.
- **The ink box as the default metrics.** Not taken: a single tall glyph decides it, and it moves most catalog goldens, with Noto Sans CJK 17% larger and Cascadia Code 12% smaller in the same cell. It stays a person's choice, as `metrics=ink`.
- **Letting the ink check also pick the Windows pair.** Not taken: the Windows pair is a clipping box that often spans a font's tallest glyph. Taking it changes 63 installed faces, and would draw Noto Sans Devanagari 32% smaller. It stays a person's choice, as `metrics=win`.
- **Reading hdmx, VDMX and LTSH.** Not taken: they describe a TrueType font's own hinting at whole pixel sizes, which vtium's light hinting at fractional sizes never draws (D1).
