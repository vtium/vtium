# ADR-1506: vtium keeps the maintainer's direction in the decision records, in signed commits and in COPYING

## Summary

- **Decided:** vtium keeps the provenance of its work in three places. The decision records keep the maintainer's direction in the maintainer's own words, dated, with the channel each answer came through, as they do now. The maintainer stays the git author of every commit and, from 2026-11-10 on, signs every commit with an SSH key. COPYING says plainly that agents write vtium's code under the maintainer's direction and review. vtium adds no transcript archive, no commit trailer and no separate mark for text the maintainer writes by hand.
- **Trades away:** The stronger evidence that the research names: quotes kept only verbatim, hashed archives of the session transcripts, a mark on the maintainer's hand-written text, and a git identity for agents. 61% of the maintainer's entries in the records are agents' paraphrases, and the practice that produces them stays.
- **One-way?** No. Each practice the record leaves out can be added later. A signature on a pushed commit stays in the history, and the commits made before 2026-11-10 stay unsigned.
- **Probes:** *Confident:* the maintainer wants the records kept as they are now, with no new marking or archive. *Not confident:* one added sentence in COPYING is all the change to the notices the maintainer wants before a lawyer reads them.
- **Inversion:** Between "keep only the code, and let git's author field speak" (it names the maintainer on commits agents wrote, and nothing shows where vtium's shape came from) and "record everything a court might ask for" (archives, timestamps and trailers on every change, for a question no court has settled). The answer keeps the record the project already writes, signs it, and states its limits.

## Context

One person, the maintainer, owns vtium and directs it. AI agents write almost all of its code and documents under that direction. Whether anyone holds copyright in work made this way is an open question, and the maintainer wants vtium's records to help establish the maintainer's creative control and authorship.

In vtium, ADR means Agent Decision Record. The project borrowed the familiar acronym of the Architecture Decision Record and gave it its own meaning. An Agent Decision Record holds a decision and its provenance. Provenance here means the record of where the work came from: who decided, in what words, through which channel, and when. Agents draft each record, and the maintainer directs and approves it. The maintainer's words sit in a record's `basis` and `considered` entries, each with the date and the channel it came through.

A steering session researched the question on 2026-11-10. The research reads the U.S. Copyright Office's guidance, the courts' decisions and the practice of other open-source projects, and it measures vtium's records and history. It is research, not legal advice. Every statement about the law in this record comes from it, with the source's name and date. The maintainer asked that the record not read too much into the case law, so the record states the law only to say what vtium's provenance establishes and what it does not.

A signed commit is a commit that carries a cryptographic signature made with the maintainer's SSH key. Because each git commit names its parent by hash, a signed commit also fixes the exact content of the whole history before it.

## Decision

### D1: The decision records are vtium's provenance record

Each decision record keeps the maintainer's direction in the maintainer's own words, with the date and the channel the words came through. This is how vtium works now, and it stays that way. vtium adds no archive of the session transcripts, no hashes of them, and no rule that every entry be verbatim. An agent that records the maintainer's words follows the record tool as it does now: it quotes a written channel verbatim, and it marks a summary with `paraphrase: true`.

### D2: The quotes in the records mark the maintainer's own expression

When the maintainer writes or rewrites something, the quote kept in a record is the mark of the maintainer's own expression. vtium adds no commit trailer and no separate marking for it.

### D3: The maintainer stays the git author, and COPYING discloses how the code is written

The maintainer stays the author and the signer of every commit, as the person who directs, reviews and signs the work. Agents do not get a git identity of their own, and their commits carry no trailer. COPYING and this record say plainly that agents write vtium's code under the maintainer's direction and review. COPYING points to this record.

### D4: Every commit is signed with the maintainer's SSH key from 2026-11-10 on

Signing began at 2026-11-10T13:19:37-06:00, and the first signed vtium commit is 2589ab1e. The maintainer's git configuration signs each commit and tag. A pre-push hook in `tools/git-hooks/` refuses a push that carries an unsigned commit made after signing began. Commits made before that moment keep their history as it is.

### D5: The records show where vtium's shape came from

The maintainer set vtium's shape: the idea of vtium, frits (vtium's font set files), the configuration as one flow, the way character sets compose, and the choice of a unified image pipeline, among other choices. The maintainer observes that an agent given only a request to replace kmscon in Rust would not have arrived at that shape. The records carry the evidence. Their entries show the design's turns, choices, rejections and redirections, in the maintainer's words, each with its date.

That evidence establishes three things, under the research. It shows that the maintainer directed and controlled the work, and how. It is a contemporaneous, dated record of the creative process, which is what the Copyright Office and several courts outside the United States looked for when they ruled on AI-assisted work. It supports a claim to the selection, coordination and arrangement of vtium as a whole.

It does not establish three other things. Copyright does not protect ideas, systems or methods of operation, even when a person creates them alone (17 U.S.C. § 102(b)), so most design decisions are not protected expression. The Copyright Office's report Copyright and Artificial Intelligence, Part 2: Copyrightability (29 January 2025) holds that choosing or approving an agent's output is not authorship: "authorship by adoption does not in itself provide a basis for claiming copyright in AI-generated outputs." The Supreme Court held in Community for Creative Non-Violence v. Reid (1989) that a party who conceives a work and directs it in detail is not, by that alone, its author.

## What vtium's provenance establishes, and its limits

The research gives a confidence for each point below. It is research, not legal advice.

- **Code that agents wrote is probably not protected in the United States.** Code that agents wrote and the maintainer only chose or approved is probably not protected by copyright there. Confidence: medium-high. The Copyright Office's statement of policy Copyright Registration Guidance: Works Containing Material Generated by Artificial Intelligence (16 March 2023) and its report of 29 January 2025 both lead there.
- **The maintainer's strongest claims are narrower.** They are to text the maintainer writes and that the work carries verbatim, to changes the maintainer makes by hand, and possibly to the selection and arrangement of the work as a whole. The last claim is untested for software and is likely thin. Confidence: low to medium for the arrangement.
- **The MIT license still works.** Where a part of vtium has no copyright, the license has nothing to grant for that part, and nobody can enforce its one condition there. Users lose nothing, because they could use that part freely anyway. Confidence: high.
- **The Copyright Office has no guidance specific to code.** Its 2023 guidance applies to every kind of work, computer programs included, and its 2025 report mentions code only in passing. Confidence: medium.
- **No court has drawn the line.** Thaler v. Perlmutter (D.C. Circuit, 18 March 2025) holds that copyright needs a human author, and that work made with an AI's help can still be protected. It left open how much human contribution is enough, and the Supreme Court declined to review it on 2 March 2026. Allen v. Perlmutter, the one United States case that tests the line for AI-assisted work, was undecided on 5 November 2026. Confidence: high for the holdings, medium that Allen is still undecided.
- **The records are evidence of authorship, not authorship itself.** Agents write a record's prose. Only the quoted words in it are the maintainer's.
- **A signature shows approval, not authorship.** A signature shows that the holder of the maintainer's key approved the history at that point. It does not show who wrote the code. Its date comes from the signer's own clock.

### A measured weakness

The research counted the entries attributed to the maintainer in vtium's 29 decision records on 2026-11-10. 395 of the 648 entries, 61%, are agents' paraphrases, not the maintainer's words. A paraphrase still shows the maintainer's direction and ideas, but it is weak evidence of the maintainer's expression. The maintainer chose to keep the practice as it is.

### Questions only a lawyer can answer

- **The wording of the notice.** LICENSE and COPYING claim copyright in the software as a whole for 2026. Whether that notice, and COPYING's statement of how the code is written, should name the human-authored parts, the arrangement of the work and the AI-written material separately is a lawyer's question.
- **Registration and its duty to disclose.** A United States registration is needed before an infringement suit (17 U.S.C. § 411(a)). The Office's 2023 guidance requires an applicant to disclose AI-generated material that is more than de minimis, to exclude it from the claim, and to describe the human author's contribution. A registration with knowingly inaccurate information risks being disregarded (17 U.S.C. § 411(b)). Whether to register vtium, when, and what the application would say needs a lawyer.

## Consequences

### Positive

- No change gains a step. Agents keep recording the maintainer's words as they do now, and git signs each commit by itself.
- Every commit from 2026-11-10 on carries the maintainer's signature, and each signature fixes the history before it.
- COPYING says how vtium's code is written, so the git author field does not mislead a reader of COPYING.
- The record stands on its own. No other record changes with it.

### Negative

- 61% of the maintainer's entries stay paraphrases, the weakest kind of entry as evidence of expression.
- Git's author field names the maintainer on commits that agents wrote. A reader of the history alone, without COPYING, could be misled.
- The session transcripts that hold the maintainer's typed words sit outside the repository, unsigned and unversioned.
- A signature's date rests on the maintainer's own clock, and the history before 2026-11-10 carries no signature.

### Neutral

- AGENTS.md's section on signed commits gives agents the rules for D4: an agent never turns signing off and never skips the hooks.
- COPYING gains one sentence that points to this record.

## Alternatives Considered

- **Keep only verbatim quotes, archive and hash the session transcripts, or publish them.** The research recommends these as harder to dispute than paraphrases. Rejected by the maintainer, who keeps the records as they are now.
- **Mark the maintainer's hand-written text, with a `Written-by:` trailer or the record tool's `authored` level.** Rejected by the maintainer: the quotes kept in the records are the mark.
- **Name agents in git, as the commit author or in a trailer on each agent commit.** Rejected by the maintainer, who stays the author and signer and discloses in COPYING. The Linux kernel dropped its optional `Assisted-by:` trailer on 13 October 2026 because about 10% of its commits carried it while far more used such tools, so a trailer's absence said nothing about how a patch was made.
- **Leave commits unsigned.** Rejected: a signature ties each commit and the history before it to the maintainer's key.
