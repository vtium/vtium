# vtium-render

vtium's renderer: frames from `vtium-term` in, display pixels out. This crate holds the CPU compositor (vtium/vtium#16), which ADR-1300 decides: a drawn-glyph cache, decorations as sprites, the cursor's shapes, the pointer, four orientations, damage handed to the display as clips, and the render timer. It forbids unsafe code and denies the determinism lints. It draws into anything that takes a run of pixels (`Target`), never reads it, and opens no device: the display backends are vtium/vtium#18. The GPU path's frame and the `Gl` trait it draws through are behind the `gpu` feature (vtium/vtium#33, ADR-1303).

## Behaviour

The C reference is the floor: its `bbulk.c`, `deco.c`, `drm2d_render.c` and `render_timer.c`. vtium keeps their flow and their per-pixel arithmetic, so that tier 2's bitmap-font goldens (vtium/vtium#17) judge it unchanged; `tests/reference.rs` and `src/deco.rs` hold it to digests of whole cells and of every sprite at 1,152 cell sizes, taken from the C reference's own code (`tests/reference/README.md`), but for the doubles and curls the quarter-cell departure below redraws. ADR-1300 records the six ways the cache and composition differ from the C reference's, none of which moves a pixel but the blank cells of the first departure below.

`draw` follows the frame contract with the displays (vtium/vtium#18): it takes the buffer the display lends and the buffer's age, 1 when it holds the last frame drawn with damage, n when it holds the frame n back, 0 when its contents are unknown. Each cell carries the serial of the frame that last changed what it shows, its cursor (place, shape and color), blink phase and wide pairing included, and a buffer at age n gets the cells that changed in the last n frames. At age 0, or at an age past the renderer's history, the buffer gets the whole picture and the border, and every other buffer's history stays as it was. Nothing assumes the buffers take turns or counts them, so a swap that failed, a third buffer and a buffer the display lost each get exactly what they lack. The frame is read since the checkpoint taken after the renderer's previous `draw`, whatever buffer that drew into.

Dim text (SGR 2) draws its foreground halfway to its background, each channel the sum of the two halved, as the C reference drew it. Dim mixes the colors reverse video left, and the cursor draws over the result: a block swaps the mix with the background. An underline or strike in the foreground's color takes the mix; SGR 58's underline color is exact. Test: `tests/dim.rs`.

Blinking text (SGR 5 and 6) draws in the text blink's off phase as `Config::blink` says, which the binary sets from `blink-text` and `blink-decorations`. `Partial`, the default, draws the glyph halfway to its background with SGR 2's arithmetic, over a glyph SGR 2 already dimmed too; `Full` draws the cell without its glyph; `Off` draws it steady, and a phase change redraws nothing. With `decorations`, the underline and strike hide with the glyph under `Full` and dim with it under `Partial`, SGR 58's color and a configured color included; without, they stay as the on phase draws them. The cell under the cursor shows in either phase. Tests: `tests/blink.rs` and `every_frame_matches_a_whole_draw_under_each_blink_option`.

The renderer draws the pointer only when `DrawState::pointer` is set, which the owner does on a display without a cursor plane; where the display drives a cursor plane, the plane shows the same arrow (`Renderer::arrow`) at the place `Renderer::pointer_at` gives, and moving the pointer involves no draw. The arrow is a cell tall, kept between 8 and `POINTER_SIDE_MAX` pixels, white on a dark outline with its hotspot at the tip, and turns with the display (`src/arrow.rs`). With `Config::pointer` naming a character (`pointer-char`), the pointer is that character's glyph from the font, one cell wide, white on the same outline, its top left on the corner of the cell under the pointer wherever in the cell the pointer is; a character the font lacks, which would draw as a fallback such as U+FFFD, and a blank one, whose glyph covers nothing, keep the arrow, and over cells so tall that the outlined glyph passes `POINTER_SIDE_MAX`, the cursor plane refuses it and the renderer draws it. Tests: `a_glyph_pointer_sits_on_the_cell_under_the_pointer`, `a_glyph_pointer_the_font_cannot_show_keeps_the_arrow`. It is blended over each run of pixels written beneath it, cells and border alike, so the cells show around it and nothing is read back. It changes the cells it covers only when it appears, moves or hides, and a cell under it that changes is drawn alone, with the arrow over it. A frame that changes nothing, pointer shown or not, writes nothing and returns no damage; the owner does not swap it, and it does not count as a frame. A change of grid size or of the default background changes every cell and the border.

A link (OSC 8) draws nothing of its own until the pointer is over it (ADR-1002, "Drawing"). Every cell of the link under the pointer, which the frame marks `hovered`, draws a dashed underline in place of its own underline style, in the color its own underline would take: SGR 58's where set, otherwise the configured underline color or the foreground. The frame marks the cells of the old and the new link changed when the hover moves, so the dashes come and go with the cells' own redraws. Tests: `tests/hover.rs`; tier 2's `link-hover-unifont` and `link-hover-unifont-1bit`. The overlay row that shows the link's address while Ctrl is held is not drawn yet: ADR-1002 leaves open what the rest of that row shows.

The console draws two things of its own over a display's frame, outside the terminal (ADR-1401 D5, D7): a `Notice`, vtium's message in a box across the top of the view, its lines wrapped to the box and kept clear of the mark, in the colors its owner gives (`Colors::NOTICE` for a problem, `Colors::STATUS` for the on-screen display's line alone), and a head's focus `Mark` in the grid cell its owner gives it, a filled or hollow circle, or a filled or hollow square from a font that lacks the circles. The console draws both with the built-in fonts. Both turn with the display and take the 1-bit mapping and stand-ins a cell takes; neither touches the renderer's record of the cells. When what the console drew over a frame changes, the owner gives the areas it covered to `Renderer::redraw_area`, which marks the cells and the border under them changed on the next frame, so each buffer the display lends draws them again, whatever its age (ADR-1401 D2). Tests: `src/notice.rs`, and `an_area_drawn_over_is_redrawn_in_every_buffer` in `tests/damage.rs`.

### Pictures

The `images` feature draws a frame's image placements (ADR-1100 D4, D5, D9; vtium/vtium#30). The binary turns it on with `vtium-term/images` and vtium-image, and the minspec build leaves it out: without it the crate draws exactly what it drew before, and the minspec binary is the same size. The binary hands the renderer nothing new. A frame built with vtium-term's `Frame::with_placements` carries its placements, and `draw` reads them; `DRAWS_PLACEMENTS` says whether a build draws them, which a head needs before it offers sixel or kitty graphics (ADR-1100 D10), and `Renderer::picture_counts` reports what the scaled-copy cache did.

- **The layers.** A cell that no picture's pixels reach is drawn exactly as without the feature. A cell that one reaches is composed in the row scratch from the bottom up, in ADR-1100 D4's order: the cell's background, with the layer below backgrounds (kitty z below -1,073,741,824) over it only where the frame cell's `default_bg` says the background is the default; the layer below text (kitty z from -1,073,741,824 to -1); the cell's text layer, premultiplied, as a cell of several layers composes it (ADR-1300, "Carried forward"); the layer above text (kitty z of 0 or more, and every sixel); and last vtium's mark. Within a layer the pictures draw in the order the frame hands them, which is the store's stack order. A picture's premultiplied pixels go over what lies beneath with the same arithmetic as the text layer, `src + div255(dst * (255 - a))`. An opaque picture over the whole cell hides what lies under it, and the cell starts from it, as the C reference's `compose_covered` started from the highest layer that hid the rest.
- **The cursor and the pointer draw above every picture, and the selection tints it.** A block cursor covers its whole cell, so that cell is drawn as if no picture were there. A selected cell is composed with its pictures, and then the selection's color goes over the whole cell at `SELECTION_TINT`, 128 out of 255, so the cell shows both the selection and the picture under it (the maintainer, 2026-11-01); a cursor in a selected cell lies above the tint. A bar cursor's bar and an underline cursor's line are drawn again over the pictures; under the bar the cell's letter is not drawn, as without pictures. The pointer's arrow is blended as each line is written, and vtium's notices are drawn over the buffer after the renderer, so both lie above every picture too.
- **A kitty Unicode placeholder cell draws no text.** A cell whose cluster starts with U+10EEEE marks where the picture of a virtual placement draws (ADR-1100 D13), so the renderer draws its background and no glyph, diacritics included, and no underline, strike-through, or the dashed underline of a hovered link. The cursor and the selection draw over it as over any cell. A cluster that holds U+10EEEE after another character is text. Without the feature U+10EEEE is a private-use character like any other. Tests: `tests/placeholder.rs`.
- **Scaling and turning.** A placement's pixel values are in the cell it was made in. A value `m` measured from the grid's origin lands at `floor(m * cell / made)` in the renderer's cell, so the pieces of one picture meet with no gap and no overlap at any cell size (ADR-1100 D8). A picture is cut at its box and at the grid. Scaling is the C reference's integer filter (kmscon `src/render/picture.c`): 16-bit fixed-point weights that sum to exactly 65,536 on each axis, a box filter where a picture shrinks and bilinear where it grows. A copy is made in two passes, as the C reference's was: each source row is filtered across once, into a bounded ring of rows, and each output pixel then sums its rows down and is rounded once. The sums are whole numbers, so a picture scales to the same pixels on every machine, and a picture scaled for one frame alone, a pixel at a time, gets the same bytes. A turned display draws the unturned picture turned, as it draws the grid.
- **The scaled-copy cache.** A picture drawn at its own size is read straight from the image's shared pixels, along each display line with a fixed step through the image on a turned display. Every other picture is drawn from a copy scaled and turned for the display, made when a cell under it is first drawn, and kept in a cache of the display's own, outside the image budget, so a copy never evicts a stored image (ADR-1100 D5). The key is the whole picture: the image, the frame shown and its pixel revision, the image's size, the placement's crop (the part of the image it shows), the size it is drawn at and the turn. The frame's entry carries the crop, the drawn size and the picture's offset in its box for the whole placement, so the pieces a cut leaves and a picture scrolling off the top all share one copy, and a copy never reads past its crop. The cache holds at most `PICTURE_CACHE_SCREENS` screens of 4-byte pixels. A copy that needs room evicts the copy placed longest ago that no placement of the frame shows, ties going by key, and only when that makes it fit; a copy that still does not fit is scaled for the frame alone and not kept, with the pixels a kept copy gives, so the cache changes speed and never pixels (ADR-1300 D3).
- **Animated pictures.** The entry of a kitty animation's placement carries the frame it shows (ADR-1102 D3), and the renderer draws that frame's pixels as it draws any picture's. The store fills in the image's current frame, the one `a=a` with `c` made current, or the root frame while no command has chosen another. The binary's animation clock will put the frame it chooses on the entry with `FramePlacement::show`, but no clock does so yet, so today every animated placement draws its current frame. The image's key, the frame's number and the frame's pixel revision name the pixels: the store never reuses an image's key, and gives a frame a new pixel revision whenever a command changes what it shows. The renderer keys both its copies and its record of what it last drew on all three, so a placement that moves to another frame, or whose frame a command changed, draws its cells again from the new pixels and never from another frame's copy. The cache keeps at most one copy of each frame of a picture at each size it is drawn: when it makes a copy of a frame at a newer pixel revision, it drops the copies of that frame's older revisions that no placement in the draw shows, since the store never shows them again. An animation whose copies all fit in the cache is scaled only in its first pass through its frames. An animation whose copies do not all fit is scaled again in every draw that shows it: each draw scales a whole copy and evicts another, as a picture replaced in every draw is scaled, and its copies stay within `PICTURE_CACHE_SCREENS` screens.
- **One image store for each renderer.** The cache's keys and the damage history name images and placements by their `ImageKey` and `PlacementKey`, which each console's store numbers from zero. A renderer therefore draws the frames of one store, one console, for its life, and the binary gives each console its own renderer, or a new renderer when a display starts showing another console; frames of two stores through one renderer could take one picture's copy or history for another's.
- **Damage.** The renderer keeps each placement as it last drew it, and for each placement that arrived, left or changed it draws the cells of its old box and its new box again, in every buffer at its age. A picture that moves or changes therefore redraws only the cells it covers. vtium-term also marks the cells under each change the store makes (ADR-1001), so the two meet; the renderer's own rule also covers a placement list that changed while the frame's cells did not.
- **Malformed and huge placements.** A placement with a zero size, a zero cell, a source rectangle outside its image or pixels of the wrong count draws nothing. A picture larger than the cache, such as one fitted to the store's largest box of 10,000 cells a side, is scaled for each frame only in the cells the grid shows.

- **What pictures cost.** These are machine figures, printed and not pinned, from a release build on the development machine on 2026-11-02, while other agents' builds kept its load average at about 10 on 32 cores. At 1366x768 in 8x16 cells, a full redraw takes a median of 0.62 ms with no picture, 0.59 ms under one full-screen picture at its own size, 0.59 ms under one scaled to the screen from its kept copy, 5.40 ms for text over one full-screen picture below text, and 0.61 ms with a ten-cell picture. Cells side by side under one opaque picture that nothing draws over are copied from the picture as a run, each display line of the run one copy, which is why a full-screen picture costs about what a screen of text does; a cell whose text lies over a picture composes each line from its layers, which is the 5.40 ms. A frame where only a ten-cell picture moves a column draws its 12 old and new cells in 0.012 ms. Scaling the full-screen picture takes about 7 ms more, on the frame that makes its copy. A picture replaced every frame, as a video player sends it, pays that every frame: a full-screen picture at its own size takes 0.58 ms a frame at 1366x768 and 13.7 ms at 7680x2160 unturned (2.0 and 46.8 ms on a display turned a quarter), and one scaled to twice its size 6.7 ms and 136 ms (7.4 and 153 ms turned). The commands are `cargo test --release -p vtium-render --features images --test picture_figures -- --ignored --nocapture`, and `--test picture_video` for a picture replaced every frame.

Tests: `tests/pictures.rs`, `tests/picture_frames.rs`, `tests/picture_goldens.rs`, and the scaler's, the cache's and the frame-alone scaling's in `src/picture/`.

### The GPU path

The `gpu` feature adds the GPU path's frame. It is off by default: the minspec build and a default build compile none of it and link no GL. `cargo test -p vtium-render --features gpu` runs its tests.

vtium-render forbids unsafe code, so it never calls GL itself. It draws through the `Gl` trait, which speaks the renderer's terms: textures of a stated format and size, filled a rectangle at a time; the upright frame and an imported display buffer as targets to draw into; programs compiled from vtium-render's own shader source with named inputs; a batch of quads drawn through a program; the GPU's limits; and a read of a rectangle back to memory, for tests. The binary implements the trait with an adapter over vtium-platform's safe GL type, which holds the unsafe calls to GL, EGL and GBM (ADR-1303 D7).

Every handle the trait gives out, a texture, a target or a program, is an owned value that names the context that made it. A handle cannot be copied, and freeing it consumes it. Each implementation keeps a `Ledger` of its context's live handles and passes every call through it before the call reaches GL. This is the one list of what the ledger refuses:

- a handle another context made, or one that was freed;
- a texture or upright frame past the GPU's largest texture or viewport, and a display buffer past its largest renderbuffer or viewport;
- an upload outside its texture, short of its bytes, or with rows a part of a pixel apart;
- a program that lists an input twice, or whose shaders declare no uniform of the input's name and type, or declare it as an array, and any program on a GPU without `highp` in fragment shaders;
- a draw that gives one of its program's inputs no value, a value twice, a value of the wrong kind, or a value for an input the program lacks, and a draw that reads the texture it draws into;
- a read outside its target, or into a buffer of the wrong length;
- every call once the context is lost.

A real driver refuses more than the ledger can see, and only a real GL shows those refusals: a shader that does not compile or a program that does not link, a uniform the driver dropped because nothing uses it (its location is -1), and a texture or framebuffer that is incomplete. The stand-in compiles any text it is given. Part 2 of slice 0, which builds the adapter, proves the shaders compile and link on llvmpipe. The ledger never gives a serial out twice, so a freed handle never names a newer object, even where GL reuses the freed object's name. The upright frame is a target that holds its own texture, and freeing the target frees the texture.

GL reports most of its errors late, through an error flag. The renderer reads that flag only at the boundaries ADR-1303 D7 names, through `Gl::check`: once after a batch of allocations and uploads, once after the composition's draws and once at the end of a frame, and only when the batch made a call, so a frame that changes nothing makes no call at all. A check after every call would make a threaded driver wait for the GPU each time; the C reference's gltex checked once a frame, after its draws (`gltex.c:2337-2396`). Each error carries its kind, out of memory, a lost context, another GL error, a refused handle, a limit, a misuse, a failed compile or a failed import, and the batch whose check found it, so the head can sort it as ADR-1303 D9 decides. A frame that drew ends with `Gl::submit`, which sends its queued commands to the GPU before the display flips the buffer; the adapter chooses how, such as `glFlush` or an EGL fence.

`StandIn` is the tests' GL. It keeps a ledger as the adapter does, so it refuses every call the list above names, records the calls that pass and draws no pixels. A test can give it smaller limits, to drive a frame past the largest texture; can raise a GL error that the next check reports; and can make the next texture, frame, import or compile fail with a kind it chooses (`StandIn::fail_next`), as a driver would. Pixels are judged on a real GL, Mesa's llvmpipe, in tier 2, once the composition and present draw.

`Renderer::draw_gpu` draws a frame on the GPU path. The renderer decides what the frame draws through the same front end as `Renderer::draw`: where the grid sits, which cells changed and how each one looks, its cursor, blink and decorations included, which parts of the border are stale, and where the pointer is. Only the back end differs. The CPU path composes each row's cell jobs in the row scratch and writes them into the display's buffer; the GPU path turns each job into one quad through the cell shader, and the border's stale parts, less the grid, into clears. The GPU path composes into the upright frame, which keeps the last frame, so the renderer treats it as a buffer one frame old, or at age 0 when the pipeline composes it whole: when it is new, after a failed frame, for a new border color, or when another renderer's glyphs were in the pipeline. A frame the CPU path draws between two GPU frames moves the renderer's history on while the upright frame stays, so the renderer takes a new identity, and its next GPU frame replaces the pipeline's glyphs and draws whole. `draw_gpu` refuses a 1-bit display until present's depth reduction is built. Present then takes what the display's buffer lacks over its own age. `draw_gpu` takes the console's notices and focus mark as `Placed` pictures, which `Notice::upright` and `Mark::upright` give: each is uploaded whole when it is new or changed since the last frame, and otherwise over what the frame drew beneath it; a notice the last frame drew and this one does not has the cells beneath it drawn again, as `redraw_area` draws them on the CPU path. Tests: `tests/gpu_compose.rs`.

The cell shader (`src/gpu/glsl/cell.frag`) draws a cell with the CPU path's integer arithmetic, `src/blend.rs`, as ADR-1300 D6 decides and the maintainer confirmed on 2026-11-03: every value a whole number in a `highp` float, `div255` with its shifts written as floors of divisions by 256, texels read whole with `texelFetch`, no dithering, and each channel `c` written as `(c + 0.25) / 255`. A quad carries the cell's foreground and the glyph's kind, its background, and its underline's and strike's colors and sprites, each kind in the top byte of a color. A cell of one layer is `mask` or `over_bg`; a cell of several composes its underline, glyph and strike premultiplied and then goes over its background, as `cell_line` does.

The glyphs live in ADR-1300's four pools, masks and color glyphs one or two cells wide, under the same budgets as the CPU path's. The glyph cache is generic over what it holds: the CPU path keeps a glyph's pixels in it, the GPU path a slot in a pool's textures, so both share one key, one LRU and one budget check. A slot is the glyph's cells, and a pool lays its slots out as a grid, masks in R8 and color glyphs in RGBA8; its first texture holds 64 slots and each later one twice the one before, up to `GLYPH_TEXTURE_SIDE` pixels a side. A slot is filled once, from the glyph as the CPU path stores it, read a line at a time at the size it is drawn, so its pixels are the CPU path's. A glyph the budget has no room for, one that would evict a glyph this frame draws, draws from a slot held for the frame and given back at its end (ADR-1300 D3), and the next frame asks the budget again, so a pool refused takes the glyph once the frame no longer draws all it holds. The decoration sprites are one R8 texture, each kind a cell. A new renderer, made for a new font or cell size, replaces the pipeline's glyphs and draws whole. Tests: `a_pool_its_budget_refuses_draws_from_a_held_slot_and_takes_the_glyph_once_the_budget_allows`, `a_pool_past_its_first_texture_takes_another_twice_its_size`, `a_new_renderer_on_the_pipeline_replaces_the_glyphs_and_draws_whole`.

The pointer's arrow is drawn upright (`Renderer::upright_arrow`, `Arrow::upright`) as one texture, uploaded when its image changes. It is drawn over the pixels the frame drew beneath it and no others, as quads cut to them a line at a time, the lines alike joined, since the upright frame keeps earlier frames' arrows where it drew nothing. In each tile the pixels beneath those quads are first copied out into the copy described under pictures below, and the arrow's shader (`glsl/arrow.frag`) goes over them with the CPU path's `over_bg` in whole numbers and writes each channel as `(c + 0.25) / 255`, so its outline, black at 220 of 255, lands on the CPU path's byte on any driver. The GPU's own blending is not used. Tests: `the_arrow_is_one_texture_drawn_over_a_copy_of_what_the_frame_drew_beneath_it`, `the_arrow_covers_each_drawn_pixel_under_it_once`, and in the binary's adapter `the_pointers_arrow_on_llvmpipe_is_the_cpu_paths_byte_for_byte`, which judges the arrow's pixels on llvmpipe, since no tier 2 screen draws the arrow.

The glyph cache, on both paths, remembers up to `FAILED_GLYPHS` glyphs the font drew nothing for, and does not ask the font for them again until `glyphs_changed` or `forget_glyphs` names any character: the font draws nothing for a glyph only after its whole fallback chain, U+25A1, `?` and its last resort, drew nothing, so any arrival may give it a glyph, and the table empties. The C reference's GPU renderer remembered such glyphs for 600 frames, since its font could fail for want of memory; vtium's font draws nothing only for a glyph it cannot draw, so the table keeps no expiry. A glyph refused for room is not remembered. Tests: `a_glyph_the_font_draws_nothing_for_is_asked_for_once_until_forgotten`, `a_glyph_the_font_draws_nothing_for_is_not_asked_for_every_frame`.

The renderer makes its underline and strike sprites, and the 1-bit stand-ins' line thickness, from the font's lines (`Face::decorations`), and takes them again when they change. An outline font's renderer is made before the glyph helper has measured the font's lines, so the font first gives lines from the cell's height and the measured ones later. `glyphs_changed` and `forget_glyphs` each compare the font's lines with the ones the sprites were made from. When they differ, the renderer makes the sprites again and empties the glyph cache, since the font draws its line sprites, the box-drawing characters among them, in the underline's thickness, and its next frame draws every cell and the border. On the GPU path the renderer takes a new identity, so the pipeline makes its glyphs and the sprites' texture anew and composes whole. Lines that did not change change nothing. Tests: `tests/lines.rs`.

The upright frame is held in tiles that `tiles` gives (ADR-1303 D2, D3): one function that, given an image's size, the largest tile and an overlap, gives each tile's held part and the part it owns, up to the middle of each overlap. The upright frame's largest tile is the smaller of the largest texture and the largest viewport, so a display that fits is one tile, the common case, and one tile and many take the same path. Its overlap is twice the spatial step's reach, so that each pixel a tile owns has the reach of neighbours on each side in that tile; with no spatial step it is 0, and the tiles meet edge to edge. The composition clears and draws each rectangle into every tile it meets, with the tile's offset taken off its quads, and present draws from each tile the part of the buffer it owns. Tests: `src/gpu/tile.rs`, `a_display_past_the_largest_texture_is_held_in_tiles`, `a_display_past_the_largest_texture_draws_each_cell_into_each_tile_it_meets`.

A display's frame passes through the `Pipeline`'s five slots in a fixed order: pictures at another size, composition into the upright frame, color, spatial effects, and present into the display's buffer (ADR-1303 D1). A slot with nothing to do holds a no-op that makes no GL call, joins no shader source and changes no pixel. The color and spatial slots hold only their no-ops until the frame shader's record defines their steps; a step that is switched on joins its GLSL into present's fragment shader, between its head and its `main`, when the pipeline is set up, so an empty slot adds no pass and no instruction. `Spatial::reach` gives how far the spatial step reads, which sets the overlap of the upright frame's tiles, and `Spatial::presents_whole` whether every present draws the whole buffer.

The pipeline's set-up makes the upright frame, the display's size with its sides swapped for a quarter turn, in RGBA8 in its tiles, compiles the cell shader, the arrow's and present's, and checks once. Each frame's `Work` names the rectangles cleared to the border's color, the cells, the arrow and the notices, and the rectangles that changed; the composition draws the whole upright frame when it is new or the border's color changes, as the CPU path draws whole when the background changes. GL's errors are read after the frame's uploads (glyph slots and the arrow's texture), after the composition's draws and uploads of notices, and at the frame's end. Present then turns into the display's buffer what that buffer lacks: the rectangles composed over the buffer's age, or the whole buffer at age 0 or at an age past `PRESENT_HISTORY` frames, whether or not this frame composed anything, as the CPU path draws the whole picture into a buffer at age 0, and one whole rectangle when what the buffer lacks includes the whole frame. A frame that composes nothing into a buffer that lacks nothing makes no call, and does not count for the ages that follow. Present takes its size from the buffer, and the pipeline refuses a buffer of another size than the display's. Present reads each pixel of the upright frame by integer coordinates, with no filtering (`texelFetch`). A frame that fails leaves the upright frame and the buffers unknown, so the next frame composes and presents whole (ADR-1303 D3, D9).

A turned display draws on the GPU path as on the CPU path (ADR-1303 D3, D6). The front end works in the display's own pixels on both paths, so the renderer's record of the cells, the border and the pointer is the same whichever path drew the last frame, and `pointer_at` gives the cursor plane's place on either. The GPU path's back end maps each rectangle the front end gives, a cell, its glyph's place, a piece of the border and the arrow's place, into the upright frame through `Turn`, which maps a pixel as the CPU path turns it: a display turned right draws the upright pixel `x`, `y` at `width - 1 - y`, `x`. The grid therefore sits in the upright frame where the CPU path puts it on the display, mapped back, and an odd margin's spare pixel lands where the CPU path leaves it once present turns the frame. The C reference's gltex centred a turned grid in its own rotated axes and drew it a pixel away from bbulk's. The arrow and the notices are drawn upright, the arrow at its turned place mapped back and each notice at its place in the text's view, and a notice that ends hands the renderer its area mapped onto the display. Present draws one quad for each tile and rectangle, its destination the display's rectangle and its source the tile's texels under the rectangle's upright top left, with the turn in its first parameter. Its fragment shader turns each display pixel's offset within the quad into the upright offset by integer arithmetic and reads that texel with `texelFetch`. The frame's damage is the rectangles present drew, in the display's pixels. Tests: `tests/gpu_turn.rs`, which reads present's quads as its shader does and compares where the grid, the pointer, the border and a notice land with the CPU path's drawing of the same screen at every turn, with 5 pixels to spare on each axis; and `src/gpu/turn.rs`.

A turn keeps the GPU path's glyphs, the arrow's texture and every program (ADR-1303 D3, D9). `Renderer::turned` gives the same renderer turned: a new renderer on the CPU path, whose cache holds turned glyphs, and on the GPU path the same identity, so the pipeline keeps the glyphs it holds for it, with the budgets their pools were made with; the decoration sprites the GPU path uploads are always upright. `turned` first takes the font's lines, as `glyphs_changed` does, so lines that changed before a turn reach the GPU path's sprites. The renderer's next GPU frame turns the pipeline (`Pipeline::turn`), which composes the upright frame whole and presents the whole buffer. A half turn, and any turn of a square display, makes no GL call beyond that frame's. A quarter turn of a display that is not square swaps the upright frame's sides, so it makes the frame's tiles anew at the new shape, one batch of allocations checked before the old tiles are freed, and compiles nothing; a turn whose allocation or check fails frees what it made and leaves the pipeline as it was, its orientation and its frame's shape together. `Pipeline::turned` sets up a pipeline for a display already turned. The C reference's gltex dropped and rebuilt all its GL state on a turn, and a turn whose set-up failed left the screen frozen. Tests: `a_turn_keeps_every_texture_and_compiles_nothing`, `a_quarter_turn_of_a_display_that_is_not_square_makes_only_the_upright_frame_anew`, `a_turn_whose_check_fails_leaves_the_pipeline_as_it_was`, `a_turn_whose_allocation_fails_leaves_the_pipeline_as_it_was`, and `the_shaders_texel_is_the_one_the_tests_read`, which keeps the tests' Rust copy of present's `texel` in step with the shader's.

Slot 1 draws pictures on the GPU path, in a build with `images` (ADR-1303 D2). The renderer hands the pipeline every picture of the frame, in the frame's order, and where the frame draws each one; the composition draws a cell that pictures reach in ADR-1100 D4's layers, as the CPU path composes it: the cell's background, each picture over it from slot 1's textures, the cell's text layer premultiplied over the pictures beneath it before the first picture above the text, the selection's tint, and vtium's mark. The pictures beneath the last opaque one over the whole cell are not drawn there, and a block cursor's cell draws no picture, as on the CPU path. Two steps fill slot 1, chosen with `Pipeline::set_picture_step` and never by vtium.conf. GPU scaling, the default, uploads each picture's own pixels, the crop its placement shows, once, and the picture shader (`glsl/picture.frag`) scales them with the CPU path's filter in whole numbers: the same 16-bit weights, summed in two 32-bit parts and rounded once, so its pixels are the CPU path's byte for byte on any GL with OpenGL ES 3.0's integers. Two corners take the copy step in place of GPU scaling, though D2 says a picture is never drawn from the CPU copy for its size, which the maintainer agreed to on 2026-11-04 as extreme cases: a picture drawn wider or taller than 32,767 pixels, or from more than 65,535 source pixels across or down, past what the shader's 32-bit integers hold; and a picture whose tiles cannot overlap by twice the filter's reach because that overlap is at least the largest texture, such as an image 10,000 pixels wide shrunk into one 8-pixel cell on a GPU whose largest texture is 2,048 pixels, the least OpenGL ES 3.0 allows. Only a picture drawn wider or taller than any display, or shrunk by a factor larger than about half the largest texture's side in pixels, meets either. The copy step uploads the CPU path's scaled copy at the drawn size, read one texel a pixel. A picture past the largest texture is held in tiles that overlap by twice the filter's reach, and each drawn pixel is drawn from the tile that owns the source pixel under its centre; `Pipeline::limit_picture_textures` holds the tiles smaller for a test. The textures count against the display's bound, `PICTURE_CACHE_SCREENS` screens, through the cache the CPU path's copies use (`src/picture/copies.rs`), which evicts as it does for the CPU path and hands back what it lets go, which slot 1 frees in GL. Where the bound has no room for a picture's own pixels, the picture is drawn from its CPU copy, kept while the bound has room for it; where the copy has no room either, the part the frame draws is scaled on the CPU into the display's one scratch texture for that frame alone. An opaque picture replaces what lies beneath it. A picture that is not opaque, a cell's text layer over the pictures beneath it, vtium's mark and the selection's tint each need the pixels beneath them, and no draw reads its own target. So each such draw first copies the pixels beneath its quads out of the tile into the copy, and then draws with the shader built with `OVER` (or the tint), which reads them from the copy and goes over them with the CPU path's arithmetic: `over_px` with `div255` for the picture, the text layer and the mark, and `mask` for the tint. Each writes every channel as `(c + 0.25) / 255`, so the GPU stores the CPU path's byte whether it rounds or truncates. The GPU's own blending is not used there: its conversion of the blended float to a byte belongs to the driver, and GitHub's runner's llvmpipe (Mesa 25.2.8) came one level below `over_px` at 72 of 195,072 sampled pixels, where Mesa 26.2.6's llvmpipe matched every one. The copy is one target for each display, which the arrow uses too in a build without `images`, kept from frame to frame. A frame that draws over more pixels in one tile than it holds, in width or in height, makes it anew at the larger of each side, and a frame that draws over fewer makes nothing; it is given back once no draw has read it for `GPU_IDLE_FRAMES` frames, and at a release. `Pipeline::picture_counts` gives the textures' counts as `Renderer::picture_counts` gives the CPU path's copies'. Tests: `tests/gpu_pictures.rs`, among them `draws_over_a_copy_read_their_own_tiles_pixels_across_the_seams`, where a picture that is not opaque, its text layers and the arrow cross the seams of six tiles, and `the_copy_of_the_pixels_beneath_grows_only_when_a_frame_needs_more`; `the_copy_of_the_pixels_beneath_lasts_until_no_frame_reads_it_for_the_idle_frames`; and tier 2's picture screens on llvmpipe.

The GPU path gives back textures it no longer uses, counting frames and never wall time (the issue on GPU texture garbage collection). A glyph pool hands out its lowest free slot, so its glyphs gather in its first textures, and gives back its last texture once no glyph has held a slot there for `GPU_IDLE_FRAMES` frames; a picture's texture no placement showed for as many frames is given back too, and so is the copy of the pixels beneath when no draw read it. The pools and the sprites never hold more than `GLYPH_TEXTURE_CEILING` bytes of textures for a display: past it a pool takes no new texture, and a glyph it has no free slot for is drawn blank, as the C reference's gltex drew one past its hard stop (`gltex.c:1031-1041`), until the budget's evictions free a slot. That matches the C reference and differs from vtium's CPU path, which draws every glyph however full its cache, and from ADR-1303 D9's rule that vtium's own budgets change no pixel; the maintainer agreed on 2026-11-04, since only cells far larger than any font's reach the ceiling ("again, extreme cases"). `Pipeline::take_notes` gives the head a line for its log when a glyph is drawn blank, at most once in `GPU_IDLE_FRAMES` frames, as gltex logged its hard stop at most once in 600 frames, and holds at most one such line until the head takes it. `Pipeline::release` frees the glyph and picture textures, the arrow's, the scratch texture, the copy of the pixels beneath and the upright frame, keeps the programs and the context, and makes the next frame draw whole, after making the upright frame again at the display's size; the head calls it when its VT is released (ADR-1303 D9). Tests: `a_picture_texture_no_frame_shows_is_given_back_after_the_idle_frames`, `a_glyph_pools_emptied_texture_is_given_back_after_the_idle_frames`, `past_the_ceiling_a_pool_takes_no_texture_and_reuses_a_slot_given_back` and `a_release_frees_the_glyph_and_picture_textures_and_the_next_frame_draws_whole`.

The quads of every program reach the vertex shader as instances of a strip of four vertices, each quad's destination at attribute location 0 and its source and parameters at 1 and 2, with the corner taken from `gl_VertexID`. vtium-render draws only into textures and imported buffers, where GL's row 0 is the first row in memory. vtium-render calls that row the top, as a display buffer does. Tests: `tests/gpu_gl.rs`, `tests/gpu_pipeline.rs`, `tests/gpu_compose.rs` and the ledger's own in `src/gpu/ledger.rs`.

## Departures

- **A full cache draws the glyph uncached.** A glyph that would evict one the frame draws, or that alone passes its pool's byte ceiling, is drawn from the font without being cached. The C reference drew such a cell blank and drew it again on the next frame (bbulk.c:1426-1432), and its GPU renderer drew blank past its hard stop (gltex.c:860-930). No specification covers a cache. The reason: a blank cell makes the cache change pixels (ADR-1300 D3). Test: `tests/cache.rs`, every frame at a cache of 1 and 7 glyphs against a cache of none.
- **A wide glyph cut at the margin shows its left half in every orientation.** On a display turned left or upside down, the C reference showed the glyph's right half in a wide cell's first column when the display held no second (aaronsb/kmscon#44, not fixed in the C fork). No specification covers turned displays. The reason: a turned display draws the unturned picture turned. That holds for the grid, not for the border around it: an odd remainder is split rounding down in the display's own coordinates in every orientation, as the C reference's goldens do, so with 5 pixels to spare a display turned upside down leaves 3 at the text's left where an unturned one leaves 2. Tests: `every_orientation_draws_the_same_picture_turned` and `a_wide_glyph_cut_at_the_margin_shows_its_left_half`.
- **A full redraw reaches each buffer once.** After a full redraw the C reference drew every cell a third time, and reported it as damage (aaronsb/kmscon#81, not fixed in the C fork). The reason: each buffer gets what its age says it lacks, and a buffer drawn whole lacks nothing. Tests: `a_full_redraw_reaches_each_buffer_once` and `buffers_at_age_0_are_each_drawn_whole_once`.
- **The pointer is an arrow over the cells.** The C reference drew the font's I in the default colors, in a cell's box centred on the pointer that hid the cells beneath it (bbulk.c:1669-1700), and marked the two by two cells its centre fell among, so at an odd cell size its moves left pixels behind (bbulk.c:1583-1613); its hardware cursor was an I-beam (terminal.c:220-301). No specification covers a pointer's shape. vtium draws an arrow, the operator's decision on vtium/vtium#92, the same in software as on the cursor plane, with its tip on the pointer's pixel kept inside the grid; its damage is every cell it covers and, where it reaches past the grid, its area clipped to the display, in which only the border is filled again; the history keeps the last 16 such areas, and a buffer older than those redraws the whole border. Tests: `the_pointer_is_an_arrow_over_the_cells`, `the_pointer_turns_with_the_display`, `the_pointer_leaves_nothing_behind_at_odd_cell_sizes`, `a_move_in_the_bottom_row_damages_only_its_cells_and_the_arrow`.
- **A keystroke damages only the cells it changes.** At 7680x2160 the C reference redrew about 70% of the screen at 20-28 flips a second while a prompt waited for typing, from a cause never found (aaronsb/kmscon#156, not fixed in the C fork). vtium's damage is the cells it draws, and a keystroke draws the cell it wrote and the cells the cursor left and entered. Test: `a_keystroke_damages_only_the_cells_it_changes`.
- **An arrival redraws only the cells that show it.** The C reference had no arrival: it rasterised a glyph inside the frame that asked for it, and on a font change marked every cell of every buffer changed (`text.c:271-323`, `text.h:214-217`). vtium's generated glyphs arrive between frames (ADR-1200 D23), and `Renderer::glyphs_changed` drops from the cache only the glyphs whose text holds an arrived character, a cluster's included; the next frame scans only the rows that show one, reading their lines' text, and draws those cells in each buffer at its age. Every other glyph stays cached: an 80x25 screen of printable ASCII redrawn whole after an arrival for one character draws 1 glyph from the font where emptying the cache drew 95, a median of 120 µs where it took 186 µs in a release build on the development machine. Tests: `tests/swap_in.rs` and `forgetting_a_character_drops_each_glyph_whose_text_holds_it`.
- **An underline takes at most a quarter of the cell.** The C reference moved a double or curly underline up into the cell as far as its band needed (deco.c's `fit`), so in an 8-pixel cell, whose letters stand on row 6, both reached rows 5 to 7 (vtium/vtium#119). No specification covers an underline's shape. vtium gives an underline at most a quarter of the cell's rows, the most the C reference let a line's thickness take (font.c:370-408). The single, dotted and dashed lines are the font's line, which a face with no metrics of its own puts in the cell's bottom rows, one row below the letters at 8 pixels. A curl lowers its wave to fit, down to the C reference's 2 rows peak to peak. A double, or a curl that still does not fit, draws as the single line. A cell 12 rows tall or more keeps the C reference's double, and its curl when at most half as wide as tall; both hold for the lines `for_height` gives, and a face with lines of its own meets the same quarter at the sizes its own thickness sets. The reason: an underline drawn over the letters hides their lower rows, and at 8 pixels the double and curl took three of the cell's eight. A strike vtium-font scales by a whole factor brings the lines of the cell it draws (vtium/vtium#124): `for_height` of the scaled height when its own lines were `for_height` of its height, and its own lines scaled otherwise. The room is the drawn cell's, so an 8-pixel strike drawn at 16 pixels draws a 16-pixel cell's double. One whose own lines scale with it, two rows thick from row 14, draws its double as the 8-pixel single line scaled, and its curl as a wave 2 rows peak to peak in rows 12 to 15, which the 8-pixel cell had no room for. Of the C reference's sprite digests (`tests/reference/cells.tsv`), 616 are of sprites the rule redraws: the double at every width from 1 to 24 at heights 2 to 11 (240), and the curl at every width at heights 1 to 11, at widths 12 to 24 at heights 12 to 15, 16 to 24 at 16 to 19, 20 to 24 at 20 to 23, and 24 at 24 to 27 (376). At each of those, `sprites_match_the_c_reference` checks that the sprite differs from the C reference's and is the fallback: the single line, or a curl lowered to the cell's bottom quarter, as wavy, when 2 rows peak to peak and the line fit there. Tests: `every_underline_at_8_pixels_lies_in_the_bottom_row`, `no_underline_takes_more_than_a_quarter_of_the_cell`, `a_double_or_curl_with_no_room_draws_as_the_single_line`, `a_curl_lowers_its_wave_to_fit_before_it_flattens`, `a_scaled_strike_takes_the_room_of_the_cell_it_draws` and `sprites_match_the_c_reference`.
- **A dash runs from the cell's left edge for five eighths of it.** The C reference drew one dash a cell over its middle two thirds (deco.c:153), which at 8-pixel cells, `·██████·`, lined up exactly with each character. No specification covers a dash's shape. vtium draws the font's line from the cell's left edge for `round(5w/8)` pixels of a cell `w` wide, a half rounded up, at least 1 and, from 2 pixels wide, at most `w - 1`, so a gap remains; the rest of the cell is gap. At 8 pixels that is `█████···`. One sprite still serves a cell size, tiled across a wide cell. The reason is the operator's (2026-10-28): a dash a character reads as each letter's own underline. The C reference's sprite digests hold 1,104 dashes vtium redraws, every width from 2 to 24 at every height from 1 to 48; a 1-pixel cell's dash fills it in both, and the digests judge every other sprite. At each redrawn size `sprites_match_the_c_reference` checks that the sprite differs from the C reference's and is the dash. The `decorations` screen of `tests/reference/screens.tsv` holds two dashed cells, so its digest is the C reference's with its dash: `a_dashed_screen_differs_from_the_c_reference_only_in_its_dashes` draws it without the dashes, gives it the C reference's, and matches the digest, then gives it vtium's and matches what vtium draws. Goldens the C reference drew that show a dash list the dashed cells in `tools/tier2/departures.tsv` and `tools/tier3/departures.tsv`. Tests: `a_dash_runs_from_the_left_edge_for_five_eighths_of_the_cell`, `sprites_match_the_c_reference` and `a_dashed_screen_differs_from_the_c_reference_only_in_its_dashes`.
- **Blinking text dims in the off phase by default.** The C reference hid a blinking cell's glyph in the off phase and kept its underline and strike (bbulk.c:1521). The maintainer decided on 2026-11-03, after the first acceptance pass on a VT, that blinking text no longer disappears by default: vtium's default `blink-text` is `partial`, so in the off phase the glyph draws halfway to its background, as SGR 2 draws it. ECMA-48 says only that SGR 5 makes text blink, and it does not say how the off phase looks. `blink-text=full`, with `blink-decorations` off as it is by default, gives the C reference's look. Test: `the_default_blink_dims_the_text_halfway`.
- **A scaled picture is kept under the whole picture.** The C reference kept a scaled picture under the part of it the screen showed, so a picture scrolling off the top was scaled again every frame (aaronsb/kmscon#132 and aaronsb/kmscon#163). No specification covers a renderer's cache. vtium keys a copy on the whole picture, so a picture moved up a row each frame is scaled once, and a turned screen scrolling twelve pictures evicts only what its first frame evicted. Tests: `a_scaled_placement_moved_up_a_row_each_frame_is_scaled_once` and `a_turned_screen_scrolling_twelve_pictures_evicts_only_what_its_first_turn_evicted`.
- **A picture that changes damages only the cells it covers.** In tmux's grids of sixel panes the C reference damaged 97 to 100 percent of the screen per flip (aaronsb/kmscon#157). vtium draws again only the old and new cells of each placement that arrived, left or changed. Test: `a_picture_that_changes_or_moves_redraws_only_the_cells_it_covers`.
- **The cursor and the pointer draw above every picture** (ADR-1100 D4), **and the selection tints it** (the maintainer, 2026-11-01). The C reference gave them no place among its picture bands, so a picture above text covered all three. Test: `the_cursor_the_selection_and_the_pointer_draw_above_every_picture`.
- **No scaled copy evicts a stored picture.** The C reference counted its scaled pictures in the image budget, so a copy for a picture on the screen could evict pictures in the scrollback (kmscon `ADR-103`, Notes, Q1). <!-- adr-cite-ignore: kmscon's record --> vtium's copies live in the display's own cache, bounded at `PICTURE_CACHE_SCREENS` screens, the C reference's per-display limit (`image_budget_cache_limit`), and a copy past it is scaled for each frame alone (ADR-1100 D5). Tests: `the_scaled_copies_never_pass_their_bound` and `a_picture_scaled_for_one_frame_draws_its_kept_copys_pixels`.
- **The GPU path draws a picture with the CPU path's pixels** (ADR-1303 D2). The C reference's gltex sampled a scaled picture bilinear with no mipmaps, so a picture shrunk past 2:1 skipped texels and aliased, and at 1.6:1 it differed from bbulk's by up to 41 levels (`kmscon/src/render/gltex.c:2262-2302`); it scaled a picture past `GL_MAX_TEXTURE_SIZE` down to fit, and drew one whose texture passed the budget at its drawn size (`gltex.c:1679-1700`, `1880-1890`). vtium's picture shader computes the CPU path's own weights in whole numbers, a picture past the largest texture is held in overlapping tiles at its own size, and the textures share the CPU path's bound. Tier 2's picture screens measure the GPU path's pictures byte-identical to the CPU path's on llvmpipe, by GPU scaling and by the copy step. Tests: `tests/gpu_pictures.rs`; tier 2's `pictures-*@gpu` and `pictures-*@gpu-copy` cases.
- **The GPU path keeps its context and programs when its VT is released.** gltex freed its pictures and its glyph caches when its session went to the background, and freed the glyphs only when the GL context was its own (`gltex.c:1646-1667`); vtium's `Pipeline::release` frees the glyph and picture textures and the upright frame, keeps the programs and the context, and the next frame makes the frame again and draws whole (ADR-1303 D9). Test: `a_release_frees_the_glyph_and_picture_textures_and_the_next_frame_draws_whole`.

## Beyond the C reference

- **The cursor takes OSC 12's color.** A block cursor's background, and an underline or bar cursor's line, take the color OSC 12 set, and the character under a block keeps the cell's background as its color; with none set, the cell is drawn inverted as the C reference drew it, which did not parse OSC 12 (aaronsb/libtsm#22). Test: `a_colored_block_takes_its_color`.
- **The selection takes OSC 17's and 19's colors.** The frame resolves them (`vtium-term`'s README), and a selected cell draws in them; the C reference always drew the selection inverted. Test: `the_selection_draws_in_its_colors`.
- **A wide half whose partner is missing draws as one cell.** The C reference's frame always paired them; vtium's frame does not promise it, so a leading half without its trailing half, or the reverse, draws in its own cell and no frame leaves a cell undrawn.
- **The text blink has options.** `Partial`, `Off` and blinking decorations are the operator's decision on vtium/vtium#81. Tests: `tests/blink.rs`.
- **A decoration color may name a palette index.** `DecoColor::Indexed` resolves through each frame's palette, as the C reference resolved its configured decoration colors on every frame (`kmscon_conf_resolve_deco`), so a program's OSC 4 recolors the decorations. Test: `a_palette_decoration_color_follows_the_palette`.
- **A 1-bit display draws in black and white** (vtium/vtium#135). With `Config::depth` at `Depth::One`, which the binary sets from `display-depth` or from the levels the display reports, every pixel the renderer writes is `0x000000` or `0xffffff`. Colors map to lit or unlit by cell, never by brightness, the operator's decision (2026-10-28), and every background is lit or unlit by one cut, the operator's decisions too: a background whose BT.601 luma is 128 or more, the cut the kernel lights a mono panel's pixel at, is lit, and one under it unlit. The default background sets the screen, so a light theme draws dark text on a lit screen; a cell's own background, reverse video and the selection included, takes the same cut, so one on the default's side draws like the default and one on the other side flips, and a light theme's black on white draws dark on lit as written. A cell's foreground and decorations are its background's opposite, even where both fall on one side of the cut, and the background's own state only where they equal it, so a foreground equal to its background stays invisible, as on a color display. What marks state is never lost to the cut, the operator's decision: reverse video (SGR 7) draws the opposite of what the cell draws unreversed, and the selection and a block cursor, which vtium draws to mark state, invert the cell's state again whatever their colors, their text always its new background's opposite, so a selected or reversed cell always differs from the same cell plain; an underline or bar cursor is always its cell's ink. Colors compare by value. The border takes the screen's state, and the pointer stays white in its black outline, which shows it on a lit screen. Each pixel's coverage, the glyph's or a sprite's, is lit at 128 and above, the threshold the kernel's `drm_fb_xrgb8888_to_mono` gives a DRM mono panel, so a frame drawn here passes that conversion unchanged. The mapping and the cut ask nothing of where a glyph came from: a vitrified set's strike, the built-in unifont, a PSF file and a strike rasterised at run time on a larger machine (vtium/vtium#123) follow the same rules, the operator's decision (2026-10-28), and nothing in them is the CPU path's alone, so a hardware path draws a 1-bit display by the same rules. A color glyph at 1 bit is the built-in unifont's bitmap of the same character, the operator's decision, where unifont draws it and its cell, at a whole scale, is the font's; elsewhere vtium draws the glyph's silhouette, its alpha cut at half in the cell's foreground, which keeps its shape where the replacement glyph would lose it. Dithering a color glyph is a set's build option (vtium/vtium#136). The curly underline at 1 bit is a wave of whole pixels, the line's thickness in each column and joined from column to column, where its 8-by-8 sampled coverage cut at half could leave gaps. vtium-font draws 124 of its sprites with partial coverage: the rounded corners U+256D to U+2570, the diagonals U+2571 to U+2573, the shades and the Legacy Computing wedges. At 1 bit their coverage is cut at half like any glyph's, but where the cut breaks a sprite, vtium-font's 1-bit shape of it stands in (`vtium_font::sprite::one_bit`): the shades U+2591 to U+2593 are ordered 2x2 patterns a quarter, half and three quarters lit, where the cut drew the light shade empty and the others solid, and U+1FB9A and U+1FBBD to U+1FBBF light every pixel they cover, where the cut split their tips and thin diagonals. A shade's pattern starts at its cell's top left, so cells of even sides join without a seam, and a cell of an odd side meets its neighbour a pixel out of phase. The hatches U+1FB98 and U+1FB99 fall into their separate stripes at 1 bit, as drawn. A sprite a bitmap font draws is 1-bit already and stays. Test: `tests/sprites_1bit.rs`, which checks every sprite keeps its pieces at 1 bit from 8x8 to 16x32. Where #135 left the rule open, vtium chooses: dim text (SGR 2) draws as plain text, since its half-way color is not the background; `blink-text=partial` hides the glyph in the off phase as `full` does, since the half-way glyph would never blink; the pointer is drawn white with a black outline as it is in color, the outline cut to black. The C reference drew no 1-bit display. Tests: `tests/mono.rs`, `src/mono.rs`'s, `a_1_bit_curl_is_a_joined_wave_of_whole_pixels` and vtium-font's `tests/shade.rs`.
- **A gray panel draws as a color display.** `Depth::Gray(n)` records a gray panel's levels, detected or set, and the frame is drawn as in color, with no mapping by cell and no cut to levels: the panel converts it to its levels itself, the operator's decision (2026-10-28), since each gray panel's conversion is its own. The one exception, the operator's decision too, is a color glyph, which is the built-in unifont's bitmap of the same character as at 1 bit, drawn in the cell's own colors; where unifont has none, it draws in color. Rules for quantizing and for contrast wait for a real gray panel to judge them on. Test: `a_gray_display_draws_as_a_color_one`.
- **The two profiles, 1-bit and scale-1 storage, and the row scratch** are ADR-1300's D2, D4 and D5: memory and composition, with no pixel changed. Every run of a row's drawn cells is composed in the scratch and reaches the display a pixel line at a time, where D5 writes a cell of one layer straight, a call for each line of each cell: at 1366x768 a full frame's writes take 0.05 ms where the cell's took 0.48 ms (vtium/vtium#78).

## Not yet

- **The GPU path draws no 1-bit display yet.** `Renderer::draw_gpu` refuses a 1-bit display. The depth reduction for a 1-bit display (ADR-1301) is not yet in present, and the cell shader draws no color glyph as its silhouette, which a 1-bit display needs. A GPU glyph slot is the size of its cells and is filled from the glyph's first column, so a glyph whose ink reaches a cell past its own on either side (ADR-1302 D6) would be cut at its cells; no pixel differs today, since the renderer draws no overhang yet. A glyph slot past the GPU's largest texture, possible only for a cell past 2048 pixels on a GPU at OpenGL ES 3.0's least largest texture, fails the frame with a limit error. The GPU path's pixels are judged on a real GL in tier 2 once vtium-platform's GL lands.
- **Conceal and overline are not drawn.** A concealed cell shows its text, and an overline draws nothing. Both are vtium/vtium#37's: a concealed cell draws as its background, and an overline as a decoration beside the underline.
- **Rapid blink (SGR 6) blinks at SGR 5's rate.** ADR-1000's `sgr.rapid-blink` asks for a faster phase, which is vtium/vtium#37's; `tests/blink.rs` pins the shared phase until then.
- **`Frame::palette()` is owed.** The frame does not expose its palette, so the owner passes the default foreground and background in `DrawState`. The frame types the renderers read are vtium/vtium#10's.
- **A full frame takes about 0.5 ms.** At 1366x768 a full redraw with a warm cache measures 0.49 ms in a release build and in the minspec build alike, on one pinned core (`tests/figures.rs`), against the 0.33 ms ADR-1300 measured for its draw kernel alone over the same screen in cached memory. That kernel, the 1-bit glyph loop and its writes, takes 0.18 ms of the release figure. The rest lies outside it: reading the frame a row at a time about 0.035 ms (derived: the 0.07 ms a cell at a time took, less the 0.037 ms the whole frame dropped when the frame was read a row at a time; a 170x48 frame read alone, outside the renderer, took 0.053 ms a cell at a time and 0.027 ms a row at a time), each cell's history 0.04 ms, building the cells' jobs 0.12 ms, the glyph cache's lookups 0.04 ms, setting up each cell's composition 0.05 ms and collecting the damage 0.03 ms. The minspec build builds this crate at `opt-level = 3` (the operator's decision on vtium/vtium#78): at the profile's `opt-level = "z"` it measured 0.93 ms, the composition and the writes taking twice as long, and the binary is 6,232 B larger for it. Reaching ADR-1300's figure, or the operator revising it, is vtium/vtium#78's.
