# vtium-render

vtium's renderer: frames from `vtium-term` in, display pixels out. This crate holds the CPU compositor (vtium/vtium#16), which ADR-1300 decides: a drawn-glyph cache, decorations as sprites, the cursor's shapes, the pointer, four orientations, damage handed to the display as clips, and the render timer. It forbids unsafe code and denies the determinism lints. It draws into anything that takes a run of pixels (`Target`), never reads it, and opens no device: the display backends are vtium/vtium#18. The GPU path's frame and the `Gl` trait it draws through are behind the `gpu` feature (vtium/vtium#33, ADR-1303).

## Behaviour

vtium draws every pixel with fixed arithmetic in whole numbers (`src/blend.rs`), so a frame draws to the same bytes on every machine and tier 2's bitmap-font goldens (vtium/vtium#17) judge it exactly. `tests/digests.rs` and `src/deco.rs` hold the renderer to digests of whole cells and of every underline and strike sprite at 1,152 cell sizes (`tests/digests/README.md`). ADR-1300 records how the cache and the composition are built. Its two profiles, its 1-bit and scale-1 storage (D2, D4) and its row scratch (D5) change memory and composition and no pixel. Every run of a row's drawn cells is composed in the scratch and reaches the display a pixel line at a time, instead of a call for each line of each cell: at 1366x768 a full frame's writes take 0.05 ms, where writing cell by cell took 0.48 ms (vtium/vtium#78).

The drawn-glyph cache never changes a pixel (ADR-1300 D3). A glyph that would evict one the frame draws, or that alone passes its pool's byte ceiling, is drawn from the font without being cached. Test: `tests/cache.rs`, every frame at a cache of 1 and 7 glyphs against a cache of none.

Glyphs that vtium generates arrive between frames (ADR-1200 D23). `Renderer::glyphs_changed` drops from the cache only the glyphs whose text holds an arrived character, a cluster's included, and the glyphs drawn as the font's missing glyph. The next frame scans only the rows that show one, reading their lines' text, and draws those cells in each buffer at its age. Every other glyph stays cached: an 80x25 screen of printable ASCII redrawn whole after an arrival for one character draws 1 glyph from the font where emptying the cache drew 95, a median of 120 µs where it took 186 µs in a release build on the development machine. Tests: `tests/swap_in.rs` and `forgetting_a_character_drops_each_glyph_whose_text_holds_it`.

`draw` follows the frame contract with the displays (vtium/vtium#18): it takes the buffer the display lends and the buffer's age, 1 when it holds the last frame drawn with damage, n when it holds the frame n back, 0 when its contents are unknown. Each cell carries the serial of the frame that last changed what it shows, its cursor (place, shape and color), blink phase and wide pairing included, and a buffer at age n gets the cells that changed in the last n frames. At age 0, or at an age past the renderer's history, the buffer gets the whole picture and the border, and every other buffer's history stays as it was. Nothing assumes the buffers take turns or counts them, so a swap that failed, a third buffer and a buffer the display lost each get exactly what they lack. The frame is read since the checkpoint taken after the renderer's previous `draw`, whatever buffer that drew into. A buffer drawn whole lacks nothing, so a full redraw reaches each buffer once, and a keystroke draws only the cell it wrote and the cells the cursor left and entered. Tests: `a_full_redraw_reaches_each_buffer_once`, `buffers_at_age_0_are_each_drawn_whole_once` and `a_keystroke_damages_only_the_cells_it_changes`.

A turned display draws the unturned picture turned, so a wide character cut at the margin shows its left half in the wide cell's first column in every orientation. The border around the grid is split in the display's own coordinates: an odd remainder rounds down in every orientation, so with 5 pixels to spare a display turned upside down leaves 3 at the text's left where an unturned one leaves 2, as tier 2's `edges` goldens show. Tests: `every_orientation_draws_the_same_picture_turned`, `a_wide_glyph_cut_at_the_margin_shows_its_left_half` and `an_odd_border_rounds_down_in_display_coordinates`. A wide character's leading half without its trailing half, or the reverse, draws in its own cell, because vtium-term's frame does not promise that the halves come paired, so no frame leaves a cell undrawn.

Dim text (SGR 2) draws its foreground halfway to its background, each channel the sum of the two halved. Dim mixes the colors reverse video left, and the cursor draws over the result: a block swaps the mix with the background. An underline or strike in the foreground's color takes the mix; SGR 58's underline color is exact. Test: `tests/dim.rs`.

A block cursor's background, and an underline or bar cursor's line, take the color OSC 12 set, and the character under a block keeps the cell's background as its color; with no color set, a block draws its cell inverted. A selected cell draws in the colors OSC 17 and 19 set, which the frame resolves (`vtium-term`'s README). A decoration color may name a palette index: `DecoColor::Indexed` resolves through each frame's palette, so a program's OSC 4 recolors the decorations. Tests: `a_colored_block_takes_its_color`, `the_selection_draws_in_its_colors` and `a_palette_decoration_color_follows_the_palette`.

Blinking text (SGR 5 and 6) draws in the text blink's off phase as `Config::blink` says, which the binary sets from `blink-text` and `blink-decorations`. `Partial`, the default, draws the glyph halfway to its background with SGR 2's arithmetic, over a glyph SGR 2 already dimmed too; `Full` draws the cell without its glyph; `Off` draws it steady, and a phase change redraws nothing. With `decorations`, the underline and strike hide with the glyph under `Full` and dim with it under `Partial`, SGR 58's color and a configured color included; without, they stay as the on phase draws them. The cell under the cursor shows in either phase. The maintainer chose `partial` as the default on 2026-11-03, after the first acceptance pass on a VT, so that blinking text no longer disappears; ECMA-48 says only that SGR 5 makes text blink, and not how the off phase looks. The options themselves are the maintainer's decision on vtium/vtium#81. Tests: `tests/blink.rs`, `the_default_blink_dims_the_text_halfway` and `every_frame_matches_a_whole_draw_under_each_blink_option`.

The renderer draws the pointer only when `DrawState::pointer` is set, which the owner does on a display without a cursor plane; where the display drives a cursor plane, the plane shows the same arrow (`Renderer::arrow`) at the place `Renderer::pointer_at` gives, and moving the pointer involves no draw. The arrow is a cell tall, kept between 8 and `POINTER_SIDE_MAX` pixels, white on a dark outline with its hotspot at the tip, kept inside the grid, and turns with the display (`src/arrow.rs`). No specification covers a pointer's shape; the arrow is the maintainer's decision on vtium/vtium#92, the same in software as on the cursor plane. With `Config::pointer` naming a character (`pointer-char`), the pointer is that character's glyph from the font, one cell wide, white on the same outline, its top left on the corner of the cell under the pointer wherever in the cell the pointer is; a character the font lacks, which would draw as a fallback such as U+FFFD, and a blank one, whose glyph covers nothing, keep the arrow, and over cells so tall that the outlined glyph passes `POINTER_SIDE_MAX`, the cursor plane refuses it and the renderer draws it. Tests: `a_glyph_pointer_sits_on_the_cell_under_the_pointer`, `a_glyph_pointer_the_font_cannot_show_keeps_the_arrow`. It is blended over each run of pixels written beneath it, cells and border alike, so the cells show around it and nothing is read back. It changes the cells it covers only when it appears, moves or hides, and a cell under it that changes is drawn alone, with the arrow over it. Its damage is every cell it covers and, where it reaches past the grid, its area clipped to the display, in which only the border is filled again; the history keeps the last 16 such areas, and a buffer older than those redraws the whole border. Tests: `the_pointer_is_an_arrow_over_the_cells`, `the_pointer_turns_with_the_display`, `the_pointer_leaves_nothing_behind_at_odd_cell_sizes` and `a_move_in_the_bottom_row_damages_only_its_cells_and_the_arrow`. A frame that changes nothing, pointer shown or not, writes nothing and returns no damage; the owner does not swap it, and it does not count as a frame. A change of grid size or of the default background changes every cell and the border.

A link (OSC 8) draws nothing of its own until the pointer is over it (ADR-1002, "Drawing"). Every cell of the link under the pointer, which the frame marks `hovered`, draws a dashed underline in place of its own underline style, in the color its own underline would take: SGR 58's where set, otherwise the configured underline color or the foreground. The frame marks the cells of the old and the new link changed when the hover moves, so the dashes come and go with the cells' own redraws. Tests: `tests/hover.rs`; tier 2's `link-hover-unifont` and `link-hover-unifont-1bit`. The overlay row that shows the link's address while Ctrl is held is not drawn yet: ADR-1002 leaves open what the rest of that row shows.

A concealed cell (SGR 8) draws its background alone: no glyph, no underline, no strike and no overline, and its ink reaches no other cell. The cursor and the selection draw over it as over a blank cell, so a block cursor on a concealed password shows the cursor and no letter. ECMA-48 and ctlseqs say only that the text is hidden; vtium hides its lines too, since a line under a hidden word would show the word's length. Test: `tests/renditions.rs`.

An overline (SGR 53) draws along the cell's top rows, as thick as the underline and the cell's width, over the glyph. It draws in the strike's color: the configured `strike-color`, else the foreground. The strike and the overline are one sprite of the two lines together where a cell has both, since the GPU path's quad keeps one slot for the lines over the glyph. Both lines are whole pixels, so the one sprite draws what the two drawn one after the other draw. The GPU path keeps the eight sprites, a cell of each side by side, in one texture, so it takes a cell at most an eighth of the largest texture wide: 256 pixels at OpenGL ES 3.0's least largest texture, 2048. Blinking text's overline follows `blink-decorations` as the strike does. ECMA-48 names the overline and gives no shape, so vtium chooses the cell's top rows. Tests: `tests/renditions.rs`, and `an_overline_names_its_sprite_in_the_strikes_slot` in `tests/gpu_compose.rs`.

The underline and strike are sprites made from the font's lines (`src/deco.rs`). No specification covers an underline's shape. vtium gives an underline at most a quarter of the cell's rows, because an underline drawn over the letters hides their lower rows: at 8 pixels a double or a curl drawn whole would take three of the cell's eight rows (vtium/vtium#119). The single, dotted and dashed lines are the font's line, which a face with no metrics of its own puts in the cell's bottom rows, one row below the letters at 8 pixels. A curl lowers its wave to fit, down to 2 rows peak to peak. A double, or a curl that still does not fit, draws as the single line. A cell 12 rows tall or more has room for the double, and for the curl when the cell is at most half as wide as tall, with the lines `for_height` gives; a face with lines of its own meets the same quarter at the sizes its own thickness sets. A strike, one size of a bitmap font, that vtium-font scales by a whole factor brings the lines of the cell it draws (vtium/vtium#124): `for_height` of the scaled height when its own lines were `for_height` of its height, and its own lines scaled otherwise. The room is the drawn cell's, so an 8-pixel strike drawn at 16 pixels draws a 16-pixel cell's double. One whose own lines scale with it, two rows thick from row 14, draws its double as the 8-pixel single line scaled, and its curl as a wave 2 rows peak to peak in rows 12 to 15, which the 8-pixel cell had no room for. Tests: `every_underline_at_8_pixels_lies_in_the_bottom_row`, `no_underline_takes_more_than_a_quarter_of_the_cell`, `a_double_or_curl_with_no_room_draws_as_the_single_line`, `a_curl_lowers_its_wave_to_fit_before_it_flattens` and `a_scaled_strike_takes_the_room_of_the_cell_it_draws`.

A dash runs from the cell's left edge for `round(5w/8)` pixels of a cell `w` wide, a half rounded up, at least 1 and, from 2 pixels wide, at most `w - 1`, so a gap remains; the rest of the cell is gap. At 8 pixels that is `█████···`. One sprite serves a cell size, tiled across a wide cell. The maintainer chose the shape on 2026-10-28, so that each character's dash reads as that letter's own underline. Test: `a_dash_runs_from_the_left_edge_for_five_eighths_of_the_cell`.

`tests/digests/cells.tsv` holds the digests of the earlier shapes at the sizes these two rules redraw: the double and the curl where they would pass a quarter of the cell, and the dash in every cell 2 or more pixels wide. At those sizes `every_sprite_matches_its_digest_or_its_redrawn_shape` checks that the sprite differs from its digest and is the single line, the lowered curl or the dash, and `a_dashed_screen_matches_its_digest_but_for_its_dashes` checks the dashed screen of `tests/digests/screens.tsv` against its digest with the earlier dash put back (`tests/digests/README.md`).

The console draws two things of its own over a display's frame, outside the terminal (ADR-1401 D5, D7): a `Notice`, vtium's message in a box across the top of the view, its lines wrapped to the box and kept clear of the mark, in the colors its owner gives (`Colors::NOTICE` for a problem, `Colors::STATUS` for the on-screen display's line alone), and a head's focus `Mark` in the grid cell its owner gives it, a filled or hollow circle, or a filled or hollow square from a font that lacks the circles. The console draws both with the built-in fonts. Both turn with the display and take the 1-bit mapping and stand-ins a cell takes; neither touches the renderer's record of the cells. When what the console drew over a frame changes, the owner gives the areas it covered to `Renderer::redraw_area`, which marks the cells and the border under them changed on the next frame, so each buffer the display lends draws them again, whatever its age (ADR-1401 D2). Tests: `src/notice.rs`, and `an_area_drawn_over_is_redrawn_in_every_buffer` in `tests/damage.rs`.

### Glyph ink past its cells

A glyph from an outline font may draw ink past its own cells: up to one cell to the left and to the right (ADR-1302) and up to one row above and below (ADR-1306), so that an accent on a capital, an Arabic mark and an italic letter's lean draw whole. The font gives such a glyph an ink box, the rectangle its ink covers, and the cache stores the glyph at that box (`src/stored.rs`). The two bounds are the font crate's constants `REACH_CELLS` and `REACH_ROWS`, which the renderer reads through `Bound` in `src/reach.rs`, so widening a bound is a change of its constant. Ink past the bounds, or past the grid's edges, is cut.

Each cell remembers its reach in one byte: how many cells the ink of the glyph it last drew reaches to the left, to the right, up and down, two bits each. To draw a cell, the renderer finds the neighbours whose reach covers it and composes each pixel in this order: the cell's background, its underline, the ink of the row above, the ink of the row below, the ink of the cells beside it with the farthest first, its own glyph, and its strike-through and overline (ADR-1306 D4). The received ink blends over the background; it is never XORed. No background is ever drawn over another cell's ink, and the display's buffer is never read. A cell that no neighbour's ink reaches draws exactly as before, so a screen of glyphs that stay in their cells, every bitmap screen without italic among them, draws the same pixels.

A cell whose ink changes damages the neighbours its old reach or its new reach covers, so every buffer at every age gets them (ADR-1306 D5). The scan of a row can damage cells it has already passed, the cells before it in the row and the cells of the rows above; the renderer draws those once the row's scan is done, so a frame whose ink stays in its cells still scans and draws each row once. The renderer reads no reach at all until the font has drawn a glyph whose ink passes its cells, so a screen in the built-in font or a PSF font without italic does no reach work; the frame that draws the first such glyph draws the whole grid again with every reach read. The GPU path reads the reaches as the CPU path does, from its own glyphs, so its stamps and its damage are the CPU path's. The letter the visible cursor stands on keeps its ink above and below its row, in its colors without the cursor, in both phases of the cursor's blink, and its ink beside it stays in its cell; a blinking letter's ink stays in its cell, and so does ink that its own background hides. Inside a block cursor's cell or a selected cell, every ink takes the mark's text color; a selected glyph's ink outside the selection takes its colors unselected, read from the frame without its selection. At 1 bit, ink that lands in another cell is the opposite of that cell's background. Received ink belongs to the text layer of the cell it lands in, so a kitty picture below the text shows it and a picture at z 0 or more, or a sixel, hides it (ADR-1306 D7). Tests: `tests/overhang.rs`, and `src/renderer/wider.rs`, which runs the composition and damage at a sideways bound of two cells against a whole draw at every buffer age; on the GPU path, `tests/gpu_ink.rs` and `src/renderer/wider/gpu.rs`, described under the GPU path below.

### 1-bit and gray displays

A 1-bit display draws in black and white (vtium/vtium#135). With `Config::depth` at `Depth::One`, which the binary sets from `display-depth` or from the levels the display reports, every pixel the renderer writes is `0x000000` or `0xffffff`. Colors map to lit or unlit by cell, never by brightness, the operator's decision (2026-10-28), and every background is lit or unlit by one cut, the operator's decisions too: a background whose BT.601 luma is 128 or more, the cut the kernel lights a mono panel's pixel at, is lit, and one under it unlit. The default background sets the screen, so a light theme draws dark text on a lit screen; a cell's own background, reverse video and the selection included, takes the same cut, so one on the default's side draws like the default and one on the other side flips, and a light theme's black on white draws dark on lit as written. A cell's foreground and decorations are its background's opposite, even where both fall on one side of the cut, and the background's own state only where they equal it, so a foreground equal to its background stays invisible, as on a color display. What marks state is never lost to the cut, the operator's decision: reverse video (SGR 7) draws the opposite of what the cell draws unreversed, and the selection and a block cursor, which vtium draws to mark state, invert the cell's state again whatever their colors, their text always its new background's opposite, so a selected or reversed cell always differs from the same cell plain; an underline or bar cursor is always its cell's ink. Colors compare by value. The border takes the screen's state, and the pointer stays white in its black outline, which shows it on a lit screen. Each pixel's coverage, the glyph's or a sprite's, is lit at 128 and above, the threshold the kernel's `drm_fb_xrgb8888_to_mono` gives a DRM mono panel, so a frame drawn here passes that conversion unchanged. The mapping and the cut ask nothing of where a glyph came from: a vitrified set's strike, the built-in unifont, a PSF file and a strike rasterised at run time on a larger machine (vtium/vtium#123) follow the same rules, the operator's decision (2026-10-28), and nothing in them is the CPU path's alone, so a hardware path draws a 1-bit display by the same rules. A color glyph at 1 bit is the built-in unifont's bitmap of the same character, the operator's decision, where unifont draws it and its cell, at a whole scale, is the font's; elsewhere vtium draws the glyph's silhouette, its alpha cut at half in the cell's foreground, which keeps its shape where the replacement glyph would lose it. Dithering a color glyph is a set's build option (vtium/vtium#136). The curly underline at 1 bit is a wave of whole pixels, the line's thickness in each column and joined from column to column, where its 8-by-8 sampled coverage cut at half could leave gaps. vtium-font draws 124 of its sprites with partial coverage: the rounded corners U+256D to U+2570, the diagonals U+2571 to U+2573, the shades and the Legacy Computing wedges. At 1 bit their coverage is cut at half like any glyph's, but where the cut breaks a sprite, vtium-font's 1-bit shape of it stands in (`vtium_font::sprite::one_bit`): the shades U+2591 to U+2593 are ordered 2x2 patterns a quarter, half and three quarters lit, where the cut drew the light shade empty and the others solid, and U+1FB9A and U+1FBBD to U+1FBBF light every pixel they cover, where the cut split their tips and thin diagonals. A shade's pattern starts at its cell's top left, so cells of even sides join without a seam, and a cell of an odd side meets its neighbour a pixel out of phase. The hatches U+1FB98 and U+1FB99 fall into their separate stripes at 1 bit, as drawn. A sprite a bitmap font draws is 1-bit already and stays. Test: `tests/sprites_1bit.rs`, which checks every sprite keeps its pieces at 1 bit from 8x8 to 16x32. Where #135 left the rule open, vtium chooses: dim text (SGR 2) draws as plain text, since its half-way color is not the background; `blink-text=partial` hides the glyph in the off phase as `full` does, since the half-way glyph would never blink; the pointer is drawn white with a black outline as it is in color, the outline cut to black. Tests: `tests/mono.rs`, `src/mono.rs`'s, `a_1_bit_curl_is_a_joined_wave_of_whole_pixels` and vtium-font's `tests/shade.rs`.

A gray panel draws as a color display. `Depth::Gray(n)` records a gray panel's levels, detected or set, and the frame is drawn as in color, with no mapping by cell and no cut to levels: the panel converts it to its levels itself, the operator's decision (2026-10-28), since each gray panel's conversion is its own. The one exception, the operator's decision too, is a color glyph, which is the built-in unifont's bitmap of the same character as at 1 bit, drawn in the cell's own colors; where unifont has none, it draws in color. Rules for quantizing and for contrast wait for a real gray panel to judge them on. Test: `a_gray_display_draws_as_a_color_one`.

### Pictures

The `images` feature draws a frame's image placements (ADR-1100 D4, D5, D9; vtium/vtium#30). The binary turns it on with `vtium-term/images` and vtium-image, and the minspec build leaves it out: without it the crate draws exactly what it drew before, and the minspec binary is the same size. The binary hands the renderer nothing new. A frame built with vtium-term's `Frame::with_placements` carries its placements, and `draw` reads them; `DRAWS_PLACEMENTS` says whether a build draws them, which a head needs before it offers sixel or kitty graphics (ADR-1100 D10), and `Renderer::picture_counts` reports what the scaled-copy cache did.

- **The layers.** A cell that no picture's pixels reach is drawn exactly as without the feature. A cell that one reaches is composed in the row scratch from the bottom up, in ADR-1100 D4's order: the cell's background, with the layer below backgrounds (kitty z below -1,073,741,824) over it only where the frame cell's `default_bg` says the background is the default; the layer below text (kitty z from -1,073,741,824 to -1); the cell's text layer, premultiplied, as a cell of several layers composes it (ADR-1300, "Carried forward"); the layer above text (kitty z of 0 or more, and every sixel); and last vtium's mark. Within a layer the pictures draw in the order the frame hands them, which is the store's stack order. A picture's premultiplied pixels go over what lies beneath with the same arithmetic as the text layer, `src + div255(dst * (255 - a))`. An opaque picture over the whole cell hides what lies under it, so the cell's composition starts from the highest such picture.
- **The cursor and the pointer draw above every picture, and the selection tints it.** A block cursor covers its whole cell, so that cell is drawn as if no picture were there. A selected cell is composed with its pictures, and then the selection's color goes over the whole cell at `SELECTION_TINT`, 128 out of 255, so the cell shows both the selection and the picture under it (the maintainer, 2026-11-01); a cursor in a selected cell lies above the tint. A bar cursor's bar and an underline cursor's line are drawn again over the pictures; under the bar the cell's letter is not drawn, as without pictures. The pointer's arrow is blended as each line is written, and vtium's notices are drawn over the buffer after the renderer, so both lie above every picture too. Test: `the_cursor_the_selection_and_the_pointer_draw_above_every_picture`.
- **A kitty Unicode placeholder cell draws no text.** A cell whose cluster starts with U+10EEEE marks where the picture of a virtual placement draws (ADR-1100 D13), so the renderer draws its background and no glyph, diacritics included, and no underline, strike-through, or the dashed underline of a hovered link. The cursor and the selection draw over it as over any cell. A cluster that holds U+10EEEE after another character is text. Without the feature U+10EEEE is a private-use character like any other. Tests: `tests/placeholder.rs`.
- **Scaling and turning.** A placement's pixel values are in the cell it was made in. A value `m` measured from the grid's origin lands at `floor(m * cell / made)` in the renderer's cell, so the pieces of one picture meet with no gap and no overlap at any cell size (ADR-1100 D8). A picture is cut at its box and at the grid. Scaling is an integer filter: 16-bit fixed-point weights that sum to exactly 65,536 on each axis, a box filter where a picture shrinks and bilinear where it grows. A copy is made in two passes: each source row is filtered across once, into a bounded ring of rows, and each output pixel then sums its rows down and is rounded once. The sums are whole numbers, so a picture scales to the same pixels on every machine, and a picture scaled for one frame alone, a pixel at a time, gets the same bytes. A turned display draws the unturned picture turned, as it draws the grid.
- **The scaled-copy cache.** A picture drawn at its own size is read straight from the image's shared pixels, along each display line with a fixed step through the image on a turned display. Every other picture is drawn from a copy scaled and turned for the display, made when a cell under it is first drawn, and kept in a cache of the display's own, outside the image budget, so a copy never evicts a stored image (ADR-1100 D5). The key is the whole picture: the image, the frame shown and its pixel revision, the image's size, the placement's crop (the part of the image it shows), the size it is drawn at and the turn. The frame's entry carries the crop, the drawn size and the picture's offset in its box for the whole placement, so the pieces a cut leaves and a picture scrolling off the top all share one copy, and a copy never reads past its crop. The cache holds at most `PICTURE_CACHE_SCREENS` screens of 4-byte pixels. A copy that needs room evicts the copy placed longest ago that no placement of the frame shows, ties going by key, and only when that makes it fit; a copy that still does not fit is scaled for the frame alone and not kept, with the pixels a kept copy gives, so the cache changes speed and never pixels (ADR-1300 D3). A picture moved up a row each frame is therefore scaled once, and a turned screen scrolling twelve pictures evicts only what its first frame evicted. Tests: `a_scaled_placement_moved_up_a_row_each_frame_is_scaled_once`, `a_turned_screen_scrolling_twelve_pictures_evicts_only_what_its_first_turn_evicted`, `the_scaled_copies_never_pass_their_bound` and `a_picture_scaled_for_one_frame_draws_its_kept_copys_pixels`.
- **Animated pictures.** The entry of a kitty animation's placement carries the frame it shows (ADR-1102 D3), and the renderer draws that frame's pixels as it draws any picture's. The store fills in the image's current frame, the one `a=a` with `c` made current, or the root frame while no command has chosen another. The binary's animation clock will put the frame it chooses on the entry with `FramePlacement::show`, but no clock does so yet, so today every animated placement draws its current frame. The image's key, the frame's number and the frame's pixel revision name the pixels: the store never reuses an image's key, and gives a frame a new pixel revision whenever a command changes what it shows. The renderer keys both its copies and its record of what it last drew on all three, so a placement that moves to another frame, or whose frame a command changed, draws its cells again from the new pixels and never from another frame's copy. The cache keeps at most one copy of each frame of a picture at each size it is drawn: when it makes a copy of a frame at a newer pixel revision, it drops the copies of that frame's older revisions that no placement in the draw shows, since the store never shows them again. An animation whose copies all fit in the cache is scaled only in its first pass through its frames. An animation whose copies do not all fit is scaled again in every draw that shows it: each draw scales a whole copy and evicts another, as a picture replaced in every draw is scaled, and its copies stay within `PICTURE_CACHE_SCREENS` screens.
- **One image store for each renderer.** The cache's keys and the damage history name images and placements by their `ImageKey` and `PlacementKey`, which each console's store numbers from zero. A renderer therefore draws the frames of one store, one console, for its life, and the binary gives each console its own renderer, or a new renderer when a display starts showing another console; frames of two stores through one renderer could take one picture's copy or history for another's.
- **Damage.** The renderer keeps each placement as it last drew it, and for each placement that arrived, left or changed it draws the cells of its old box and its new box again, in every buffer at its age. A picture that moves or changes therefore redraws only the cells it covers. vtium-term also marks the cells under each change the store makes (ADR-1001), so the two meet; the renderer's own rule also covers a placement list that changed while the frame's cells did not. Test: `a_picture_that_changes_or_moves_redraws_only_the_cells_it_covers`.
- **Malformed and huge placements.** A placement with a zero size, a zero cell, a source rectangle outside its image or pixels of the wrong count draws nothing. A picture larger than the cache, such as one fitted to the store's largest box of 10,000 cells a side, is scaled for each frame only in the cells the grid shows.

- **What pictures cost.** These are machine figures, printed and not pinned, from a release build on the development machine on 2026-11-02, while other agents' builds kept its load average at about 10 on 32 cores. At 1366x768 in 8x16 cells, a full redraw takes a median of 0.62 ms with no picture, 0.59 ms under one full-screen picture at its own size, 0.59 ms under one scaled to the screen from its kept copy, 5.40 ms for text over one full-screen picture below text, and 0.61 ms with a ten-cell picture. Cells side by side under one opaque picture that nothing draws over are copied from the picture as a run, each display line of the run one copy, which is why a full-screen picture costs about what a screen of text does; a cell whose text lies over a picture composes each line from its layers, which is the 5.40 ms. A frame where only a ten-cell picture moves a column draws its 12 old and new cells in 0.012 ms. Scaling the full-screen picture takes about 7 ms more, on the frame that makes its copy. A picture replaced every frame, as a video player sends it, pays that every frame: a full-screen picture at its own size takes 0.58 ms a frame at 1366x768 and 13.7 ms at 7680x2160 unturned (2.0 and 46.8 ms on a display turned a quarter), and one scaled to twice its size 6.7 ms and 136 ms (7.4 and 153 ms turned). The commands are `cargo test --release -p vtium-render --features images --test picture_figures -- --ignored --nocapture`, and `--test picture_video` for a picture replaced every frame.

Tests: `tests/pictures.rs`, `tests/picture_frames.rs`, `tests/picture_goldens.rs`, and the scaler's, the cache's and the frame-alone scaling's in `src/picture/`.

### The GPU path

The `bidi` feature draws right-to-left text (ADR-1004 D6, D7, D9), from the order, the joining sides and the mirrored characters vtium-term's frame gives each row, and the minspec build leaves it out. Each letter of a joining script is looked up by its key: the letter and its marks with U+200D on each side it joins (`joined::text`). Lam followed by alef, where the row draws alef on the left and lam beside it on the right, draws as one ligature across the two cells, which the alef's cell draws as a wide character's first half does, keyed by both letters (`joined::span`, `joined::pair_text`); with the visible cursor on either cell, each letter draws its own joined form instead, so the cursor marks one letter. A cluster whose first character is mirrored draws the counterpart with its marks. A bar cursor on a character that resolves right to left draws U+2595, RIGHT ONE EIGHTH BLOCK, on the right side of its cell, and on a wide character takes its right half. The console asks the glyph helper for the same keys through `joined`, so what it asks for and what the renderer looks up are the same text. Tests: `tests/bidi.rs`, run with `cargo test -p vtium-render --features bidi`.

The `gpu` feature adds the GPU path's frame. It is off by default: the minspec build and a default build compile none of it and link no GL. `cargo test -p vtium-render --features gpu` runs its tests.

vtium-render forbids unsafe code, so it never calls GL itself. It draws through the `Gl` trait, which speaks the renderer's terms: textures of a stated format and size, filled a rectangle at a time; the upright frame and an imported display buffer as targets to draw into; programs compiled from vtium-render's own shader source with named inputs; a batch of quads drawn through a program; a frame's end, which gives a fence the render thread waits on before the display shows the buffer; the GPU's limits; and a read of a rectangle back to memory, for tests. The binary implements the trait with an adapter over vtium-platform's safe GL type, which holds the unsafe calls to GL, EGL and GBM (ADR-1303 D7).

Every handle the trait gives out, a texture, a target, a program or a fence, is an owned value that names the context that made it. A handle cannot be copied, and freeing it consumes it. Each implementation keeps a `Ledger` of its context's live handles and passes every call through it before the call reaches GL. This is the one list of what the ledger refuses:

- a handle another context made, or one that was freed, and a fence already waited on;
- a texture or upright frame past the GPU's largest texture or viewport, and a display buffer past its largest renderbuffer or viewport;
- an upload outside its texture, short of its bytes, or with rows a part of a pixel apart;
- a program that lists an input twice, or whose shaders declare no uniform of the input's name and type, or declare it as an array, and any program on a GPU without `highp` in fragment shaders;
- a draw that gives one of its program's inputs no value, a value twice, a value of the wrong kind, or a value for an input the program lacks, and a draw that reads the texture it draws into;
- a read outside its target, or into a buffer of the wrong length;
- every call once the context is lost.

A real driver refuses more than the ledger can see, and only a real GL shows those refusals: a shader that does not compile or a program that does not link, a uniform the driver dropped because nothing uses it (its location is -1), and a texture or framebuffer that is incomplete. The stand-in compiles any text it is given. Part 2 of slice 0, which builds the adapter, proves the shaders compile and link on llvmpipe. The ledger never gives a serial out twice, so a freed handle never names a newer object, even where GL reuses the freed object's name. The upright frame is a target that holds its own texture, and freeing the target frees the texture.

GL reports most of its errors late, through an error flag. The renderer reads that flag only at the boundaries ADR-1303 D7 names, through `Gl::check`: once after a batch of allocations and uploads, once after the composition's draws and once at the end of a frame, and only when the batch made a call, so a frame that changes nothing makes no call at all. A check after every call would make a threaded driver wait for the GPU each time. Each error carries its kind, out of memory, a lost context, another GL error, a refused handle, a limit, a misuse, a failed compile or a failed import, and the batch whose check found it, so the head can sort it as ADR-1303 D9 decides. A frame that drew ends with `Gl::submit`, which gives a fence after the frame's commands, and `Gl::wait`, which waits until the GPU has passed it and frees it, so the buffer goes back to the display finished; vtium-platform makes the fence an EGL fence sync, or ends the frame with `glFinish` where it can make none, and fails a wait that passes `GPU_FENCE_WAIT_MS` as a lost context.

`StandIn` is the tests' GL. It keeps a ledger as the adapter does, so it refuses every call the list above names, and records the calls that pass. It keeps what each texture is filled with, and draws the clears and the quads of the cell program into the upright frames it made, through `src/gpu/model.rs`, a model of the cell shader that reads the quads, the textures and the uniforms as the shader reads them and does each blend with `src/blend.rs`; it draws no other program's pixels, so present, the pictures, the arrow and a text layer over a picture draw nothing there. A test reads an upright frame with `StandIn::frame_pixels`. A test can give it smaller limits, to drive a frame past the largest texture; can raise a GL error that the next check reports; and can make the next texture, frame, import, compile or wait fail with a kind it chooses (`StandIn::fail_next`), or one a number of calls later (`StandIn::fail_after`), as a driver would. Pixels are judged on a real GL, Mesa's llvmpipe, in tier 2, once the composition and present draw.

`Renderer::draw_gpu` draws a frame on the GPU path. The renderer decides what the frame draws through the same front end as `Renderer::draw`: where the grid sits, which cells changed and how each one looks, its cursor, blink and decorations included, which parts of the border are stale, and where the pointer is. Only the back end differs. The CPU path composes each row's cell jobs in the row scratch and writes them into the display's buffer; the GPU path turns each job into one quad through the cell shader, and the border's stale parts, less the grid, into clears. The GPU path composes into the upright frame, which keeps the last frame, so the renderer treats it as a buffer one frame old, or at age 0 when the pipeline composes it whole: when it is new, after a failed frame, for a new border color, or when another renderer's glyphs were in the pipeline. A frame the CPU path draws between two GPU frames moves the renderer's history on while the upright frame stays, so the renderer takes a new identity, and its next GPU frame replaces the pipeline's glyphs and draws whole. `draw_gpu` refuses a 1-bit display until present's depth reduction is built. Present then takes what the display's buffer lacks over its own age. `draw_gpu` takes the console's notices and focus mark as `Placed` pictures, which `Notice::upright` and `Mark::upright` give: each is uploaded whole when it is new or changed since the last frame, and otherwise over what the frame drew beneath it; a notice the last frame drew and this one does not has the cells beneath it drawn again, as `redraw_area` draws them on the CPU path. Tests: `tests/gpu_compose.rs`.

The cell shader (`src/gpu/glsl/cell.frag`) draws a cell with the CPU path's integer arithmetic, `src/blend.rs`, as ADR-1300 D6 decides and the maintainer confirmed on 2026-11-03: every value a whole number in a `highp` float, `div255` with its shifts written as floors of divisions by 256, texels read whole with `texelFetch`, no dithering, and each channel `c` written as `(c + 0.25) / 255`. A quad carries the cell's foreground and the glyph's kind, its background, and the colors and sprites of its underline and of the lines over its glyph, the strike, the overline or both, each kind in the top byte of a color. A cell of one layer is `mask` or `over_bg`; a cell of several composes its underline, glyph and strike premultiplied and then goes over its background, as `cell_line` does.

A glyph's ink that reaches past its cells is drawn into the cells around it as the CPU path draws it (ADR-1306 D9, ADR-1300 D6). A glyph whose ink passes its cells goes to a fifth pool, the boxes, whose slots each hold the largest box its ink may have at the bound: a wide glyph's two cells and the bound's cells to each side, by its row and the bound's rows above and below. The boxes count against the masks' budgets and lie in one R8 texture, so that the shader samples every neighbour's glyph from it; when the texture is full, a texture with twice its rows takes its place, filled again from the glyphs the boxes keep for it, so a slot keeps its place, until the texture reaches `GLYPH_TEXTURE_SIDE`. A glyph that the boxes have no room for after that is drawn cut at its cells, its ink reaching no other cell, and the glyphs give a note for the log. Each frame names, in the ink grid (`src/gpu/ink.rs`), the neighbours whose ink the cells it draws receive: for each, the reach byte the renderer keeps for its cell once the frame is scanned, its span, its box and the color its ink takes in a cell no mark covers. Each cell of the grid also holds a bit for each display line through it. Once the frame is scanned, the renderer sets the bits of each cell that receives ink: a bit is set when a neighbour's ink reaches that display line anywhere in the cell's job. The renderer reads that from the boxes. When a glyph takes its box, the glyphs note which rows and columns of each cell of the box hold ink, so a frame only combines those notes. A display line runs across the job, the same in each of its cells, or, on a display turned a quarter, down one column of the upright frame. A cell holds as many bits as the larger of its width and its height, 32 to a texel. The grid is an RGBA8 texture with two texels a cell for its entry and one for each 32 of its line bits, and a frame fills only the rows whose entries or bits changed; an entry the last frame named and this one does not is emptied. A cell that receives ink is flagged in its quad and drawn by the cell shader built with `#define INK`, in batches of its own; every other cell is drawn by the cell shader built without it, so a screen whose ink stays in its cells costs what it did. Both programs are compiled when the pipeline is set up, so no frame compiles one, not even the frame in which the first glyph whose ink passes its cells takes its box. Mesa's llvmpipe still turns a program into machine code the first time a draw uses it, so on llvmpipe with an empty shader cache that frame waits for the ink program's machine code; Mesa's shader cache keeps it for later runs. The shader takes the bound as the compile-time constants `REACH_SIDE` and `REACH_ROWS`. A pixel on a display line whose bit is clear receives no ink, and the shader draws it as in a cell that receives none. For each pixel on a line whose bit is set, the shader walks the neighbours in ADR-1306 D4's order, in loops those constants bound: the rows above, the rows below, each from the column before the cell to the column after it, then the cells beside it, farthest first and, at one distance, the one before it first. Each neighbour whose reach byte covers the cell and whose block covers the pixel lends its box's coverage there, tinted with its color, or with the cell's foreground where a block cursor or the selection marks the cell (ADR-1306 D6). The received ink is blended premultiplied into one layer, as `receive` does, which goes over the underline and under the cell's own glyph, as `received_over` does; it is never XORed. A display line whose bit is set is composed as a cell of several layers, as `cell_line` composes it, whose rounding differs from a cell of one mask's where the mask's coverage is partial. A cell's text layer over pictures carries the ink it receives (ADR-1306 D7). The renderer draws a cell a second time in a frame once the row below has sent it ink (ADR-1306 D5). The GPU path draws each batch of quads whole, so that cell's second drawing replaces its first in every list, its cell quad, its text layer and mark, its part of each picture and its tint, and each cell is drawn once, as the CPU path's drawing in order leaves it; a frame that draws no cell twice does no more work for it. Tests: `tests/gpu_ink.rs`, which compares the stand-in's upright frame with the CPU path's whole draw for an accent over the row above, a descender meeting an accent, the letter under a block cursor in both blink phases, a cell the frame draws twice and a typing script at each turn of the display, and checks the order of a text layer's ink and the pictures, that a cell under a picture drawn twice draws each layer once, and that no frame compiles a program once the first reaching glyph takes its box; the unit tests in `src/gpu/ink.rs`, which check the line bits set for a known box and layout, across a job and down a turned one, and where they lie in the texture; and `src/renderer/wider/gpu.rs`, which does the same as `tests/gpu_ink.rs` at a sideways bound of two cells and at two rows, with glyph budgets large enough that neither path evicts the glyphs the tests put in. Both draw through the stand-in's model of the shader, so the shader itself is judged by the `ink` tests in vtium's `src/gpu.rs` on llvmpipe. They feed bytes to a terminal, draw each frame on the GPU path into a buffer one frame old, read the display back and compare it pixel for pixel with the CPU path's whole drawing: a descender meeting an accent, a block cursor through three blink phases, the selection, reverse video, ink reaching a picture's cell at four layers, a cell drawn twice with and without a picture and a typing script, each at the display's four turns, and a typing script at a bound of two cells. For that last test, `Renderer::set_reach_bound_for_test` sets the bound, and `Renderer::keep_glyph_for_test` and `Renderer::put_glyph_for_test` give the two paths glyphs whose ink reaches two cells, which no font draws while the font crate's bound is one cell. Tier 2's `@gpu` cases draw its screens on llvmpipe too, but the shader defects these tests catch, received ink drawn in the wrong order, over the cell's own glyph, without a mark's color or along the wrong lines of a turned display, moved no tier 2 result.

The glyphs live in ADR-1300's four pools, masks and color glyphs one or two cells wide, under the same budgets as the CPU path's. The glyph cache is generic over what it holds: the CPU path keeps a glyph's pixels in it, the GPU path a slot in a pool's textures, so both share one key, one LRU and one budget check. A slot is the glyph's cells, and a pool lays its slots out as a grid, masks in R8 and color glyphs in RGBA8; its first texture holds 64 slots and each later one twice the one before, up to `GLYPH_TEXTURE_SIDE` pixels a side. A slot is filled once, from the glyph as the CPU path stores it, read a line at a time at the size it is drawn, so its pixels are the CPU path's. A glyph the budget has no room for, one that would evict a glyph this frame draws, draws from a slot held for the frame and given back at its end (ADR-1300 D3), and the next frame asks the budget again, so a pool refused takes the glyph once the frame no longer draws all it holds. The decoration sprites are one R8 texture, each kind a cell, laid out as a grid whose larger side is as small as the kinds allow, so that the texture stays near square as the kinds grow: an 8×16 cell's eight sprites lie four across and two down, in 32×32 pixels, where a row of them would be 64×16. The cell shader takes the sprites across from the `cell` input's third value. A GPU whose largest texture is too small for the grid fails to make the glyphs with an error that names the sprites. OpenGL ES 3.0 promises a largest texture of at least 2048 pixels, so only a cell hundreds of pixels wide or tall reaches that limit. Tests: `the_sprites_lie_in_a_grid_that_fits_a_largest_texture_a_row_of_them_passes`, `sprites_past_the_largest_texture_fail_with_an_error_that_names_them`, `every_decoration_draws_on_the_gpu_path_as_on_the_cpu_path`. A new renderer, made for a new font or cell size, replaces the pipeline's glyphs and draws whole. Tests: `a_pool_its_budget_refuses_draws_from_a_held_slot_and_takes_the_glyph_once_the_budget_allows`, `a_pool_past_its_first_texture_takes_another_twice_its_size`, `a_new_renderer_on_the_pipeline_replaces_the_glyphs_and_draws_whole`.

The pointer's arrow is drawn upright (`Renderer::upright_arrow`, `Arrow::upright`) as one texture, uploaded when its image changes. It is drawn over the pixels the frame drew beneath it and no others, as quads cut to them a line at a time, the lines alike joined, since the upright frame keeps earlier frames' arrows where it drew nothing. In each tile the pixels beneath those quads are first copied out into the copy described under pictures below, and the arrow's shader (`glsl/arrow.frag`) goes over them with the CPU path's `over_bg` in whole numbers and writes each channel as `(c + 0.25) / 255`, so its outline, black at 220 of 255, lands on the CPU path's byte on any driver. The GPU's own blending is not used. Tests: `the_arrow_is_one_texture_drawn_over_a_copy_of_what_the_frame_drew_beneath_it`, `the_arrow_covers_each_drawn_pixel_under_it_once`, and in the binary's adapter `the_pointers_arrow_on_llvmpipe_is_the_cpu_paths_byte_for_byte`, which judges the arrow's pixels on llvmpipe, since no tier 2 screen draws the arrow.

The glyph cache, on both paths, remembers up to `FAILED_GLYPHS` glyphs the font drew nothing for, and does not ask the font for them again until `glyphs_changed` or `forget_glyphs` names any character: the font draws nothing for a glyph only after its whole fallback chain, U+25A1, `?` and its last resort, drew nothing, so any arrival may give it a glyph, and the table empties. vtium's font draws nothing only for a glyph it cannot draw, never for want of memory, so the table keeps no expiry. A glyph refused for room is not remembered. Tests: `a_glyph_the_font_draws_nothing_for_is_asked_for_once_until_forgotten`, `a_glyph_the_font_draws_nothing_for_is_not_asked_for_every_frame`.

A character no font holds draws the font's missing glyph, which comes from the same chain, so its drawing can change when a set arrives that does not hold the character itself, as when the first set brings its own U+25A1. The font says when it draws its missing glyph (`Drawn::missing`, which vtium-font's README describes), and each cached glyph on both paths records it. On any arrival, through `glyphs_changed` or `forget_glyphs`, the cache drops each glyph it recorded as the missing glyph. The renderer keeps the characters of each text it drew as the missing glyph, or drew nothing for, since the last arrival, and the next frame draws again each cell that shows one of them, whether or not a cell shows an arrived character. When more than `MISSING_CHARS` characters drew that way, it keeps none, and the next arrival draws every cell again. `Renderer::redraws_missing` says whether that frame has such cells to draw, and the console then asks for the frame at once. Tests: `tests/missing_glyph.rs`, and in vtium, `a_set_that_brings_u25a1_redraws_a_missing_character_at_once` in `tests/boot.rs` and `a_later_u25a1_redraws_the_lone_base_of_a_held_cluster` in `src/deferred.rs`.

The renderer makes its underline and strike sprites from the font's lines (`Face::decorations`), and takes them again when they change. An outline font's renderer is made before the glyph helper has measured the font's lines, so the font first gives lines from the cell's height and the measured ones later. `glyphs_changed` and `forget_glyphs` each compare the font's lines with the ones the sprites were made from. When they differ, the renderer makes the sprites again, and its next frame draws every cell and the border. The glyph cache keeps its glyphs, because the font draws the box-drawing sprites in a weight that follows the cell's height (`vtium_font::sprite::light_line`), not the font's lines; `tests/box_lines.rs` holds a 20x38 cell's light, heavy and double lines to 2, 4 and 2+2 pixels after the lines change. On the GPU path the renderer takes a new identity, so the pipeline makes its glyphs and the sprites' texture anew and composes whole. Lines that did not change change nothing. Tests: `tests/lines.rs`.

The upright frame is held in tiles that `tiles` gives (ADR-1303 D2, D3): one function that, given an image's size, the largest tile and an overlap, gives each tile's held part and the part it owns, up to the middle of each overlap. The upright frame's largest tile is the smaller of the largest texture and the largest viewport, so a display that fits is one tile, the common case, and one tile and many take the same path. Its overlap is twice the spatial step's reach, so that each pixel a tile owns has the reach of neighbours on each side in that tile; with no spatial step it is 0, and the tiles meet edge to edge. The composition clears and draws each rectangle into every tile it meets, with the tile's offset taken off its quads, and present draws from each tile the part of the buffer it owns. Tests: `src/gpu/tile.rs`, `a_display_past_the_largest_texture_is_held_in_tiles`, `a_display_past_the_largest_texture_draws_each_cell_into_each_tile_it_meets`.

A display's frame passes through the `Pipeline`'s five slots in a fixed order: pictures at another size, composition into the upright frame, color, spatial effects, and present into the display's buffer (ADR-1303 D1). A slot with nothing to do holds a no-op that makes no GL call, joins no shader source and changes no pixel. The color and spatial slots hold only their no-ops until the frame shader's record defines their steps; a step that is switched on joins its GLSL into present's fragment shader, between its head and its `main`, when the pipeline is set up, so an empty slot adds no pass and no instruction. `Spatial::reach` gives how far the spatial step reads, which sets the overlap of the upright frame's tiles, and `Spatial::presents_whole` whether every present draws the whole buffer.

The pipeline's set-up makes the upright frame, the display's size with its sides swapped for a quarter turn, in RGBA8 in its tiles, compiles the cell shader, the arrow's and present's, and checks once. Each frame's `Work` names the rectangles cleared to the border's color, the cells, the arrow and the notices, and the rectangles that changed; the composition draws the whole upright frame when it is new or the border's color changes, as the CPU path draws whole when the background changes. GL's errors are read after the frame's uploads (glyph slots and the arrow's texture), after the composition's draws and uploads of notices, and at the frame's end. Present then turns into the display's buffer what that buffer lacks: the rectangles composed over the buffer's age, or the whole buffer at age 0 or at an age past `PRESENT_HISTORY` frames, whether or not this frame composed anything, as the CPU path draws the whole picture into a buffer at age 0, and one whole rectangle when what the buffer lacks includes the whole frame. A frame that composes nothing into a buffer that lacks nothing makes no call, and does not count for the ages that follow. Present takes its size from the buffer, and the pipeline refuses a buffer of another size than the display's. Present reads each pixel of the upright frame by integer coordinates, with no filtering (`texelFetch`). A frame that fails leaves the upright frame and the buffers unknown, so the next frame composes and presents whole (ADR-1303 D3, D9).

A turned display draws on the GPU path as on the CPU path (ADR-1303 D3, D6). The front end works in the display's own pixels on both paths, so the renderer's record of the cells, the border and the pointer is the same whichever path drew the last frame, and `pointer_at` gives the cursor plane's place on either. The GPU path's back end maps each rectangle the front end gives, a cell, its glyph's place, a piece of the border and the arrow's place, into the upright frame through `Turn`, which maps a pixel as the CPU path turns it: a display turned right draws the upright pixel `x`, `y` at `width - 1 - y`, `x`. The grid therefore sits in the upright frame where the CPU path puts it on the display, mapped back, and an odd margin's spare pixel lands where the CPU path leaves it once present turns the frame. The arrow and the notices are drawn upright, the arrow at its turned place mapped back and each notice at its place in the text's view, and a notice that ends hands the renderer its area mapped onto the display. Present draws one quad for each tile and rectangle, its destination the display's rectangle and its source the tile's texels under the rectangle's upright top left, with the turn in its first parameter. Its fragment shader turns each display pixel's offset within the quad into the upright offset by integer arithmetic and reads that texel with `texelFetch`. The frame's damage is the rectangles present drew, in the display's pixels. Tests: `tests/gpu_turn.rs`, which reads present's quads as its shader does and compares where the grid, the pointer, the border and a notice land with the CPU path's drawing of the same screen at every turn, with 5 pixels to spare on each axis; and `src/gpu/turn.rs`.

A turn keeps the GPU path's glyphs, the arrow's texture and every program (ADR-1303 D3, D9). `Renderer::turned` gives the same renderer turned: a new renderer on the CPU path, whose cache holds turned glyphs, and on the GPU path the same identity, so the pipeline keeps the glyphs it holds for it, with the budgets their pools were made with; the decoration sprites the GPU path uploads are always upright. `turned` first takes the font's lines, as `glyphs_changed` does, so lines that changed before a turn reach the GPU path's sprites. The renderer's next GPU frame turns the pipeline (`Pipeline::turn`), which composes the upright frame whole and presents the whole buffer. A half turn, and any turn of a square display, makes no GL call beyond that frame's. A quarter turn of a display that is not square swaps the upright frame's sides, so it makes the frame's tiles anew at the new shape, one batch of allocations checked before the old tiles are freed, and compiles nothing; a turn whose allocation or check fails frees what it made and leaves the pipeline as it was, its orientation and its frame's shape together. `Pipeline::turned` sets up a pipeline for a display already turned. Tests: `a_turn_keeps_every_texture_and_compiles_nothing`, `a_quarter_turn_of_a_display_that_is_not_square_makes_only_the_upright_frame_anew`, `a_turn_whose_check_fails_leaves_the_pipeline_as_it_was`, `a_turn_whose_allocation_fails_leaves_the_pipeline_as_it_was`, and `the_shaders_texel_is_the_one_the_tests_read`, which keeps the tests' Rust copy of present's `texel` in step with the shader's.

Slot 1 draws pictures on the GPU path, in a build with `images` (ADR-1303 D2). The renderer hands the pipeline every picture of the frame, in the frame's order, and where the frame draws each one; the composition draws a cell that pictures reach in ADR-1100 D4's layers, as the CPU path composes it: the cell's background, each picture over it from slot 1's textures, the cell's text layer premultiplied over the pictures beneath it before the first picture above the text, the selection's tint, and vtium's mark. The pictures beneath the last opaque one over the whole cell are not drawn there, and a block cursor's cell draws no picture, as on the CPU path. Two steps fill slot 1, chosen with `Pipeline::set_picture_step` and never by vtium.conf. GPU scaling, the default, uploads each picture's own pixels, the crop its placement shows, once, and the picture shader (`glsl/picture.frag`) scales them with the CPU path's filter in whole numbers: the same 16-bit weights, summed in two 32-bit parts and rounded once, so its pixels are the CPU path's byte for byte on any GL with OpenGL ES 3.0's integers. Two corners take the copy step in place of GPU scaling, though D2 says a picture is never drawn from the CPU copy for its size, which the maintainer agreed to on 2026-11-04 as extreme cases: a picture drawn wider or taller than 32,767 pixels, or from more than 65,535 source pixels across or down, past what the shader's 32-bit integers hold; and a picture whose tiles cannot overlap by twice the filter's reach because that overlap is at least the largest texture, such as an image 10,000 pixels wide shrunk into one 8-pixel cell on a GPU whose largest texture is 2,048 pixels, the least OpenGL ES 3.0 allows. Only a picture drawn wider or taller than any display, or shrunk by a factor larger than about half the largest texture's side in pixels, meets either. The copy step uploads the CPU path's scaled copy at the drawn size, read one texel a pixel. A picture past the largest texture is held in tiles that overlap by twice the filter's reach, and each drawn pixel is drawn from the tile that owns the source pixel under its centre; `Pipeline::limit_picture_textures` holds the tiles smaller for a test. The textures count against the display's bound, `PICTURE_CACHE_SCREENS` screens, through the cache the CPU path's copies use (`src/picture/copies.rs`), which evicts as it does for the CPU path and hands back what it lets go, which slot 1 frees in GL. Where the bound has no room for a picture's own pixels, the picture is drawn from its CPU copy, kept while the bound has room for it; where the copy has no room either, the part the frame draws is scaled on the CPU into the display's one scratch texture for that frame alone. An opaque picture replaces what lies beneath it. A picture that is not opaque, a cell's text layer over the pictures beneath it, vtium's mark and the selection's tint each need the pixels beneath them, and no draw reads its own target. So each such draw first copies the pixels beneath its quads out of the tile into the copy, and then draws with the shader built with `OVER` (or the tint), which reads them from the copy and goes over them with the CPU path's arithmetic: `over_px` with `div255` for the picture, the text layer and the mark, and `mask` for the tint. Each writes every channel as `(c + 0.25) / 255`, so the GPU stores the CPU path's byte whether it rounds or truncates. The GPU's own blending is not used there: its conversion of the blended float to a byte belongs to the driver, and GitHub's runner's llvmpipe (Mesa 25.2.8) came one level below `over_px` at 72 of 195,072 sampled pixels, where Mesa 26.2.6's llvmpipe matched every one. The copy is one target for each display, which the arrow uses too in a build without `images`, kept from frame to frame. A frame that draws over more pixels in one tile than it holds, in width or in height, makes it anew at the larger of each side, and a frame that draws over fewer makes nothing; it is given back once no draw has read it for `GPU_IDLE_FRAMES` frames, and at a release. `Pipeline::picture_counts` gives the textures' counts as `Renderer::picture_counts` gives the CPU path's copies'. Tests: `tests/gpu_pictures.rs`, among them `draws_over_a_copy_read_their_own_tiles_pixels_across_the_seams`, where a picture that is not opaque, its text layers and the arrow cross the seams of six tiles, and `the_copy_of_the_pixels_beneath_grows_only_when_a_frame_needs_more`; `the_copy_of_the_pixels_beneath_lasts_until_no_frame_reads_it_for_the_idle_frames`; and tier 2's `pictures-*@gpu` and `pictures-*@gpu-copy` cases, which measure the GPU path's pictures byte-identical to the CPU path's on llvmpipe, by GPU scaling and by the copy step.

The GPU path gives back textures it no longer uses, counting frames and never wall time (the issue on GPU texture garbage collection). A glyph pool hands out its lowest free slot, so its glyphs gather in its first textures, and gives back its last texture once no glyph has held a slot there for `GPU_IDLE_FRAMES` frames; a picture's texture no placement showed for as many frames is given back too, and so is the copy of the pixels beneath when no draw read it. The pools and the sprites never hold more than `GLYPH_TEXTURE_CEILING` bytes of textures for a display: past it a pool takes no new texture, and a glyph it has no free slot for is drawn blank until the budget's evictions free a slot. That differs from vtium's CPU path, which draws every glyph however full its cache, and from ADR-1303 D9's rule that vtium's own budgets change no pixel; the maintainer agreed on 2026-11-04, since only cells far larger than any font's reach the ceiling. `Pipeline::take_notes` gives the head a line for its log when a glyph is drawn blank, at most once in `GPU_IDLE_FRAMES` frames, and holds at most one such line until the head takes it. `Pipeline::release` frees the glyph and picture textures, the arrow's, the scratch texture, the copy of the pixels beneath and the upright frame, keeps the programs and the context, and makes the next frame draw whole, after making the upright frame again at the display's size; the head calls it when its VT is released (ADR-1303 D9). Tests: `a_picture_texture_no_frame_shows_is_given_back_after_the_idle_frames`, `a_glyph_pools_emptied_texture_is_given_back_after_the_idle_frames`, `past_the_ceiling_a_pool_takes_no_texture_and_reuses_a_slot_given_back` and `a_release_frees_the_glyph_and_picture_textures_and_the_next_frame_draws_whole`.

The quads of every program reach the vertex shader as instances of a strip of four vertices, each quad's destination at attribute location 0 and its source and parameters at 1 and 2, with the corner taken from `gl_VertexID`. vtium-render draws only into textures and imported buffers, where GL's row 0 is the first row in memory. vtium-render calls that row the top, as a display buffer does. Tests: `tests/gpu_gl.rs`, `tests/gpu_pipeline.rs`, `tests/gpu_compose.rs` and the ledger's own in `src/gpu/ledger.rs`.

## Not yet

- **The GPU path draws no 1-bit display yet.** `Renderer::draw_gpu` refuses a 1-bit display. The depth reduction for a 1-bit display (ADR-1301) is not yet in present, and the cell shader draws no color glyph as its silhouette, which a 1-bit display needs. A glyph slot past the GPU's largest texture, possible only for a cell past 2048 pixels on a GPU at OpenGL ES 3.0's least largest texture, fails the frame with a limit error. The GPU path's pixels are judged on a real GL in tier 2 once vtium-platform's GL lands.
- **The ink grid holds one ink color for each glyph that sends ink.** On a 1-bit display the CPU path draws ink that lands in another cell as the opposite of that cell's background (ADR-1302 D8), so the color depends on the cell that receives the ink. When the GPU path draws 1-bit displays, the ink grid or the shader needs that color for each receiving cell.
- **A glyph cut because the boxes were full stays cut while it is cached.** A glyph whose ink passes its cells, drawn when the boxes' texture has reached `GLYPH_TEXTURE_SIDE` and has no free slot, is drawn cut at its cells and cached that way. It takes a box only after the cache evicts it and the font draws it again.
- **The letter under a bar cursor keeps its ink above and below.** Under a bar cursor the letter is not drawn, as ADR-1302 D8 has it, but its ink in the rows above and below still is, so that no neighbour changes with the cursor's blink (ADR-1306 D6).
- **An underline cursor in a cell a picture covers is not crossed by received ink.** There vtium draws the cursor's line above the pictures (ADR-1100 D4), and so above the cell's received ink; in every other cell an accent from the row below crosses it (ADR-1306 D6).
- **The reach byte is pinned in `src/damage.rs`.** ADR-1306 D4 names `tests/memory.rs`; the reach is crate-private, so the unit test `a_cells_history_keeps_its_reach_in_its_old_size` pins a cell's history at 16 bytes, the size it had before the reach.
- **Ink beside a right-to-left letter is ordered left to right.** ADR-1302 D7 orders the ink a cell receives from the cells beside it by the cell's direction, right to left when its character's Bidi_Class is R or AL. vtium-term keeps its Bidi_Class table to itself, so the renderer orders every cell left to right. The order shows only where two neighbours' ink covers the same pixel of a right-to-left letter's cell.
- **Rapid blink (SGR 6) blinks at SGR 5's rate.** ADR-1000's `sgr.rapid-blink` asks for a faster phase, which is vtium/vtium#37's; `tests/blink.rs` pins the shared phase until then.
- **`Frame::palette()` is owed.** The frame does not expose its palette, so the owner passes the default foreground and background in `DrawState`. The frame types the renderers read are vtium/vtium#10's.
- **A full frame takes about 0.5 ms.** At 1366x768 a full redraw with a warm cache measures 0.49 ms in a release build and in the minspec build alike, on one pinned core (`tests/figures.rs`), against the 0.33 ms ADR-1300 measured for its draw kernel alone over the same screen in cached memory. That kernel, the 1-bit glyph loop and its writes, takes 0.18 ms of the release figure. The rest lies outside it: reading the frame a row at a time about 0.035 ms (derived: the 0.07 ms a cell at a time took, less the 0.037 ms the whole frame dropped when the frame was read a row at a time; a 170x48 frame read alone, outside the renderer, took 0.053 ms a cell at a time and 0.027 ms a row at a time), each cell's history 0.04 ms, building the cells' jobs 0.12 ms, the glyph cache's lookups 0.04 ms, setting up each cell's composition 0.05 ms and collecting the damage 0.03 ms. The minspec build builds this crate at `opt-level = 3` (the operator's decision on vtium/vtium#78): at the profile's `opt-level = "z"` it measured 0.93 ms, the composition and the writes taking twice as long, and the binary is 6,232 B larger for it. `tests/figures.rs` measures each figure twice, with unifont and with unifont whose vowels carry an accent above their row (ADR-1306 D4). With unifont, whose ink stays in its cells, the renderer reads no reach, and a full redraw costs what it did before glyph ink reached past its cells: 0.579 ms, the median of 15 runs on one pinned core, before and after. With the accents, about a third of the letters send ink into the row above, and a full redraw in which every letter changes measures 2.6 ms: each cell an accent lands in is composed through the layered path, and composed a second time once the row below it is scanned. Reaching ADR-1300's figure, or the operator revising it, is vtium/vtium#78's.

## Bounds

The frame comes from a program, the font from a file and the cell size from the configuration. Every glyph is bounded by the font's `CELL_SIDE_MAX`, a cluster by `vtium-term`'s `CLUSTER_MAX`, the cache by its budgets and the row scratch by its size.
