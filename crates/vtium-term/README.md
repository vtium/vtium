# vtium-term

vtium-term holds vtium's terminal state. It applies the actions that `vtium-parse` produces to these parts of the state:

- the screen, its cells and lines;
- the scrollback;
- the modes, SGR, the character sets, and the replies.

The crate also decides what keys, the pointer, focus and pastes send the program. It builds the frames that a renderer reads.

The crate forbids unsafe code. It has one dependency outside the workspace, servo's `unicode-bidi` crate (MIT or Apache-2.0). vtium takes that crate from its own fork of it, https://github.com/vtium/unicode-bidi, pinned by revision. Only a build with the `bidi` feature has the crate. The maintainer chose to compute the order of right-to-left text with that crate (ADR-1004 D13). More people have used and checked it than will use code that vtium writes itself. [Right-to-left text](#right-to-left-text) lists the guards that vtium-term holds the crate to.

These parts are in place:

- the cell, line and frame model (vtium/vtium#10, ADR-1001);
- the screen, scrollback, selection and Unicode tables (vtium/vtium#11);
- the modes, SGR, the replies, and the input encoding (vtium/vtium#12);
- the character sets (vtium/vtium#13);
- OSC 52's copy-out (vtium/vtium#39);
- the colors a program sets, queries and resets (vtium/vtium#38);
- OSC 8's links and OSC 133's prompt marks (vtium/vtium#40, ADR-1002);
- OSC 50's font requests (vtium/vtium#126, ADR-1200).

## Behaviour

vtium-term follows the specifications that each item below names. These are ECMA-48, the DEC manuals, xterm's ctlseqs and its manual, console_codes(4) for the Linux console's own sequences, and kitty's protocol documents. The DEC manuals are the VT100 User Guide, the VT220 and VT510 manuals, and DEC STD 070. A specification may leave room or say nothing, or two of them may disagree. Where that happens, this section states what vtium-term does and why, with the test of each case. ADR-1000's tables say which functions vtium supports, ignores or refuses.

vtium-term cuts text into cells by UAX #29's extended grapheme clusters, at the Unicode version the tables carry. With mode 2027 reset, it cuts text by codepoint width instead. A codepoint takes no cell when its General_Category is Mn, Me, Cf or Cc, apart from U+00AD and the Prepend characters. A Hangul medial vowel or final consonant also takes no cell. A codepoint takes two cells when its East_Asian_Width is W or F, or when it has Emoji_Presentation. Every other codepoint takes one cell. A VS16 makes an emoji-style base two cells wide.

The rectangular area functions follow xterm's ctlseqs and DEC STD 070. They are DECERA, DECSERA, DECFRA, DECCRA, DECCARA, and DECRARA with DECSACE. In origin mode, a rectangle's corners count from the top and left margins. A rectangle clips to the screen, or to the region inside all four margins. DECCRA's page numbers name the one page. vtium differs from xterm in nine places:

- **An omitted corner in origin mode.** In origin mode, an omitted corner is the margin's row or column, as DEC STD 070 has it. xterm 411 adds the top and left margins twice to an omitted corner. Its `minRectRow` already holds the margin, and `limitedParseRow` adds the margin again. So in origin mode, xterm's `CSI $ z` starts as far below the top margin as the top margin is below the screen's first row. It also starts as far right of the left margin as the left margin is right of the first column.
- **Erasing.** DECERA and DECSERA erase as ED does, to empty cells in the pen's background. xterm wrote spaces in the pen's attributes for DECERA, and it kept each cell's attributes for DECSERA. vtium's reason is that a rectangle erase and ED then leave the same cell.
- **DECCARA on an empty cell.** DECCARA in the rectangle extent gives an empty cell the attributes, and it leaves the cell empty. xterm wrote a space into the cell. The two draw the same, and a selection copies no space that the program never wrote.
- **DECCARA's parameters.** DECCARA takes every SGR parameter the way SGR applies it to the pen. This is kitty's extension (`deccara`). So DECCARA's 0 also returns the colors to their defaults. xterm's 0 resets bold, underline, blink and inverse. An omitted attribute parameter of DECCARA or DECRARA is 0, as an omitted SGR parameter is. xterm ignores an omitted parameter.
- **DECFRA and wide characters.** DECFRA refuses a character two cells wide. xterm writes such a character into each cell.
- **A wide character at a rectangle's edge.** When a rectangle's edge cuts a wide character, the character goes whole, as it does when an edit cuts it. xterm also does this for DECFRA, DECERA and DECSERA, and it clears both halves. DECCARA and DECRARA change the wide character whole. DECCRA's source edge brings an empty cell in the wide character's style. In these cases xterm changed or copied half of the character.
- **The stream extent.** In the stream extent, DECCARA and DECRARA skip every empty cell. That includes the cells that ECH, EL 1 or DECERA emptied in the middle of a line. xterm marks an erased cell as drawn, and it changes that cell. vtium's cell keeps no drawn mark apart from its content.
- **Wraps.** DECERA, DECSERA, DECFRA and DECCRA end wraps where xterm's rectangle operations keep them. The wrap rules below list these cases.
- **DECRQSS.** DECRQSS reports DECSACE's stream as 0, whatever value other than 2 selected it. xterm reports the value as it was set.

Tests: `tests/rect.rs`, and `decrqss_reports_decsca_and_decsace`.

The modes and controls of vtium/vtium#54 follow xterm's ctlseqs and xterm's own code. Each row's spec column in `docs/modes.md` gives the row's rule.

- **Mode 45** reverse-wraps BS and CUB only into a row that printing wrapped from, as xterm's `CursorBack` does. It never wraps past the screen's first row.
- **Mode 66**, DECNKM, holds the state that DECKPAM and DECKPNM set.
- **Mode 1007** starts set, as foot and Ghostty start it. xterm starts it reset.
- **Mode 1015** is one of the three encodings. Only one encoding is set at a time, as xterm keeps them.
- **Modes 1034 and 1036** choose what Alt sends, as xterm's `Input` does. Both modes start set. So Alt puts ESC before a key that sends a character, or a control such as Tab. The editing, cursor, function and keypad keys never take that ESC. See Input, below.
- **Mode 1035** starts set, as xterm's `numLock` resource does. It changes only how NumLock reads, since vtium has one Alt modifier.
- **Mode 1042** is xterm's bellIsUrgent. vtium has no window to mark urgent. So while the mode is set, a BEL reaches the host. While the mode is reset, vtium ignores BEL. The mode starts set, so BEL rings.
- **XTSAVE and XTRESTORE** keep one saved state for each group of modes that share a state, as xterm's `savemodes` does. Tracking modes 1000 to 1003 share one state, and mode 9 has its own. The encodings share one state. The alternate screen modes share one state.
- **An empty list** saves and restores every mode that is free of side effects. kitty's misc-protocol has it so, and the maintainer ruled the same. These modes are every supported DEC private mode apart from DECOM, the alternate screen modes 47, 1047 and 1049, mode 1048, and mode 2026. No ANSI mode is among them. So the empty list's restore never moves the cursor or switches the screen.
- **XTRESTORE of a mode** that nothing was saved for leaves the mode as it is. xterm restores from a saved value that starts at zero.
- **DECSTR** keeps what XTSAVE saved, and it keeps XTSHIFTESCAPE, as xterm and Ghostty do. RIS clears both. See Resets, below.
- **DECALN** follows xterm's `CASE_DECALN`. It turns origin mode off and sets the margins to the whole screen. It makes every line single size and fills every cell with an E in the default style. It moves the cursor home. The pen keeps its colors and DECSCA's protection, as xterm's `resetRendition` keeps them. The pen loses its other attributes and its underline color. xterm has no underline color, and Ghostty drops it.

Tests: the mode rows' tests in `tests/modes.rs`, `xtrestore_restores_each_mode_xtsave_saved`, `xtsave_keeps_one_state_for_modes_that_share_one`, `the_empty_list_saves_and_restores_every_mode`, `the_empty_list_leaves_the_cursor_and_the_screen_alone`, `xtshiftescape_says_whether_shift_reaches_the_mouse_reports`, `decaln_fills_the_screen_with_e_and_resets_the_margins_and_cursor`, `decaln_returns_a_double_width_line_to_single_size`.

The `images` feature carries the image pipeline (ADR-1100). The default build, minspec, carries no image code. Its input call, dispatch and screen events hold no image hook.

With the feature, the screen holds one image store (`vtium-image`) for its main and alternate screens. The screen uses the `LineId` of each line as the store's key. It tells the store of each of these events:

- a write, an erase, a rectangle operation, ICH and DCH;
- a scroll, IL and DL, and a line the scrollback drops;
- ED 2 and ED 3, an alternate screen switch, RIS, and a rewrap.

The screen marks the cells under each box that the store reports changed (ADR-1001). The frame carries the view's placements in draw order. Each placement shares its image's pixels.

The terminal routes three kinds of string to the handler installed for each. It does so while that string's protocol is switched on. The strings are DCS `q`, APC strings starting with `G`, and a complete OSC `1337;File=`. No handler is installed by default. The host installs vtium-image's sixel handler and the kitty graphics handler (vtium/vtium#49).

Sixel is offered while three things hold: sixel is switched on, its handler is installed, and the budget is above 0. While sixel is offered:

- DA1 reports attribute 4.
- The mode table's rows 80 (DECSDM), 1070 (private sixel color registers) and 8452 (sixel cursor to the right) report their state.
- XTSMGRAPHICS (`CSI ? Pi ; Pa ; Pv S`) reads, sets and resets the color register count. It also reads the geometry, `max-graphic-size`.

While sixel is not offered, the three rows report their fixed state. XTSMGRAPHICS then answers status 1 for every item. A build without images has no row for the three modes, so DECRQM answers 0 for them. That build answers XTSMGRAPHICS with status 1 too (ADR-1100, D10). Tests: `tests/sixel.rs`.

A handler's request to the image helper may be out. While the request is out, the input call takes nothing, so a later string cannot land before an earlier image. An input call stops once its image work reaches `INPUT_WORK_MAX`. The call stops right after the byte that passed the bound. A handler takes only as much of a run of DCS data as the work left allows (vtium-parse's `Consumer::dcs_put_run`, vtium-image's `Handler::feed_some`). The rest of the run comes in the next call.

A handler may hold an upload in chunks open between its strings (vtium-image's `Handler::upload_open`). While it does, any other output the terminal takes makes the handler drop the upload (`Handler::interrupt`). Other output means text, a control, or another escape sequence or string. The terminator that ends a string, `ESC \` or an 8-bit ST, is not other output. The host calls `Terminal::session_ended` when the program in the console exits, and that call drops the upload too. RIS also drops the upload. vtium-image's README lists this as a departure from kitty, which keeps the upload open. Tests: `tests/kitty.rs`.

The image replies are written under one query. The reply rule's test sends hostile text through that query, with a stand-in kitty handler. Tests: `tests/images.rs`, and the store's own tests in `vtium-image`.

**Notes for the log.** With the `images` feature, a handler may drop or ignore what a program sent. The handler then says why in a fixed phrase, through vtium-image's `Context::note`. The note carries a `Cause`, which says who can act on the note.

- `Cause::Program` marks a string that breaks its protocol. Examples are an iTerm2 picture without `inline=1`, a sixel picture with no size, and a kitty upload's data past the pixels that `s` and `v` call for. The kitty handler ignores that extra data and answers OK. Only the program's author can act on these notes.
- `Cause::Console` marks a picture that the console refused, although the program sent it as its protocol asks. The refusal comes from a setting, one of vtium's bounds, the image budget, memory, or the image helper, missing or failing. Such a picture never draws, and the operator can act on why. So the kitty handler notes these refusals even when the program asked for no answer, with `q=2` or with no id.

The program that made a request may have gone. The host then drops the request with `Terminal::abandon_image_request`. The handler that asked is cancelled. Nothing is answered or noted, since the helper did not fail.

The terminal counts each protocol, cause and reason. It keeps a note the first time one comes, and again each time its count reaches 10, 100, 1,000 and so on. The note is an `ImageNote`: the protocol, the cause, the reason, and the count so far. The host takes the notes with `Terminal::take_image_notes` and writes them to its log. vtium writes a program's note at debug level. It writes a console's note at info level, which it shows by default. So an operator whose pictures never draw finds why in the default log. A program that sends the same bad picture every frame therefore costs the log seven lines in its first million pictures. RIS keeps the counts, so a program cannot start them again. The terminal counts at most `IMAGE_NOTE_REASONS_MAX` reasons, and it keeps at most `IMAGE_NOTE_BYTES_MAX` bytes of each reason.

Tests: `a_handlers_note_reaches_the_host_once_with_its_protocol`, `a_reason_that_repeats_reaches_the_host_at_each_power_of_ten`, `each_protocol_and_reason_is_counted_on_its_own`, `the_notes_hold_a_bounded_count_of_reasons_and_bytes`, `a_dropped_picture_is_noted_for_the_log` and `a_picture_the_store_refuses_a_place_is_noted_for_the_log` (`tests/sixel.rs`), and `data_past_the_pixels_is_noted_for_the_log_and_answered_ok`, `a_picture_the_budget_refuses_is_noted_even_unanswered`, `a_failing_helper_is_noted_and_an_abandoned_request_is_not` and `a_picture_past_vtiums_bounds_is_noted` (`tests/kitty.rs`).

### Right-to-left text

ADR-1004 decides how vtium shows right-to-left text. The screen keeps the order that a program wrote. vtium draws each paragraph in the order that Unicode's bidirectional algorithm, UAX #9, gives. Here a paragraph is the rows that a wrap joins. The `bidi` feature carries this work, and the default build, minspec, carries none of it.

In a build with the feature, vtium-term holds the Unicode data and the algorithm that the frame uses. These are `src/unicode/bidi.rs` and its generated tables, and `src/unicode/bidi/levels.rs`, which runs the algorithm.

vtium-term also keeps the paragraph state that the modes set (D2). It keeps that state by the rules of the Terminal Working Group's recommendation, which ADR-1004 follows. Each row holds its paragraph's four properties in `LineFlags`:

- `EXPLICIT` for BDSM reset;
- `RTL` for the direction that SCP and SPD set;
- `DETECT` for mode 2501;
- `BOX_MIRROR` for mode 2500.

The screen keeps a current value of each property. These rules set the values:

- A change always sets the current value. The change sets the rows of the cursor's paragraph only when the cursor stands at the paragraph's first logical position. That position is the first column of the paragraph's first row. For this rule, the screen's first row counts as a paragraph's first row.
- A line feed into a new paragraph gives the paragraph the current values. ED gives them to every row that it clears whole. The clears that RIS and mode 1049 make count here too.
- Each new row takes the current values. A wrap carries the paragraph's values to the row that the wrap starts. A rewrap gives each row it makes the values of the paragraph's first row.
- DECSTR and RIS return the current values to the defaults. The mode rows' initial states and `text-direction` give the defaults.

vtium-term keeps mode 1243's state for the arrow keys. The frame draws by these properties, as the items below describe. A build without the feature has no row for BDSM, DCSM, 1243, 2500 or 2501, so DECRQM answers 0 for each of them. That build ignores SCP and SPD.

- **Joining.** A cell joins the character before it when that character's Joining_Type is D, L or C, and the cell's own type is D, R or C. A cell joins the character after it when the two types are the other way round. Marks (type T) are skipped, and they join nothing themselves. The caller passes the cells in the order the letters are read. That is logical order in implicit mode, and the order drawn from right to left in explicit mode (ADR-1004 D6). The Arabic presentation forms are Non_Joining, since a program has shaped them already.
- **Mirroring.** A character drawn at an odd level draws its Bidi_Mirroring_Glyph. A character with no such glyph draws as it is (D7). While mode 2500 is set, a box-drawing character in a right-to-left paragraph draws a counterpart. The counterpart is the character whose Unicode name swaps the words LEFT and RIGHT. For two characters, the name with LEFT and RIGHT swapped has the halves around AND in the other order. So `gen-unicode` matches those halves either way round. For example, U+257C BOX DRAWINGS LIGHT LEFT AND HEAVY RIGHT pairs with U+257E BOX DRAWINGS HEAVY LEFT AND LIGHT RIGHT. 78 of the block's 128 characters have a counterpart.
- **The guards on `unicode-bidi`** (D13). vtium-term depends on vtium's fork of `unicode-bidi`, with the crate's default features off. The dependency is pinned to the commit `5927633397bc767eb23e0d0c80541272cb88b4ab` of the fork's branch `vtium/fixes`. The fork's README says what the branch changes from servo's main branch.
  - **The tables.** With the default features off, the crate's own tables are never linked. `src/unicode/bidi/levels.rs` passes the crate vtium-term's Bidi_Class and bracket tables, through the crate's `BidiDataSource` trait. These tables are at the version that the other tables pin. The fork's tables are at Unicode 18.0.0 too, so the two give the same answers. vtium-term still passes its own tables, for two reasons. First, the binary then carries one Bidi_Class table, and not a second copy of it in the crate. vtium-term needs that table anyway, for rule L1, for detection, and for joining. Second, vtium-term's Unicode version then moves only when `gen-unicode`'s pins move. `brackets_new_in_unicode_18_pair` checks that U+2E62 and U+2E63 pair by rule N0. These are the parentheses with a middle ring that Unicode 18.0.0 adds.
  - **What vtium-term calls.** `src/unicode/bidi/levels.rs` is the only module that names the crate. The module builds a paragraph with the crate's `ParagraphBidiInfo::new_with_data_source`, which runs rules X1 to I2 over the paragraph once. The module reads the paragraph's level and each character's level from the result's public `paragraph_level` and `levels` fields. It reads them through the crate's `Level` helpers (`Level::ltr`, `Level::rtl`, `Level::new` and `Level::number`). The module applies rule L1 to each drawn row itself, with vtium-term's class table. It orders a row with the crate's `reorder_visual`. It calls no other function of the crate's.
  - **The calls clippy refuses.** The root `clippy.toml` lists `visual_runs`, `reorder_line`, `reordered_levels` and `reordered_levels_per_char` of both `ParagraphBidiInfo` and `BidiInfo` as disallowed. So clippy refuses a call to any of them. Each of them copies and walks the whole paragraph's levels for the row it is asked about. A frame that called them for every row would cost the paragraph's length times its rows.
  - **Panics.** The fork's `visual_runs` panics neither on an empty line nor on the level overflow input that the crate's maintainers were sent. unicode-bidi 0.3.18 panicked on both, and vtium's builds abort on a panic.
  - **Paragraph separators.** A character of Bidi_Class B, such as U+2029 PARAGRAPH SEPARATOR, may stand inside a screen paragraph. There it neither starts a new paragraph nor ends the embeddings before it. This departs from UAX #9's rules P1 and X8. The reason is that a screen paragraph is the rows a wrap joins (ADR-1004 D2). The crate passes over a B in the middle of its text (`a_paragraph_separator_inside_a_paragraph_does_not_split_it`).
  - **Direction.** vtium-term decides a detected paragraph's direction itself, by rules P2 and P3. So a paragraph with no strong character can fall back to right to left.
  - **Moving the pin.** A change that moves the pinned revision reviews each guard against the new revision.
- **The frame** (D3 to D10, `src/order.rs`). When the screen builds a frame, it orders each paragraph that has a row in view and meets one of two tests. Either the paragraph runs right to left, or it holds a row that has held a right-to-left or joining character (`LineFlags::REORDERS`). The algorithm reads the whole paragraph once, up to `BIDI_PARAGRAPH_ROWS_MAX` rows. That read includes the paragraph's rows outside the view. Only the rows that the frame draws are ordered. So a frame's work grows with the paragraph's length, and not with its length times its rows (`a_frame_reads_a_paragraph_once_and_each_drawn_row_once_more`). A paragraph of more rows draws as explicit.
  - Each drawn cell carries its logical column, its direction, its joining sides, and its mirrored character. A cluster's marks are drawn on the counterpart.
  - The cursor is drawn where its logical cell is. The selection stays logical. The pointer, a link's hover and a mouse report name the logical cell under the pointer. The arrow keys swap under mode 1243.
  - A change in a reordered paragraph, a change of its properties, and an edit that splits it mark its rows changed.
  - vtium-term orders a double-width or double-height row over the screen's full width, as it orders any row. Such a row shows only half as many cells, each twice as wide. Once vtium draws these rows at their size (DECDWL and DECDHL), the order must cover only the cells the row shows, as D8 says.
- **Tests.** `every_case_of_bidi_test_passes` and `every_case_of_bidi_character_test_passes` run Unicode's BidiTest.txt (770,241 cases) and BidiCharacterTest.txt (91,707 cases) at 18.0.0. They run the cases through `resolve` and `visual_order`, as the frame calls them. They check the paragraph's level, each character's level, and the visual order. The crate's own conformance tests run the same files at the same version, through the crate's own tables and its own rule L1.
  - `the_level_overflow_input_resolves_without_a_panic` resolves the input of the crate's report. The test lays the input out as the line that 0.3.18's `visual_runs` panicked on. `empty_paragraphs_and_rows_resolve_without_a_panic` resolves empty paragraphs and rows.
  - `bracket_pairing_stops_when_its_stack_overflows` checks rule BD16's limit of 63 opening brackets, which no case in Unicode's files reaches. One opening bracket past the limit leaves every bracket of the isolating run sequence unpaired, as UAX #9 says.
  - `the_forks_visual_runs_accepts_an_empty_line` calls the refused `visual_runs` and `reorder_line` on an empty line, to pin the fork's fix. Pointed back at unicode-bidi 0.3.18, those last two tests fail.
  - The screen's fuzz target draws a frame of every stream it feeds, so the fuzz target reaches this code.

### Kitty's Unicode placeholders

With the `images` feature, vtium-term reads kitty's Unicode placeholder cells (ADR-1100, D13). A placeholder cell is a cell whose text is U+10EEEE followed by up to three marks. The marks come from the kitty spec's list of 297 row and column diacritics. The first mark's place in the list is the cell's row in the picture. The second mark's place is the cell's column. The third mark's place is the top byte of the image id.

The cell's foreground color gives the image id's low 24 bits, as a palette index or as a direct color's 24-bit value. The cell's underline color gives the placement id the same way. The default foreground, or an image id of 0, names no image. The default underline color names no placement.

A cell may leave marks out. It then takes them from the placeholder cell to its left, when that cell has the same foreground and underline colors:

- With no mark, the cell takes the left cell's row, the left cell's column plus one, and its top byte.
- With only a row mark, the cell takes the left cell's column plus one and its top byte, when the left cell is on the same picture row.
- With a row mark and a column mark, the cell takes the left cell's top byte. It does so when the left cell is on the same picture row and in the column before.

A value that no rule gives is 0. A mark that is not one of the 297 ends the cell's marks. A third mark past 255 gives no top byte.

vtium-term groups the placeholder cells of each row into runs. A run is a group of side-by-side cells that name the same image id and placement id. The cells lie on the same picture row, and they show consecutive picture columns. A run holds the line's key, its first column, its cell count, the 32-bit image id, the placement id, and the picture row and column of its first cell. Its type is vtium-image's `PlaceholderRun`, which vtium-term re-exports.

vtium-term keeps no runs. Three functions read them when they are needed:

- `Screen::placeholder_runs` reads the runs from the rows the view shows, for a frame.
- `Screen::screen_placeholder_runs` reads the runs from the screen's own lines of a buffer. It serves a store decision that needs a virtual placement's position.
- `Screen::mark_placeholders` marks changed the placeholder cells of one image on the screen's own lines, for damage.

The last two functions count the cells they read as input work.

A write, an erase, a scroll or a rewrap moves the cells as text. The next read finds the cells where they are. So a picture row that a rewrap splits over two rows is two runs. The row before may have soft-wrapped into a cell's own row. A cell there that leaves its marks out then inherits from the last cell of the row before. So the second piece of a split picture row keeps counting columns from the first piece. A hard line break restarts the count.

A read may start at the top of the screen or of the view. That read first walks back through the rows above it that soft-wrapped and end in a placeholder cell. It walks back at most as many rows as a picture row of 297 cells fills. So a row that wrapped down from the scrollback keeps its columns. `screen_placeholder_runs` counts those cells as work too.

A frame resolves a placeholder cell's colors with the default foreground and no underline color, since those colors name the cell's image and its placement. So under SGR 7, DECSCNM or a selection drawn by swapping, the cell takes the colors that a space with the same background and attributes takes. The cell never takes the color that encodes its image id. The cell keeps its glyph, and by that glyph vtium-render draws no text, underline or strike-through there. A build without images resolves U+10EEEE's colors as it resolves any private-use character's.

vtium-term hands the image store the runs at three times:

- **For the frame.** `Screen::placements` builds the frame with the runs on the rows the view shows (`Store::frame_with_runs`). So a virtual placement draws its slice of picture under each run, and the picture moves with its cells. `Screen::placements` reads the runs only while the shown buffer holds a virtual placement (`Store::has_virtual`).
- **For the store's decisions.** vtium-term reads the runs from the screen lines of each buffer that holds a child of a virtual placement (`Store::has_virtual_children`), and only then.
  - vtium-term reads the runs for every call that reaches the store through `Screen::with_images`. These calls are every kitty command, every picture that a protocol handler stores, `Screen::add_image`, and `Screen::place_image`. The store then holds the runs through the call (`Store::with_runs`).
  - Some decisions take such a child by the cells it draws on, and those decisions use the runs. They are a kitty delete by cell, row or column, a kitty delete of every placement, and a reservation that may evict.
  - vtium-term cannot tell those calls from the others, such as a transmission that evicts nothing. The reason is that a handler reaches the store through one call. So vtium-term reads the runs for each call.
  - ED 2 reads only the screen in use, before its erase removes the cells. ED 2 runs its rule inside `Store::with_runs` the same way.
- **For damage.** After each store call, vtium-term marks the placeholder cells of every image whose virtual placement the store reports changed (`Store::take_changed_virtual`). It also marks the cells where each relative placement that follows a virtual placement drew before the call, or draws after it (`Store::take_changed_floating`). Such a placement has no box of its own. So the store reports the placement by the virtual placement it follows, and by its offset and size in cells. vtium-term finds that placement's origin from the runs. One read of the shown screen serves both kinds of mark. When the store has more such boxes than it keeps notes of, it reports the buffer changed whole. vtium-term then marks every cell.

Tests: `tests/placeholders.rs`, `tests/placeholder_pictures.rs`, `a_placeholder_cell_takes_a_blank_cells_colors_under_reverse_video`, `every_diacritic_is_an_above_mark_in_the_pinned_ucd` and `the_table_is_ghosttys_list_entry_for_entry`.

### The screen

- **A line leaving the top of the main screen's scroll region enters the scrollback, wherever the region starts.** On the alternate screen, such a line is cleared. No specification covers a scrollback. A program that scrolls a region below a status line keeps its history this way. xterm keeps only the lines that leave a region at the screen's top. Test: `a_line_leaving_any_region_enters_the_scrollback`.
- **DL at the screen's first row clips the placements on the lines it deletes.** In a build with images, the lines that DL deletes go, and so does what is anchored on them, as at any other row. No specification covers this case. The kitty spec has a placement wholly inside a scrolled band clipped at its edges. Test: `dl_at_the_first_row_clips_what_it_deletes`.
- **Cells past a line's end read in the line's fill, never the pen.** A scrollback line narrower than the screen is padded with empty cells in its fill when it is drawn. The fill is the default style, unless the program erased the line's end in a color (ADR-1003). No specification covers padding. The reason for this rule is that padding is not text the program wrote, so the pen has no claim on it. A pen change that moved no change age would also leave a display drawing stale colors. Tests: `cells_past_the_end_read_blank_in_the_default_style` and `trim_keeps_the_fill_as_the_style_past_the_end` (the line), `a_pen_change_while_scrolled_back_leaves_the_padding_alone` (the screen).
- **A width change rewraps the main screen and its scrollback (ADR-1003, vtium/vtium#41).** Rows that a wrap joined become one logical line, and that line wraps at the new width. A line that the program ended with a newline, or placed by moving the cursor, joins nothing. A wide character that no longer fits moves whole to the next row and leaves a pad. On a screen one column wide, the wide character becomes an empty cell. The cursor, the main screen's saved cursor, the selection and the view keep their place in the text. The rows below the cursor keep their number, less what the text above the cursor grew, as Ghostty keeps them. Spec: contour's line reflow mode, mode 2028. Rows printed while that mode is reset are cut instead. Tests: `tests/reflow.rs`, among them `a_wrapped_line_rejoins_when_the_screen_widens` (the probe `reflow`), `the_cursor_keeps_its_place_in_the_text` and the three property tests.
- **No row keeps a cell past the right margin.** Three kinds of row are cut at a narrower width and never rewrapped: the alternate screen's rows, rows printed with mode 2028 reset, and rows of double size. The main screen wraps its text onto extra rows instead. No specification covers a resize's cells. The reason for this rule is that a hidden cell that reappeared on a widening would be text the screen no longer shows. Also, a full-screen program redraws on SIGWINCH. Tests: `the_alternate_screen_is_cut_and_never_rewrapped`, `a_row_printed_with_mode_2028_reset_is_cut`, `fixed_rows_are_cut_and_marks_go_to_the_first_row`.
- **A widening's new columns take the line's fill.** On the main screen, the new columns are empty cells in the color that the program erased the line's end with. A line keeps that color as its fill. Where the line has no such color, the new columns are in the default style. On the alternate screen, the new columns are blank. No specification covers them. The reason for this rule is that a line erased in a color to its end then reads as erased to the new end, as a pager's colored lines do. Tests: `columns_a_widening_resize_adds_take_the_lines_fill`, `a_widening_paints_the_lines_fill`.
- **Outside the region, IND, RI, a line feed and a wrap stop at the screen's edge.** Spec: the VT510 manual, IND: "If the cursor is at the bottom margin, then the screen performs a scroll-up." The VT510 manual, DECAWM: "Any text on the page scrolls up if the cursor is at the end of the scrolling region." The VT220 manual, chapter 4, RI: "If cursor is at top margin, screen performs a scroll down." The VT510 manual has no RI page. Below the region, a wrap on the last row returns to the row's first column, as a wide character or a widened cluster does. The wrap leaves the row unmarked as wrapped, since the row's text does not continue. Test: `index_outside_the_region_stops_at_the_screens_edge`.
- **An inverted or one-row DECSTBM is ignored, and a bottom margin past the screen is its last row.** The VT510 manual says only "The value of the top margin (Pt) must be less than the bottom margin (Pb)." So vtium does as xterm does (`charproc.c`, `CASE_DECSTBM`). The region and the cursor stay, and a bottom past the screen clamps. DECSTBM homes the cursor, and an omitted bottom margin is the last row (the VT510 manual). Tests: `an_invalid_decstbm_is_ignored_and_a_bottom_past_the_screen_clamps`, `decstbm_homes_the_cursor_and_an_omitted_bottom_is_the_last_line`.
- **A private marker or an intermediate makes another function.** CUD, CUF, CUB, CNL, CPL, CHA, VPA, VPR, ED, EL, ECH, IL, DL, CHT, CBT and REP act only without either. ED and EL also act with `?` (DECSED, DECSEL). Spec: ECMA-48 5.4, "Together with the Final Byte F, [the intermediate bytes] identify the control function". By ECMA-48 5.4.1 b), a private marker makes the parameter string private (vtium-parse's README, "Notes for vtium-term"). Test: `a_marker_or_an_intermediate_makes_another_function`.
- **A printed DEL does nothing.** Spec: vt100.net, "terminals only claiming VT220 compatibility can always ignore 7F". Only a 96-character set in GL draws DEL, and vtium designates none. Test: `a_printed_del_does_nothing`.
- **A line keeps 6 bytes of clusters a cell (ADR-1001's cluster budget).** So on a screen one column wide, a heart and VS16 keep the heart. Together they take 7 bytes with their length byte.
  - A row may be cut to fewer cells, or land on a screen narrower than the width its clusters were written at. Such a row keeps the clusters that printing it at the narrower width keeps. So a family emoji of five codepoints, narrowed from 5 columns to 3, keeps its first four.
  - A row may instead land on a screen as wide or wider. There a widening may have gathered onto the row the clusters of parts of three source rows. Such a row keeps, with the same cut, the clusters that fit twice its budget, which is its arena's limit.
  - The scrollback keeps every cluster of each row that rewraps.
  - No specification bounds a cluster. The reason for the budget is that a program cannot grow a line's memory past it.
  - In a build with images, a kitty placeholder cluster keeps its first three marks outside the budget (ADR-1001, "Placeholder cells").
  - Tests: `the_cluster_budget_cuts_a_cluster_on_a_one_column_line`, `a_placeholder_keeps_three_marks_whatever_the_budget`, `a_row_narrowed_past_its_budget_keeps_what_printing_there_keeps`, `a_screen_narrowed_from_a_wide_grid_holds_its_rows_to_their_bound`, `a_screen_widened_by_a_fraction_holds_its_rows_to_their_bound`.

### Left and right margins

Mode 69 (DECLRMM) lets DECSLRM, `CSI Pl ; Pr s`, set left and right margins. Resetting the mode returns the margins to the whole width. RIS resets the mode. DECSTR keeps the mode and the left and right margins, since the VT510 manual's DECSTR table leaves out DECLRMM and DECSLRM. DECALN and a resize return the margins to the whole width, and they leave the mode as it is. The left and right margins, with the top and bottom margins, bound printing, the cursor's movement, the editing and scrolling functions, and origin mode. They do so as xterm's ctlseqs and the VT510 manual define them.

A black-box run of xterm 411 printed the same screen and cursor as vtium for every stream in `tests/margins.rs`, apart from the places this section names. The run used `-ti vt420 -u8` under Xvfb. It printed xterm's screen through `printerCommand`, and it read xterm's cursor with CPR. Spec: ctlseqs, DECLRMM, DECSLRM, DECIC, DECDC, DECBI and DECFI; the VT510 manual, the same pages. Tests: `tests/margins.rs`.

- **Printed text wraps at the right margin to the left margin of the next row.** From right of the right margin, text wraps at the screen's last column. A pending wrap at a right margin before the last column keeps the cursor on the margin's column. So `Screen::cursor` reports that column there, and DECSC and DECRC keep the pending wrap. The wrap scrolls the region at the bottom margin only from between the left and right margins, as a line feed does. Test: `printing_wraps_at_the_right_margin_to_the_left_margin`, `decsc_keeps_a_pending_wrap_at_the_right_margin`.
- **A row is marked as continuing on the next row only when it wraps from the screen's last column to the next row's first.** So a wrap at a right margin, or to a left margin past the first column, leaves the row unmarked. xterm marks every wrapped row. The reason is that reflow and selection read a marked row's text as going on at the next row's first column. A row cut at a margin has other text beside it. Mode 45 then reverse-wraps BS and CUB only into rows marked that way. So inside narrower margins, BS stops at the left margin. xterm goes back to the right margin of the row above. Test: `printing_wraps_at_the_right_margin_to_the_left_margin`.
- **A wide character that does not fit before a right margin inside the screen leaves the column it skips erased.** The character wraps whole, as it does at the screen's edge. At the screen's edge, the skipped column becomes a pad. At a margin, the row is not marked as continuing, so the column is erased. The column is erased the same way where a wrap returns to the same row. xterm leaves the column as it was. No specification covers wide characters at a margin.
- **With autowrap reset (DECAWM), a wide character that reaches the last column, or the right margin, is not printed.** The character needs two cells, and only one is left. With autowrap reset, the cursor cannot move to the next row. vtium leaves the row and the cursor as they were, so no row holds half of a wide character. No specification covers this case. A narrow character at the last column still overwrites that column, as the VT510 manual's DECAWM says. Test: the tier 1 case `wide-edges`, its fifth screen.
- **CUF stops at the right margin, and at the last column from right of it. HT stops at the right margin, from right of it too.** The VT510 manual has HT move the cursor "to the right margin" when no tab stop is left. xterm's `TabToNextStop` holds a tab to the right margin wherever the cursor starts, and vtium does as xterm does. CUB, BS and CR stop at the left margin, and at the first column from left of it. CBT stops at the left margin only in origin mode, as xterm's `TabToPrevStop` stops. Test: `cursor_movement_stops_at_the_margins`.
- **In origin mode, CUP, HVP, CHA, HPA and VPA count columns from the left margin and stay inside the margins, and CPR counts from it.** DECSLRM and DECSTBM home the cursor to the region's top left corner. DECRC restores the cursor to where it was on the screen, whatever the origin mode. xterm moves a cursor saved outside the margins inside them. Test: `origin_mode_counts_columns_from_the_left_margin`.
- **ICH, DCH, IL and DL do nothing while the cursor is outside the margins.** ICH and DCH move the cells between the cursor and the right margin. IL and DL move the cells between the left and right margins, in the rows from the cursor's row to the bottom margin. IL and DL also put the cursor on the left margin. In insert mode, text printed between the margins moves the cells up to the right margin. Text printed outside the margins moves nothing. Spec: the VT510 manual, ICH, DCH, IL and DL; xterm's `InsertChar`, `DeleteChar`, `InsertLine` and `DeleteLine`. Tests: `ich_and_dch_move_the_cells_up_to_the_right_margin`, `il_and_dl_move_the_cells_between_the_margins`, `insert_mode_moves_the_cells_up_to_the_right_margin`.
- **IND, LF, NEL and RI scroll the region between all four margins only while the cursor is between the left and right margins.** SU and SD scroll the region wherever the cursor is. Outside the margins, a line feed at the bottom margin moves nothing, as xterm's `xtermIndex` has it. A scroll inside narrower margins moves cells, not lines, so nothing enters the scrollback. The pictures over the rectangle move with the scroll, as ADR-1100 D6 gives. The part of a sixel inside the rectangle moves. A kitty placement moves only when it lies wholly inside the rectangle. Tests: `line_feeds_scroll_only_between_the_margins`, `su_and_sd_scroll_the_region_between_the_margins`.
- **ED, EL and ECH erase whole rows' cells, whatever the margins,** as the VT510 manual has them. Test: `erases_ignore_the_margins`.
- **DECIC and DECDC insert and delete columns at the cursor's column, in each row from the top margin to the bottom, up to the right margin.** They do nothing while the cursor is outside the region. Without left and right margins, they work on the whole width. DECBI and DECFI move the cursor one column. At the left or right margin, they move the region's columns instead. Spec: the VT510 manual, DECIC, DECDC, DECBI and DECFI; xterm's `xtermColScroll` and `xtermColIndex`. Tests: `decic_and_decdc_insert_and_delete_columns`, `decbi_and_decfi_move_the_cursor_or_the_columns`.
- **A wide character across a left or right margin goes whole before the cells between the margins move.** xterm's `scrollInMargins` clears such a character the same way. An edit that changes a row's last column ends the row's wrap. An edit that changes the first column of the region's first row ends the wrap of the row above. The wrap rules below say the same for whole lines. Test: `a_wide_character_across_a_margin_goes_whole_when_the_region_scrolls`.
- **DECRQSS reports DECSLRM as `Pl ; Pr s`,** the whole width while DECLRMM is reset, as xterm reports it. Test: `decrqss_reports_the_left_and_right_margins`.

### Change tracking and the frame

- **A region scroll marks only its rows.** Scrolling inside margins, IL and DL mark the rows that moved. They leave the rows outside the margins unchanged. No specification covers change tracking. The reason for this rule is that a renderer redraws what is marked. A one-row scroll in a status pane should not redraw the whole screen. Tests: `a_region_scroll_marks_only_the_region` (`Damage`'s rule, and the screen's), and the property test `a_frames_changed_set_covers_every_cell_that_differs`. A planted skip turns that property test red.
- **Moving the cursor marks nothing.** The frame carries the cursor. A renderer redraws the cells that the cursor left and covers (ADR-1001, "Change tracking"). So after a cut in the replay view, a caller's next `frame-since` counts no cell for the cursor. Test: `after_a_checkpoint_nothing_is_changed`.
- **The frame marks the default background.** A frame cell says whether its background is the default one and not inverted. So an image below cell backgrounds shows through exactly there. A background that a program set to the default's color hides the image. Spec: the kitty graphics protocol, z-index below -1,073,741,824: drawn "under cells with non-default background colors". Test: `default_bg_marks_only_the_default_background_uninverted`.

### Character sets

So after `ESC ) 0`, ñ still prints as ñ. TERM=linux's `enacs`, GNU screen and elinks send that sequence. Spec: xterm's UTF-8 mode. The xterm manual (patch 411), `vt100Graphics`, has xterm "interpret VT100 graphic character escape sequences while in UTF-8 mode". A black-box run of xterm 411 shows ñ for `ESC ) 0 ñ` and ─ for `ESC ) 0 SO q SI`. The run used `-u8` under Xvfb, and it printed xterm's screen through `printerCommand`. xterm stops decoding UTF-8 while GR holds a set other than ASCII after LS1R, LS2R or LS3R (`ptydata.c`). So the same run shows `Ã±` for `ESC ) 0 ESC ~ ñ`. vtium keeps decoding. Test: `a_utf8_character_prints_as_itself_whatever_g1_to_g3_hold`.
- **The default mapping is the VT220's: G0 to G3 hold ASCII, GL holds G0 and GR holds G2.** DECSCL 62 to 64 choose 8-bit input, and they designate DEC supplemental graphics into G2 and G3. DECSCL 61 designates nothing. So SO or SS3 with nothing designated print ASCII. Spec: the VT220 manual, 4.4, "The default mapping in VT200 mode is ASCII graphics in GL and DEC supplemental graphics in GR". The manual's 4.4.3 gives LS2R: "Invoke G2 into GR (VT200 mode only). After `ESC ) 0` the byte F1 prints ñ, and after `ESC * 0` it prints ─. ### Text attributes

- **SGR 8 conceals text, and SGR 28 reveals it.** Spec: ECMA-48 8.3.117 and ctlseqs. A concealed cell keeps its text, so a selection over the cell copies the text. The frame hands the renderer the attribute, and the renderer draws the cell as its background. DECRQSS reports the attribute as 8.
- **SGR 53 sets an overline, and SGR 55 clears it.** Spec: ECMA-48 8.3.117. DECRQSS reports it as 53.
- **SGR 73 sets superscript, SGR 74 sets subscript, and SGR 75 clears both.** Spec: mintty's CtrlSeqs. Each of 73 and 74 takes the place of the other, and DECRQSS reports the one set as 73 or 74.
- **SGR 6 sets rapid blink, and SGR 25 clears it with SGR 5's blink.** Spec: ECMA-48 8.3.117. A cell can carry both blinks, and DECRQSS reports rapid blink as 6.
- **DECCARA takes every one of these, since it applies SGR's parameters.** DECRARA's 8 reverses the conceal attribute, as DEC STD 070 gives.

### Colors

- **Programs set, query and reset colors.** Spec: ctlseqs, "Operating System Commands".
  - OSC 4 sets palette colors, and OSC 104 resets them.
  - OSC 10 to 14, 17 and 19 set and query these colors: the default foreground and background, the cursor, the pointer's foreground and background, and the selection's background and foreground. A list goes on to the next number. OSC 110 to 114, 117 and 119 reset these colors.
  - A reset returns a color to the palette that the host configured. The host's `set_palette` replaces the colors a program set.
  - DECSTR returns the 256 palette colors to the configured palette. It keeps the colors that OSC 10 to 19 set, as xterm's soft reset does (`ReallyReset` in xterm 412's `charproc.c`).
  - An unset cursor color draws the cell inverted, and its query answers the foreground. An unset selection background draws the selection inverted. A set selection background takes the cell's foreground over it, unless OSC 19 sets a selection foreground too. The unset queries answer as xterm's defaults do. The cursor and the selection background answer the foreground, and the selection foreground answers the background.
  - ctlseqs's color specs are X11's. vtium reads `rgb:` with one to four hex digits a channel, scaled. It reads `#` with one to four hex digits a channel, as the channel's high bits. Each prefix and digit may be in either case. vtium keeps each channel's top 8 bits, so a query answers `rgb:` with each byte written twice.
  - A color name is X11's, from the `rgb.txt` of X.Org's rgb-1.1.1, in every build (vtium/vtium#103). vtium reads a name in any case and with any spaces (color.names). The X server matched a name that `rgb.txt` lists in any case. The file lists each name of several words both spaced and joined. So vtium reads every name that xterm reads. It also reads spacings that the file does not list, such as `darkslate gray`.
  - `rgbi:` and the Xcms spaces set nothing, since vtium holds no Xcms color spaces. An index past 255 sets nothing either. OSC 15, 16 and 18 are Tektronix's, and they are skipped in a list (color.tektronix).
  - Tests: `tests/colors.rs`, and the table against `rgb.txt` in `src/terminal/color.rs`.
- **RIS returns every color to the configured palette.** xterm's RIS resets the 256 palette colors. It keeps the colors that OSC 10 to 19 set, as its DECSTR does (`ReallyReset` in xterm 412's `charproc.c`). vtium's RIS also returns the defaults and the cursor, pointer and selection colors, since RIS returns the terminal to its initial state. Test: `decstr_resets_the_palette_and_ris_every_color`.
- **OSC 21 is kitty's color protocol.** xterm's OSC 21 is DECSWT, a title, which vtium ignores. ADR-1000 takes kitty's meaning, which Ghostty also implements.
  - The keys are `foreground`, `background`, `cursor`, `selection_background`, `selection_foreground`, and the indices 0 to 255. `key=spec` sets a key. `key=?` queries a key, and the queries are answered in one reply. A bare key resets the key.
  - `key=` unsets the cursor and the selection colors. It is ignored for the other keys, which kitty does not let a program make dynamic.
  - vtium leaves out of the reply a key that it does not know. Such keys include kitty's `cursor_text`, `visual_bell` and `transparent_background_color1` to `7`. kitty answers `unknown=` with the key's name in base64. vtium does not, because a reply holds no text from the request (docs/architecture.md, the reply rule).
  - The colors are read as OSC 4 reads them. A color name is X11's, from `rgb.txt`. `rgbi:` and `@alpha` set nothing.
  - Tests: `osc_21_sets_queries_and_resets_by_key`, `color_names_set_the_defaults_and_kittys_keys`.
- **XTPUSHSGR and XTPOPSGR keep pens on a stack.** `CSI Pm # {` pushes the pen, and `CSI # }` pops it. `CSI Pm # p` and `CSI # q` are the same functions. Spec: ctlseqs, XTPUSHSGR and XTPOPSGR.
  - With no parameters, or with one omitted, the pop restores every part of the pen that SGR sets. Otherwise each parameter names a part by its SGR code. Codes 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 name bold, faint, italic, underlined and blink. Codes 7, 8 and 9 name inverse, invisible and crossed-out. Code 21 names doubly underlined, 30 the foreground, and 31 the background. xterm's older 10 and 11 stand for 30 and 31. A code that the list leaves out names nothing, as in xterm 412 (`charproc.c`, `CASE_XTERM_PUSH_SGR`).
  - vtium holds the underline's style and color (SGR 58) as one part, which 4 and 21 both name. Blink takes rapid blink with it, as SGR 25 clears both. The overline and the script are not in the list, so they return only with every part. DECSCA's protection is not SGR's, and it stays, as it does with SGR 0.
  - The stack keeps `SGR_STACK_MAX` pens. A push past that bound keeps nothing and is counted. The pop that pairs with that push restores nothing, as xterm's `xtermPushSGR` and `xtermPopSGR` do (`misc.c`). A pop of an empty stack does nothing.
  - Tests: `tests/stacks.rs`, `a_pop_leaves_the_protection`, `the_parts_xterm_does_not_list_return_with_every_part`.
- **XTPUSHCOLORS, XTPOPCOLORS and XTREPORTCOLORS keep palettes on a stack, which kitty's OSC 30001 and 30101 push and pop.** Spec: ctlseqs, XTPUSHCOLORS, XTPOPCOLORS and XTREPORTCOLORS; kitty's `color-stack`.
  - An entry holds all of `Terminal::palette()`: the 256 colors, the defaults, and the cursor's, pointer's and selection's colors.
  - `CSI # P` stores the palette in the entry after the current one. `CSI # Q` restores the palette from the current entry.
  - A parameter of 1 to `COLOR_STACK_MAX` stores into that entry or restores from it. After a push, that entry becomes the current one. After a pop, the entry after it becomes the current one.
  - A parameter of 0 pushes and pops, as ctlseqs says. xterm 412 stored an explicit 0 and then made the stack empty (`xtermPushColors`, `misc.c`).
  - Several parameters act in turn.
  - A push with the stack full does nothing, and so does a parameter past the bound. A pop of an empty stack, and a pop of an entry never stored, do nothing too. Each of these follows xterm.
  - A pop that changes a color marks every cell changed.
  - `CSI # R` answers `CSI ? Pc ; Ps # Q`. The reply gives the current entry, and then the entries stored, which is the highest entry that holds a palette. This follows ctlseqs's wording, and foot answers the same way (foot's `csi.c` at `152487e`, MIT). xterm 412 answered with the most entries that a push without a parameter had reached. So xterm left out an entry stored by number.
  - OSC 30001 and 30101, each with nothing after the number, push and pop the same stack as `CSI # P` and `CSI # Q`. kitty takes xterm's functions too, and it documents neither a depth nor two stacks.
  - Tests: `tests/stacks.rs`, `no_reply_carries_the_requests_bytes`.
- **A color push with the stack full keeps nothing, where kitty drops the oldest entry.** kitty 0.49.2 was run as a program and driven through its pty. It kept the newest 10 of 11 pushes, by XTPUSHCOLORS and OSC 30001 alike. So its tenth pop restored the second push's palette. It reported `CSI ? 9 ; 10 # Q` after the pushes, and `CSI ? 0 ; 0 # Q` once the pops emptied the stack. vtium keeps the first 10 pushes, as xterm 412 does (`xtermPushColors`, `misc.c`). So vtium's tenth pop restores the first push's palette. The stack reports `CSI ? 10 ; 10 # Q`, and then `CSI ? 0 ; 10 # Q`. The pops still pair with the pushes that the stack kept. kitty's `color-stack` documents neither behaviour. Test: `a_push_past_the_color_bound_keeps_nothing`.
- **RIS empties the SGR and color stacks, and DECSTR keeps them.** xterm 412 kept both stacks through RIS and DECSTR (`ReallyReset`, `charproc.c`). foot empties its color stack on RIS and keeps it on DECSTR (`term_reset` in foot's `terminal.c` at `152487e`). RIS returns the terminal to its initial state, which holds no pushed pens or palettes. Test: `ris_empties_the_stacks_and_decstr_keeps_them`.
- **Mode 2031 reports a change between dark and light, and nothing as it is set.** contour's color palette update notifications send `CSI ? 997 ; Ps n` on each update of the palette. vtium sends the report only when the default background crosses between dark and light, since the report carries only that. The crossing can come from OSC 11, 111 or 21, a color stack pop, or the configured palette. A change of a palette color, of the foreground, or to a background of the same scheme sends nothing. Dark is a luma below half, Y = 0.299 R + 0.587 G + 0.114 B (ITU-R BT.601). neovim applies this rule to OSC 11's answer. contour's spec and foot(7) give no rule. Tests: `mode_2031_reports_a_change_of_scheme`, `a_color_stack_pop_reports_a_change_of_scheme`, `the_scheme_query_answers_by_the_backgrounds_luma`.

### Input

- **Keys are sent in xterm's encoding, modifiers included.**
  - With Shift, Alt or Ctrl held, the cursor keys, Home and End send `CSI 1 ; m X`, in either DECCKM state. F1 to F4 send `CSI 1 ; m P` to `CSI 1 ; m S`. Insert, Delete, Page Up, Page Down and F5 to F20 send `CSI code ; m ~`. Here m is 1, plus 1 for Shift, 2 for Alt, and 4 for Ctrl.
  - Shift with F1 to F10 is Shift's parameter, so Shift+F1 sends `CSI 1 ; 2 P`.
  - With NumLock off, the keypad's Insert, Delete and Begin send what Insert, Delete and `CSI E` send, in either keypad mode.
  - In application keypad mode, the keypad's other keys send `SS3 final`, and `SS3 m final` with a modifier. So the key * sends `SS3 j`, / sends `SS3 o`, = sends `SS3 X`, Tab sends `SS3 I`, and the space sends `SS3 SP`.
  - In numeric keypad mode, those keys send their character, = included. vtium drops any modifier on them, Alt too.
  - Shift+Tab sends `CSI Z` with any other modifier held.
  - Alt never puts ESC before any of these keys.
  - Spec: ctlseqs, "PC-Style Function Keys" and "VT220-Style Keypad", with xterm's defaults modifyCursorKeys 2, modifyFunctionKeys 2 and modifyKeypadKeys 0. xterm 411, driven under Xvfb, sends each of these sequences.
  - Tests: `editing_and_cursor_keys_carry_xterms_modifier_parameter`, `function_keys_carry_xterms_modifier_parameter`, `alt_with_an_editing_or_function_key_is_its_parameter`, `keypad_operators_follow_the_specification`, `the_keypads_editing_keys_send_the_editing_keys_sequences`, `the_keypads_tab_and_space_follow_the_specification`, `shift_tab_is_csi_z_with_any_modifier`.
- **A paste is filtered as xterm filters one.** In every paste, bracketed or not, these controls each become one space: each C0 control apart from HT, LF and CR, DEL, and each C1 control. A paste is UTF-8 text in every input. So a C1 control is a code point, U+0080 to U+009F, and its two bytes become one space. Ill-formed bytes are read as U+FFFD. Since ESC and the C1 controls are filtered, a bracketed paste never carries its own end marker.
  - Spec: the xterm manual (patch 412), `allowPasteControls`, false by default: "Other C0 control characters are suppressed unless this resource is enabled". The same manual's `disallowedPasteControls` turns each control it lists into a space. By default it lists `BS,DEL,ENQ,EOT,ESC,NUL,STTY`. ADR-1000's row for this rule is input.paste-end-marker.
  - vtium differs from xterm's default in three ways. First, vtium filters every C0 control apart from HT, LF and CR, where xterm filters the C0 controls in its list. Second, vtium filters the C1 controls in UTF-8 input too, where xterm filters them only outside UTF-8. Third, vtium makes a C1 control a space, where xterm outside UTF-8 removes it (`button.c`, `removeControls` skips it).
  - Tests: `a_bracketed_paste_never_carries_its_own_end`, `a_paste_keeps_only_the_formatting_controls`, `a_character_whose_bytes_look_like_c1_arrives_whole`, `ill_formed_utf8_in_a_paste_becomes_the_replacement_character`, `a_paste_is_sent_in_the_inputs_encoding`, `an_ordinary_paste_arrives_unchanged`.
- **A paste sends each LF as CR,** so a pasted line end reaches a program in raw mode as the Return key, not Ctrl-J. Spec: xterm's `_WriteSelectionData` (`button.c`). It turns each LF of a paste into CR unless `pasteLiteralNl` is set, and by default it is not set. Tests: `a_paste_sends_each_line_end_as_cr`, `an_ordinary_paste_arrives_unchanged`, `a_paste_keeps_only_the_formatting_controls`.
- **A paste is sent in the input's encoding.** vtium reads a paste as UTF-8 and filters it by code point. vtium then sends each character as a typed one is sent. In UTF-8 input the character goes as UTF-8. In 8-bit input it goes as its byte up to U+00FF, and in 7-bit input as its byte up to U+007F. Past that it goes as `?`. So in 8-bit input, a pasted "€" reaches the program as `?`. It does not arrive as three Latin-1 characters with a C1 control among them.
  - Spec: outside UTF-8, xterm turns a UTF-8 selection into Latin-1 before it pastes it (`button.c`, `UTF8toLatin1`). Each character past U+00FF becomes an ASCII equivalent, or xterm's `defaultString`, which is `#` by default. vtium sends `?`, as it does for a typed character that the input cannot carry. In 7-bit input, vtium sends `?` for U+0080 to U+00FF too, where `UTF8toLatin1` keeps their bytes.
  - A program can set text with OSC 52, and the host pastes that text from the same buffer. That text is UTF-8 in every input too. It holds U+FFFD for any ill-formed bytes from the moment it is set. See OSC 52, below.
  - Tests: `a_paste_is_sent_in_the_inputs_encoding`, and `osc_52_text_is_pasted_in_the_inputs_encoding` in `tests/clipboard.rs`.
- **A key's character in 7-bit input is `?` past 0x7F.** No specification covers this case. The reason for the rule is that a 7-bit program cannot read the character. Test: `decscl_resets_and_sets_the_input`.

### The kitty keyboard protocol

The kitty keyboard protocol lets a program ask the terminal to send every key in a form that cannot be mistaken for another key (ADR-1005). A program asks for it by pushing a set of flags. Each flag adds to what the terminal reports. The flags in force are 0 until a program pushes or sets them. While the flags are 0, every key sends what the Input section above describes. Spec for every bullet: kitty's "Comprehensive keyboard handling in terminals", <https://sw.kovidgoyal.net/kitty/keyboard-protocol/>, read 2026-11-07.

- **vtium keeps one stack of flags for the main screen and one for the alternate screen.**
  - `CSI > flags u` saves the flags in force and puts `flags` in force. A left-out number means 0.
  - `CSI < n u` pops n sets, and a left-out number means 1. The newest saved set that remains comes back into force.
  - `CSI = flags ; mode u` changes the flags in force and saves nothing. Mode 1, or no mode, sets exactly `flags`. Mode 2 sets its bits, and mode 3 clears its bits. Any other mode changes nothing.
  - `CSI ? u` is answered `CSI ? flags u`, with the flags in force on the screen shown.
  - A stack holds `KEYBOARD_FLAGS_MAX` sets: the set in force and seven saved sets. A push onto a full stack drops the oldest saved set.
  - A pop of more sets than are saved empties the stack and puts 0 in force. `CSI < 0 u` pops nothing.
  - A switch between the screens by mode 47, 1047 or 1049 leaves each stack as it is. RIS and DECSTR empty both stacks.
  - Spec: "Progressive enhancement"; ADR-1005, D3 to D5. Tests: `a_ninth_push_drops_the_oldest_level`, `each_screen_keeps_its_own_flags_across_a_switch`, `ris_and_decstr_clear_both_stacks`, and the two stack tests in `src/terminal/kitty_keyboard.rs`.
- **vtium keeps the five flags that the specification documents.** They are 1, disambiguate escape codes; 2, report event types; 4, report alternate keys; 8, report all keys as escape codes; and 16, report associated text. A push or a set drops every other bit as it arrives. So a program that pushes 63 and then asks reads back 31. Test: `the_query_reports_only_the_flags_vtium_implements`.
- **While the flags in force include flag 1 or flag 8, the protocol's escape codes decide every key's bytes.** The specification says that flag 8 implies flag 1, so flag 8 alone acts as flags 1 and 8 together. Escape is `CSI 27 u`.
  - A key that makes text sends its text while nothing but Shift and the locks are held. With Alt, Ctrl, Logo, Hyper or Meta held, the key is `CSI code ; modifiers u`. The code is the key's unshifted character. The modifiers are 1 plus shift 1, alt 2, ctrl 4, super 8, hyper 16, meta 32, caps_lock 64 and num_lock 128. So Ctrl+I is `CSI 105 ; 5 u` and no longer Tab, and Ctrl+Shift+A is `CSI 97 ; 6 u`.
  - Every key that makes no text is written as the specification's table of functional keys gives it, whatever modifiers are held. For example, Up is `CSI A`, Ctrl+Up `CSI 1 ; 5 A`, F1 `CSI P`, F3 `CSI 13 ~`, Insert `CSI 2 ~`, and F13 `CSI 57376 u`.
  - Without flag 2, a repeat is sent as a press, and a release sends nothing.
  - vtium does not consult DECCKM, DECKPAM and mode 66, modifyOtherKeys, or modes 1034, 1035 and 1036. modifyOtherKeys keeps its level, which `CSI ? 4 m` still reports. modifyOtherKeys works again once the flags in force are 0 (ADR-1005, D6).
  - Spec: "Disambiguate escape codes", "Key codes", "Modifiers" and "Functional key definitions". Tests: `a_push_of_1_sends_escape_alt_and_ctrl_keys_as_codes`, `a_push_of_1_sends_the_functional_keys_in_the_specifications_forms`, `flags_at_0_send_xterms_bytes`, `modify_other_keys_waits_while_the_flags_are_not_0`.
- **Enter, Tab and Backspace keep their legacy bytes only when no modifier but the locks is held.** The specification says these three keys "still generate the same bytes as in legacy mode". The reason is that a user can then type `reset` after a program dies with the flags set. The specification does not say whether that holds with a modifier. vtium sends the legacy bytes for the key alone, because typing `reset` needs that. The legacy bytes are CR (CR LF under LNM), HT, and DEL or BS as the host's Backspace setting says. With Shift, Alt, Ctrl or another modifier held, vtium sends `CSI 13 u`, `CSI 9 u` and `CSI 127 u` with the modifiers. These are the numbers that the table gives these keys. So a program can tell Shift+Enter from Enter. Shift+Tab is `CSI 9 ; 2 u`, not `CSI Z`. Test: `a_push_of_1_keeps_enter_tab_and_backspace_unmodified`.
- **Flag 2 reports a key's repeats and its release.** The event type follows the modifiers after a colon: 2 for a repeat and 3 for a release. A press carries no event type. With no modifier held, the modifiers field is 1. So Escape's press, repeat and release are `CSI 27 u`, `CSI 27 ; 1:2 u` and `CSI 27 ; 1:3 u`. Up's are `CSI A`, `CSI 1 ; 1:2 A` and `CSI 1 ; 1:3 A`. Enter, Tab and Backspace keep their legacy bytes and send no release, so that a user can still type `reset`. Held with a modifier, their repeat is `CSI 13 ; 5:2 u` and the like. A key that makes text sends its text on a press and a repeat, and nothing on its release. The reason is that the specification reports events for such keys only under flag 8. Spec: "Report event types" and "Event types". Tests: `a_push_of_3_reports_repeats_and_releases`, `flag_2_sends_no_release_for_enter_tab_backspace_or_text`.
- **Whether a key gets a release follows from its press.** vtium decides it as the press goes out, under the flags in force then (ADR-1005, D7). A key gets a release when flag 2 was in force at its press and the press was one that reports events. With flag 1 or flag 8 that is a press sent as an escape code. Without them it is a press the legacy section gives an escape code with a modifiers field, or a key that makes text.
  - The release then always comes, as an escape code, whatever the flags are at the release. It has the code its press used and the modifiers held at the release. So under flags 3, Ctrl+A's press is `CSI 97 ; 5 u`, and with Ctrl let go first, A's release is `CSI 97 ; 1:3 u`.
  - A key pressed while flag 2 was not in force gets no release, even if a program sets flag 2 before the key is let go.
  - vtium-term keeps the keys held that are owed a release, at most `KEYS_HELD_MAX` of them, and forgets them at RIS and DECSTR. A release for which vtium-term holds no press sends nothing. The host reports a release only for a press it reported, so a release finds its press by the keysym the press reported.
  - Tests: `a_release_follows_from_its_press`.
- **Without flag 1 and flag 8, the specification's legacy section decides each key.** Each flag acts alone. The specification says that a key not covered by a flag the program set follows its legacy section, "Legacy key event encoding". So under flag 2 alone, or flag 4 alone, most keys send on a press what they send while the flags are 0. modifyOtherKeys is not consulted while the flags are not 0 (ADR-1005, D6).
  - The specification carries an event type only inside an escape code's modifiers field. vtium reads that as follows. Two groups of keys send a repeat and a release as escape codes, and a third group sends a release only.
  - The first group is the keys that the legacy section encodes: the cursor keys, Home, End, Insert, Delete, Page Up, Page Down, F1 to F12 and Menu. Their press is xterm's encoding, except Menu's, which is the legacy section's `CSI 29 ~`. Their repeat and release take the forms of the table of functional keys. The locks are left out, as the legacy encoding leaves them out. So Shift+F3's press is `CSI 1 ; 2 R`, and its release is `CSI 13 ; 2:3 ~`.
  - The second group is the keys that the legacy section does not describe. The section says such a key is "sent using the standard `CSI u` encoding". F13 to F35, Scroll Lock, Print, Pause and the media keys are such keys, with numbers of 57344 and above in the table. Their press is `CSI number u` too. So under flag 2 alone F13's press is `CSI 57376 u`, where it is xterm's `CSI 25 ~` while the flags are 0.
  - The third group is the keys that make text. They send their text on a press and a repeat, and their release as `CSI code u`, as the maintainer decided on 2026-11-13 (ADR-1005). So `a` is `a` on a press and `CSI 97 ; 1:3 u` on its release. The locks are left out of the release.
  - The legacy section says that "all keypad keys are reported as their equivalent non-keypad keys". So the keypad's End is End, and sends End's repeat and release, `CSI 1 ; 1:2 F` and `CSI 1 ; 1:3 F`. The keypad's 1 is the text key 1, whose release is `CSI 49 ; 1:3 u`. The keypad's Enter is Enter.
  - Escape, Enter, Tab, Backspace and a key held with Ctrl or Alt send their legacy bytes on a press and a repeat. They send nothing on a release.
  - Tests: `flag_2_alone_reports_events_for_the_legacy_escape_codes`, `flag_2_alone_reports_f13_and_the_keypad_keys_events`, `flag_2_alone_reports_the_release_of_a_text_key`.
- **Caps Lock and NumLock appear in every escape code, and never turn a text key into one.** The specification says that a lock that is on sets its bit. It also says that the lock modifiers are not reported for keys that make text. vtium reads that as follows. A key that would send text still sends text with a lock on. A key sent as an escape code carries the locks' bits. So Escape with Caps Lock on is `CSI 27 ; 65 u`, and Up is `CSI 1 ; 65 A`. Test: `a_push_of_1_sends_escape_alt_and_ctrl_keys_as_codes`.
- **DECCKM and the keypad modes change nothing while the flags are not 0.** The specification says that under flag 1 "all key events that do not generate text are represented in one of the following two forms". The forms are `CSI number ; modifier u`, and `CSI 1 ; modifier` with a letter or `~`. SS3 is neither form, so vtium never sends SS3 under flag 1. So F1, F2 and F4 are `CSI P`, `CSI Q` and `CSI S`. Test: `a_push_of_1_sends_the_functional_keys_in_the_specifications_forms`.
- **The keypad's keys are keys of their own.** Some keypad keys make text: a digit, an operator, the decimal point, the separator, =, and the space. Such a key sends its text while nothing but Shift and the locks are held. Under any other modifier, the key is `CSI number u` with its own number from the table. So Ctrl+KP_1 is `CSI 57400 ; 5 u`. Every other keypad key is always `CSI number u`, and that includes Enter on the keypad, `CSI 57414 u`.
  - The table lists KP_BEGIN as "1 E or 57427 ~". vtium sends `CSI 57427 u`, in the form that flag 1 gives every other keypad key. The reason is that `CSI 57427 ~` is neither of flag 1's forms, and `CSI E` is Begin on the main keys.
  - The table names none of PF1 to PF4, the keypad's Tab, and the keypad's space. So PF1 to PF4 are sent as F1 to F4, the keypad's Tab as Tab, and its space with the code 32.
  - Test: `a_push_of_1_sends_the_keypad_as_keys_of_its_own`.
- **Without flag 8, modifier keys send nothing, and so does a key the table does not name.** The specification reports the modifier keys only under flag 8. So under flag 1, these keys send nothing: Shift, Ctrl, Alt, Super, Hyper, Meta, AltGr, the level-five shift, Caps Lock and NumLock. Scroll Lock, Print, Pause, Menu, and the media keys that xkbcommon names are ordinary keys with numbers in the table. Some keys make no text and get no number from the table. Examples are Linefeed, Clear, Sys_Req, Find, Select, and Begin on the main keys. Such a key sends nothing while the flags are not 0. In the same way, a key with no xterm encoding sends nothing while the flags are 0 (ADR-1005, D6). Test: `a_push_of_1_sends_the_functional_keys_in_the_specifications_forms`.
- **The key's code is the host's unshifted key where the host reports one.** vtium-platform reports the unshifted key, the keysym at the first level of the key's layout (ADR-1005, D9). So Ctrl+Shift+2 on a US layout is `CSI 50 ; 6 u`, as the specification wants. vtium-term reads the unshifted key's character when the keysym is Latin-1 or a Unicode keysym. vtium-platform gives every other keysym that stands for a character as its Unicode keysym. Where the host reports no unshifted key, vtium-term takes the character the key made, and it lowers a letter's case. So Ctrl+Shift+A is 97 even then. A host reports no unshifted key where AltGr chose the key's symbol. So Alt with AltGr+Q on a German layout, which makes @, is `CSI 64 ; 3 u`. A key of a non-Latin layout is sent by its own character. So Ctrl+С on a Russian layout is `CSI 1089 ; 5 u`. Tests: `a_push_of_1_sends_escape_alt_and_ctrl_keys_as_codes`, and tier 1's `key-kitty-unshifted-key`.
- **Flag 4 reports the shifted key and the base layout key.** The specification calls them the alternate keys. vtium adds them only to a key that makes text and that is sent as `CSI code u`. The specification says this flag affects only a key "already going to be represented as an escape code". So under flag 4 alone, no key changes.
  - The shifted key follows the code after a colon, and only while Shift is held, as the specification requires. It is the host's shifted key, the keysym at the key's Shift level, which vtium-platform reports. Where the host reports none, it is the character the key made. So Ctrl+Shift+A is `CSI 97:65 ; 6 u`. With Caps Lock on, Shift makes `a`, and the shifted key is still `A`: Ctrl+Shift+A is `CSI 97:65 ; 70 u`.
  - The base layout key is the key's character on the US PC-101 layout, which vtium-platform reports from the kernel's key code. It follows the shifted key after a second colon. Where only the base layout key is sent, the shifted key's place is empty. So Ctrl+С on a Russian layout is `CSI 1089::99 ; 5 u`, and Ctrl+Shift+С is `CSI 1089:1057:99 ; 6 u`.
  - vtium leaves out a shifted key or a base layout key that equals the code. So Ctrl+A on a US layout stays `CSI 97 ; 5 u`. ADR-1005 D9 decides this for the base layout key. The specification says nothing about an equal shifted key, and Shift with a key that has no shifted form, such as the space, would only repeat the code.
  - The functional keys, Enter, Tab, Backspace and the keypad carry no alternate keys. The specification describes the alternate keys as forms of a Unicode key code, and these keys have numbers from the table instead.
  - Test: `a_push_of_5_reports_the_shifted_and_base_layout_keys`, and tier 1's `key-kitty-shifted-key` and `key-kitty-base-layout-key`.
- **Flag 8 reports every key as an escape code, and sends no text.** The specification says that "all keys are reported as escape codes, including Enter, Tab, Backspace etc.". So under flag 8:
  - A key that makes text is `CSI code ; modifiers u`, with no modifier field when none is held. So `a` is `CSI 97 u`. The locks appear among the modifiers, as the specification's disambiguate section points to flag 8 for them. So `a` with NumLock on is `CSI 97 ; 129 u`.
  - Enter, Tab and Backspace are `CSI 13 u`, `CSI 9 u` and `CSI 127 u`, with their repeats and releases under flag 2. A program that dies with flag 8 set leaves the shell without a plain Enter. vtium's reset key, Ctrl+Shift+Delete by default, resets the shown console as RIS does (ADR-1005, D10).
  - A modifier key reports its press, and under flag 2 its release, with its own number from the table. Its own bit is set as the event leaves it, as "Modifiers" requires: the host reports Shift set on Shift's press and clear on its release. So Shift's press and release are `CSI 57441 ; 2 u` and `CSI 57441 ; 1:3 u`. Shift_Lock has no number in the table and sends nothing.
  - The keypad's text keys are `CSI number u` with their own numbers. So the keypad's 1 is `CSI 57400 ; 129 u` with NumLock on.
  - Tests: `a_push_of_9_reports_every_key_as_an_escape_code`, `a_push_of_11_reports_the_release_of_every_key`, and tier 1's `key-kitty-all-keys` and `key-kitty-all-keys-events`.
- **Flag 16 puts the text a key makes inside its escape code, under flag 8.** The text is the third field, as code points, after the modifiers field. The modifiers field is empty when no modifier is held. So `a` is `CSI 97 ;; 97 u`, and Shift+A is `CSI 97 ; 2 ; 65 u`, the specification's own example.
  - A key makes text only while nothing but Shift and the locks is held. A key held with Ctrl, Alt, Super, Hyper or Meta carries no text. On the console the host reports no text that Alt made, as a desktop's input method can. The specification says the text "must not contain control codes", and Ctrl+A's text would be the control code 0x01.
  - A press and a repeat carry the text, and a release carries none, since letting go of a key makes no text.
  - The specification calls flag 16 "undefined if used without" flag 8. vtium adds no text without flag 8. So under flags 1 and 16, a text key still sends its text, and Alt+A is still `CSI 97 ; 3 u`.
  - Test: `a_push_of_25_reports_the_text_inside_the_code`, and tier 1's `key-kitty-associated-text`.
- **These rulings follow the specification's text alone.** ADR-1005 settles an unclear case by running kitty as a program. But in the runs that the record made, keys sent to kitty under Xvfb did not reach kitty's window. So no ruling above rests on kitty's behaviour. kitty's source code is GPL-licensed, and vtium never reads it.
- **The protocol is in every build, minspec included.** It costs each terminal two stacks of eight bytes. The minspec binary for x86_64 is built with `make minspec`. It measures 3,689,288 bytes before the first slice and 3,694,360 bytes after it. That is 5,072 bytes more, inside the 4 MiB budget (ADR-1005, D11).

### OSC strings and the Linux console's sequences

- **OSC 52 sets the selection, and its query gets no reply.** A set reaches the host through `Terminal::take_clipboard_write`, decoded. The query is refused (ADR-1000, osc.52.read). These are the maintainer's decisions in vtium/vtium#39. A set past `CLIPBOARD_MAX` is dropped whole, whether in its decoded bytes or in its text once replaced. A set that the parser truncated is dropped whole too. Spec: ctlseqs, OSC 52, with three differences:
  - Data that is not base64 is dropped, where ctlseqs clears the selection. RFC 4648, 3.3 has a decoder "reject the encoded data". The decoding is strict, apart from the padding, which may be left off, as Ghostty takes it.
  - `c`, `p`, `s` and the empty list, ctlseqs's `s 0`, set vtium's one selection. `q` and the cut buffers `0` to `7` name none of vtium's selections, so they set nothing. xterm sets each one it has.
  - The decoded bytes are UTF-8 text in every input. When the selection is set, each ill-formed sequence in them is replaced by U+FFFD. Each maximal subpart gets one U+FFFD (Unicode 16.0, 3.9). So the selection holds valid text from then on. A paste sends that text in the input's encoding (Input, above). This is the maintainer's decision in vtium/vtium#101. So in 8-bit input, the Latin-1 byte E9 that a program sets is pasted as `?`. The UTF-8 é, C3 A9, is pasted as E9.
  - Tests: `tests/clipboard.rs`, and `a_truncated_osc_52_sets_nothing` and `the_decoder_returns_every_byte` in `src/terminal/clipboard.rs`.
- **OSC 50 asks for a font set, and its query names the one in use.** A name reaches the host through `Terminal::take_font_request`. The query, `OSC 50 ; ?`, is answered `OSC 50 ; NAME`, ended as it was asked. NAME is the set that the host named with `set_font_name`, never the name asked for. Until the host turns them on with `set_font_ops`, both the request and the query are ignored (ADR-1200 D5). The input call returns after a set is asked for, so the host switches before a query that follows is answered. Spec: ctlseqs, OSC 50, and xterm's `allowFontOps`, with three differences:
  - Pt names one of vtium's built sets: at most 64 characters of `A-Z a-z 0-9 . _ + -`, not starting with `.` (ADR-1200 D1). xterm takes an X font name or a TrueType face. vtium ignores anything else, such as a path or an X font name.
  - A Pt starting with `#` is xterm's font menu: absolute, relative, and its query. vtium ignores such a Pt, because vtium has no menu.
  - The query is answered only while font operations are on, as the set is.
  - Tests: `tests/font.rs`.
- **OSC 8 links the cells printed while a link is open, and the link store holds each distinct link once.** Spec: Egmont Koblinger's "Hyperlinks in terminal emulators", with ADR-1002's bounds and one difference.
  - A URI is 1 to 2,083 bytes, and an id is 1 to 250. These are VTE's and iTerm2's limits.
  - Some URIs close the open link: a URI past its bound, one with a byte outside 0x21 to 0x7E, and one with no RFC 3986 scheme. An id past its bound is ignored.
  - The one difference: the specification allows the space, 0x20, which vtium refuses, since a URI percent-encodes a space. With every byte printable ASCII, the overlay that shows a URI shows what a copy carries.
  - A link with the same id and URI takes the store's entry again. Past `LINKS_BYTES_MAX`, the store sweeps (Bounds).
  - RIS and a switch of screens end the open link, as VTE ends it. Erased cells take no link.
  - Tests: `tests/links.rs`.
- **OSC 133 marks a prompt, its input and its output on their lines.** Spec: the semantic prompts proposal (FinalTerm's sequences).
  - A and N do a fresh line, and they mark the cursor's line a prompt. A fresh line is a carriage return and a line feed when the cursor is not in the first column. L does the fresh line alone. kitty and foot mark A where the cursor is, with no fresh line.
  - B and I mark the input, and C marks the output.
  - P, D and the options are taken and change nothing. D's exit status is not kept.
  - ED 2, ED 3 and RIS clear the marks of the lines they erase. EL and the partial erases keep the marks, since shells redraw a prompt with those erases.
  - A rewrap gives a rejoined line the marks of all its rows (ADR-1003).
  - Tests: `tests/marks.rs`.
- **DECSCA sets the protection DECSED, DECSEL and DECSERA leave.** Spec: ctlseqs, DECSCA. A value of 1 protects, and 0, 2 and the default do not. SGR 0 keeps the protection, and DECSTR and RIS end it, as in xterm. Test: `decsca_protects_printed_cells_from_decsed_and_decsel`.
- **The Linux console's blank sequences reach the host.** `CSI 9 ; n ]` hands the host a blank timeout of n minutes, and a wake. The timeout is at most 60, as the Linux console caps it. A value of 0, or none, turns blanking off. The wake follows the Linux console, which wakes the screen as it sets the timeout. `CSI 13 ]` hands the host a wake. RIS hands the host its own timeout again (`Terminal::take_blank_request`). `CSI 14 ; n ]`, the VESA powerdown interval, is ignored, since vtium has one DPMS step. This is the maintainer's decision on vtium/vtium#79. Spec: console_codes(4). Tests: `tests/blank.rs`.

### Resets

- **DECSTR resets the mouse modes and mode 1004, as RIS does.** xterm resets them on RIS only. This is the maintainer's decision, recorded in each row's spec column. Test: `decstr_returns_the_modes_to_their_defaults`.
- **DECSTR keeps the tab stops, and RIS sets a stop every eighth column.** Spec: the VT510 manual, DECSTR. Its table of what a soft reset changes leaves out the tab stops. ctlseqs names DECSTR without saying what it resets. Test: `decstr_keeps_the_tab_stops_and_ris_resets_them`.
- **DECSTR sets autowrap (DECAWM), where the VT510 manual's DECSTR table gives "No autowrap".** The reason is that xterm's terminfo entries send DECSTR in `is2` and `rs2`. They also declare `am`, the capability that says the terminal wraps. If vtium followed the table, `tput init` or `tput reset` under such an entry would leave the terminal without autowrap. Yet the terminal's terminfo entry says it wraps. Test: `decstr_leaves_autowrap_on`.
- **RIS clears what XTSAVE saved and returns XTSHIFTESCAPE to 0, and DECSTR keeps both.** xterm keeps both through RIS and DECSTR (`ReallyReset` in xterm's `charproc.c` touches neither). vtium splits them as Ghostty does. This is the maintainer's ruling, recorded in ADR-1000. The reason is that RIS returns the terminal to its initial state, which holds no saved modes. Tests: `decstr_keeps_what_xtsave_saved_and_ris_clears_it`, `xtshiftescape_says_whether_shift_reaches_the_mouse_reports`.
- **`Terminal::reset`, the host's reset, leaves the terminal as RIS does, and drops what the program left in flight.** vtium's reset key calls `Terminal::reset` (ADR-1005 D10). The reset also does these things:
  - It drops the replies queued for the host, since the program that asked for them is being reset.
  - It abandons a request out to the image helper, as `Terminal::abandon_image_request` does. So the terminal takes output again at once.
  - It returns the parser to its ground state. So a sequence that the program had half written is dropped, as the ESC of RIS would drop it.
  - Tests: `a_host_reset_leaves_the_terminal_as_ris_does`, `a_host_reset_abandons_the_request_out`.

### The scrollback

- **The scrollback holds at most `sb-memory` bytes as well as `sb-size` lines,** and it drops its oldest lines past either bound. Each line costs its struct and its heap, waiting or rewrapped. A line that alone passes the budget does not enter. No specification covers a scrollback's size. The reason for a byte budget is that the program sizes the lines, so a count of lines bounds no memory.
  - **The worst row.** A row whose every cell has its own style, link and cluster, concentrated by a rewrap, costs up to 7,560 bytes at 170 columns. In a build with images it costs up to 10,450 bytes. There a kitty placeholder cluster of 17 bytes may fill each cell that its budget leaves. 1,000 of these rows pass the 16 MiB minspec peak. The minspec build is vtium's default, smallest build.
  - **The default budget.** When the configuration does not set `sb-memory`, the budget follows the grid. The budget is what the 16 MiB minspec peak leaves after the rest of the process (`MINSPEC_REST`), the two screens, and a full link store. The two screens count with every row at its worst (`screens_bytes_max`, each row at `Line::bytes_max`). The budget is rounded down to 64 KiB (`sb_memory_default`). Each resize works the budget out again from the rows the screens hold. The resize then drops the oldest lines past the budget.
  - **A larger grid.** A grid larger than the 170×48 minspec grid keeps that grid's value, and never less. That value is 5,242,880 bytes, or 4,653,056 in a build with images. The console of a larger grid uses more memory than the minspec peak by design. The default does not grow with the display or the machine. So a user on a capable machine who wants a deeper scrollback raises `sb-memory`.
  - **What the default holds.** At the minspec grid, the default holds about 693 of the worst rows, or 445 with images. 1,000 full-width shell lines take about 29% of it, or 33% with images. At 80×30 and smaller, the default holds 1,000 of the worst rows.
  - **A set budget.** `sb-memory` sets a fixed budget. The budget is fenced from `SB_MEMORY_MIN` to the lesser of `SB_MEMORY_MAX` and a quarter of the machine's memory (`sb_memory`). `sb-size` can only lower what the budget allows.
  - Tests: `the_scrollback_drops_its_oldest_lines_past_its_byte_budget`, `the_byte_budget_drops_hostile_lines_and_keeps_a_thousand_plain_ones` (`tests/flood.rs`), `the_byte_budget_holds_through_resizes_and_its_count_is_exact` (`tests/reflow.rs`) and `the_worst_default_scrollback_fits_the_minspec_peak` and `the_scrollback_budget_follows_the_grid` (`tests/memory.rs`).

## Notes for the next issues

- **The replay view (vtium/vtium#14)** reads the screen's state.
  - It reads the rows of `Screen::lines()`, or the view while scrolled back.
  - It reads `Screen::cursor()`, whose column is the screen's width while a wrap is pending.
  - It reads the flags from `insert_mode`, `auto_wrap`, `origin_mode`, `reverse_video`, `cursor_visible` and `alternate`.
  - It reads the change counts from the frame's cells, and `Terminal::cursor_style()` for the frame view's cursor style.
  - It reads the replies from `Terminal::take_replies()`. These replies also carry what `key`, `mouse`, `focus` and `paste` send.
  - The replay host is `set_sync_enabled(true)`, `set_version("tsm-replay")`, `set_cell_size(8, 16)` and `set_cursor_blink(true)`. The `termcap` command builds a `Termcap` from the fixture.
  - The screen view prints a space with no underline as an empty cell, so a row's trailing spaces trim.
- **The host (vtium/vtium#19, #21)** drives the terminal in these ways.
  - It reports keys as X11 keysyms with `Terminal::key`, the pointer with `mouse`, focus with `focus`, and pastes with `paste`. It reads `auto_repeat` (DECARM), `mouse_tracking` and `synchronized`.
  - It sets the XTGETTCAP table from vtium's terminfo entry (`Termcap::new`). It also sets XTVERSION's text, the cell and display sizes, the palette it configures, the cursor blink, and the width method that RIS returns to. It sets whether it presents by mode 2026, and it ends a hold with `end_sync` when its timer runs out.
  - It resizes with `Terminal::resize`, after the cell size and the pty's size. So mode 2048 reports a resize once the resize is complete.
  - It tells `set_visible` when the VT is switched away or back, and when the display blanks or unblanks. Mode 2033 reports these changes.
  - It takes the selection that a program sets with OSC 52 from `Terminal::take_clipboard_write` into its selection buffer. It drops the selection instead when the configuration turns copy-out off, an option that vtium/vtium#21 adds.
  - It takes what a program asked of the screen blank from `Terminal::take_blank_request`. It acts on that request while its configuration lets a program do so (vtium/vtium#79).
  - It turns XTWINOPS 8 on with `set_window_ops` under `allow-window-ops`. It takes a text area asked for from `Terminal::take_grid_request`. It feeds no more output while `grid_requested` holds.
  - It turns OSC 50 on with `set_font_ops` under `allow-font-ops`, and it names the set in use with `set_font_name`. It takes a set asked for from `Terminal::take_font_request`. It feeds no more output while `font_requested` holds (vtium/vtium#126).
  - It reports a key's press, repeat and release in `Key::event`. It reports `Mods::NUM` among a key's modifiers while NumLock is on. Mode 1035 and the kitty keyboard protocol read that modifier.
  - When it does not report a wheel step, it offers the step to `Terminal::alternate_scroll` before it scrolls its view. While mode 1007 is set, that call sends cursor keys on the alternate screen.
  - It takes a BEL from `Terminal::take_bell`, and it rings the VT's bell when its bell setting is on.
  - While `Terminal::mouse_captures_shift` holds (XTSHIFTESCAPE 1), it lets a pointer event with Shift held reach the program. It does not take that event for its own selection.
- **Links and prompt marks (vtium/vtium#40, ADR-1002)** serve the renderer and the host.
  - For the renderer, a frame's `hovered()` is the link under the pointer. Each of that link's visible cells is `FrameCell::hovered`. Such a cell draws a dashed underline in its underline color, in place of its own style. `overlay()` is the row and the URI to draw inverted over the row, cut at the row's end with `…`.
  - For the host, `Screen::set_pointer` takes the view cell under the pointer and whether Ctrl is held. The host calls it at each move, and at each press or release of Ctrl. `link_at` and `link_uri` give a Ctrl+click's URI, to copy into the selection buffer. `jump_to_prompt` moves the view for Ctrl+Shift+Up and Down on the main screen. `select_output` selects a command's output for Ctrl+right-click, and the host copies that output with `selection_text`.
  - The replay interface shows neither links nor marks. So the probes `osc-8-hyperlink` and `osc-133-marks` need commands of their own.
- **The pointer's arrow (vtium/vtium#92)** draws in fixed colors. Drawing the arrow in `Terminal::palette().pointer()` is recorded on vtium/vtium#98. Those colors are the ones that OSC 13 and 14 set, or the default foreground and background while they are unset.
- **Tier 1:** `make conformance` (`tools/conformance/`, vtium/vtium#14) judges vtium-term against the conformance goldens.

## Bounds

The byte stream comes from an untrusted program, so nothing it sends grows memory without a bound. These are the bounds:

- **The screen.** A screen is at most `COLUMNS_MAX` by `ROWS_MAX`, and its size comes from the display and the font. Every path that writes, moves or erases cells gives back the cluster bytes it drops through the line. So a line's arena keeps its budget on each of those paths (`tests/flood.rs`). A rectangle operation is linear in the rectangle's cells, and DECCRA holds one source line at a time. REP prints at most `PARAM_VALUE_CAP` copies (vtium-parse), as printed text does.
- **Replies.** The replies and input reports waiting for the host take at most `REPLIES_PENDING_MAX` bytes, plus the replies of the byte that reaches the bound. The input call then returns, so the host can take them. At the bound, the input call takes nothing.
  - One string at vtium-parse's `OSC_MAX` can hold a quarter of a million OSC 4 queries. So an OSC 4 list skips its queries past the bound, and it still applies its sets. What a string sets therefore does not depend on when the host took the replies. OSC 21's one reply skips the queries past the bound the same way.
  - A color specification is read at most four hex digits a channel, into a color of 3 bytes.
  - XTVERSION's text is at most `VERSION_MAX` bytes.
  - XTGETTCAP's table holds at most `TERMCAP_ENTRIES_MAX` names. Each name is at most `TERMCAP_NAME_MAX` bytes, and each value at most `TERMCAP_VALUE_MAX`. One request is answered for `XTGETTCAP_NAMES` names, in a reply of at most `XTGETTCAP_REPLY_MAX` bytes.
  - DECRQSS keeps the request's first two bytes, which is as long as any request it answers. A longer request gets the error form.
- **Style and link tables.** A line's style and link tables hold at most twice its cells before they compact. So a program that writes a new style or link into every cell, over and over, never grows them past that.
- **Clusters.** A grapheme cluster keeps at most `CLUSTER_MAX` codepoints. The clusters that a line's cells name take at most `CLUSTER_BYTES_PER_CELL` bytes a cell, of the width the line was written at. The arena compacts at twice that. A line trimmed for the scrollback keeps what its cells name.
- **Placeholder clusters.** In a build with images, a cluster that starts with U+10EEEE sits outside that budget. U+10EEEE is the kitty graphics protocol's Unicode placeholder. Such a cluster keeps the placeholder and its first `PLACEHOLDER_MARKS` codepoints, at most 17 bytes, wherever it starts in its line. The line counts the cells that name such a cluster. It compacts its arena at twice its budget, plus 17 bytes for each of those cells (ADR-1001, "Placeholder cells"). So a row that `write` fills holds at most `Line::bytes_max`, 128 + 90 bytes a cell. That is 15,428 bytes at 170 columns. A build without images holds 128 + 56, or 9,648 bytes.
- **Rewraps.** A rewrap moves each cluster with its cell, so a rewrapped row may name more than its own cells' budget. A logical line of `n` rows names at most the budget of `2n + 1` rows of the widest screen. The scrollback and the screen together name at most twice the widest screen's budget a row. They name, in addition, what a last row's few cells hold past 6 bytes each, with `CLUSTER_MAX` bounding a cell. That comes to 3,012 bytes a row at 170 columns, 2.95 times the 1,020 that ADR-1001 took. With images, placeholder clusters add up to 17 bytes for each cell of the widest screen, 5,902 bytes a row in all.
- **The worst row.** ADR-1001's worst case grows to 7,560 bytes a row, or 10,450 with images. That is 7.21 MiB at 1,000 rows and 720.98 MiB at 100,000 rows. With images, it is 9.97 MiB and 996.59 MiB. `tests/flood.rs` derives each figure and holds the scrollback to it. Its shapes reach about 1.4 times the budget a row on average.
- **Damage and the scrollback.** Damage tracks at most `COLUMNS_MAX` by `ROWS_MAX` positions. The scrollback keeps at most `sb-size` rows at the current width. A narrowing past that count drops the oldest rows for good. By default the count is capped by the machine's memory, or by a configured cap, and it is at most `SB_SIZE_MAX`. The screen is made with the cap that `sb_size_cap` gives, and it clamps `sb-size` to that cap. The scrollback keeps at most `sb-memory` bytes too. The configuration can set `sb-memory` inside its fence. When the configuration does not set it, the budget follows the grid, and the budget is never less than `SB_MEMORY_DEFAULT`. See The scrollback, above. A rewrap that the view or a drop reaches may make rows cost more than they did waiting. Such a rewrap drops the oldest lines past the budget, as a new line does.
- **OSC 52.** The text an OSC 52 sets waits for the host, at most `CLIPBOARD_MAX` bytes and one set at a time. vtium checks a set against that bound twice. It checks the set's decoded length before anything is decoded or allocated. It checks the length of the set's text before the text is built, counting each ill-formed sequence as the three bytes of U+FFFD.
- **The stacks.** XTPUSHSGR keeps at most `SGR_STACK_MAX` pens, and it counts the pushes past that bound. The color stack keeps at most `COLOR_STACK_MAX` palettes. `tests/stacks.rs` floods both.
- **The link store.** The link store holds each distinct OSC 8 link once. It charges each link its id and URI bytes and 64, at most `LINKS_BYTES_MAX` in all. Its heap stays at or under its charge (`the_stores_heap_stays_at_or_under_its_charge`).
  - An open that would pass the bound sweeps the store. The sweep reads once every line's link table, on both screens and in the scrollback, and the hovered link. It frees the links that no line names.
  - With less than a quarter of the bound then free, the scrollback's oldest lines lose their links until a quarter is free. When the screens alone hold the bound, the open is refused, and the text prints unlinked.
  - A sweep is linear in the lines held. It runs at most once per quarter of the bound of links opened, added or refused. So an open that comes sooner and does not fit is refused unswept (`refused_opens_sweep_once_per_quarter_of_the_bound`).
  - A sweep runs only on a byte from the stream. So the store follows the bytes, whether they are fed whole or in pieces (`tests/links.rs`).
- **The worst default scrollback.** Take the store full, the two screens at their worst, and every scrollback row at 7,560 bytes. The worst default scrollback at the minspec grid then comes to 16,776,192 bytes (16.00 MiB), against the 16 MiB minspec peak. The byte budget keeps 693 of the 1,000 lines. With images, at 10,450 bytes a row, the worst case comes to 16,741,248 bytes (15.97 MiB), and the budget keeps 445 (`the_worst_default_scrollback_fits_the_minspec_peak`).

## Reflow cost

A resize that changes the width rewraps some rows at once. It rewraps the screen, the main screen's rows below it, and the newest scrollback rows that could reach the screen. It also rewraps every row from the view's top or the selection's start down to the screen. A logical line with no wide character breaks at multiples of the width. Of such a line, the resize takes only the newest rows it needs, and the line's head waits. The resize counts the rest of the scrollback at the new width from each line's length. It can, because each line keeps a mark that it may hold a wide character. It lays out cell by cell only a long line with such a mark.

The rest waits. It rewraps when one of these reaches it: the view, a drop past `sb-size`, a taller screen, the newest line's wrap, or `Screen::rewrap_scrollback`. A drop cuts a long line with no wide character by length, unrewrapped. The result equals rewrapping everything at each resize. `reflow_is_the_same_chunked_and_lazy_or_eager` holds this over random streams, resizes, view moves and selections.

In a build with images, a waiting logical line that an image placement is anchored on never waits at an older width. Each resize rewraps such a line at once and carries its anchors. A placement that a drop moves onto a waiting line has that line rewrapped first. A rewrap keeps an anchor past a row's text inside the row. So carrying the anchor through each width in turn can land it in another column than carrying it once. This way, the anchor lands where rewrapping at each resize puts it. The rewrapping that a resize does for anchors costs the anchored lines, not the scrollback's depth. But the resize still walks every waiting row, to count the rows it takes at the new width. `tests/images_reflow.rs` holds this against an eager terminal, and against one that takes the whole scrollback at each resize.

Past `sb-memory`, the lines dropped follow what the rows cost as held, waiting or rewrapped. So there a lazy rewrap and an eager one may keep a different number of the oldest lines. The byte count stays exact and inside the budget either way (`the_byte_budget_holds_through_resizes_and_its_count_is_exact`). A resize to one column, where a wide character becomes an empty cell, rewraps the whole scrollback at once. A waiting row that never rewraps is cut at each narrowing. So a row that waited loses what a row rewrapped at each resize loses. A rewrap consumes each row as it builds the new ones, so it holds a row or two beyond its result.

`reflow_cost` measured the figures below (`cargo test --release -p vtium-term --test reflow reflow_cost -- --ignored --nocapture`). It ran on the development machine, a Ryzen 9 9950X3D, on 2026-10-28. It used 100,000 scrollback rows (10,000 for a style a cell) behind a screen of 170×48. It resized to 169 columns, back to 170, and to 100:

| Scrollback | The resize | The rest of the scrollback | In all, a cell | Heap before and after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Full rows wrapped in pairs | 2.0-3.1 ms | 51-56 ms | 3.1-5.2 ns | 136 MB, 68 MB |
| One logical line spanning it | 1.8-2.5 ms | 41-69 ms | 2.6-4.2 ns | 136 MB, 80 MB |
| A shell's lines of 40 cells | 0.6-1.1 ms | 2.5 ms | 0.2 ns | 32 MB, 32 MB |
| Wrapped pairs, the view at the top | 55-85 ms | none | 3.4-5.0 ns | 136 MB, 68 MB |
| A style a cell, 10,000 rows | 0.2-0.7 ms | 4.7-6.0 ms | 2.9-5.2 ns | 14.5 MB, 7.8 MB |

The resize's own time is mostly the count, except with the view scrolled back. Then every row below the view rewraps at once. Keeping that to the rows the view shows needs a second stretch of waiting rows. The operator decides on that (ADR-1003's note on Cost). The rest is paid only when something reaches it. The minspec board's figures are the gate that vtium/vtium#41 names, and they are not measured yet. No board is at hand. Tier 3's VM runs on a host core, so it measures memory, not a board's speed. ADR-1003 takes the board as ten times slower.

## Selection

The screen holds the selection (`src/screen/selection.rs`). A selection runs from one cell to another (`select_start`, `select_to`). It may also be a word, which is the run of cells between spaces or empty cells (`select_word`). Or it may be a whole line (`select_line`). The screen holds the selection by line identity as the screen scrolls. `selection_text` copies the selection, at most the text of the scrollback and the screen. The host drives the selection from the pointer and keeps the selection buffer. vtium's README describes how.

- **A line selection takes the whole line, and its copy ends with a line end.** Spec: xterm's `line` unit, with `cutNewline`, on by default, which ends the copy with a line end. xterm leaves the line end off on the screen's last row, and vtium does not. Test: `a_line_selection_copies_the_row_and_its_end`.
- **A wrapped line is one line to the selection.** A word goes on across the wrap, and a line selection takes each row of the line. The copy has no line end where the line wrapped. Spec: xterm, which tests `LINEWRAPPED` to extend a word or line selection across rows (`button.c`, `ComputeSelect`). xterm also tests the flag to leave out the line end (`SaveText`'s `eol`). The alternate screen's first row never continues the scrollback, which holds the main screen's lines. Test: `a_selection_follows_a_wrapped_line`.
- **A copied wide character brings no space.** No specification covers copying. The reason for the rule is that the space for the character's second column is not text the program wrote. Test: `a_copied_wide_character_brings_no_space`.
- **A wide character is one character of a word,** so a double click on the `a` of `a中b` takes `a中b`. No specification covers word selection. The reason for the rule is that the second column is part of the character. Test: `a_word_selection_takes_a_wide_character_whole`.
- **An empty cell is a space to a word selection.** An empty cell is one that no character was written to, such as a cell past the end of a row's text or a cell the cursor moved over. A double click on an empty cell selects nothing, as a double click on a space does. An empty cell ends a word on its left as well as on its right. A wide character that wrapped whole leaves empty cells at the end of its row. The selection passes over a wrapped row's empty cells at its end, so the word goes on at the next row. Spec: xterm(1), CHARACTER CLASSES, whose default table puts NUL, the empty cell, in the class of the space. Test: `a_word_selection_on_an_empty_cell_selects_nothing`.

A line records that printing wrapped it at the right margin (`LineFlags::WRAPPED`). The selection and reflow (ADR-1003) read that record. An edit that clears the row's last column ends the wrap, as xterm's does. These edits are EL 0, ECH, DCH, and ED on the cursor's row from the cursor (`ClearRight`, `ScrnDeleteChar`). ED also ends the wrap on each row it clears whole (`ClearBufRows`). EL 2 ends the wrap too, as Ghostty's `eraseLine` does, where xterm keeps it. DECSED ends the wrap on each row it clears whole. A row where DECSED leaves a protected cell keeps its wrap. xterm keeps every row's wrap under DECSED once DECSCA has protected a cell, and otherwise ends it as ED does (`do_erase_display`). EL 1, ICH, and the cursor's row under ED 1 keep the wrap. A line feed's scroll, and SU, move lines with their flags, into the scrollback too, and they bring in unflagged lines. Tests: `an_edit_that_clears_the_last_column_ends_the_wrap`, `a_wrap_ends_where_its_next_row_is_gone`.

Four rules depart from xterm, which joins a line to text that merely took the place of its next row:

- IL, DL and SD (and RI at the top margin) end the wrap of each row they shift, and of the row above them. Ghostty's `insertLines` and `deleteLines` do the same in a region as wide as the screen. A scroll ends the wrap of the row above the region. xterm moves the flags, so DL at a wrapped line's second row joins its first row to an unrelated one.
- Some edits end the wrap of the scrollback's newest line, whose next row was the main screen's first row. One is an erase that clears that row whole: ED 2, ED 1 below it, RIS, or any erase of that whole row. IL, DL, SD or RI that replace that row do the same. xterm keeps the wrap, so a line wrapped before a clear joins the prompt printed after it. The alternate screen's rows are no line's next row.
- A scroll region may start below the first row. A line that such a region pushes into the scrollback enters it unwrapped, since the line's next row stays in the region. xterm keeps the scrollback only for a region at the screen's top.
- DECERA, DECSERA, DECFRA and DECCRA end the wrap of each row whose last column they reach, as EL 0 does. One that clears or writes the main screen's first row whole ends the wrap of the scrollback's newest line. xterm's rectangle operations never touch the wrap (`ScrnFillRectangle`, `ScrnWipeRectangle`, `ScrnCopyRectangle`).
