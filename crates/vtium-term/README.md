# vtium-term

vtium's terminal state: the screen, its cells and lines, the scrollback, the modes, SGR, the character sets and the replies, applied from the actions `vtium-parse` produces; what keys, the pointer, focus and pastes send the program; and the frames a renderer reads. It forbids unsafe code. Its one dependency outside the workspace is servo's `unicode-bidi` crate (MIT or Apache-2.0), taken from vtium's own fork of it, https://github.com/vtium/unicode-bidi, pinned by revision, and present only in a build with the `bidi` feature: the maintainer chose to compute the order of right-to-left text with a crate that more people have used and checked than will use code vtium writes itself (ADR-1004 D13), and [Right-to-left text](#right-to-left-text) lists the guards vtium-term holds it to. The cell, line and frame model (vtium/vtium#10, ADR-1001), the screen, scrollback, selection and Unicode tables (vtium/vtium#11), the modes, SGR, the replies and the input encoding (vtium/vtium#12), the character sets (vtium/vtium#13), OSC 52's copy-out (vtium/vtium#39), the colors a program sets, queries and resets (vtium/vtium#38), OSC 8's links and OSC 133's prompt marks (vtium/vtium#40, ADR-1002), and OSC 50's font requests (vtium/vtium#126, ADR-1200) are in place.

## Behaviour

vtium-term follows the specifications each item below names: ECMA-48, the DEC manuals (the VT100 User Guide, the VT220 and VT510 manuals and DEC STD 070), xterm's ctlseqs and its manual, console_codes(4) for the Linux console's own sequences, and kitty's protocol documents. Where a specification leaves room, says nothing, or two of them disagree, this section states what vtium-term does and why, with the test of each. ADR-1000's tables say which functions vtium supports, ignores or refuses.

Text is cut into cells by UAX #29's extended grapheme clusters at the Unicode version the tables carry, or by codepoint width with mode 2027 reset. A codepoint takes no cell when its General_Category is Mn, Me, Cf or Cc (but U+00AD and Prepend characters), or it is a Hangul medial vowel or final consonant; two when its East_Asian_Width is W or F or it has Emoji_Presentation; one otherwise. A VS16 makes an emoji-style base two cells.

The rectangular areas, DECERA, DECSERA, DECFRA, DECCRA, DECCARA and DECRARA with DECSACE, follow xterm's ctlseqs and DEC STD 070. A rectangle's corners count from the top and left margins in origin mode and clip to the screen, or to the region inside all four margins, and DECCRA's page numbers name the one page. vtium differs from xterm in nine places. In origin mode an omitted corner is the margin's row or column, as DEC STD 070 has it; xterm 411 adds the top and left margins twice to an omitted corner (`minRectRow` already holds the margin, and `limitedParseRow` adds it again), so in origin mode its `CSI $ z` starts as far below the top margin as the top margin is below the screen's first row, and as far right of the left margin as the left margin is right of the first column. DECERA and DECSERA erase as ED does, to empty cells in the pen's background, where xterm wrote spaces in the pen's attributes for DECERA and kept each cell's attributes for DECSERA: the reason is that a rectangle erase and ED leave the same cell. DECCARA in the rectangle extent gives an empty cell the attributes and leaves it empty, where xterm wrote a space into it; the two draw the same, and a selection copies no space the program never wrote. DECCARA takes every SGR parameter as SGR applies it to the pen, kitty's extension (`deccara`), so its 0 also returns the colors to their defaults, where xterm's 0 resets bold, underline, blink and inverse; an omitted attribute parameter of DECCARA or DECRARA is 0, as an omitted SGR parameter is, where xterm ignores it. DECFRA refuses a character two cells wide, which xterm writes into each cell. A wide character a rectangle's edge cuts goes whole, as one an edit cuts does, which xterm also does for DECFRA, DECERA and DECSERA, clearing both halves; DECCARA and DECRARA change it whole, and DECCRA's source edge brings an empty cell in its style, where xterm changed or copied half of it. In the stream extent DECCARA and DECRARA skip every empty cell, those ECH, EL 1 or DECERA emptied in the middle of a line among them, where xterm marks an erased cell as drawn and changes it; vtium's cell keeps no drawn mark apart from its content. DECERA, DECSERA, DECFRA and DECCRA end wraps where xterm's keep them, as the wrap rules below list. DECRQSS reports DECSACE's stream as 0, whatever value other than 2 selected it, where xterm reports the value as set. Tests: `tests/rect.rs`, and `decrqss_reports_decsca_and_decsace`.

The modes and controls of vtium/vtium#54 follow xterm's ctlseqs and xterm's own code, and each row's spec column in `docs/modes.md` gives its rule. Mode 45 reverse-wraps BS and CUB only into a row that printing wrapped from, as xterm's `CursorBack` does, and never past the screen's first row. Mode 66, DECNKM, holds the state DECKPAM and DECKPNM set. Mode 1007 starts set, as foot and Ghostty start it, where xterm starts it reset. Mode 1015 is one of the three encodings, one set at a time as xterm keeps them. Modes 1034 and 1036 choose what Alt sends as xterm's `Input` does, both starting set, so Alt puts ESC before a key that sends a character or a control such as Tab; the editing, cursor, function and keypad keys never take that ESC (Input, below). Mode 1035 starts set, as xterm's `numLock` resource does, and changes only how NumLock reads, since vtium has one Alt modifier. Mode 1042 is xterm's bellIsUrgent, and vtium has no window to mark urgent, so while the mode is set a BEL reaches the host, and while it is reset vtium ignores BEL; it starts set, so BEL rings. XTSAVE and XTRESTORE keep one saved state for each group of modes that share a state, as xterm's `savemodes` does: tracking modes 1000 to 1003 share one and mode 9 has its own, the encodings share one, and the alternate screen modes share one. An empty list saves and restores every mode free of side effects, as kitty's misc-protocol has it and the maintainer ruled: every supported DEC private mode but DECOM, the alternate screen modes 47, 1047 and 1049, 1048 and 2026, and no ANSI mode, so its restore never moves the cursor or switches the screen. XTRESTORE of a mode nothing was saved for leaves the mode as it is, where xterm restores from a saved value that starts at zero. DECSTR keeps what XTSAVE saved and XTSHIFTESCAPE, as xterm and Ghostty do; RIS clears both (Resets, below). DECALN follows xterm's `CASE_DECALN`: origin mode off, the margins the whole screen, every line single size, every cell an E in the default style, the cursor home, and the pen keeping its colors and DECSCA's protection, as xterm's `resetRendition` keeps them, and losing its other attributes and its underline color, which xterm does not have and Ghostty drops. Tests: the mode rows' tests in `tests/modes.rs`, `xtrestore_restores_each_mode_xtsave_saved`, `xtsave_keeps_one_state_for_modes_that_share_one`, `the_empty_list_saves_and_restores_every_mode`, `the_empty_list_leaves_the_cursor_and_the_screen_alone`, `xtshiftescape_says_whether_shift_reaches_the_mouse_reports`, `decaln_fills_the_screen_with_e_and_resets_the_margins_and_cursor`, `decaln_returns_a_double_width_line_to_single_size`.

The `images` feature carries the image pipeline (ADR-1100); the default build, minspec, carries no image code: its input call, dispatch and screen events hold no image hook. With it, the screen holds one image store (`vtium-image`) for its main and alternate screens, tells it of every write, erase, rectangle operation, ICH and DCH, scroll, IL and DL, line the scrollback drops, ED 2 and ED 3, alternate screen switch, RIS and rewrap, with the `LineId` of each line as the store's key, and marks the cells under each box the store reports changed (ADR-1001). The frame carries the view's placements in draw order, each with its image's pixels shared. The terminal routes DCS `q`, APC strings starting with `G` and a complete OSC `1337;File=` to the handler installed for each, while its protocol is switched on; no handler is installed by default, and vtium-image's sixel handler and the kitty graphics handler (vtium/vtium#49) are installed by the host. Sixel is offered while it is switched on, its handler is installed and the budget is above 0: then DA1 reports attribute 4, the mode table's rows 80 (DECSDM), 1070 (private sixel color registers) and 8452 (sixel cursor to the right) report their state, and XTSMGRAPHICS (`CSI ? Pi ; Pa ; Pv S`) reads, sets and resets the color register count and reads the geometry, `max-graphic-size`. While sixel is not offered, the three rows report their fixed state and XTSMGRAPHICS answers status 1 for every item; a build without images has no row for them, so DECRQM answers 0, and answers XTSMGRAPHICS status 1 too (ADR-1100, D10). Tests: `tests/sixel.rs`. While a handler's request to the image helper is out, the input call takes nothing, so a later string cannot land before an earlier image, and an input call stops once its image work reaches `INPUT_WORK_MAX`, right after the byte that passed the bound: a handler takes only as much of a run of DCS data as the work left allows (vtium-parse's `Consumer::dcs_put_run`, vtium-image's `Handler::feed_some`), and the rest of the run comes in the next call. While a handler holds an upload in chunks open between its strings (vtium-image's `Handler::upload_open`), any other output the terminal takes, text, a control, or another escape sequence or string, makes the handler drop it (`Handler::interrupt`); the terminator that ends a string, `ESC \` or an 8-bit ST, is no other output. `Terminal::session_ended`, which the host calls when the program in the console exits, drops it too, and so does RIS. vtium-image's README lists this as a departure from kitty, which keeps the upload open. Tests: `tests/kitty.rs`. The image replies are written under one query, which the reply rule's test sends hostile text through with a stand-in kitty handler. Tests: `tests/images.rs`, and the store's own in `vtium-image`.

**Notes for the log.** With the `images` feature, a handler that drops or ignores what a program sent says why in a fixed phrase through vtium-image's `Context::note`, with a `Cause` that says who can act on it. `Cause::Program` marks a string that breaks its protocol, such as an iTerm2 picture without `inline=1`, a sixel picture with no size, or a kitty upload's data past the pixels `s` and `v` call for, which the kitty handler ignores and answers OK; only the program's author can act on these. `Cause::Console` marks a picture the program sent as its protocol asks that the console refused: a setting, one of vtium's bounds, the image budget, memory, or the image helper, missing or failing. Such a picture never draws, and the operator can act on why, so the kitty handler notes these refusals even when the program asked for no answer with `q=2` or no id. When the program that made a request has gone, the host drops the request with `Terminal::abandon_image_request`: the handler that asked is cancelled, and nothing is answered or noted, since the helper did not fail. The terminal counts each protocol, cause and reason, and keeps a note (`ImageNote`: the protocol, the cause, the reason and the count so far) the first time one comes and again each time its count reaches 10, 100, 1,000 and so on. The host takes the notes with `Terminal::take_image_notes` and writes them to its log; vtium writes a program's note at debug level and a console's at info level, which it shows by default, so an operator whose pictures never draw finds why in the default log. A program that sends the same bad picture every frame therefore costs the log seven lines in its first million pictures. RIS keeps the counts, so a program cannot start them again. The terminal counts at most `IMAGE_NOTE_REASONS_MAX` reasons and keeps at most `IMAGE_NOTE_BYTES_MAX` bytes of each. Tests: `a_handlers_note_reaches_the_host_once_with_its_protocol`, `a_reason_that_repeats_reaches_the_host_at_each_power_of_ten`, `each_protocol_and_reason_is_counted_on_its_own`, `the_notes_hold_a_bounded_count_of_reasons_and_bytes`, `a_dropped_picture_is_noted_for_the_log` and `a_picture_the_store_refuses_a_place_is_noted_for_the_log` (`tests/sixel.rs`), and `data_past_the_pixels_is_noted_for_the_log_and_answered_ok`, `a_picture_the_budget_refuses_is_noted_even_unanswered`, `a_failing_helper_is_noted_and_an_abandoned_request_is_not` and `a_picture_past_vtiums_bounds_is_noted` (`tests/kitty.rs`).

### Right-to-left text

ADR-1004 decides how vtium shows right-to-left text: the screen keeps the order a program wrote, and each paragraph (the rows a wrap joins) is drawn in the order Unicode's bidirectional algorithm, UAX #9, gives. The `bidi` feature carries it, and the default build, minspec, carries none of it. In a build with the feature, vtium-term holds the Unicode data and the algorithm the frame will use: `src/unicode/bidi.rs` and its generated tables, and `src/unicode/bidi/levels.rs`, which runs the algorithm. It also keeps the paragraph state that the modes set (D2), by the rules of the Terminal Working Group's recommendation, which ADR-1004 follows: each row holds its paragraph's four properties in `LineFlags`, `EXPLICIT` for BDSM reset, `RTL` for the direction SCP and SPD set, `DETECT` for mode 2501 and `BOX_MIRROR` for mode 2500. The screen keeps a current value of each. A change always sets the current value, and sets the rows of the cursor's paragraph only when the cursor stands at the paragraph's first logical position, the first column of its first row; the screen's first row counts as a paragraph's first row there. A line feed into a new paragraph gives it the current values, and so does ED to every row it clears whole, the clear of RIS and of mode 1049 included. Each new row takes the current values, a wrap carries the paragraph's values to the row it starts, and a rewrap gives each row it makes the values of the paragraph's first row. DECSTR and RIS return the current values to the defaults, which the mode rows' initial states and `text-direction` give. Mode 1243's state is kept for the arrow keys. The frame draws by these properties (below). A build without the feature has no row for BDSM, DCSM, 1243, 2500 or 2501, so DECRQM answers 0 for each, and it ignores SCP and SPD.

- **Joining.** A cell joins the character before it when that character's Joining_Type is D, L or C and its own is D, R or C, and it joins the character after it when the two types are the other way round. Marks (type T) are skipped and join nothing themselves. The caller passes the cells in the order the letters are read: logical order in implicit mode, and the order drawn from right to left in explicit mode (ADR-1004 D6). The Arabic presentation forms, which a program has shaped already, are Non_Joining.
- **Mirroring.** A character drawn at an odd level draws its Bidi_Mirroring_Glyph, and a character with none draws as it is (D7). While mode 2500 is set, a box-drawing character in a right-to-left paragraph draws the character whose Unicode name swaps the words LEFT and RIGHT. Two characters' swapped names give the halves around AND in the other order, so `gen-unicode` matches those halves either way round: U+257C BOX DRAWINGS LIGHT LEFT AND HEAVY RIGHT pairs with U+257E BOX DRAWINGS HEAVY LEFT AND LIGHT RIGHT. 78 of the block's 128 characters have a counterpart.
- **The guards on `unicode-bidi`** (D13). vtium-term depends on vtium's fork of `unicode-bidi`, pinned to the commit `5927633397bc767eb23e0d0c80541272cb88b4ab` of the fork's branch `vtium/fixes`, with the crate's default features off. The fork's README says what the branch changes from servo's main branch. With the default features off, the crate's own tables are never linked: `src/unicode/bidi/levels.rs` passes it vtium-term's Bidi_Class and bracket tables, at the version the other tables pin, through the crate's `BidiDataSource` trait. The fork's tables are at Unicode 18.0.0 too, so the two give the same answers. vtium-term still passes its own, for two reasons. The binary then carries one Bidi_Class table, which vtium-term needs anyway for rule L1, for detection and for joining, and not a second copy of it in the crate. And vtium-term's Unicode version then moves only when `gen-unicode`'s pins move. `brackets_new_in_unicode_18_pair` checks that U+2E62 and U+2E63, the parentheses with a middle ring that Unicode 18.0.0 adds, pair by rule N0. That module is the only one that names the crate. The module builds a paragraph with the crate's `ParagraphBidiInfo::new_with_data_source`, which runs rules X1 to I2 over the paragraph once. It reads the paragraph's level and each character's level from the result's public `paragraph_level` and `levels` fields, through the crate's `Level` helpers (`Level::ltr`, `Level::rtl`, `Level::new` and `Level::number`). It applies rule L1 to each drawn row itself, with vtium-term's class table, and orders a row with the crate's `reorder_visual`. It calls no other function of the crate's. The root `clippy.toml` lists `visual_runs`, `reorder_line`, `reordered_levels` and `reordered_levels_per_char` of both `ParagraphBidiInfo` and `BidiInfo` as disallowed, so clippy refuses a call to any of them. Each of them copies and walks the whole paragraph's levels for the row it is asked about, so a frame that called them for every row would cost the paragraph's length times its rows. The fork's `visual_runs` panics neither on an empty line nor on the level overflow input the crate's maintainers were sent, where unicode-bidi 0.3.18 panicked on both, and vtium's builds abort on a panic. A character of Bidi_Class B, such as U+2029 PARAGRAPH SEPARATOR, inside a screen paragraph neither starts a new paragraph nor ends the embeddings before it, which departs from UAX #9's rules P1 and X8: a screen paragraph is the rows a wrap joins (ADR-1004 D2), and the crate passes over a B in the middle of its text (`a_paragraph_separator_inside_a_paragraph_does_not_split_it`). vtium-term decides a detected paragraph's direction itself, by rules P2 and P3, so that a paragraph with no strong character can fall back to right to left. A change that moves the pinned revision reviews each guard against the new revision.
- **The frame** (D3 to D10, `src/order.rs`). When the screen builds a frame, it orders each paragraph with a row in view that runs right to left, or that holds a row that has held a right-to-left or joining character (`LineFlags::REORDERS`). The algorithm reads the whole paragraph once, its rows outside the view included, up to `BIDI_PARAGRAPH_ROWS_MAX` rows, and only the rows the frame draws are ordered, so a frame's work grows with the paragraph's length and not with its length times its rows (`a_frame_reads_a_paragraph_once_and_each_drawn_row_once_more`). A paragraph of more rows draws as explicit. Each drawn cell carries its logical column, its direction, its joining sides and its mirrored character, a cluster's marks drawn on the counterpart. The cursor is drawn where its logical cell is; the selection stays logical; the pointer, a link's hover and a mouse report name the logical cell under the pointer; the arrow keys swap under mode 1243. A change in a reordered paragraph, a change of its properties, and an edit that splits it mark its rows changed. vtium-term orders a double-width or double-height row over the screen's full width, as it orders any row. Such a row shows only half as many cells, each twice as wide. Once vtium draws these rows at their size (DECDWL and DECDHL), the order must cover only the cells the row shows, as D8 says.
- **Tests.** `every_case_of_bidi_test_passes` and `every_case_of_bidi_character_test_passes` run Unicode's BidiTest.txt (770,241 cases) and BidiCharacterTest.txt (91,707 cases) at 18.0.0 through `resolve` and `visual_order`, as the frame calls them, and check the paragraph's level, each character's level and the visual order. The crate's own conformance tests run the same files, at the same version, through the crate's own tables and its own rule L1. `the_level_overflow_input_resolves_without_a_panic` resolves the input of the crate's report, laid out as the line 0.3.18's `visual_runs` panicked on, and `empty_paragraphs_and_rows_resolve_without_a_panic` resolves empty paragraphs and rows. `bracket_pairing_stops_when_its_stack_overflows` checks rule BD16's limit of 63 opening brackets, which no case in Unicode's files reaches: one opening bracket past the limit leaves every bracket of the isolating run sequence unpaired, as UAX #9 says. `the_forks_visual_runs_accepts_an_empty_line` calls the refused `visual_runs` and `reorder_line` on an empty line, to pin the fork's fix. Pointed back at unicode-bidi 0.3.18, those last two tests fail. The screen's fuzz target draws a frame of every stream it feeds, so it reaches this code.

### Kitty's Unicode placeholders

With the `images` feature, vtium-term reads kitty's Unicode placeholder cells (ADR-1100, D13). A placeholder cell is a cell whose text is U+10EEEE followed by up to three marks from the kitty spec's list of 297 row and column diacritics: the first mark's place in the list is the cell's row in the picture, the second its column, and the third the top byte of the image id. The cell's foreground color gives the image id's low 24 bits, a palette index or a direct color's 24-bit value, and its underline color gives the placement id the same way; the default foreground, or an image id of 0, names no image, and the default underline color names no placement. A cell that leaves marks out takes them from the placeholder cell to its left, when that cell has the same foreground and underline colors: with no mark, its row, its column plus one and its top byte; with only a row, on the same picture row, its column plus one and its top byte; with a row and a column, on the same picture row and in the column before, its top byte. A value no rule gives is 0. A mark that is not one of the 297 ends the cell's marks, and a third mark past 255 gives no top byte.

vtium-term groups the placeholder cells of each row into runs: side-by-side cells that name the same image id and placement id, lie on the same picture row and show consecutive picture columns. A run is the line's key, its first column, its cell count, the 32-bit image id, the placement id and the picture row and column of its first cell (vtium-image's `PlaceholderRun`, which vtium-term re-exports). vtium-term keeps no runs: `Screen::placeholder_runs` reads them from the rows the view shows, for a frame; `Screen::screen_placeholder_runs` from the screen's own lines of a buffer, for a store decision that needs a virtual placement's position; and `Screen::mark_placeholders` marks changed the placeholder cells of one image on the screen's own lines, for damage. The last two count the cells they read as input work. A write, an erase, a scroll or a rewrap moves the cells as text, and the next read finds them where they are, so a picture row that a rewrap splits over two rows is two runs. A cell that leaves its marks out inherits from the last cell of the row before when that row soft-wrapped into its own, so the second piece of a split picture row keeps counting columns from the first; a hard line break restarts the count. A read that starts at the top of the screen or of the view first walks back through the rows above it that soft-wrapped and end in a placeholder cell, at most as many as a picture row of 297 cells fills, so a row that wrapped down from the scrollback keeps its columns; `screen_placeholder_runs` counts those cells as work too.

A frame resolves a placeholder cell's colors with the default foreground and no underline color, since those colors name its image and its placement. Under SGR 7, DECSCNM or a selection drawn by swapping, the cell therefore takes the colors a space with the same background and attributes takes, and never the color that encodes its image id. The cell keeps its glyph, by which vtium-render draws no text, underline or strike-through there. A build without images resolves U+10EEEE's colors as any private-use character's.

vtium-term hands the image store the runs at three times. `Screen::placements` builds the frame with the runs on the rows the view shows (`Store::frame_with_runs`), so a virtual placement draws its slice of picture under each run, and the picture moves with its cells; it reads them only while the shown buffer holds a virtual placement (`Store::has_virtual`). For the store's decisions, vtium-term reads the runs from the screen lines of each buffer that holds a child of a virtual placement (`Store::has_virtual_children`), and only then. It reads them for every call that reaches the store through `Screen::with_images`: every kitty command and every picture a protocol handler stores, and `Screen::add_image` and `Screen::place_image`. The store then holds them through the call (`Store::with_runs`), and the decisions that take such a child by the cells it draws on use them: a kitty delete by cell, row or column or of every placement, and a reservation that may evict. vtium-term cannot tell those calls from the others, such as a transmission that evicts nothing, because a handler reaches the store through one call, so it reads the runs for each. ED 2 reads only the screen in use, before its erase removes the cells, and runs its rule inside `Store::with_runs` the same way. After each store call, vtium-term marks the placeholder cells of every image whose virtual placement the store reports changed (`Store::take_changed_virtual`). It also marks the cells where each relative placement that follows a virtual placement drew before the call or draws after it (`Store::take_changed_floating`). Such a placement has no box of its own, so the store reports it by the virtual placement it follows and its offset and size in cells, and vtium-term finds that placement's origin from the runs. One read of the shown screen serves both. When the store has more such boxes than it keeps notes of, it reports the buffer changed whole, and vtium-term marks every cell.

Tests: `tests/placeholders.rs`, `tests/placeholder_pictures.rs`, `a_placeholder_cell_takes_a_blank_cells_colors_under_reverse_video`, `every_diacritic_is_an_above_mark_in_the_pinned_ucd` and `the_table_is_ghosttys_list_entry_for_entry`.

### The screen

- **A line leaving the top of the main screen's scroll region enters the scrollback, wherever the region starts;** on the alternate screen it is cleared. No specification covers a scrollback, and a program that scrolls a region below a status line keeps its history this way; xterm keeps only the lines leaving a region at the screen's top. Test: `a_line_leaving_any_region_enters_the_scrollback`.
- **DL at the screen's first row clips the placements on the lines it deletes.** In a build with images, the lines DL deletes go, and so does what is anchored on them, as at any other row. No specification covers it; the kitty spec has a placement wholly inside a scrolled band clipped at its edges. Test: `dl_at_the_first_row_clips_what_it_deletes`.
- **Cells past a line's end read in the line's fill, never the pen.** A scrollback line narrower than the screen is padded, when drawn, with empty cells in its fill: the default style, unless the program erased the line's end in a color (ADR-1003). No specification covers padding. The reason: padding is not text the program wrote, so the pen has no claim on it, and a pen change that moved no change age would leave a display drawing stale colors. Tests: `cells_past_the_end_read_blank_in_the_default_style` and `trim_keeps_the_fill_as_the_style_past_the_end` (the line), `a_pen_change_while_scrolled_back_leaves_the_padding_alone` (the screen).
- **A width change rewraps the main screen and its scrollback (ADR-1003, vtium/vtium#41).** Rows a wrap joined become one logical line and wrap at the new width; a line the program ended with a newline, or placed by moving the cursor, joins nothing. A wide character that no longer fits moves whole to the next row and leaves a pad, and on a screen one column wide becomes an empty cell. The cursor, the main screen's saved cursor, the selection and the view keep their place in the text, and the rows below the cursor keep their number less what the text above it grew, as Ghostty keeps them. Spec: contour's line reflow mode, mode 2028, whose rows printed while it is reset are cut instead. Tests: `tests/reflow.rs`, among them `a_wrapped_line_rejoins_when_the_screen_widens` (the probe `reflow`), `the_cursor_keeps_its_place_in_the_text` and the three property tests.
- **No row keeps a cell past the right margin.** The alternate screen, rows printed with mode 2028 reset and rows of double size are cut at a narrower width and never rewrapped; the main screen wraps its text onto extra rows instead. No specification covers a resize's cells; the reason: a hidden cell that reappeared on a widening would be text the screen no longer shows, and a full-screen program redraws on SIGWINCH. Tests: `the_alternate_screen_is_cut_and_never_rewrapped`, `a_row_printed_with_mode_2028_reset_is_cut`, `fixed_rows_are_cut_and_marks_go_to_the_first_row`.
- **A widening's new columns take the line's fill.** On the main screen they are empty cells in the color the program erased the line's end with, which a line keeps as its fill, and in the default style otherwise; on the alternate screen they are blank. No specification covers them; the reason: a line erased in a color to its end reads as erased to the new end, as a pager's colored lines do. Tests: `columns_a_widening_resize_adds_take_the_lines_fill`, `a_widening_paints_the_lines_fill`.
- **Outside the region, IND, RI, a line feed and a wrap stop at the screen's edge.** Spec: the VT510 manual, IND: "If the cursor is at the bottom margin, then the screen performs a scroll-up."; DECAWM: "Any text on the page scrolls up if the cursor is at the end of the scrolling region."; the VT220 manual, chapter 4, RI: "If cursor is at top margin, screen performs a scroll down." (the VT510 manual has no RI page). Below the region a wrap on the last row returns to its first column, as a wide character or a widened cluster does, and leaves the row unmarked as wrapped, since its text does not continue. Test: `index_outside_the_region_stops_at_the_screens_edge`.
- **An inverted or one-row DECSTBM is ignored, and a bottom margin past the screen is its last row.** The VT510 manual says only "The value of the top margin (Pt) must be less than the bottom margin (Pb).", so vtium does as xterm does (`charproc.c`, `CASE_DECSTBM`): the region and the cursor stay, and a bottom past the screen clamps. DECSTBM homes the cursor, and an omitted bottom margin is the last row (the VT510 manual). Tests: `an_invalid_decstbm_is_ignored_and_a_bottom_past_the_screen_clamps`, `decstbm_homes_the_cursor_and_an_omitted_bottom_is_the_last_line`.
- **A private marker or an intermediate makes another function.** CUD, CUF, CUB, CNL, CPL, CHA, VPA, VPR, ED, EL, ECH, IL, DL, CHT, CBT and REP act only without either, ED and EL also with `?` (DECSED, DECSEL). Spec: ECMA-48 5.4, "Together with the Final Byte F, [the intermediate bytes] identify the control function", and 5.4.1 b), a private marker makes the parameter string private (vtium-parse's README, "Notes for vtium-term"). Test: `a_marker_or_an_intermediate_makes_another_function`.
- **A printed DEL does nothing.** Spec: vt100.net, "terminals only claiming VT220 compatibility can always ignore 7F"; only a 96-character set in GL draws it, and vtium designates none. Test: `a_printed_del_does_nothing`.
- **A line keeps 6 bytes of clusters a cell (ADR-1001's cluster budget),** so on a screen one column wide a heart and VS16, 7 bytes with its length byte, keep the heart. A row cut to fewer cells, or landing on a screen narrower than the width its clusters were written at, keeps the clusters that printing it at the narrower width keeps, so a family emoji of five codepoints narrowed from 5 columns to 3 keeps its first four. A row that lands on a screen as wide or wider, where a widening has gathered onto it the clusters of parts of three source rows, keeps with the same cut the clusters that fit twice its budget, its arena's limit; the scrollback keeps every cluster of each row that rewraps. No specification bounds a cluster; the reason: a program cannot grow a line's memory past its budget. In a build with images, a kitty placeholder cluster keeps its first three marks outside the budget (ADR-1001, "Placeholder cells"). Tests: `the_cluster_budget_cuts_a_cluster_on_a_one_column_line`, `a_placeholder_keeps_three_marks_whatever_the_budget`, `a_row_narrowed_past_its_budget_keeps_what_printing_there_keeps`, `a_screen_narrowed_from_a_wide_grid_holds_its_rows_to_their_bound`, `a_screen_widened_by_a_fraction_holds_its_rows_to_their_bound`.

### Left and right margins

Mode 69 (DECLRMM) lets DECSLRM, `CSI Pl ; Pr s`, set left and right margins, and resetting it returns them to the whole width. RIS resets the mode. DECSTR keeps the mode and the left and right margins, since the VT510 manual's DECSTR table leaves out DECLRMM and DECSLRM. DECALN and a resize return the margins to the whole width and leave the mode. With the top and bottom margins, the left and right margins bound printing, the cursor's movement, the editing and scrolling functions and origin mode, as xterm's ctlseqs and the VT510 manual define them. A black-box run of xterm 411 (`-ti vt420 -u8` under Xvfb, its screen printed through `printerCommand` and its cursor read with CPR) printed the same screen and cursor as vtium for every stream in `tests/margins.rs`, apart from the places this section names. Spec: ctlseqs, DECLRMM, DECSLRM, DECIC, DECDC, DECBI and DECFI; the VT510 manual, the same pages. Tests: `tests/margins.rs`.

- **Printed text wraps at the right margin to the left margin of the next row,** and from right of the right margin it wraps at the screen's last column. A pending wrap at a right margin before the last column keeps the cursor on the margin's column, so `Screen::cursor` reports that column there, and DECSC and DECRC keep the pending wrap. The wrap scrolls the region at the bottom margin only from between the left and right margins, as a line feed does. Test: `printing_wraps_at_the_right_margin_to_the_left_margin`, `decsc_keeps_a_pending_wrap_at_the_right_margin`.
- **A row is marked as continuing on the next row only when it wraps from the screen's last column to the next row's first,** so a wrap at a right margin, or to a left margin past the first column, leaves the row unmarked. xterm marks every wrapped row. The reason: reflow and selection read a marked row's text as going on at the next row's first column, and a row cut at a margin has other text beside it. Mode 45 then reverse-wraps BS and CUB only into rows marked that way, so inside narrower margins BS stops at the left margin, where xterm goes back to the right margin of the row above. Test: `printing_wraps_at_the_right_margin_to_the_left_margin`.
- **A wide character that does not fit before a right margin inside the screen leaves the column it skips erased.** The character wraps whole, as it does at the screen's edge, where the skipped column becomes a pad; at a margin the row is not marked as continuing, so the column is erased, as it is where a wrap returns to the same row. xterm leaves the column as it was. No specification covers wide characters at a margin.
- **CUF stops at the right margin, and at the last column from right of it; HT stops at the right margin, from right of it too.** The VT510 manual has HT move the cursor "to the right margin" when no tab stop is left; xterm's `TabToNextStop` holds a tab to the right margin wherever the cursor starts, and vtium does as xterm does. CUB, BS and CR stop at the left margin, and at the first column from left of it; CBT stops at the left margin only in origin mode, as xterm's `TabToPrevStop` stops. Test: `cursor_movement_stops_at_the_margins`.
- **In origin mode, CUP, HVP, CHA, HPA and VPA count columns from the left margin and stay inside the margins, and CPR counts from it.** DECSLRM and DECSTBM home the cursor to the region's top left corner. DECRC restores the cursor to where it was on the screen, whatever the origin mode, where xterm moves a cursor saved outside the margins inside them. Test: `origin_mode_counts_columns_from_the_left_margin`.
- **ICH, DCH, IL and DL do nothing while the cursor is outside the margins.** ICH and DCH move the cells between the cursor and the right margin; IL and DL move the cells between the left and right margins of the rows from the cursor's to the bottom margin, and put the cursor on the left margin. In insert mode, text printed between the margins moves the cells up to the right margin, and text printed outside them moves nothing. Spec: the VT510 manual, ICH, DCH, IL and DL; xterm's `InsertChar`, `DeleteChar`, `InsertLine` and `DeleteLine`. Tests: `ich_and_dch_move_the_cells_up_to_the_right_margin`, `il_and_dl_move_the_cells_between_the_margins`, `insert_mode_moves_the_cells_up_to_the_right_margin`.
- **IND, LF, NEL and RI scroll the region between all four margins only while the cursor is between the left and right margins; SU and SD scroll it wherever the cursor is.** Outside the margins a line feed at the bottom margin moves nothing, as xterm's `xtermIndex` has it. A scroll inside narrower margins moves cells, not lines, so nothing enters the scrollback, and a picture over the moving cells loses them, since a picture moves only with whole lines. Tests: `line_feeds_scroll_only_between_the_margins`, `su_and_sd_scroll_the_region_between_the_margins`.
- **ED, EL and ECH erase whole rows' cells, whatever the margins,** as the VT510 manual has them. Test: `erases_ignore_the_margins`.
- **DECIC and DECDC insert and delete columns at the cursor's column, in each row from the top margin to the bottom, up to the right margin,** and do nothing while the cursor is outside the region. Without left and right margins they work on the whole width. DECBI and DECFI move the cursor one column, and at the left or right margin move the region's columns instead. Spec: the VT510 manual, DECIC, DECDC, DECBI and DECFI; xterm's `xtermColScroll` and `xtermColIndex`. Tests: `decic_and_decdc_insert_and_delete_columns`, `decbi_and_decfi_move_the_cursor_or_the_columns`.
- **A wide character across a left or right margin goes whole before the cells between the margins move,** as xterm's `scrollInMargins` clears it. An edit that changes a row's last column ends the row's wrap, and one that changes the first column of the region's first row ends the wrap of the row above, as the wrap rules below say for whole lines. Test: `a_wide_character_across_a_margin_goes_whole_when_the_region_scrolls`.
- **DECRQSS reports DECSLRM as `Pl ; Pr s`,** the whole width while DECLRMM is reset, as xterm reports it. Test: `decrqss_reports_the_left_and_right_margins`.

### Change tracking and the frame

- **A region scroll marks only its rows.** Scrolling inside margins, IL and DL mark the rows that moved and leave the rows outside them unchanged. No specification covers change tracking. The reason: a renderer redraws what is marked, and a one-row scroll in a status pane should not redraw the whole screen. Tests: `a_region_scroll_marks_only_the_region` (`Damage`'s rule, and the screen's), and the property test `a_frames_changed_set_covers_every_cell_that_differs`, which a planted skip turns red.
- **Moving the cursor marks nothing.** The frame carries the cursor, and a renderer redraws the cells it left and covers (ADR-1001, "Change tracking"), so after a cut in the replay view a caller's next `frame-since` counts no cell for the cursor. Test: `after_a_checkpoint_nothing_is_changed`.
- **The frame marks the default background.** A frame cell says whether its background is the default one and not inverted, so an image below cell backgrounds shows through exactly there, and a background a program set to the default's color hides it. Spec: the kitty graphics protocol, z-index below -1,073,741,824: drawn "under cells with non-default background colors". Test: `default_bg_marks_only_the_default_background_uninverted`.

### Character sets

Test: `a_utf8_character_prints_as_itself_whatever_g1_to_g3_hold`.
- **The default mapping is the VT220's: G0 to G3 hold ASCII, GL holds G0 and GR holds G2.** DECSCL 62 to 64, which choose 8-bit input, designate DEC supplemental graphics into G2 and G3; DECSCL 61 designates nothing. SO or SS3 with nothing designated therefore print ASCII. Spec: the VT220 manual, 4.4, "The default mapping in VT200 mode is ASCII graphics in GL and DEC supplemental graphics in GR", and 4.4.3, LS2R: "Invoke G2 into GR (VT200 mode only). (default)"; in VT100 mode "Only ASCII, national replacement characters, and DEC special graphics are available". A black-box run of xterm 411 in 8-bit mode (`+u8`) agrees: after `ESC ) 0` the byte F1 prints ñ, and after `ESC * 0` it prints ─. Tests: `shift_out_invokes_g1_into_gl`, `single_shifts_take_the_next_character_only`, `gr_maps_the_upper_half_in_eight_bit_input`, `seven_bit_input_reaches_only_gl`.
- **LS2, LS3, LS1R, LS2R and LS3R act in every input,** as in xterm's UTF-8 mode; the VT220 takes them in VT200 mode only. Test: `locking_shifts_invoke_g2_and_g3_into_gl`.
- **A single shift is taken by the next printed character, whatever it is,** so in `ESC N ñ q` the ñ takes the shift and the `q` prints as itself. Spec: the VT220 manual, 4.4.4, "All single shifts are active for only the next single graphic character"; ctlseqs, SS2: "This affects next character only". A control before the character leaves the shift waiting, as in xterm. xterm 411 departs from VT220 4.4.4 and from vtium/vtium#13's rule in UTF-8 mode: after `ESC * 0 ESC N` it stops decoding UTF-8 for the shifted character, so `ñ q` prints `Ã±q` and `€ q` prints `␉¬q` where vtium prints `ñq` and `€q`. Test: `single_shifts_take_the_next_character_only`.
- **REP repeats the character a single shift mapped.** `ESC * 0 ESC N q CSI 2 b` prints `───`; xterm 411 repeats the unmapped character and prints `─qq`. Spec: ECMA-48 8.3.103, REP repeats "the preceding graphic character", which is the one the shift drew. Test: `rep_repeats_the_character_a_single_shift_mapped`.
- **DECSC saves what G0 to G3 hold and a pending single shift, and DECRC with nothing saved returns the default mapping.** Spec: VT510, DECSC saves the "Character sets (G0, G1, G2, or G3) currently in GL and GR" and "Any single shift 2 (SS2) or single shift 3 (SS3) functions sent"; xterm saves the designations with them and resets the sets when nothing was saved (`cursor.c`, `CursorRestore`), and the black-box run shows ─ for `ESC ( 0 ESC 7 ESC ( B ESC 8 q`. Test: `decsc_saves_the_character_sets`.
- **A national replacement set's final designates ASCII.** ADR-1000 refuses DECNRCM (DEC-42), and xterm draws a national set as ASCII while DECNRCM is reset: the black-box run shows `#a` after `ESC ( A`, and `[` after `ESC ( K` in both modes. Test: `national_replacement_sets_designate_ascii`.
- **DEC special graphics draws 5/15 as a blank, and a reserved position of DEC supplemental graphics prints nothing.** Spec: the VT220 manual, table 2-4, 5/15 "(BLANK)"; xterm prints a space. No specification gives a reserved position a character. Tests: `dec_special_graphics_draws_lines`, `dec_supplemental_graphics_only_in_eight_bit_input`, `gr_maps_the_upper_half_in_eight_bit_input`.
- **DEC special graphics draws 6/0 as U+25C6 and 7/14 as U+00B7.** The VT220 manual gives glyphs, not code points; xterm (its manual's table), Konsole (`vt100_graphics`), Ghostty (`charsets.zig`) and the Linux console (`consolemap.c`) all draw U+25C6 and U+00B7. ### Replies and the cursor

Tests: `xtgettcap_answers_from_the_table_in_uppercase`, and `no_reply_carries_the_requests_bytes` in `src/terminal/reply.rs`.
- **DECRQSS for DECSCL reports the level DECSCL set,** 61 to 64, and 62 until one is set and after DECSTR, with Pc 1 since the replies stay 7-bit. Spec: VT510, DECRQSS reports the current setting; xterm reports its level and its C1 setting. Test: `decrqss_reports_the_conformance_level`.

- **CPR counts rows from the region's top in origin mode, and a pending wrap reports the last column.** Spec: VT100 User Guide, CPR: "The numbering of lines depends on the state of the Origin Mode (DECOM)"; the cursor stands on the last column while the wrap is pending (VT510, DECAWM). Test: `dsr_reports_the_status_and_the_cursor`.
- **DECSC saves the pending wrap, and DECRC restores the cursor to where it was on the screen, in a slot for each screen.** Spec: VT510, DECSC: it saves the "wrap flag"; xterm keeps a saved cursor for each screen. Test: `decsc_saves_and_decrc_restores_the_cursor_state`.
- **SCOSC and SCORC save and restore the cursor as DECSC and DECRC do.** `CSI s` and `CSI u` with no parameter use DECSC's slot, as xterm's ctlseqs gives them and xterm does. With any parameter, even 0 or an empty list, `CSI s` and `CSI u` do nothing, as in xterm. SCOSC holds only while DECLRMM (mode 69) is reset; while it is set, `CSI s` is DECSLRM (Left and right margins, below). `CSI u` keeps its one meaning. Programs that lay pictures out in a grid save the cursor this way before each picture and return to it after, as vtium's own case `chafa-grid` plays. Spec: ctlseqs, SCOSC, SCORC and DECSLRM. Test: `scosc_and_scorc_save_and_restore_the_cursor_as_decsc_and_decrc_do`.

- **Mode 12 under the default cursor style leaves the cursor as the host blinks it,** and DECRQM reports the host's blink. Test: `mode_12_blinks_a_chosen_cursor_style`.

- **XTWINOPS reports a size only where the host gave it, 15 reports the display, 14 ; 2 gets no reply, and 21 reports a fixed empty title.** Spec: ADR-1000, "Replies and policy"; ctlseqs, XTWINOPS. Tests: `xtwinops_reports_the_sizes_vtium_has`, `the_title_report_is_empty`, `window_reports_vtium_has_no_window_for_are_ignored`.
- **A host setting resets the sixel handler only when its value changes.** `Terminal::set_image_limits` cancels a sixel string, resets the handler and drops the shared registers when `max-graphic-size`, `num-color-registers` or `private-color-registers` changes, and returns the register count and modes 80, 1070 and 8452 to their state at reset when one of the last two does; a call that sets the values already in force changes nothing. A host can then set the same limits again, as it may when it rereads its configuration, without dropping a program's registers and modes. Test: `a_change_of_the_sixel_settings_resets_the_handler`.
- **Modes 80, 1070 and 8452 answer DECRQM with 0 in a build without images,** as every mode with no row does, and SM and RM of them change nothing. A build with images keeps the three rows and answers their fixed state while sixel is off (ADR-1100, D10, the maintainer's choice). Test: `the_sixel_modes_have_no_row_and_da1_no_sixel_without_images`.

### Text attributes

- **SGR 8 conceals text, and SGR 28 reveals it.** Spec: ECMA-48 8.3.117 and ctlseqs. A concealed cell keeps its text, so a selection over it copies the text, and the frame hands the renderer the attribute, which draws the cell as its background. DECRQSS reports it as 8.
- **SGR 53 sets an overline, and SGR 55 clears it.** Spec: ECMA-48 8.3.117. DECRQSS reports it as 53.
- **SGR 73 sets superscript, SGR 74 sets subscript, and SGR 75 clears both.** Spec: mintty's CtrlSeqs. Each of 73 and 74 takes the place of the other, and DECRQSS reports the one set as 73 or 74.
- **SGR 6 sets rapid blink, and SGR 25 clears it with SGR 5's blink.** Spec: ECMA-48 8.3.117. A cell can carry both blinks, and DECRQSS reports rapid blink as 6.
- **DECCARA takes every one of these, since it applies SGR's parameters.** DECRARA's 8 reverses the conceal attribute, as DEC STD 070 gives.

### Colors

- **Programs set, query and reset colors.** OSC 4 sets palette colors and OSC 104 resets them; OSC 10 to 14, 17 and 19 set and query the default foreground and background, the cursor, the pointer's foreground and background and the selection's background and foreground, a list going on to the next number; OSC 110 to 114, 117 and 119 reset them. Spec: ctlseqs, "Operating System Commands". A reset returns a color to the palette the host configured, and the host's `set_palette` replaces the colors a program set. DECSTR returns the 256 palette colors to the configured palette and keeps the colors OSC 10 to 19 set, as xterm's soft reset does (`ReallyReset` in xterm 412's `charproc.c`). An unset cursor color draws the cell inverted and its query answers the foreground; an unset selection background draws the selection inverted, a set one takes the cell's foreground over it unless OSC 19 sets one too; the unset queries answer as xterm's defaults, the cursor and the selection background the foreground and the selection foreground the background. ctlseqs's specs are X11's: vtium reads `rgb:` with one to four hex digits a channel, scaled, and `#` with one to four a channel as its high bits, each prefix and digit in either case, and keeps each channel's top 8 bits, so a query answers `rgb:` with each byte written twice. A color name is X11's, from the `rgb.txt` of X.Org's rgb-1.1.1, in every build (vtium/vtium#103), read in any case and with any spaces (color.names). The X server matched a name `rgb.txt` lists in any case, and the file lists each name of several words both spaced and joined, so vtium reads every name xterm reads, and spacings the file does not list, such as `darkslate gray`, as well. `rgbi:` and the Xcms spaces set nothing, since vtium holds no Xcms color spaces, nor does an index past 255; OSC 15, 16 and 18, Tektronix's, are skipped in a list (color.tektronix). Tests: `tests/colors.rs`, and the table against `rgb.txt` in `src/terminal/color.rs`.
- **RIS returns every color to the configured palette.** xterm's RIS resets the 256 palette colors and keeps the colors OSC 10 to 19 set, as its DECSTR does (`ReallyReset` in xterm 412's `charproc.c`). vtium's RIS also returns the defaults and the cursor, pointer and selection colors, since RIS returns the terminal to its initial state. Test: `decstr_resets_the_palette_and_ris_every_color`.
- **OSC 21 is kitty's color protocol.** xterm's OSC 21 is DECSWT, a title, which vtium ignores; ADR-1000 takes kitty's meaning, which Ghostty also implements. `foreground`, `background`, `cursor`, `selection_background`, `selection_foreground` and the indices 0 to 255 are set by `key=spec`, queried by `key=?` in one reply, and reset by a bare key; `key=` unsets the cursor and the selection colors, and is ignored for the others, which kitty does not let a program make dynamic. A key vtium does not know, kitty's `cursor_text`, `visual_bell` and `transparent_background_color1` to `7` among them, is left out of the reply, where kitty answers `unknown=` with its name in base64: a reply holds no text from the request (docs/architecture.md, the reply rule). The colors are read as OSC 4 reads them: a color name is X11's, from `rgb.txt`, and `rgbi:` and `@alpha` set nothing. Tests: `osc_21_sets_queries_and_resets_by_key`, `color_names_set_the_defaults_and_kittys_keys`.
- **XTPUSHSGR and XTPOPSGR keep pens on a stack.** `CSI Pm # {` pushes the pen and `CSI # }` pops it, with `CSI Pm # p` and `CSI # q` the same functions. Spec: ctlseqs, XTPUSHSGR and XTPOPSGR. With no parameters, or one omitted, the pop restores every part of the pen SGR sets; each parameter otherwise names a part by its SGR code, 1 bold, 2 faint, 3 italic, 4 underlined, 5 blink, 7 inverse, 8 invisible, 9 crossed-out, 21 doubly underlined, 30 the foreground and 31 the background, with xterm's older 10 and 11 for 30 and 31, and a code it does not list names nothing, as in xterm 412 (`charproc.c`, `CASE_XTERM_PUSH_SGR`). vtium holds the underline's style and color (SGR 58) as one part, which 4 and 21 both name; blink takes rapid blink with it, as SGR 25 clears both; the overline and the script, which the list does not name, return only with every part; DECSCA's protection is not SGR's and stays, as with SGR 0. The stack keeps `SGR_STACK_MAX` pens; a push past it keeps nothing and is counted, and the pop that pairs with it restores nothing, as xterm's `xtermPushSGR` and `xtermPopSGR` do (`misc.c`). A pop of an empty stack does nothing. Tests: `tests/stacks.rs`, `a_pop_leaves_the_protection`, `the_parts_xterm_does_not_list_return_with_every_part`.
- **XTPUSHCOLORS, XTPOPCOLORS and XTREPORTCOLORS keep palettes on a stack, which kitty's OSC 30001 and 30101 push and pop.** Spec: ctlseqs, XTPUSHCOLORS, XTPOPCOLORS and XTREPORTCOLORS; kitty's `color-stack`. An entry holds all of `Terminal::palette()`: the 256 colors, the defaults, and the cursor's, pointer's and selection's colors. `CSI # P` stores the palette in the entry after the current one and `CSI # Q` restores it from the current one; a parameter of 1 to `COLOR_STACK_MAX` stores into or restores from that entry, which becomes the current one or, for a pop, the one after it; 0 pushes and pops, as ctlseqs says, where xterm 412 stored an explicit 0 and then made the stack empty (`xtermPushColors`, `misc.c`); several parameters act in turn. A push with the stack full, a parameter past the bound, a pop of an empty stack and a pop of an entry never stored do nothing, as in xterm. A pop that changes a color marks every cell changed. `CSI # R` answers `CSI ? Pc ; Ps # Q`, the current entry and the entries stored, the highest that holds a palette, as ctlseqs words it and foot answers (foot's `csi.c` at `152487e`, MIT); xterm 412 answered with the most entries a push without a parameter had reached, so an entry stored by number was left out. OSC 30001 and 30101, each with nothing after the number, push and pop the same stack as `CSI # P` and `CSI # Q`: kitty takes xterm's functions too and documents neither a depth nor two stacks. Tests: `tests/stacks.rs`, `no_reply_carries_the_requests_bytes`.
- **A color push with the stack full keeps nothing, where kitty drops the oldest entry.** kitty 0.49.2, run as a program and driven through its pty, kept the newest 10 of 11 pushes, by XTPUSHCOLORS and OSC 30001 alike, so its tenth pop restored the second push's palette; it reported `CSI ? 9 ; 10 # Q` after the pushes and `CSI ? 0 ; 0 # Q` once the pops emptied it. vtium keeps the first 10, as xterm 412 does (`xtermPushColors`, `misc.c`), so its tenth pop restores the first push's palette, the stack reports `CSI ? 10 ; 10 # Q` and then `CSI ? 0 ; 10 # Q`, and the pops still pair with the pushes the stack kept. kitty's `color-stack` documents neither. Test: `a_push_past_the_color_bound_keeps_nothing`.
- **RIS empties the SGR and color stacks, and DECSTR keeps them.** xterm 412 kept both stacks through RIS and DECSTR (`ReallyReset`, `charproc.c`); foot empties its color stack on RIS and keeps it on DECSTR (`term_reset` in foot's `terminal.c` at `152487e`). RIS returns the terminal to its initial state, which holds no pushed pens or palettes. Test: `ris_empties_the_stacks_and_decstr_keeps_them`.
- **Mode 2031 reports a change between dark and light, and nothing as it is set.** contour's color palette update notifications send `CSI ? 997 ; Ps n` on each update of the palette. vtium sends it only when the default background crosses between dark and light, by OSC 11, 111 or 21, a color stack pop or the configured palette, since the report carries only that; a palette color, the foreground or a background of the same scheme sends nothing. Dark is a luma below half, Y = 0.299 R + 0.587 G + 0.114 B (ITU-R BT.601), the rule neovim applies to OSC 11's answer; contour's spec and foot(7) give none. Tests: `mode_2031_reports_a_change_of_scheme`, `a_color_stack_pop_reports_a_change_of_scheme`, `the_scheme_query_answers_by_the_backgrounds_luma`.

### Input

- **Keys are sent in xterm's encoding, modifiers included.** With Shift, Alt or Ctrl held, the cursor keys, Home and End send `CSI 1 ; m X` in either DECCKM state, F1 to F4 send `CSI 1 ; m P` to `CSI 1 ; m S`, and Insert, Delete, Page Up, Page Down and F5 to F20 send `CSI code ; m ~`, where m is 1 plus 1 for Shift, 2 for Alt and 4 for Ctrl. Shift with F1 to F10 is Shift's parameter, so Shift+F1 sends `CSI 1 ; 2 P`. With NumLock off, the keypad's Insert, Delete and Begin send what Insert, Delete and `CSI E` send, in either keypad mode. In application keypad mode the keypad's other keys send `SS3 final`, with a modifier `SS3 m final`: * sends `SS3 j`, / `SS3 o`, = `SS3 X`, Tab `SS3 I` and the space `SS3 SP`. In numeric keypad mode they send their character, = included, and vtium drops any modifier on them, Alt too. Shift+Tab sends `CSI Z` with any other modifier held. Alt never puts ESC before any of these keys. Spec: ctlseqs, "PC-Style Function Keys" and "VT220-Style Keypad", with xterm's defaults modifyCursorKeys 2, modifyFunctionKeys 2 and modifyKeypadKeys 0; xterm 411, driven under Xvfb, sends each of these sequences. Tests: `editing_and_cursor_keys_carry_xterms_modifier_parameter`, `function_keys_carry_xterms_modifier_parameter`, `alt_with_an_editing_or_function_key_is_its_parameter`, `keypad_operators_follow_the_specification`, `the_keypads_editing_keys_send_the_editing_keys_sequences`, `the_keypads_tab_and_space_follow_the_specification`, `shift_tab_is_csi_z_with_any_modifier`.
- **A paste is filtered as xterm filters one.** In every paste, bracketed or not, each C0 control but HT, LF and CR, DEL, and each C1 control becomes one space: a paste is UTF-8 text in every input, so a C1 control is a code point, U+0080 to U+009F, and its two bytes become one space, with ill-formed bytes read as U+FFFD. Since ESC and the C1 controls are filtered, a bracketed paste never carries its own end marker. Spec: the xterm manual (patch 412), `allowPasteControls`, false by default: "Other C0 control characters are suppressed unless this resource is enabled", and `disallowedPasteControls`, which turns each control it lists into a space, by default `BS,DEL,ENQ,EOT,ESC,NUL,STTY`; ADR-1000, input.paste-end-marker. vtium differs from xterm's default in three ways: it filters every C0 control but HT, LF and CR where xterm filters those in its list; it filters the C1 controls in UTF-8 input too, where xterm filters them only outside UTF-8; and it makes a C1 control a space, where xterm outside UTF-8 removes it (`button.c`, `removeControls` skips it). Tests: `a_bracketed_paste_never_carries_its_own_end`, `a_paste_keeps_only_the_formatting_controls`, `a_character_whose_bytes_look_like_c1_arrives_whole`, `ill_formed_utf8_in_a_paste_becomes_the_replacement_character`, `a_paste_is_sent_in_the_inputs_encoding`, `an_ordinary_paste_arrives_unchanged`.
- **A paste sends each LF as CR,** so a pasted line end reaches a program in raw mode as the Return key, not Ctrl-J. Spec: xterm's `_WriteSelectionData` (`button.c`), which turns each LF of a paste into CR unless `pasteLiteralNl` is set, as it is not by default. Tests: `a_paste_sends_each_line_end_as_cr`, `an_ordinary_paste_arrives_unchanged`, `a_paste_keeps_only_the_formatting_controls`.
- **A paste is sent in the input's encoding.** vtium reads a paste as UTF-8, filters it by code point, and sends each character as a typed one is sent: as UTF-8 in UTF-8 input, as its byte in 8-bit input to U+00FF and in 7-bit input to U+007F, and as `?` past that. So in 8-bit input a pasted "€" reaches the program as `?`, not as three Latin-1 characters with a C1 control among them. Spec: xterm, outside UTF-8, turns a UTF-8 selection into Latin-1 before it pastes it (`button.c`, `UTF8toLatin1`), each character past U+00FF becoming an ASCII equivalent or its `defaultString`, `#` by default; vtium sends `?`, as it does for a typed character the input cannot carry, and in 7-bit input sends `?` for U+0080 to U+00FF too, where `UTF8toLatin1` keeps their bytes. OSC 52 data a program sets, which the host pastes from the same buffer, is read as UTF-8 in every input too, pending vtium/vtium#101. Test: `a_paste_is_sent_in_the_inputs_encoding`.
- **A key's character in 7-bit input is `?` past 0x7F.** No specification covers it; the reason: a 7-bit program cannot read the character. Test: `decscl_resets_and_sets_the_input`.

### The kitty keyboard protocol

The kitty keyboard protocol lets a program ask the terminal to send every key in a form that cannot be mistaken for another key (ADR-1005). A program asks for it by pushing a set of flags, and each flag adds to what the terminal reports. While the flags in force are 0, which they are until a program pushes or sets them, every key sends what the Input section above describes. Spec for every bullet: kitty's "Comprehensive keyboard handling in terminals", <https://sw.kovidgoyal.net/kitty/keyboard-protocol/>, read 2026-11-07.

- **vtium keeps one stack of flags for the main screen and one for the alternate screen.** `CSI > flags u` saves the flags in force and puts `flags` in force, 0 when the number is left out. `CSI < n u` pops n sets, 1 when the number is left out, and the newest saved set that remains comes back into force. `CSI = flags ; mode u` changes the flags in force and saves nothing: mode 1 or no mode sets exactly `flags`, mode 2 sets its bits, mode 3 clears its bits, and any other mode changes nothing. `CSI ? u` is answered `CSI ? flags u` with the flags in force on the screen shown. A stack holds `KEYBOARD_FLAGS_MAX` sets, the set in force and seven saved sets, and a push onto a full stack drops the oldest saved set. A pop of more sets than are saved empties the stack and puts 0 in force, and `CSI < 0 u` pops nothing. A switch between the screens by mode 47, 1047 or 1049 leaves each stack as it is, and RIS and DECSTR empty both stacks. Spec: "Progressive enhancement"; ADR-1005, D3 to D5. Tests: `a_ninth_push_drops_the_oldest_level`, `each_screen_keeps_its_own_flags_across_a_switch`, `ris_and_decstr_clear_both_stacks`, and the two stack tests in `src/terminal/kitty_keyboard.rs`.
- **vtium keeps only flag 1, disambiguate escape codes.** A push or a set drops every other bit as it arrives, flags 2, 4, 8 and 16 included, so a program that pushes 31 and then asks reads back 1, as the specification's note on terminals that implement only some flags asks. Releases, alternate keys, every key as an escape code and the text inside the code arrive in later slices of ADR-1005 (D13). Test: `the_query_reports_only_the_flags_vtium_implements`.
- **While the flags in force are not 0, flag 1 decides every key's bytes.** Escape is `CSI 27 u`. A key that makes text sends its text while nothing but Shift and the locks are held, and with Alt, Ctrl, Logo, Hyper or Meta held it is `CSI code ; modifiers u`, where the code is the key's unshifted character and the modifiers are 1 plus shift 1, alt 2, ctrl 4, super 8, hyper 16, meta 32, caps_lock 64 and num_lock 128. So Ctrl+I is `CSI 105 ; 5 u` and no longer Tab, and Ctrl+Shift+A is `CSI 97 ; 6 u`. Every key that makes no text is written as the specification's table of functional keys gives it, with every modifier held: Up is `CSI A`, Ctrl+Up `CSI 1 ; 5 A`, F1 `CSI P`, F3 `CSI 13 ~`, Insert `CSI 2 ~` and F13 `CSI 57376 u`. A repeat is sent as a press, and a release sends nothing. DECCKM, DECKPAM and mode 66, modifyOtherKeys and modes 1034, 1035 and 1036 are not consulted. modifyOtherKeys keeps its level, which `CSI ? 4 m` still reports, and it works again once the flags in force are 0 (ADR-1005, D6). Spec: "Disambiguate escape codes", "Key codes", "Modifiers" and "Functional key definitions". Tests: `a_push_of_1_sends_escape_alt_and_ctrl_keys_as_codes`, `a_push_of_1_sends_the_functional_keys_in_the_specifications_forms`, `flags_at_0_send_xterms_bytes`, `modify_other_keys_waits_while_the_flags_are_not_0`.
- **Enter, Tab and Backspace keep their legacy bytes only when no modifier but the locks is held.** The specification says these three keys "still generate the same bytes as in legacy mode", so that a user can type `reset` after a program dies with the flags set. It does not say whether that holds with a modifier. vtium sends the legacy bytes for the key alone, CR (CR LF under LNM), HT, and DEL or BS as the host's Backspace setting says, because that is what typing `reset` needs. With Shift, Alt, Ctrl or another modifier held, vtium sends `CSI 13 u`, `CSI 9 u` and `CSI 127 u` with the modifiers, the numbers the table gives these keys, so a program can tell Shift+Enter from Enter. Shift+Tab is `CSI 9 ; 2 u`, not `CSI Z`. Test: `a_push_of_1_keeps_enter_tab_and_backspace_unmodified`.
- **Caps Lock and NumLock appear in every escape code, and never turn a text key into one.** The specification says a lock that is on sets its bit, and that the lock modifiers are not reported for keys that make text. vtium reads that as: a key that would send text still sends text with a lock on, and a key sent as an escape code carries the locks' bits, so Escape with Caps Lock on is `CSI 27 ; 65 u` and Up is `CSI 1 ; 65 A`. Test: `a_push_of_1_sends_escape_alt_and_ctrl_keys_as_codes`.
- **DECCKM and the keypad modes change nothing while the flags are not 0.** The specification says that under flag 1 "all key events that do not generate text are represented in one of the following two forms", `CSI number ; modifier u` and `CSI 1 ; modifier` with a letter or `~`. SS3 is neither form, so vtium never sends SS3 under flag 1, and F1, F2 and F4 are `CSI P`, `CSI Q` and `CSI S`. Test: `a_push_of_1_sends_the_functional_keys_in_the_specifications_forms`.
- **The keypad's keys are keys of their own.** A keypad key that makes text, a digit, an operator, the decimal point, the separator, = or the space, sends its text while nothing but Shift and the locks are held, and is `CSI number u` with its own number from the table under any other modifier, so Ctrl+KP_1 is `CSI 57400 ; 5 u`. Every other keypad key is always `CSI number u`, Enter on the keypad `CSI 57414 u` included. The table lists KP_BEGIN as "1 E or 57427 ~"; vtium sends `CSI 57427 u`, in the form flag 1 gives every other keypad key, because `CSI 57427 ~` is neither of flag 1's forms and `CSI E` is Begin on the main keys. PF1 to PF4 are sent as F1 to F4, the keypad's Tab as Tab and its space with the code 32, since the table names none of them. Test: `a_push_of_1_sends_the_keypad_as_keys_of_its_own`.
- **Modifier keys send nothing, and so does a key the table does not name.** The specification reports the modifier keys only under flag 8, so Shift, Ctrl, Alt, Super, Hyper, Meta, AltGr, the level-five shift, Caps Lock and NumLock send nothing under flag 1. Scroll Lock, Print, Pause, Menu and the media keys xkbcommon names are ordinary keys with numbers in the table. A key that makes no text and that the table gives no number, such as Linefeed, Clear, Sys_Req, Find, Select and Begin on the main keys, sends nothing while the flags are not 0, as a key with no xterm encoding sends nothing while they are 0 (ADR-1005, D6). Test: `a_push_of_1_sends_the_functional_keys_in_the_specifications_forms`.
- **The key's code is the host's unshifted key where the host reports one.** Today vtium-platform reports no unshifted key (ADR-1005, D9, slice 3), so vtium-term takes the character the key made and lowers a letter's case, so Ctrl+Shift+A is 97, as the specification requires. A shifted symbol keeps the symbol the key made until the host reports the unshifted key, so Ctrl+Shift+2 on a US layout is `CSI 64 ; 6 u` where the specification wants 50. A key of a non-Latin layout is sent by its own character, so Ctrl+С on a Russian layout is `CSI 1089 ; 5 u`; the base layout key 99 comes with flag 4. Test: `a_push_of_1_sends_escape_alt_and_ctrl_keys_as_codes`.
- **These rulings follow the specification's text alone.** ADR-1005 settles an unclear case by running kitty as a program, but keys sent to kitty under Xvfb did not reach its window in the runs that record made, so no ruling above rests on kitty's behaviour. kitty's source code is GPL-licensed, and vtium never reads it.
- **The protocol is in every build, minspec included.** It costs each terminal two stacks of eight bytes. The minspec binary for x86_64, built with `make minspec`, measures 3,689,288 bytes before the first slice and 3,694,360 bytes after it, 5,072 bytes more, inside the 4 MiB budget (ADR-1005, D11).

### OSC strings and the Linux console's sequences

- **OSC 52 sets the selection, and its query gets no reply.** A set reaches the host through `Terminal::take_clipboard_write`, decoded, and the query is refused (ADR-1000, osc.52.read): the maintainer's decisions in vtium/vtium#39. Spec: ctlseqs, OSC 52, with three differences. Data that is not base64 is dropped where ctlseqs clears the selection, as RFC 4648, 3.3 has a decoder "reject the encoded data"; the decoding is strict but for the padding, which may be left off, as Ghostty takes it. `c`, `p`, `s` and the empty list, ctlseqs's `s 0`, set vtium's one selection, and `q` and the cut buffers `0` to `7`, which name none of vtium's, set nothing; xterm sets each it has. A set past `CLIPBOARD_MAX`, or one the parser truncated, is dropped whole. Tests: `tests/clipboard.rs`, and `a_truncated_osc_52_sets_nothing` in `src/terminal/clipboard.rs`.
- **OSC 50 asks for a font set, and its query names the one in use.** A name reaches the host through `Terminal::take_font_request`, and the query, `OSC 50 ; ?`, is answered `OSC 50 ; NAME`, ended as it was asked, with the set the host named with `set_font_name`, never the name asked for; until the host turns them on with `set_font_ops`, both are ignored (ADR-1200 D5). The input call returns after a set asked for, so the host switches before a query that follows is answered. Spec: ctlseqs, OSC 50, and xterm's `allowFontOps`, with three differences. Pt names one of vtium's built sets, at most 64 characters of `A-Z a-z 0-9 . _ + -` not starting with `.` (ADR-1200 D1), where xterm takes an X font name or a TrueType face; anything else, a path or an X font name, is ignored. A Pt starting with `#`, xterm's font menu, absolute, relative and its query, is ignored: vtium has no menu. The query is answered only while font operations are on, as the set is. Tests: `tests/font.rs`.
- **OSC 8 links the cells printed while a link is open, and the link store holds each distinct link once.** Spec: Egmont Koblinger's "Hyperlinks in terminal emulators", with ADR-1002's bounds and one difference. A URI is 1 to 2,083 bytes and an id 1 to 250, VTE's and iTerm2's limits; a URI past its bound, with a byte outside 0x21 to 0x7E or with no RFC 3986 scheme closes the open link, and an id past its bound is ignored. The specification allows the space, 0x20, which vtium refuses, as a URI percent-encodes it: with every byte printable ASCII, the overlay that shows a URI shows what a copy carries. A link with the same id and URI takes the store's entry again; past `LINKS_BYTES_MAX` the store sweeps (Bounds). RIS and a switch of screens end the open link, as VTE ends it, and erased cells take none. Tests: `tests/links.rs`.
- **OSC 133 marks a prompt, its input and its output on their lines.** Spec: the semantic prompts proposal (FinalTerm's sequences). A and N do a fresh line, a carriage return and a line feed when the cursor is not in the first column, and mark the cursor's line a prompt; L does the fresh line alone; B and I mark the input, C the output; P, D and the options are taken and change nothing, and D's exit status is not kept. kitty and foot mark A where the cursor is, with no fresh line. ED 2, ED 3 and RIS clear the marks of the lines they erase, and EL and the partial erases keep them, since shells redraw a prompt with them. A rewrap gives a rejoined line the marks of all its rows (ADR-1003). Tests: `tests/marks.rs`.
- **DECSCA sets the protection DECSED, DECSEL and DECSERA leave.** Spec: ctlseqs, DECSCA: 1 protects, 0, 2 and the default do not; SGR 0 keeps it, DECSTR and RIS end it, as in xterm. Test: `decsca_protects_printed_cells_from_decsed_and_decsel`.
- **The Linux console's blank sequences reach the host.** `CSI 9 ; n ]` hands the host a blank timeout of n minutes, at most 60 as the Linux console caps it, with 0 or none turning blanking off, and a wake, as the Linux console wakes the screen as it sets the timeout; `CSI 13 ]` hands it a wake, and RIS the host's own timeout again (`Terminal::take_blank_request`). `CSI 14 ; n ]`, the VESA powerdown interval, is ignored, since vtium has one DPMS step (the maintainer's decision on vtium/vtium#79). Spec: console_codes(4). Tests: `tests/blank.rs`.

### Resets

- **DECSTR resets the mouse modes and mode 1004, as RIS does;** xterm resets them on RIS only. The maintainer's decision, recorded in each row's spec column. Test: `decstr_returns_the_modes_to_their_defaults`.
- **DECSTR keeps the tab stops, and RIS sets a stop every eighth column.** Spec: the VT510 manual, DECSTR, whose table of what a soft reset changes leaves out the tab stops; ctlseqs names DECSTR without saying what it resets. Test: `decstr_keeps_the_tab_stops_and_ris_resets_them`.
- **DECSTR sets autowrap (DECAWM), where the VT510 manual's DECSTR table gives "No autowrap".** The reason: xterm's terminfo entries send DECSTR in `is2` and `rs2` and declare `am`, the capability that says the terminal wraps. If vtium followed the table, `tput init` or `tput reset` under such an entry would leave the terminal without autowrap while its terminfo entry says it wraps. Test: `decstr_leaves_autowrap_on`.
- **RIS clears what XTSAVE saved and returns XTSHIFTESCAPE to 0, and DECSTR keeps both.** xterm keeps both through RIS and DECSTR (`ReallyReset` in xterm's `charproc.c` touches neither); vtium splits them as Ghostty does, the maintainer's ruling recorded in ADR-1000. The reason: RIS returns the terminal to its initial state, which holds no saved modes. Tests: `decstr_keeps_what_xtsave_saved_and_ris_clears_it`, `xtshiftescape_says_whether_shift_reaches_the_mouse_reports`.
- **`Terminal::reset`, the host's reset, leaves the terminal as RIS does, and drops what the program left in flight.** vtium's reset key calls it (ADR-1005 D10). It also drops the replies queued for the host, since the program that asked for them is being reset, abandons a request out to the image helper as `Terminal::abandon_image_request` does, so the terminal takes output again at once, and returns the parser to its ground state, so a sequence the program had half written is dropped as the ESC of RIS would drop it. Tests: `a_host_reset_leaves_the_terminal_as_ris_does`, `a_host_reset_abandons_the_request_out`.

### The scrollback

- **The scrollback holds at most `sb-memory` bytes as well as `sb-size` lines,** and drops its oldest lines past either. Each line costs its struct and its heap, waiting or rewrapped; a line that alone passes the budget does not enter. No specification covers a scrollback's size. The reason: the program sizes the lines, so a count of them bounds no memory. A row whose every cell has its own style, link and cluster, concentrated by a rewrap, costs up to 7,560 bytes at 170 columns, and up to 10,450 in a build with images, where a kitty placeholder cluster of 17 bytes may fill each cell its budget leaves; 1,000 of them pass the minspec peak. When the configuration does not set `sb-memory`, the budget follows the grid: it is what the 16 MiB minspec peak leaves after the rest of the process (`MINSPEC_REST`), the two screens with every row at its worst (`screens_bytes_max`, each row at `Line::bytes_max`) and a full link store, rounded down to 64 KiB (`sb_memory_default`), and each resize works it out again from the rows the screens hold, dropping the oldest lines past it. A grid larger than the 170 by 48 minspec grid keeps that grid's value, 5,242,880 bytes, or 4,653,056 in a build with images, and never less: its console uses more memory than the minspec peak by design, and the default does not grow with the display or the machine, so a user on a capable machine who wants a deeper scrollback raises `sb-memory`. At the minspec grid the default holds about 693 such rows (445 with images) while 1,000 full-width shell lines take about 29% of it (33% with images), and at 80 by 30 and smaller it holds 1,000 of the worst rows. `sb-memory` sets a fixed budget, fenced from `SB_MEMORY_MIN` to the lesser of `SB_MEMORY_MAX` and a quarter of the machine's memory (`sb_memory`), and `sb-size` can only lower what it allows. Tests: `the_scrollback_drops_its_oldest_lines_past_its_byte_budget`, `the_byte_budget_drops_hostile_lines_and_keeps_a_thousand_plain_ones` (`tests/flood.rs`), `the_byte_budget_holds_through_resizes_and_its_count_is_exact` (`tests/reflow.rs`) and `the_worst_default_scrollback_fits_the_minspec_peak` and `the_scrollback_budget_follows_the_grid` (`tests/memory.rs`).

## Bounds

The byte stream comes from an untrusted program, so nothing it sends grows memory without a bound. A screen is at most `COLUMNS_MAX` by `ROWS_MAX`, and its size comes from the display and the font. Every path that writes, moves or erases cells gives back the cluster bytes it drops through the line, so a line's arena keeps its budget on each of them (`tests/flood.rs`). A rectangle operation is linear in the rectangle's cells, and DECCRA holds one source line at a time. REP prints at most `PARAM_VALUE_CAP` copies (vtium-parse), as printed text does. The replies and input reports waiting for the host take at most `REPLIES_PENDING_MAX` bytes, and the replies of the byte that reaches the bound, before the input call returns for the host to take them; at the bound the input call takes nothing. An OSC 4 list, which one string at vtium-parse's `OSC_MAX` can hold a quarter of a million queries in, skips its queries past the bound and still applies its sets, so what a string sets does not depend on when the host took the replies; OSC 21's one reply skips the queries past the bound the same way. A color specification is read at most four hex digits a channel, into a color of 3 bytes. XTVERSION's text is at most `VERSION_MAX` bytes; XTGETTCAP's table holds at most `TERMCAP_ENTRIES_MAX` names of `TERMCAP_NAME_MAX` bytes and values of `TERMCAP_VALUE_MAX`, and one request is answered for `XTGETTCAP_NAMES` names in a reply of at most `XTGETTCAP_REPLY_MAX` bytes. DECRQSS keeps the request's first two bytes, as long as any it answers, and a longer one gets the error form. A line's style and link tables hold at most twice its cells before they compact, so a program that writes a new style or link into every cell, over and over, never grows them past that. A grapheme cluster keeps at most `CLUSTER_MAX` codepoints, and the clusters a line's cells name take at most `CLUSTER_BYTES_PER_CELL` bytes a cell of the width the line was written at, with the arena compacting at twice that; a line trimmed for the scrollback keeps what its cells name. In a build with images, a cluster that starts with U+10EEEE, the kitty graphics protocol's Unicode placeholder, sits outside that budget: it keeps the placeholder and its first `PLACEHOLDER_MARKS` codepoints, at most 17 bytes, wherever it starts in its line, and the line counts the cells that name one and compacts its arena at twice its budget and 17 bytes for each of them (ADR-1001, "Placeholder cells"), so a row that `write` fills holds at most `Line::bytes_max`, 128 + 90 bytes a cell: 15,428 bytes at 170 columns, where a build without images holds 128 + 56, 9,648 bytes. A rewrap moves each cluster with its cell, so a rewrapped row may name more than its own cells' budget: a logical line of `n` rows names at most the budget of `2n + 1` rows of the widest screen, and the scrollback and the screen together at most twice the widest screen's budget a row and what a last row's few cells hold past 6 bytes each, `CLUSTER_MAX` bounding a cell: 3,012 bytes a row at 170 columns, 2.95 times the 1,020 ADR-1001 took, and with images placeholder clusters add up to 17 bytes for each cell of the widest screen, 5,902 bytes a row in all. ADR-1001's worst case grows to 7,560 bytes a row, 10,450 with images: 7.21 MiB at 1,000 rows and 720.98 MiB at 100,000 rows, 9.97 MiB and 996.59 MiB with images (`tests/flood.rs` derives each figure and holds the scrollback to it; its shapes reach about 1.4 times the budget a row on average). Damage tracks at most `COLUMNS_MAX` by `ROWS_MAX` positions, and the scrollback keeps at most `sb-size` rows at the current width, a narrowing past it dropping the oldest for good, capped by default by the machine's memory or by a configured cap, at most `SB_SIZE_MAX`: the screen is made with the cap `sb_size_cap` gives, and clamps `sb-size` to it. It keeps at most `sb-memory` bytes too, `SB_MEMORY_DEFAULT` unless the configuration sets it inside its fence (The scrollback, above); a rewrap that the view or a drop reaches, whose rows may cost more than they did waiting, drops the oldest past it as a new line does. The selection an OSC 52 sets waits for the host at most `CLIPBOARD_MAX` bytes, decoded, one set at a time, and a set past it is checked by its length before anything is decoded or allocated. XTPUSHSGR keeps at most `SGR_STACK_MAX` pens and counts the pushes past it, and the color stack at most `COLOR_STACK_MAX` palettes (`tests/stacks.rs` floods both). The link store holds each distinct OSC 8 link once, charged its id and URI bytes and 64, at most `LINKS_BYTES_MAX` in all, and its heap stays at or under its charge (`the_stores_heap_stays_at_or_under_its_charge`). An open that would pass the bound sweeps it: every line's link table on both screens and in the scrollback, and the hovered link, are read once, and the links no line names are freed. With less than a quarter of the bound then free, the scrollback's oldest lines lose their links until a quarter is; when the screens alone hold the bound, the open is refused and the text prints unlinked. A sweep is linear in the lines held and runs at most once per quarter of the bound of links opened, added or refused, so an open that comes sooner and does not fit is refused unswept (`refused_opens_sweep_once_per_quarter_of_the_bound`); it runs only on a byte from the stream, so the store follows the bytes, fed whole or in pieces (`tests/links.rs`). With the store full, the two screens at their worst and every scrollback row at 7,560 bytes, the worst default scrollback at the minspec grid comes to 16,776,192 bytes (16.00 MiB) against the 16 MiB minspec peak, the byte budget keeping 693 of the 1,000 lines; with images, at 10,450 bytes a row, it comes to 16,741,248 bytes (15.97 MiB), keeping 445 (`the_worst_default_scrollback_fits_the_minspec_peak`).

## Reflow cost

A resize that changes the width rewraps at once the screen, the main screen's rows below it, the newest scrollback rows that could reach the screen, and every row from the view's top or the selection's start down to the screen. Of a logical line with no wide character, which breaks at multiples of the width, it takes only the newest rows it needs, and the line's head waits. It counts the rest of the scrollback at the new width from each line's length, since each line keeps a mark that it may hold a wide character, laying out cell by cell only a long line with one. The rest waits and rewraps when the view, a drop past `sb-size`, a taller screen or the newest line's wrap reaches it, or `Screen::rewrap_scrollback`; a drop cuts a long line with no wide character by length, unrewrapped. The result equals rewrapping everything at each resize, which `reflow_is_the_same_chunked_and_lazy_or_eager` holds over random streams, resizes, view moves and selections. In a build with images, a waiting logical line that an image placement is anchored on never waits at an older width: each resize rewraps it at once and carries its anchors, and a placement that a drop moves onto a waiting line has that line rewrapped first. A rewrap keeps an anchor past a row's text inside the row, so carrying it through each width in turn can land it in another column than carrying it once; this way it lands where rewrapping at each resize puts it. The rewrapping a resize does for anchors costs the anchored lines, not the scrollback's depth, but the resize still walks every waiting row to count the rows it takes at the new width. `tests/images_reflow.rs` holds it against an eager terminal and one that takes the whole scrollback at each resize. Past `sb-memory` the lines dropped follow what the rows cost as held, waiting or rewrapped, so there a lazy rewrap and an eager one may keep a different number of the oldest lines; the byte count stays exact and inside the budget either way (`the_byte_budget_holds_through_resizes_and_its_count_is_exact`). A resize to one column, where a wide character becomes an empty cell, rewraps the whole scrollback at once, and a waiting row that never rewraps is cut at each narrowing, so a row that waited loses what one rewrapped at each resize loses. A rewrap consumes each row as it builds the new ones, so it holds a row or two beyond its result.

Measured by `reflow_cost` (`cargo test --release -p vtium-term --test reflow reflow_cost -- --ignored --nocapture`) on the development machine, a Ryzen 9 9950X3D, 2026-10-28, with 100,000 scrollback rows (10,000 for a style a cell) behind a screen of 170 by 48, resizing to 169, back to 170 and to 100 columns:

| Scrollback | The resize | The rest of the scrollback | In all, a cell | Heap before and after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Full rows wrapped in pairs | 2.0-3.1 ms | 51-56 ms | 3.1-5.2 ns | 136 MB, 68 MB |
| One logical line spanning it | 1.8-2.5 ms | 41-69 ms | 2.6-4.2 ns | 136 MB, 80 MB |
| A shell's lines of 40 cells | 0.6-1.1 ms | 2.5 ms | 0.2 ns | 32 MB, 32 MB |
| Wrapped pairs, the view at the top | 55-85 ms | none | 3.4-5.0 ns | 136 MB, 68 MB |
| A style a cell, 10,000 rows | 0.2-0.7 ms | 4.7-6.0 ms | 2.9-5.2 ns | 14.5 MB, 7.8 MB |

The resize's own time is mostly the count, except with the view scrolled back, where every row below the view rewraps at once; keeping that to the rows the view shows needs a second stretch of waiting rows, which the operator decides on (ADR-1003's note on Cost). The rest is paid only when something reaches it. The minspec board's figures, the gate vtium/vtium#41 names, are not measured yet: no board is at hand, and tier 3's VM runs on a host core, so it measures memory, not a board's speed. ADR-1003 takes the board as ten times slower.

## Selection

The screen holds the selection (`src/screen/selection.rs`): from one cell to another (`select_start`, `select_to`), a word, the run of cells between spaces or empty cells (`select_word`), or a whole line (`select_line`), held by line identity as the screen scrolls. `selection_text` copies it, at most the text of the scrollback and the screen. The host drives it from the pointer and keeps the selection buffer; vtium's README describes how.

- **A line selection takes the whole line, and its copy ends with a line end.** Spec: xterm's `line` unit, with `cutNewline`, on by default, ending the copy with a line end; xterm leaves it off on the screen's last row, and vtium does not. Test: `a_line_selection_copies_the_row_and_its_end`.
- **A wrapped line is one line to the selection.** A word goes on across the wrap, a line selection takes each row of the line, and the copy has no line end where it wrapped. Spec: xterm, which tests `LINEWRAPPED` to extend a word or line selection across rows (`button.c`, `ComputeSelect`) and to leave out the line end (`SaveText`'s `eol`). The alternate screen's first row never continues the scrollback, which holds the main screen's lines. Test: `a_selection_follows_a_wrapped_line`.
- **A copied wide character brings no space.** No specification covers copying; the reason: the space for its second column is not text the program wrote. Test: `a_copied_wide_character_brings_no_space`.
- **A wide character is one character of a word,** so a double click on the `a` of `a中b` takes `a中b`. No specification covers word selection; the reason: the second column is part of the character. Test: `a_word_selection_takes_a_wide_character_whole`.
- **An empty cell is a space to a word selection.** An empty cell is one that no character was written to, such as a cell past the end of a row's text or a cell the cursor moved over. A double click on an empty cell selects nothing, as a double click on a space does, and an empty cell ends a word on its left as well as on its right. A wrapped row's empty cells at its end, which a wide character that wrapped whole leaves, are passed over, so the word goes on at the next row. Spec: xterm(1), CHARACTER CLASSES, whose default table puts NUL, the empty cell, in the class of the space. Test: `a_word_selection_on_an_empty_cell_selects_nothing`.

A line records that printing wrapped it at the right margin (`LineFlags::WRAPPED`), which the selection and reflow (ADR-1003) read. An edit that clears the row's last column ends the wrap, as xterm's does: EL 0, ECH, DCH, ED on the cursor's row from the cursor (`ClearRight`, `ScrnDeleteChar`), and ED on each row it clears whole (`ClearBufRows`). EL 2 ends it too, as Ghostty's `eraseLine` does, where xterm keeps it. DECSED ends it on each row it clears whole, and a row where it leaves a protected cell keeps it; xterm keeps every row's wrap under DECSED once DECSCA has protected a cell, and ends it as ED does otherwise (`do_erase_display`). EL 1, ICH and the cursor's row under ED 1 keep it. A line feed's scroll, and SU, move lines with their flags, into the scrollback too, and bring in unflagged lines. Tests: `an_edit_that_clears_the_last_column_ends_the_wrap`, `a_wrap_ends_where_its_next_row_is_gone`.

Four rules depart from xterm, which joins a line to text that merely took the place of its next row. IL, DL and SD (and RI at the top margin) end the wrap of each row they shift and of the row above them, as Ghostty's `insertLines` and `deleteLines` do in a region as wide as the screen, and a scroll ends the wrap of the row above the region; xterm moves the flags, so DL at a wrapped line's second row joins its first row to an unrelated one. An erase that clears the main screen's first row whole (ED 2, ED 1 below it, RIS, or any erase of that whole row), and IL, DL, SD or RI that replace that row, end the wrap of the scrollback's newest line, whose next row it was; xterm keeps it, so a line wrapped before a clear joins the prompt printed after it. The alternate screen's rows are no line's next row. A line that a scroll region starting below the first row pushes into the scrollback enters it unwrapped, since its next row stays in the region; xterm keeps the scrollback only for a region at the screen's top. DECERA, DECSERA, DECFRA and DECCRA end the wrap of each row whose last column they reach, as EL 0 does, and one that clears or writes the main screen's first row whole ends the scrollback's newest line's; xterm's rectangle operations never touch the wrap (`ScrnFillRectangle`, `ScrnWipeRectangle`, `ScrnCopyRectangle`). Tests: `a_wrap_ends_where_its_next_row_is_gone`, `a_rectangle_over_the_last_column_ends_the_wrap`.
