# vtium

The binary: the configuration and command line, the terminfo entry and the XTGETTCAP table generated from it, the session's command and environment, the respawn policy, the rules the terminal follows, and the seat and its heads wired to them through vtium-platform's loop. The rules are functions of what they are given, time included, so their tests need no VT, display or pty. The seat takes the time from the loop too: `tests/boot.rs` boots it with the fake VT, a video device in memory and a real child on a real pty.

## Heads

The seat runs the heads of `/etc/vtium/heads.conf` (ADR-1401), read only when root owns it and neither group nor others can write it, or the compiled-in configuration's one `console` head when the file is absent or `vtium.heads=builtin` is on the kernel command line, which leaves the file unread for that boot; a file that fails a check is logged, each error with its line, left as it is, and the compiled-in configuration runs in its place, its errors shown as vtium's notice on every head. A head is a terminal of its own: its session's pty, screen and scrollback, its grid, laid out over its displays by the scaled rule (see "The font"), its density, zoom and rotation, and its font, `font-name` and `font-size` from kmscon.conf with the head's profile and its own keys over them; a profile other than `default` names the frit of its name until profiles have files. Each display walks the rules alone when it comes and joins the head the first match names; a display a rule sends `dark`, or that no rule claims, is set off and logged, the latter with the rule that would give it the first head ready to paste. A DRM card sets such a display off before it announces it, a frame another master left included; a framebuffer's is set off after each wake-up and unblank. A head whose displays never appear starts no session, under `display-wait=forever` too. Each input device walks the rules alone when it comes: one a rule sends to a head delivers to that head alone, and one sent to `focus`, or that no rule claims, delivers to the head holding the focus; one sent to `ignore` is never opened, but a keyboard, opened for the rescue key alone. Under kmscon.conf's `mouse` off, the shipped rules send each pointer class to `ignore`, so no pointer opens, one whose node also has udev's key class included. The focus starts on the head of the boot display, the connector lit at start, else the first connected one on the boot card, then the other cards, in the kernel's order, else the first head in the file with a display, and starts again as each display lights until the first key reaching a head or the first focus chord, over every display lit since the start, one that left included, so a boot display lighting a turn late takes it and a display leaving never moves it; after, only the focus chord and the anti-lockout guard move it: Ctrl-Super-Up to the next head with a lit display, Ctrl-Super-Down to the previous, matched after the power keys and before the terminal's grabs, once a press. A pointer moves within the grid of the head it delivers to, with the modifiers of the keyboards delivering there. The terminal of each head a keyboard delivers to has mode 1004's focus, so several hold it at once. The blank, the selection buffer and the power keys are the seat's. Each head holds consoles, a session each, one shown on every display of the head: it starts the head file's `consoles` as its first display lights, at most `head-consoles-max`, 4 by default, and every head together at most `consoles-max`, 8 by default, each head keeping its first and the others opening in the file's order, so the share does not depend on which display comes first. Ctrl-Super-Return opens a console in the head the keyboard delivers to and shows it, within the caps; Ctrl-Super-Right and Ctrl-Super-Left show the next and the previous, wrapping; Ctrl-Super-Backspace closes the one shown, hanging its programs up, and shows the next; a head's last console is not closed. Each console keeps its own grid and zoom, and the head draws whole when the shown console changes. The font and the displays are the head's, so a hidden console's OSC 50 and blank sequences wait until it is shown, and its output after an OSC 50 waits with them; each console's OSC 52 selection is taken at once. A head's consoles run its head file's `login`, else kmscon.conf's. A console whose program exits while others stay open closes, and the next shows; the last console's exit follows the respawn policy, each console counting its own quick exits: under respawn its login comes back and no other head is touched; under `oneshot` the head ends with the notice "session ended"; and quick exits past the limit end that head alone, with a notice. vtium exits once every head has ended, a head whose display never lit counting as ended, since it has nothing to run: with status 1 when a head gave up, so the unit's getty takes the VT, and 0 under `oneshot`. Heads drawing one frit share its helper and its store, and a head drawing another frit has its own. The memory of every console and frit together is bounded (ADR-1401 D10; "Bounds"): each console's scrollback takes `sb-memory`, at most the most `sb-memory` may be on the machine, a quarter of its memory, divided by `consoles-max`, or by the heads when the head file declares more, since each head keeps its first console, so every console's together stay within that quarter; on a 512 MiB machine that is 128 MiB, and the default, 5,963,776 bytes a console, fits `consoles-max`'s 8 at 47,710,208 bytes, while 50 consoles take 2,684,354 bytes each. Each frit's glyph store takes the machine's store limit, 16 MiB below 2 GiB, divided by the heads, since a head draws one frit at a time, so every frit's store together stays within that limit: 16 MiB with one head, 1 MiB each with 16.

vtium draws its own over a head's frames, outside the terminal's cells, so no pty, scrollback or reply sees it (ADR-1401 D5, D7), with vtium-render's `Notice` and `Mark` in the head's font. A notice is a box of vtium's lines across the top of each of the head's displays: an invalid head file's errors on every head, no keyboard found by the start's scan on every head, the anti-lockout guard's and the rescue key's on the active head, and a head's notice that its session ended for good, under `oneshot` or after too many quick exits. Each says in plain words what happened. A head shows each notice once and the newest four (`NOTICES_MAX`), a repeat changing nothing. A notice ends on any key reaching its head, or 30 s after it is first drawn, but an ended head's, which stays and keeps no time; the frame beneath is drawn whole once it goes. The on-screen display (`src/osd.rs`, ADR-1401 D5, vtium/vtium#169) draws each head's status in the same places. Under `osd = minimal`, the default, it is a `Mark` in the head's top-right cell, filled on the active head and hollow on the others, shown on every head when the roaming chord or the anti-lockout guard moves the active head, and never in normal use. Under `osd = full` it is a line at the top of the box, such as `● desk · console 2 of 3`, shown on every head at those moves and on one head when it shows another console, its number of consoles changes, or its display returns after it had none. The box is drawn in the notice's red while a notice is in it, and in `Colors::STATUS`, light on dark, while the line is alone. Under `off` nothing of the status is drawn, and every notice still shows. The display stays for `osd-timeout`, 3 s by default, counted on the loop's clock from its first frame, or always under `never`. `focus-mark` is read as the older name of the two keys, where they are unset, with a line in the log naming them.

The guards run after every display and input added or removed, and after a rescue (ADR-1401 D9), each on unless the head file's `[guards]` turns it off, which the start logs. The display guard lights the boot display in the first head while the rules light no display, a `console` head where the file declares none, and lets it go dark once the rules light another; a DRM card announces a display the rules leave dark while vtium has none lit, so the guard can light it, and one the card set off at its bind while another was lit, never announced, the guard lets go for the card to scan again and announce once that one goes. A display the guard lit goes dark again by its power state. The anti-lockout guard, with a keyboard-class device present, sends every keyboard to the focus, opening an ignored one to deliver, while no keyboard reaches a head with a lit display, and moves the focus to the boot display's head when lit, else the first lit head, with a notice and the focus marks; each keyboard goes back to its rule once one reaches a lit head. `Ctrl-Alt-F<n>` for vtium's own VT, pressed on any keyboard, the ignored ones included, moves that keyboard to the focus for the rest of the boot, through an unplug for the newest 16 devices (`RESCUED_MAX`), with a notice on the focus's head, unless `rescue-key` is off or the head its rule names has `rescue = off`; nothing is written. A keyboard the rescue key moved reaches a lit head while the focus's head is lit.

Each head composes its frames on a render thread of its own (ADR-1401 D2), so one head's redraw delays neither another head's frames nor the loop, which keeps the input, the ptys, the hotplug, the VT's switches and the devices: their buffers, cursor planes, blanking and flips. A frame is the head's screen, taken by the loop, which the thread composes into the display's shadow buffer, a width by height by 4 bytes copy of the display; the loop then copies what changed into the display's buffer, with what the frames before it changed where the buffer is older, and presents it. The shadow costs one more buffer per display: 307,200 bytes at 320x240, 8,294,400 at 1920x1080 and 33,177,600 at 3840x2160. A display has one frame on its way at a time; a draw asked for meanwhile is taken from the screen as it is when the frame comes back, so no frame queues behind another. A frame that comes back once the VT is released or the displays blanked is not shown, and the display draws whole at the next acquire. Its thread starts at its first frame. The thread's stack is 128 KiB, and it is joined when its head is dropped, at a new head file and at the exit. At debug level, under the `render` target, each head logs once a second while it draws its frames, the time each took to compose and the time its copy took, mean and largest, for the VT review to weigh the copy against a buffer lent to the thread (vtium/vtium#156).

## The blank

After `dpms-timeout` with no activity every display powers down, and the next activity powers it up. Activity is the user's input: a key press, a pointer's move, a button or the wheel, and the wake-up after the user switches back to vtium's VT. A key release, a key with no keysym, a press partway through a compose sequence (a dead key's among them), the end of a pointer frame and the pointer's hide neither wake the displays nor restart the countdown, as the C reference decided (`src/input/input_uxkb.c:431-545`, `src/input/input_pointer.c:67`, `src/seat.c:578-590`). A change in which displays are attached counts too, for now: the last display going ends a blank in force and resets the idle clock, and the blank does not run while none is attached; the first display, and each that comes after none, starts the countdown afresh. A display coming or going among others does not count, nor does a re-take: a re-take sets the displays up again and keeps the blank and its timer as they were, and while blanked its set-up waits for the input that ends the blank (the operator's decision on vtium/vtium#21). The idle lock (vtium/vtium#87) will count the user's input alone.

A program commands the blank with the Linux console's sequences while `program-blank` is on, as it is by default (the operator's decisions on vtium/vtium#79). `CSI 9 ; n ]` sets the timeout to n minutes, at most 60, and 0 turns the blank off; it also wakes the displays, as on the Linux console. `CSI 13 ]` wakes them as a key does. The program's timeout lasts until a program changes it, and RIS returns it to `dpms-timeout`; a change restarts the countdown and leaves a blank in force. Output alone never wakes the displays, so a clock redrawing every second does not keep them lit. `setterm --blank N` writes `CSI 9` only under `TERM=linux`; under vtium's own `TERM` it refuses. A program cannot blank the displays at once: the Linux console's only way, the `TIOCLINUX` ioctl, has no counterpart on a pty.

From outside the session, `SIGRTMIN+1` blanks every display at once and `SIGRTMIN+2` wakes them as a key does, so a script or a power daemon acts without writing to the session's terminal (the operator's decisions on vtium/vtium#79). The blank is the timer's, and input, a program's `CSI 13 ]` or `SIGRTMIN+2` ends it; while the VT is another's, a blank sent is dropped, since its displays are not vtium's to blank. Neither `dpms-timeout=0` nor `no-program-blank` holds the signals back. `vtium blank --vt N` and `vtium wake --vt N` find the vtium on VT N, the process named `vtium` holding VT N's device (major 4, minor N) on a descriptor past its stdin, stdout and stderr, which may be the VT it was started from; they hold it by its pidfd, look again that it is still that vtium, and send it the signal through the pidfd, so a process that took its pid in between is never signalled; they exit 1 when none is found or the signal fails, and 2 for an argument they cannot take. Under systemd, `systemctl kill --kill-whom=main -s SIGRTMIN+1 kmsconvt@ttyN` sends it too; without `--kill-whom=main` the signal also reaches the login session, whose programs a real-time signal ends by default. Reading a process's descriptors and signalling it take the same right, so root and the user vtium runs as may send these, and a program on the terminal running as another user may not; a vtium started as root takes them from root alone. vtium's other signals are SIGTERM, SIGINT and SIGPIPE, and SIGUSR1 and SIGUSR2 for the VT's switches; the `SIGRTMIN+4` it sends PID 1 for `grab-power-off` is systemd's, never one vtium takes. The C reference had no blank from outside and used no real-time signal.

## No display

At start vtium waits `display-wait` (10 s by default) for a frame on a display's screen, counted from the first time its VT is shown, and with none by then it exits with status 1, so the unit's `OnFailure=getty@%i` takes the VT. A device whose displays are never set up, as when the modeset fails, counts as none, as does a display announced whose every frame fails to show. `display-wait=0` exits at once without one; `forever` waits for the first display however long it takes, for a machine whose only display is a USB adapter (DisplayLink, `udl`) plugged in at any time after boot. A frame reaches the screen at its flip, which the turn after its draw reads at the earliest, so the wait runs out at the end of a turn after its due time, and while a frame is on its way to a screen it waits up to 1 s more for the flip, the C reference's wait for a page flip (`src/video/drm_shared.c:834-860`); under `display-wait=0` a display set up when the VT is first shown is drawn, and its flip read, before the wait decides.

A head's command starts once one of its displays is set up, on the grid of every display of the head set up by then, so it sees no SIGWINCH as it starts. While the wait runs with none, the command waits, and if vtium exits it never starts. Under `forever` with none, it waits for the head's first display however long that takes.

Once a display has come, vtium never exits for want of one. When a head's last goes, it logs it and does what the head file's `on-loss` says for that head (ADR-1401 D8); a mirrored head keeps its sessions while one of its displays remains. Under `nohup`, the default, every head's and the compiled-in `console` head's, the head's sessions run on: the grid keeps the last display's size, so the program sees no SIGWINCH; the keys of the devices reaching the head still type into it, as the kernel console's tty takes them through an unplugged monitor; and the blank does not run (see "The blank"). Under `hangup` the head's sessions are hung up at once, as a serial line losing its carrier: every console's pty closes, its programs get SIGHUP, and the head follows the respawn policy, coming back with the consoles its file asks for under respawn and ending under `oneshot`; a hang-up is vtium's, so it counts as no quick exit, and an exit read in its turn is the hung-up session's. Under `hangup DURATION`, such as `hangup 30s`, they are hung up the same way once the time runs out with no display back, each head keeping its own timer, which wakes the loop. A head comes back only on a display its rules match, logged and drawn whole, and the grid takes its size where that differs, one SIGWINCH (the operator's decisions on vtium/vtium#21). A head whose display never lit receives no key, even from a keyboard its rule claims, though the focus chord, the seat's, acts from that keyboard too; and the focus never moves because a head's display left or came back, but by the anti-lockout guard (see "Heads").

## Selection, copy and paste

The pointer drives vtium-term's selection, and the seat keeps one selection buffer: what the user copied last, or what a program set last with OSC 52 while `clipboard-write` is on. The console has no system clipboard. The middle button and the keys `grab-paste` binds, Shift+Insert by default, paste the buffer through vtium-term's `Terminal::paste`, filtered, and bracketed while the program asks with mode 2004. The buffer holds at most the text of the scrollback and the screen, or vtium-term's `CLIPBOARD_MAX` bytes from one OSC 52.

## The font

vtium draws frits, generated at run time by the helper (ADR-1200, ADR-1301 D2), and its built-in fonts, and parses no font file: vtium-font's PSF loader is behind a feature the binary leaves out.

- **At start** the font is `font-name` when a file or the command line sets it, else vconsole's (`vconsole.font=` on the kernel command line over `FONT=` in `/etc/vconsole.conf`), else none, and the built-ins draw with no line; `font-engine=unifont` or `8x16` draws the built-ins too (ADR-1200 D9). A head's own `font-name`, else its profile's frit, wins over these (see "Heads"). A name is the frit `NAME.vtfrit` in `/etc/vtium/fonts`; a name no frit bears becomes a one-source frit made on the fly, `source=file:PATH` for a path, as `font-engine=psf` gives one, `source=font:FAMILY` for an outline family, as `font-engine=freetype` or `pango` gives one, `source=vconsole` for vconsole's font, `source=kbd:NAME` for another name, which the helper is handed as text with no file, under the name it was given when that is a frit's name and as `generated` when it is not, as for a path or a family, since the helper refuses a request naming anything else (D1). A frit whose `cell=` or `size=` vtium cannot read draws the built-ins with one line naming the font and why. A source the helper cannot read gives the helper's own line, and the built-ins go on drawing.
- **A frit** is generated in the cell it states with `cell=WIDTHxHEIGHT`, in pixels, read as text before the first frame; else, when its sources are all bitmap fonts, the fonts' own cell; else, with an outline source and `size=`, that size in points (see "Sizes are points"); else the display's (#145). The fonts' own cell is the helper's to tell, since the console parses no font: the console asks for it before the first frame and waits at most 100 ms (`OWN_CELL_MS`), the one wait on the helper before a frame, and takes it at the largest whole multiple not over the display's automatic height, at least once, as the C reference scaled a PSF font (the operator's decision on #145, 2026-10-28). The helper answers the first source's cell when every other source's fits in it; an outline source, a source whose cell does not fit, a refusal or no answer in time leaves the display's cell, with one line saying why. An outline frit's `size=` is the size of its glyphs in the cell; a bitmap frit's is left aside. A configured `font-size` wins over the frit's cell and size (ADR-1200 D16); `font-size=0` does not. A cell vtium picks a height for, from `size=`, `font-size` or the display, is as wide as the built-in font at that height (D17); the display's height is `font-size=0`'s, the C reference's automatic rule with vtium/vtium#118's floor: 8x8 at 320x240, 8x16 at 640x480, 8x19 at 1366x768. The built-ins draw in the cell from the first frame: unifont at the largest whole factor that fits, else Unscii-8, centered in a larger cell and cropped to a smaller one. The helper generates for that cell and never moves it, so the grid resizes only at a zoom or, while the cell is the display's, a display hotplug. The frit's `glyph-source=` and `glyph-source-<range>=` lines are the font's sprite policy, and the configuration's keys override them (D1). The console starts `vtium-vitrify serve` on the first need, as `nobody` when vtium is root, under the operator's fill model (2026-10-28): the characters the screen shows, or newly shows, are asked for and answered first; while nothing is asked the helper fills each display cell's coverage, the screen's characters, then printable Latin and box drawing, then the rest, a waiting ask answered before its next batch. Each answer is checked for bounds and dimensions and for being what was asked, kept in memory for the boot, and redraws only the cells that show its characters; a character the frit lacks draws in the built-in font (vtium/vtium#141). The frits' stores together hold 16 MiB below 2 GiB of memory, 64 MiB from 2 GiB and 128 MiB from 4 GiB, each frit's that limit divided by the heads (see "Heads"): a fill stops at its store's limit, and an ask past it evicts the sets least recently on the screen, which are asked for again when the screen shows them. A helper that answers what is not a frame or not asked, or fails, is given up for the boot and logged once, and the built-ins go on drawing; one that crashes, reaches its CPU limit, gives no answer for 10 s (`ANSWER_MS`), leaves an ask unanswered for 60 s (`REQUEST_MS`) or exits with work outstanding is started again, at most 3 times a boot (`RESTARTS_MAX`). The console reads the kernel's font from its VT once at start, before the VT turns graphical, and hands it to the helper with each request for a frit's `console:` source (D8).
- **The built-ins** are Unscii-8 for cells under 16 pixels and unifont from 16, but in a zh, ja or ko locale, whose characters Unscii-8 lacks, unifont at every size (D7). The locale is the first of `LC_ALL`, `LC_CTYPE` and `LANG` vtium starts with.
- **Sizes are points,** 1/72 inch (the operator's decision on #145, 2026-10-28): `font-size` and an outline frit's `size=` draw at points × DPI ÷ 72 pixels, to the nearest pixel, a half away from zero (`font::points_to_pixels`), at most vtium-font's `CELL_SIDE_MAX`. The density is `display-dpi`, with no line logged; else each display's, measured from the physical size it reports (vtium-platform's `Density`), down its rows as vtium draws it; else 96 DPI, with one warning for each display that reports none. Where displays differ, the lowest density converts, and a larger display draws larger by the layout rule; the team lead accepted this reading for the operator to confirm (2026-10-28). A configured `font-size` follows the displays' density until a zoom. `vtium check-config` opens no display, so it notes the density's source on standard output, outside its exit status, and the start logs each display's. Points do not apply to bitmap fonts: an all-bitmap frit takes its stated `cell=` or its fonts' own cell, and the built-ins step by their strikes. A `cell=` stays in pixels, an exact override.
- **`font-size`** picks the built-in's strike of its height in pixels, else the tallest under it scaled by the largest whole factor that fits. `font-size=0` picks it from the smallest display: the C reference's rule, at least 16, but where 16-pixel cells would leave fewer than 80 by 25 cells, the font's smallest height.
- **Zoom** steps as the C reference's font's size step did (D17): a frit with an outline source, `outline:` or `font:`, by 1 point, to the next whole point whose pixels at the density differ, each height but its stated cell's as wide as the built-in font there, down to the built-in's smallest; a bitmap frit by its stated or own cell's whole multiples; the built-ins, and a bitmap frit with no cell, stated or own, through Unscii-8's 8 and unifont's 16, 32 and on. Below the smallest it stops with one line naming the font and its height.
- **OSC 50,** under `allow-font-ops`, on by default, switches the live console to a frit in `/etc/vtium/fonts` or a built-in the name names, on a new grid with a pty resize, as a zoom does; a frit gets the helper and the store every head drawing it shares, and the built-ins draw what the helper has not yet sent. Anything else, a path, a name no frit bears or a frit whose cell cannot be read, leaves the font with one line saying why. Its query names the frit or built-in in use, a frit made on the fly by the name it was given. The output after a font asked for waits for the turn that switches, so a query written with it, as `vtium-vitrify set` writes it, names the new font. A switch changes the live console only; the configuration decides the font at the next start (D5).

## Bounds

A configuration file is untrusted input. The bounds of each key's value are in `docs/config.md`.

| Bound | Value | Past it |
|---|---|---|
| `FILE_MAX` | 65,536 bytes of a file | The rest is not read, and the file is reported |
| `LINE_MAX` | 8,192 bytes of a line | The line is ignored and reported |
| `TEXT_MAX` | 4,095 bytes of a text value or a `login` command, `PATH_MAX` less its NUL | The value is ignored and reported |
| `REPORTS_MAX` | 64 reports | The rest are counted in one report |
| A head's render thread | a stack of 128 KiB, 16 frames each way, a frame on its way per display | A frame the full queue refuses goes back to its display, which draws at the next turn; the thread waits while the loop has not taken what it composed |
| A display's damage | the last 3 frames' damage kept, 64 rectangles gathered | A buffer older than 3 frames takes the whole shadow, as does a gathering past 64 rectangles |
| A seat's name | letters, digits, `_` and `-`, as logind names seats, and a file name of at most 255 bytes | Its file is not read, and the name is reported |
| `head-consoles-max` | 4 consoles a head by default, 1 to 50 | The head file's `consoles` past it opens this many, with a warning; Ctrl-Super-Return opens none, with a line |
| `consoles-max` | 8 consoles every head together by default, 1 to 50 | Each head keeps its first; the others past it, in the file's order, do not open, with a warning; Ctrl-Super-Return opens none, with a line |
| `HEADS_MAX` | 16 heads a head file declares (vtium-config's bounds) | The file is invalid, and the shipped rules run |
| A console's scrollback | `sb-memory`, at most a quarter of the machine's memory divided by `consoles-max` or by the heads, whichever is more | Its oldest lines go, as past `sb-memory` |
| A frit's glyph store | the machine's store limit (16 MiB below 2 GiB, 64 MiB from 2 GiB, 128 MiB from 4 GiB) divided by the heads | A fill stops at it, as at the store's limit |

## Departures

Each was the C reference's behaviour at kmscon `b1f7a7c5`.

- **A file is read whole up to `FILE_MAX`,** where the C reference read any length into memory (`src/misc/conf.c:533-590`).
- **Only a regular file is read.** A FIFO, a device or a directory at a configuration file's path is reported and skipped, and the file is opened without blocking; the C reference opened any path that existed (`src/misc/conf.c:542-556`), so a FIFO with no writer held the start forever.
- **A value that cannot be read is reported and the rest of the file is read.** The C reference stopped reading the file at a value its parser refused, such as `render-timer=fast` (`src/misc/conf.c:486-490, 504-512`), and read numbers with `atoi`, so `font-size=12px` was 12, `sb-size=abc` 0 and `sb-size=-1` 4294967295. vtium takes digits only, clamps a number to its key's bound, and reports both.
- **An unknown `palette` name is reported, and the value a lower source set stands.** The C reference drew `legacy` for any name it did not know (`src/tsm/tsm-vte.c:453-478` at libtsm eeaf2931).
- **A seat's file that cannot be read is reported and skipped;** the C reference failed the seat (`src/seat.c:786-790`).
- **Each option name is matched whole.** The C reference's `getopt_long` took any unique prefix (`--lo` for `--login`).
- **A command-line word that is no option refuses the start, with `--login` too.** The C reference's `getopt_long` moved it to the end of the arguments, so with `--login` it joined the command, and without it the C reference refused to start (`src/misc/conf.c:310-313`). `check-config` is the one such word vtium takes, wherever it stands before `--`.
- **A mode line is read whole:** a size is digits, each side 1 to 65535, and a name at most 63 bytes; anything else is reported. The C reference read it with `sscanf`, which took a leading number and ignored the rest (`src/config.c:564-617`).
- **`no-` turns off a switch only.** The C reference read `no-font-size=12` as `font-size=12` (`src/misc/conf.c:318-341`).
- **`configdir` is set on the command line only.** The C reference let `kmscon.conf` set the directory the seat's file was read from.
- **A seat's name is checked before it names a file;** the C reference formatted any name into the path (`src/config.c:1571`).
- **The respawn limit times each start from that start, on the monotonic clock.** The C reference recorded a start only when the exit before it was quick, in whole seconds of the wall clock (`src/misc/pty.c:747-767`), so after one slow restart a command that failed at once restarted without end. A slow exit after quick restarts now starts the command again with the count reset, where the C reference exited at the next exit whatever its timing.
- **The font fallback is tried once,** then the error is returned; the C reference retried for as long as the lookup failed (aaronsb/kmscon#63).
- **`font-engine=psf` with a path, a kbd name, or a family under `font-engine=freetype` or `pango`, draws a one-source frit made on the fly,** generated in the helper; the C reference read the font at start in its root process (`src/font/font_psf.c`, `font_freetype.c`, `font_pango.c`). An unset `font-name` takes vconsole's font, which the C reference never read. Source: ADR-1200 D9, the operator's answer of 2026-10-28. ## C notes: glyph arrival and damage

How the C reference's flow drew, as the logical reference for vtium's damage path, at libtsm `eeaf293` and kmscon `b1f7a7c5`:

- libtsm's screen stamped each cell and each line with an `age` from the screen's counter `age_cnt` as it changed (`src/tsm/tsm-screen.c:114-127, 176, 544-552`), raised the counter once a frame (`screen_inc_age`, `src/tsm/libtsm-int.h:546-550`), and on the counter's wrap set every age to 1 and raised `age_reset` (`screen_age_wrap`, `src/tsm/tsm-screen.c:214-236`). The draw took a cell's age as the largest of its own, its line's and the screen's, or 0 under `age_reset` (`src/tsm/tsm-render.c:284-292`), and the renderer drew the cells newer than the age its buffer held.
- kmscon's text layer emptied a buffer's ages with `kmscon_text_redraw_all` (`src/render/text.h:214-217`), so its next draw marked every cell changed, on each font set and unset (`kmscon_text_unset`, `src/render/text.c:322`, which `kmscon_text_set` calls first), each resize (`src/render/text.c:351`), each rotation (`src/render/text.c:432`), a switch of screen and a failed draw or swap (`src/render/text.c:751, 766, 864`), and in bbulk for two frames after the default or decoration colors changed (`src/render/bbulk.c:1797-1810`).

- `soft-cursor`, off by default, has the renderer draw the pointer on every display and sets no cursor plane up (`src/config.c:1387`, `src/terminal.c:1269-1270`). Test: `soft_cursor_draws_the_pointer_in_software`.
- `grab-reboot` is unbound by default (`src/config.c:1409`). Bound, it is matched after the VT's keys and before the terminal's grabs, only while the VT is ours, and leaves the selection; it asks init with `SIGINT` while `/proc/sys/kernel/ctrl-alt-del` is 0 or cannot be read, and otherwise calls `sync()` and `reboot(RB_AUTOBOOT)` (`src/seat.c:479-513, 584-636`).
