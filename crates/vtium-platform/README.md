# vtium-platform

vtium's platform layer: everything that touches the kernel and the session. This crate holds the pty, the event loop, the displays (vtium/vtium#18), and the VT, the seat's devices through udev and the keyboards and pointers (vtium/vtium#19), all in one loop. Syscalls go through rustix (vtium/vtium#6), so descriptors are owned values and errors are `Result`s; DRM's ioctls go through `drm-ffi`, keys through libxkbcommon and the device monitor through libudev, the two C libraries at the crate's boundary. The crate denies `unsafe_code`, and each site that needs it carries an `#[expect]` with its reason and a SAFETY comment: the ioctls in `src/ioctl.rs` and the console font's in `src/vt/font.rs`, the mmap in `src/mmap.rs`, the close-on-exec marking in `src/fds.rs`, the real-time signals' set, mask, signalfd and `pidfd_send_signal` in `src/rt_signal.rs`, power-off's kill in `src/power.rs` and the pty's pre-exec hook. `src/rt_signal.rs` and `src/power.rs` call libc, an exception to vtium/vtium#6's rustix-only rule: rustix exposes no signalfd and no safe signal-mask, kill or pidfd signal call for real-time signals. The pty and the event loop face the child's output, so the crate also denies the determinism lints vtium-parse denies.

## Behaviour

The C reference's pty (`src/misc/pty.c`) is the floor. vtium opens the pty, starts the child as the leader of a new session with the pty as its controlling terminal, sets `VERASE` and the window size, reads the child's output edge-triggered at most `READS_PER_TURN` reads a turn, and lets the consumer take part of what is read, holding the rest for later turns so the loop serves its other sources in between. A pause stops reading and feeding until the resume, as the C reference's did for an image decode. What the user sends (keys, pastes, button reports) is queued whole and never stalls the reading. A pty closed and opened again starts with nothing queued: the queue belongs to the `Pty`, which is dropped on close.

The child starts with every signal at its default disposition and none blocked, so a vtium started in the background, which ignores SIGINT and SIGQUIT, and the loop's blocked signals pass nothing on to the session. The C reference emptied the mask and reset the signals below SIGSYS (pty.c:313-320); vtium resets every signal (see Departures). `tests/pty.rs` spawns a child from a parent that ignores SIGINT and blocks SIGTERM and reads the child's `/proc/<pid>/status`.

The child is watched through a pidfd, so SIGCHLD keeps its default disposition and the loop handles no signal for it. Signals the console handles go through one signalfd in `Loop`; the C reference's eloop used a signalfd the same way.

What the consumer is fed, and every reply it writes, depend only on the bytes the child sent (docs/architecture.md, "Determinism"). A stall delays them and drops nothing, as long as the consumer stops once `Replies::full` is true. `src/channel/tests.rs` holds the chunking-invariance property test, with random read sizes, random takes, a child that reads at random and pauses at random. `tests/pty.rs` repeats it through a real pty.

## Displays

The C reference's video layer (`src/video/`) is the floor. A video device is one DRM card (`Drm2d`) or one fbdev node (`Fbdev`), opened by the seat and handed over. Each lends its back buffer to the compositor as a `Canvas` in XRGB8888 with the buffer's age, takes the frame back with its damage through `swap`, and reports through events the owner drains once a turn: a display added, removed or to be redrawn whole, a frame flipped, the device awake or asleep.

- **drm2d** draws into two dumb buffers a display. A display is a connected connector with modes, driven through a free CRTC and, with atomic modesetting, its primary plane and cursor plane. Its mode is the configured `mode=WxH[@rate]`, or with `use-original-mode` the mode the console left, or the panel's preferred mode, or its first listed mode when none is preferred. A modeset of every display is tried as a test-only commit first; a failed test is tried again with each display's default mode. Where the displays online and the new ones still cannot be set together, the new ones are left off until the next hotplug and the displays online are set alone. A modeset makes new buffers only for a display whose mode needs them, and only once it takes; a display online keeps its buffers and commits the front one it shows. A swap is a non-blocking commit with a flip event, carrying the frame's damage as `FB_DAMAGE_CLIPS` where the primary plane takes them, and the hardware cursor on the cursor plane. A device with universal planes and no atomic commits, or neither, uses the legacy calls and presents whole frames (aaronsb/kmscon#84).
- **Flips.** A swap, a cursor commit or a modeset puts a flip in flight on the display, and a swap while it is in flight is `Busy`. Each flip in flight is bounded by `FLIP_WAIT_MS`: without its completion event by then, it is taken as done, logged once at warn level, and the display reports `Flipped`. An event that arrives later still reports `Flipped`; one that arrives after a newer commit was accepted is taken as that commit's, since drm-ffi's commit and page flip carry no user data of vtium's to tell them apart.
- **Sleep and wake-up.** `sleep` blanks the shown buffers, waits for any flip in flight, hides the cursor and overlay planes and drops DRM master, then reports `Asleep`, on which the VT release is acknowledged. `wake_up` takes master and sets every display again; while it fails, as while another process holds master, it is tried every `WAKE_RETRY_MS`, at most `WAKE_RETRIES` times, and then reports `WakeFailed`. A hotplug scan (`poll`) lets go of displays gone, binds new ones, and sets modes when anything is new; the last display's going drops master, and a display coming back takes it again. A failed hotplug, or a failed modeset that `unblank` runs, is tried again the same way, setting every display; these retries report no `Awake`, which the owner counts as user activity, and a failed one keeps DRM master and the displays online, so the blank still powers them down. Only a failed wake-up lets master go, as another process may hold it. After `WakeFailed`, the user's next key press or pointer action (a move, a button or the wheel), through `input`, tries again: asleep in the foreground, the wake-up, with every display on; awake, a modeset of every display, owed to `unblank` while blanked. The next hotplug tries the wake-up again too. Each try is retried the same way, so the tries stay bounded.
- **fbdev** keeps the mode it is given, in one buffer, at 32, 24 or 16 bits of true color, else at 1 bit where the device's visual is `FB_VISUAL_MONO01` or `FB_VISUAL_MONO10`. XRGB8888 is drawn straight into the framebuffer; any other layout is drawn into an XRGB8888 copy and converted where the damage says. A 1-bit device is packed a whole byte at a time from the copy, a pixel lit where its BT.601 luma is at least 128, the threshold the kernel's `drm_fb_xrgb8888_to_mono` gives a DRM panel; the first pixel of a byte is its low bit, or its high bit on a big-endian host, and the reverse under `FB_NONSTD_REV_PIX_IN_B`, as the kernel's own drawing orders them (`fb_draw.h`). The kernel's `FBINFO_FOREIGN_ENDIAN` is not visible to userspace, so a device that sets it packs in the wrong order. A timer at the refresh interval stands in for the flip event. It has no cursor plane.
- **A 1-bit or gray display** is reported by `levels`: 2 for an fbdev device at 1 bit, and for a card whose driver `src/display/depth.rs` lists, by the name `DRM_IOCTL_VERSION` gives and the device-tree node sysfs shows, its panel's levels, 2, 4 or 16 (vtium/vtium#135). No connector or plane property says a DRM panel is 1-bit or gray: its driver offers XRGB8888 and turns each frame into its levels itself, so the owner draws a 1-bit display in black and white and a gray one as color, which the panel converts. fbdev's gray visuals are not read: a gray framebuffer is refused as before.
- **Density** is reported by `density`, in dots per inch across and down, from the display's mode and its physical size: a DRM connector's `mm_width` and `mm_height`, which the kernel reads from the EDID, or where a side of those is 0 the image size in centimetres that the base block of the connector's EDID in sysfs states (`card<N>-<connector>/edid` under the card's directory, read with no card node opened), and fbdev's `width` and `height` in millimetres. A side of 0, fbdev's all ones, or a density outside `DPI_MIN` to `DPI_MAX`, 20 to 1,000, as a projector that reports 1 mm gives, is no physical size, and the density is `Density::ASSUMED`, 96 DPI, marked as not measured so the owner can say so once per display (the operator's decision on vtium/vtium#145). Neither read needs DRM master (ADR-1301 D3).
- **Identity.** `identity` names each display as the head file's rules match it (ADR-1401 D4): its connector's name (`DP-8`), its card's udev `ID_PATH` as the owner gives it through `set_card_path`, the monitor its EDID names (`EdidId`, `MAKER:PRODUCT:SERIAL`), and whether its connector was visible when the card was first scanned, its CRTC showing a mode and on, by `ACTIVE` on an atomic card and by the connector's DPMS on a legacy one, the mark of the boot display (D9). The EDID is the base block the density reads, read once: its PNP maker id, three letters of five bits each, a value past `Z` read as the character it lands on as the kernel reads it; its product code, shown as four hex digits; and its serial string, display descriptor `0xff`'s 13 bytes up to its line feed without the spaces that pad it, each byte that is no printable ASCII, a space within it included, and `#`, which starts the head file's comments, read as `_`, else its serial number where that is not 0, shown as eight hex digits, else nothing. A connector with no EDID to read has none.
- **Dark.** `set_dark` gives the predicate that leaves a display dark (ADR-1401 D3), asked of each connector's identity as the scan first finds it connected; unset, every display is lit. A connector left dark is announced as no display and listed by `dark`, in the card's connector order, until it is unplugged or let go by `release_dark`, which the head file's display guard asks for (D9); plugged back or let go, it is asked again at the next scan. Each modeset sets it off where another master left it on a CRTC, a wake-up's included, so a frame that master left goes dark: the connector leaves its CRTC, and a CRTC no display takes is turned off with every plane on it and every connector on it, an unplugged one included, taken off it. A CRTC that is not active shows nothing and stays out of the commit, which asks no flip event of it. A modeset the card refuses with the dark connectors set off is tried again without them, so a dark connector never costs the lit displays; the first such refusal is a warning naming them, later ones are logged at debug level. With every display dark, the scan takes DRM master for a commit that sets them off, then lets it go as with no display. The legacy calls turn the CRTC off through `Kms::disable_crtc`, whose default, which `Card` keeps, says `Unsupported`, logged as a warning, so a legacy card shows what another master left on a dark connector until `Card` implements it, a follow-up vtium/vtium#156 carries.
- **DPMS** is set through the connector's DPMS property or `FBIOBLANK`. On an atomic device it is the CRTC's `ACTIVE`, so every commit's `ACTIVE` follows the display's recorded DPMS. A wake-up, the VT acquired, counts as user activity (the operator's decision on vtium/vtium#18): its modeset's `ACTIVE=1` brings every display on, blanked ones included, each is recorded on and drawn whole, and fbdev unblanks. Where fbdev's `FBIOBLANK` fails to unblank, in `unblank` or a wake-up, it is tried again every `WAKE_RETRY_MS`, at most `WAKE_RETRIES` times, and then fbdev reports `WakeFailed`; its `input` tries the unblank again at the next key press or pointer action. A hotplug while the displays are on sets modes as above. A hotplug while blanked rescans the connectors and records the change, commits nothing and leaves a modeset owed: every display stays dark, one just plugged in included, until the input that ends the blank, whose `unblank` runs that modeset with every display on. A hotplug's modeset that waits on a flip in flight, and the retries of a failed hotplug, are owed the same way when the blank comes first. A modeset owed that fails is retried as a failed hotplug is, and the blank stays ended, since no later input comes to `unblank`. A display whose connector has no DPMS property stays lit through the blank but counts as blanked, so it lets no hotplug commit. Without a modeset owed, `unblank` powers each display on through its DPMS; where any display refuses, the rest are still powered on and a modeset is owed, retried the same way, with every display on. While asleep `unblank` does nothing, as the wake-up brings every display on. vtium so commits no modeset while blanked but a wake-up's, which ends the blank, and the recorded DPMS always matches what the hardware shows. A display just bound is set on. `Blank` powers the displays down after `dpms-timeout` with no key press or pointer action (600 s by default, `DPMS_TIMEOUT`) and up at the next one; the owner wires input and wake-ups to it, and a program's timeout and wake (vtium/vtium#79). A timeout change restarts the countdown and keeps a blank in force; `Blank::deadline` follows it, and is none while blanking is off.

### The frame contract

The displays and the renderer (vtium-render, vtium/vtium#16) share one contract, set by the steering session on 2026-10-27; vtium-render's README states its side in the same terms.

1. **A frame.** The owner takes the display's `Canvas`, which carries the back buffer and its age: 1 holds the previous presented-or-attempted frame drawn into this buffer's display, n the frame n back, 0 unknown. The renderer draws every cell changed in the last `age` frames, or the whole buffer at age 0, and returns the frame's damage, which the owner passes to `swap`.
2. **The renderer's history** is its own per-cell stamps. The display never assumes the renderer counts its buffers.
3. **Empty damage means nothing changed.** The owner does not swap a frame whose damage is empty. The whole screen is one rectangle covering it, sent with no clips. A swap with nothing to send, no damage, none kept from a failed swap and no cursor change, commits nothing and returns `Swapped::Nothing`. A cursor change is one from what the cursor plane shows, so a change undone before the swap is none; `need_commit` says whether a swap with no new damage has something to send. Damage passed without a canvas lent is ignored.
4. **The pointer.** `hardware_cursor` says whether a cursor plane carries the pointer: true once `setup_cursor` put an image on it, false on fbdev, on the legacy calls, where the plane refused the image, and once a commit with it failed and one without it took. There, `show_cursor`, `hide_cursor` and `set_cursor_offset` are followed by `swap(id, &[])`, a cursor-only commit that leaves the primary plane and the front buffer as they are, takes no canvas and returns `Swapped::Cursor`. Elsewhere the renderer draws the pointer in software.
5. **A failed swap** (`Busy`, `Failed` or `Off`) keeps the frame's damage and the cursor's state for the next swap that succeeds, which may have no new damage. The buffer the frame was drawn into holds it, so lent again it is age 1.
6. **Display power.** While a display is not on (DPMS standby, suspend or off) it lends no canvas, and a swap is `Off`. Powered on, by `set_dpms`, by `unblank` or by a wake-up's modeset, it reports every buffer at age 0, sends its next frame whole and emits `Redraw`.
7. **Flipped.** On `Event::Flipped` the owner may draw again; with nothing changed, rule 3 means no swap. `Redraw` follows a modeset that gave a display new buffers, a wake-up and a power-on; a display kept through a hotplug keeps its buffers' ages and gets no `Redraw`.

### What only tier 3 proves

The stand-in card follows the kernel where drm2d depends on it, and only a real KMS device in tier 3 (vtium/vtium#22) proves it does: the property reads of planes, CRTCs and connectors; the CRTC and connector lists GETRESOURCES reads on a primary node; dumb buffers created, mapped, added as framebuffers and committed on a primary node; `FB_DAMAGE_CLIPS`, the cursor plane and its hotspot properties, and the plane taken off its CRTC when the framebuffer it shows is removed; flip events and the CRTC each names; a cursor-only commit's event and its leaving the primary plane's damage untouched; `EACCES` under another master; the legacy `set_crtc` and page flip; DPMS and `ACTIVE` being one state, so that a blanked display comes on with a wake-up's modeset and stays dark through a hotplug, a sleeping monitor's hotplug pulse included, and a flip event refused on a CRTC that is off and stays off and on a commit that names no CRTC; a monitor past the card's bandwidth refused while the others stay set; a dark connector's CRTC turned off with its planes, the modeset checks the stand-in copies from the kernel (`drm_atomic_helper_check_modeset`, `drm_atomic_helper_check_plane_state`), the flip event of a CRTC turned off, and the connector's encoder naming the CRTC another master left it on; and the real fbdev ioctls and mapping.

## The VT

The C reference's VT layer (`src/uterm/vt*.c`) is the floor. A seat has one `Vt` backend: the kernel's VT (`KernelVt`), or the fake VT (`FakeVt`) on a system without `/dev/tty0`; libseat is the third (vtium/vtium#34). Each reports `VtEvent`s the owner drains once a turn.

- **Opening.** The VT is the configured one, or the one stderr is, or the first free one (`VT_OPENQRY`), opened read-write and never as the controlling terminal; its number is the device's minor, logged as `vt: using tty /dev/ttyN`. `KernelVt::new` first adds SIGUSR1 and SIGUSR2 to the loop's signalfd, which blocks them, so a switch before the loop runs waits there and ends nothing; then it puts the VT in graphics mode, takes its switching with `VT_PROCESS` (SIGUSR1 to acquire, SIGUSR2 to release), saves the keyboard mode (`K_UNICODE` in place of a `K_OFF`, `K_RAW` or `K_MEDIUMRAW` left by an earlier program) and sets `K_RAW`, then `K_OFF`, so keys reach only evdev; a refused `K_OFF` leaves `K_RAW` with a warning. It saves the tty's line settings and puts it in raw mode with no echo; a refusal leaves it as it was, with a warning. A VT shown at the open reports `Acquired` at the first drain.
- **The guard.** `KernelVt` owns the VT's state: dropping it puts back the tty's saved line settings, the saved keyboard mode, `VT_AUTO` and `KD_TEXT`, each only where it set it, so an open that fails half way, a setup that fails after the open, and a return from the loop restore them. Once the VT is put back, its drop sets SIGUSR1 and SIGUSR2 to be ignored, so the loop's signalfd, which unblocks them when it drops, lets no pending release end the process; the VT therefore drops before the `Loop`. The release and minspec profiles abort on a panic without running `Drop`; `KernelVt::restorer` gives a `Restore` holding its own handle on the VT, which restores once however often it runs; a run in a second thread waits for the first to finish before its hook returns and the process aborts. The panic hook that runs it and the SIGTERM and SIGINT handling that ends the loop are the binary's (vtium/vtium#21). Nothing in the process runs at SIGKILL, the OOM killer or a stack overflow; the kernel puts the VT back to text mode, its default keyboard mode and `VT_AUTO` at the next switch away, finding its `VT_PROCESS` owner gone (vt_ioctl.c, `reset_vc`), and a getty started on the VT with `TTYReset` does the same.
- **Release and acquire.** SIGUSR2 for this VT while it is ours reports `Release` and answers nothing. The owner puts the input and the displays to sleep and calls `acknowledge_release` once every display reported `Event::Asleep`, which a pending flip delays by at most `FLIP_WAIT_MS` with the loop running, or `refuse_release` to stay. When the kernel refuses the answer, as after another process set the VT's switching while the release waited, the release is gone, and vtium takes the VT again, `VT_PROCESS` included, so the next switch to it signals the acquire. On a VT still shown, an acknowledgement reports `Acquired` and a refusal `Retaken`; on one no longer shown, an acknowledgement reports nothing and a refusal reports `Release`, whose acknowledgement then decides the same way, so a switch back while the owner sleeps reports `Acquired` only at that acknowledgement. Either fails only when `VT_GETSTATE` fails too. A release while the VT is not ours is allowed at once. SIGUSR1 for this VT, while `VT_GETSTATE` shows it, reports `Acquired` and answers nothing, and while a release waits it is ignored; the owner wakes the input and the displays, and the wake-up counts as user activity. Signals for another VT are ignored.
- **Keys.** First in the key order, `Vt::key` takes Ctrl+Alt+F1 to F12 and `XF86Switch_VT_1` to 12 while the VT is ours and shown, and asks the kernel for that VT. Unless `VT_GETMODE` shows vtium's own `VT_PROCESS` (SIGUSR2 to release, SIGUSR1 to acquire), the key sets it first: the kernel drops a switch from a VT another process left in `VT_AUTO` and graphics mode, and sends another process's `VT_PROCESS` the release. vtium's own `VT_PROCESS` is left as it is, so a release not yet read still completes; another process that set `VT_PROCESS` with vtium's signals cannot be told from vtium. This VT's own key is taken and does nothing; `Vt::is_own_key` says a key is it, while the VT is ours and shown, for the rescue (ADR-1401 D9), and switches nothing. The fake VT takes Ctrl+Super+F12, which gives it up or takes it back. It reports no VT number and has no own key; one a test numbers (`FakeVt::with_number`) has the kernel VT's own key for that number while it is ours.
- **The tty.** vtium keeps its VT's tty silent and re-takes the VT if anything arrives on it: graphics mode, `VT_PROCESS`, the keyboard off and the tty silent again, each tried on its own so one refusal leaves the rest set, with the settings found at the open still the ones a drop puts back. `VT_PROCESS` is set only where it is not vtium's own, since `VT_SETMODE` would cancel a switch the kernel waits on, and the kernel waits on vtium only in vtium's own `VT_PROCESS`. A tty the open could not silence is left as it is, then and at every exit. What arrived is dropped (`TCIFLUSH`). A re-take while the VT is ours reports `VtEvent::Retaken`; one while a release waits reports it if the owner refuses the release, and one while the VT is not ours reports nothing, since the next `Acquired` sets everything up, unless the VT is shown with no acquire signal, which it then reports. The first re-take is logged as a warning, and so is the first after `RETAKE_QUIET_MS` with none; the others are counted at debug level. `Vt::restore` sets the same modes the same way. A hangup, or a flush that fails as on a hung-up tty, reports `Hangup` once, and every later switch fails with `EPIPE`.
- **The VT shown.** `KernelVt::new` watches `/sys/class/tty/tty0/active`, which names the VT the kernel shows and notifies on every switch, and reads it from its start after each notification (edge-triggered: sysfs reports the attribute readable at every poll). The kernel notifies before it signals an acquire, and whichever of the two the loop reads second changes nothing. This VT shown while not ours, as when another process set `VT_AUTO` while it was not ours, is taken again and reported as `Acquired`, with no input on the tty needed. Another VT shown while this one is ours, a switch the kernel made with no release, as under `VT_AUTO`, is reported as `Release`; the owner sleeps and acknowledges, and that acknowledgement decides as for any release the kernel no longer waits on, so the VT is taken again in the background and a switch back while the owner sleeps is acquired only at the acknowledgement. While a release waits, the answer decides. Several vtium processes, one per VT, each read the same attribute and act only for their own VT; several sessions in one process are vtium/vtium#82. The attribute exists only on the seat with kernel VTs, and the fake VT does not open it. If it cannot be opened, a warning is logged once and vtium runs without it, as the C reference did: a switch made with no signal then goes unseen until the next signal, key or input on the tty.
- **Switching.** `activate` asks for this VT unless it is shown. `leave`, at exit, asks for the VT that was shown at the open, unless this VT is not shown or was that one; while it is pending the loop serves SIGUSR2. `wait` says whether the VT is shown, and an `Acquired` or a `Hangup` ends the wait with the loop running (aaronsb/kmscon#250).
- **Not carried.** The C reference's `real_retry`, which retried a pending switch for 3 s, had no caller.

## Devices

The C reference's monitor (`src/uterm/monitor.c`) is the floor. `Udev` enumerates the `drm`, `graphics` and `input` subsystems at `scan` and then reads udev's monitor socket from the loop. A card named `cardN`, a framebuffer `fbN` and an `eventN` node with an input parent are kept when their `ID_SEAT` (an event node's from its parent input device) is the seat's, `seat0` when unset, and reported as `DeviceEvent::Added`; a node added twice is reported once. A card is primary when its PCI parent has `boot_vga` set, or when it is a platform display controller not on USB, and auxiliary on the `udl` driver; a framebuffer is DRM-backed unless its id names no DRM driver, auxiliary as `udlfb` and primary as `VESA VGA`. A `change` carrying `HOTPLUG=1` on a known card reports `Hotplug`, for `Drm2d::poll`; a known device whose `ID_SEAT` changes is removed and added again, and a `change` on an unknown one may add it. Without a udev daemon the scan still reads sysfs, every device is `seat0`'s, and no hotplug arrives.

An event node's `Device` carries its `InputIdentity`, what a head file's rules match it by (ADR-1401 D4), read without opening it: its classes, its `ID_PATH`, its `ID_VENDOR_ID`, `ID_MODEL_ID` and `ID_SERIAL_SHORT`, the unique id, vendor, product and name its parent input device shows in sysfs, and its udev properties for `udev:`. The properties the identity reads by name come first, then the rest in udev's order, at most `UDEV_PROPERTIES_MAX` of them and each at most `UDEV_PROPERTY_MAX` bytes; one past either bound is left out with a warning, never cut short. The classes are udev's `ID_INPUT_*` tags, each counting at the value `1`, so a hwdb entry setting one to `0` takes it away, wherever udev's `input_id` ran (`ID_INPUT=1`). Without that, vtium applies udev's rule to the capability bitmaps sysfs shows (`capabilities/ev`, `key`, `rel`, `abs` and `properties`): `switch` for `EV_SW`; `keyboard` for every key code 1 to 31, Escape through S, and `key` for any key code below the buttons or in the key ranges above them; and at most one kind of pointer, `accelerometer` by its property, else `tablet` by a pen, `touchpad` by a finger tool without `INPUT_PROP_DIRECT`, `mouse` by its buttons with relative axes or none, or with absolute axes and no touch, `touchscreen` by touch or `INPUT_PROP_DIRECT`, `joystick` by its buttons or axes; `pointingstick` by its property, and `tablet-pad` by its numbered buttons. `trackball` comes only from udev's hwdb. The rule is written from ADR-1401's account of udev's and the kernel's device types, and checked against udev's tags on every event node of the operator's machine (`the_evidence_devices_get_the_same_class_from_udev_tags_and_from_their_bits`). The machine has no tablet, tablet pad, joystick, touchscreen or accelerometer, so `tablet`, `tablet-pad`, `joystick`, `touchscreen` and `accelerometer` are unchecked until tier 3 compares the guest's emulated devices with `udevadm`. The bitmaps are read in the reading process's word width, as the kernel prints them to it: 32-bit words to a 32-bit vtium.

`listing::list` lists the seat's devices as a head file's rules see them for `vtium check-config` (ADR-1401 D7), from a sysfs root and a udev database root, `/sys` and `/run/udev/data` on the machine, and opens nothing under `/dev`. It keeps what the monitor keeps, by the same seat rule. An event node's identity is the monitor's `InputIdentity`, from its parent input device's sysfs attributes and its udev database entry (`c13:N`), with udev's classes where the entry has them, else the bitmaps' rule. Its properties are the ones sd-device, behind libudev, gives the start: `DEVPATH`; the `uevent`'s, with `DEVNAME` under `/dev/`; `SUBSYSTEM` and `DRIVER`; and from the entry `USEC_INITIALIZED` (`I:`), the `E:` properties, each replacing one of the same name, `DEVLINKS` (`S:`, each under `/dev/`, joined by spaces), and `TAGS` (`G:`) and `CURRENT_TAGS` (`Q:`) as `:a:b:`. sd-device joins the links and tags in a hash order that changes from process to process, so the start matches a glob over their order differently from run to run; the listing joins them in the entry's order. A card carries its entry's `ID_PATH` and is primary by the monitor's rule, read from the `boot_vga`, PCI, USB and platform parents sysfs shows. Its displays are its connectors whose `status` is `connected` and whose `modes` lists a mode, ordered by `connector_id`, then by name in natural order (`DP-2` before `DP-10`), each with the monitor its `edid` names; the start registers a card's displays in its own order, which can differ. A listed display is never lit at start, which takes opening the card. A framebuffer no DRM driver backs is listed by the monitor's rule, read from the id its sysfs `name` shows (`VESA VGA` primary), as the start's one display `fb`. A database entry or `uevent` is read to `UDEV_PROPERTIES_MAX` times `UDEV_PROPERTY_MAX` bytes, its lines past that left out whole.

## Input

The C reference's input (`src/input/`) is the floor. `Input` holds every keyboard and pointer of the seat. A device udev adds is opened and probed at once while awake, else at the next wake-up; it is a keyboard with any key from Escape to Mute, a pointer with relative axes and a left button, or absolute axes with touch or a left button, taken only with `mouse` on; anything else is dropped. A device added with a disposition (`add_with`, `set_disposition`), as a head file's rules place it, is taken by that instead: `Deliver` keeps it whatever it reports, a keyboard for any key; `Rescue` opens it to watch for the rescue key alone: `device_events` keeps only its presses of the VT's own key (`Vt::is_own_key`), marked `rescue`, and drops its other keys, its repeats and its pointer events, an ignored OTP token's characters included, and its modifiers are in no one else's; `Closed` never opens it. `events` drains every event but a watched device's; `device_events`, given the VT, drains each event with the device it came from. Wake-ups and sleeps are counted: the devices open at the first wake-up and close at the matching sleep, which stops the repeat. A device that ends, fails a read or hangs up is closed and kept, since a seat manager revokes devices on a switch, and opened again at the next wake-up; udev reports one unplugged. Each readable device is read 16 events at a time until it has none, at most `INPUT_READS_PER_TURN` reads a turn, and its events become `InputEvent`s the owner drains in order.

- **Keys.** The keymap is the `xkb-keymap` file, else the rules' names (`xkb-model`, `xkb-layout`, `xkb-variant`, `xkb-options`), else the system's default names, else the built-in keymap. The compose table is `xkb-compose-file`, else the locale's, found as locale(7) says without setting it, else compose is off. Each keyboard keeps its own xkb state and compose state. A key's keysyms are read, a press feeds compose, the state is updated and the keyboard's LEDs follow it; a press that composes nothing yet, cancels a sequence or completes one that makes a string and no keysym sends nothing, and a completed sequence sends its keysym alone. Only presses are sent, with the xkb key code, the ASCII keysym for shortcuts, Shift, Lock, Control, Alt and Super, the keysyms and their characters.
- **Repeat.** The kernel's repeat is ignored. A press of a key the keymap marks as repeating repeats from a loop timer after `xkb-repeat-delay` and then every `xkb-repeat-rate` milliseconds (250 and 50 by default, 999 at most), marked as a repeat, until its release or another repeating press; a modifier pressed or released while it repeats changes what repeats.
- **Waking.** A keyboard opened again starts a fresh xkb state that keeps the locked modifiers and layout and resets compose, so a key held across a VT switch does not stick; its LEDs are written again.
- **Pointer.** Relative motion moves the pointer within the screen the owner sets (`set_pointer_max`), or within its own head's screen (`set_pointer_max_of`). A touchscreen or a virtual machine's tablet puts it where its axes say, scaled to the screen; a touchpad moves it by the finger's motion from where it is, held at the edges. Buttons report press and release, the left one a double click within 500 ms of the last; the wheel reports its steps; each evdev frame of a pointer device ends with `Sync`, and the hide timeout without one, `POINTER_HIDE` unless the owner sets another or none (`InputConfig::with_pointer_hide`), reports `Hide`.
- **Modifiers and LEDs.** `mods` reads the modifiers held on every keyboard but those watched for the rescue key, and `mods_of` those of a set of devices, a head's. A program's DECLL reaches every keyboard but those watched for the rescue key through `set_leds`, and a set of devices through `set_leds_of`.

### What only tier 3 and the VT review prove

The stand-ins follow the kernel where the VT and the input depend on it, and only a real VT shows they do. Tier 3 (vtium/vtium#22) proves the VT ioctls and the switch between two vtium VTs: the release waiting for `Asleep`, the acquire's wake-up, and the log lines it reads. The operator's VT review proves what needs a person at the keyboard, its VT steps in `make vt-check GUIDE=vt`: keys reaching vtium and not the kernel console in `K_OFF`, Ctrl+Alt+Fn and the switch back at exit, the VT left in text mode with a working keyboard after vtium exits, a kill included, the keyboard LEDs, the pointer on a real mouse and touchpad, compose with the locale's table, and a USB keyboard plugged in and out while vtium runs.

A known limit for tier 3 to look at, read from the kernel's source and not observed: when vtium's VT was left in `VT_AUTO` and text mode and the kernel switches from it to another VT in text mode with no release, the VT switched to can stay black until the next switch. Neither VT is in graphics mode, so the kernel's fbdev emulation does not redraw it while vtium still holds DRM master, and vtium's sleep, on the `Release` the switch reports, blanks its buffers and drops master without restoring the console.

## Departures

- **The wake-up tries outlast a flip wait.** A DRM card that fails to wake is tried `WAKE_RETRIES` times, `WAKE_RETRY_MS` apart, which is twice `FLIP_WAIT_MS`: a switch with no handshake between two vtium processes leaves the one switched away from up to `FLIP_WAIT_MS` to sleep and drop DRM master, and the one switched to waits that out instead of reporting `Event::WakeFailed`. The C reference tried 20 times, 1 s in all (`MAX_VT_TIMEOUT_RETRIES`, drm_shared.c:51). Spec: vtium/vtium#19.
- **A card opened while its VT is not shown lets DRM master go.** vtium opens the seat's cards at start and on hotplug whichever VT is shown, and the kernel makes the first process to open a card no process holds master of its master. The seat puts a card opened while its VT is not shown, or not yet acquired, to sleep, and a card's sleep drops master whether or not it woke, so the vtium of the VT shown takes master; the acquire takes it back. A card opened for the VT shown is woken at once and keeps the master its open gave it. The C reference scanned the seat's devices only once its VT was shown and opened a video device only while its seat was awake (seat.c:1099-1104, 1135-1150, 1041-1045), so it never held a card for a VT not shown. Spec: vtium/vtium#139, the kernel's DRM master rules (`drm_auth.c`). Tests: `a_card_opened_while_the_vt_is_not_shown_lets_master_go_until_the_acquire`, `a_device_added_while_the_vt_is_not_shown_sleeps_until_the_acquire`.

- **The consumer stops at the reply that fills the queue, and no reply is dropped.** Once replies the child has not read reach `REPLY_HIGH`, `Replies::full` is true and the consumer returns after the sequence whose reply reached it; nothing more is read or fed until the replies drain below `REPLY_LOW`. The C reference set the same stall after its input callback returned, so the callback parsed the whole read and could pass `REPLY_MAX`, dropping replies, and whether it did depended on how reads split the stream (pty.c:474-517, 579-618). Spec: docs/architecture.md, "Determinism", chunking invariance. `REPLY_MAX` stays as a ceiling for a consumer that does not stop.
- **The child's every signal starts at its default disposition.** vtium resets each signal from 1 to the kernel's last, SIGKILL and SIGSTOP aside, before the exec. The C reference stopped below SIGSYS (pty.c:319-320), so an ignored SIGSYS or real-time signal reached the session. Spec: POSIX `exec`, under which an ignored signal stays ignored, and vtium/vtium#90.
- **The children hold none of the descriptors vtium inherited.** Before the pty's child and the sysfs suspend's shell are started, every descriptor above stdio is marked close-on-exec through `cloexec_above_stdio`, those vtium's parent passed down without the flag included, so each child holds only its stdio. The mark is made in the parent before the fork, as listing `/proc/self/fd` where `close_range` cannot set it allocates, which the hook between fork and exec may not; it holds because vtium starts both children from its one thread, so nothing opens a descriptor between the mark and the fork. The C reference's pty child called `execve` closing nothing (pty.c:252-297), and only its own opens were close-on-exec, so a descriptor its parent leaked reached the login session. The suspend shell also starts with every signal at its default disposition and none blocked, as the pty's child does, though the loop blocks the signals it reads, a vtium started in the background ignores SIGINT and SIGQUIT, and an exec keeps both. Spec: vtium/vtium#133. Tests: `the_child_holds_only_the_pty`, `the_sysfs_shell_holds_only_its_stdio_with_default_signals`.
- **Pointer motion is coalesced for a child that does not read.** From `MOTION_COALESCE_AT` bytes queued, a motion report replaces the motion reports queued after the last other write; button reports, keys and replies are queued whole and keep their order. The C reference queued every motion report, so a child that enabled any-motion tracking (mode 1003) and never read grew the queue for as long as the pointer moved (aaronsb/kmscon#247). Spec: vtium/vtium#20.
- **The child's exit reaches the consumer after the output it left, in bounded time.** The exit is told once a read after it finds nothing more, or after `EXIT_FEED_MAX` bytes more than the pty holds, so a process the child started that ignores the hangup and writes on cannot hold it back; the host then closes the pty and drops the rest. Replies written after the exit are dropped and never stall the reading. The C reference told the exit at the child's reaping and closed the pty with the child's own output still unread (pty.c:737-768, 830-848; terminal.c:1856-1868), so how much of it the screen showed depended on timing. FIONREAD cannot count what the child left: on Linux 7.2 it reports the line discipline's 4 KiB while the flip buffers hold 16 KiB more. Spec: docs/architecture.md, "Determinism", chunking invariance.

- **Nothing waits by blocking the loop, and every flip in flight is bounded.** A modeset or a sleep that finds a page flip in flight waits for the flip's completion event with the loop running; the VT release is acknowledged on `Asleep`. Modesets are committed non-blocking with a flip event. Every flip in flight, a swap's included, is taken as done after `FLIP_WAIT_MS` without its event. The C reference polled the card for up to 1 s with its loop stopped, committed modesets blocking, and bounded no swap's flip, so a lost event left the display swapping for good (drm_shared.c:834-859, 861-937, 1089-1098). Spec: the operator's decision on vtium/vtium#18, 1 s for flip waits; `lib.rs`, a loop that never blocks.
- **A failed swap is not a presented frame.** Its error reaches the caller, `EBUSY` as `SwapError::Busy`, the buffers stay as they were, and the frame's damage and the cursor's state go with the next swap that succeeds, bounded by `DAMAGE_CLIPS_MAX`. The buffer's age tells the compositor the back buffer holds the frame that failed. The C reference dropped the swap's result (video.c:305-313), so a failed or busy commit counted as presented, and freed the damage of a failed swap. Spec: aaronsb/kmscon#93; the kernel's `FB_DAMAGE_CLIPS`, damage since the plane's last update.
- **A commit the cursor plane fails gives the plane up.** A commit showing the cursor that fails, other than as busy, is sent again without it; when that takes, the cursor plane is given up and `hardware_cursor` turns false, so the owner draws the pointer, and when it fails too the cursor is kept. The C reference logged the failed commit and returned its error (drm_shared.c:1028-1060), and its next frame committed the same cursor state, so a plane that refused it kept the display frozen; it fell back to the software pointer only when setting the cursor up failed (terminal.c:318-325). No specification covers it. Tests: `a_commit_the_cursor_plane_fails_is_sent_without_it`, `a_cursor_only_commit_that_fails_gives_the_plane_up`, `a_commit_that_fails_either_way_keeps_the_cursor`.
- **An image larger than the cursor plane is refused, not cut.** `setup_cursor` refuses an image wider or taller than the plane, at most `CURSOR_MAX` a side, as `Unsupported`, so the owner draws it whole in software. The C reference copied the part that fit (drm_shared.c:426-441). No specification covers it. Test: `an_image_larger_than_the_plane_is_refused`.
- **Damage clips go only with atomic commits.** A device with universal planes and no atomic commits presents whole frames through the legacy page flip and reports no damage support (aaronsb/kmscon#84, the operator's decision of 2026-10-26).
- **A monitor's power cycle skips the gone connector and takes master back.** A connector the kernel lists but no longer answers for is skipped, and a display that comes back after the last one went takes DRM master again before its modeset. An `EACCES` from the test-only commit, as while another process holds master, is logged at debug level and not tried again with default modes. The C reference dropped master when its last display went and committed without it when one came back, logging `test-only atomic commit failed, -13` at error level for each try and its default-mode retry (aaronsb/kmscon#237).
- **A display that cannot be set beside the others is left off, and the others keep running.** Where the modeset of the displays online and the new ones fails with the configured and the default modes, the new ones are left off until the next hotplug, with a warning, and the displays online are set alone. A failed retry of a hotplug keeps DRM master. After `WakeFailed`, the next key press, pointer action or hotplug tries again. The C reference returned the hotplug's error and retried the whole wake-up every 50 ms; each failed try dropped DRM master, and after 20 tries it stopped with master dropped and the old displays marked online but unable to commit, and a later hotplug did not take master back (drm_shared.c:1188-1210, 1455-1569, 1596-1604). A monitor past the card's bandwidth so took every display down. Spec: vtium/vtium#18, review finding 1; the kernel's atomic commits, all or nothing, so a failed modeset leaves the displays online as they were.
- **A modeset keeps what a running display shows.** A display online whose buffers fit its mode commits its front buffer, keeps both buffers and their ages, and is not asked to redraw; new buffers are made only for a display whose mode needs them, and given to it only once the commit takes. The C reference committed a running display's back buffer, which held the frame before last, until the redraw (drm2d_video.c:156-169).
- **Empty damage is nothing to send, and a pointer move commits the cursor plane alone.** The whole screen is a rectangle covering it. A swap with no damage, none kept, and no cursor change commits nothing; a cursor change alone is a commit of the cursor plane that leaves the primary plane as it is. The C reference sent a swap with no rectangles as the whole frame, with no clips (drm_shared.c:826-827), and swapped a frame that drew nothing to move the cursor, flipping to the back buffer (video.c:362-374, drm2d_video.c:212-225), so drivers that copy damage uploaded the whole frame for a pointer move. Spec: the frame contract above, rules 3 and 4; the kernel's `FB_DAMAGE_CLIPS`, where a plane updated with no clips is damaged whole.
- **The recorded DPMS matches the hardware: a wake-up brings every display on, and a hotplug while blanked waits for input.** Every atomic commit's `ACTIVE` follows the recorded DPMS, and a modeset records every display on, as its `ACTIVE=1` turns it on; while a display is not on it lends no canvas, and powered on it is drawn whole. A hotplug while blanked commits nothing and leaves its modeset to the input that ends the blank. A display just bound is set on. The C reference's seat turned every display on and reset the blank timer when it took the foreground (seat.c:179-253), which vtium keeps. But its modeset set `ACTIVE` to 1 (drm_shared.c:602) while it kept the DPMS it had recorded: a wake-up restored a blanked display's DPMS just before the modeset turned it on (drm_shared.c:1508-1513), and a hotplug while blanked, a sleeping monitor's pulse included, turned the blanked displays on while it still refused their swaps as off (drm_shared.c:1067-1068), so they showed a frozen frame until the next input. It read a display's DPMS from its connector when it bound it (drm_shared.c:1410). Spec: the operator's decision on vtium/vtium#18, a wake-up counts as user activity and a hotplug while blanked waits for input; the kernel's atomic DPMS, where the connector's DPMS property sets the CRTC's `ACTIVE`; the frame contract above, rule 6.
- **The default-mode retry sets the default mode.** The retry after a failed test commit makes a mode blob of each display's default mode, and buffers of its size. The C reference made a display's mode blob once, when it bound the display, so its retry sent the first attempt's mode again (drm_shared.c:994-1015 with 598 and 1421-1422), and an online display kept buffers of the first attempt's size (drm2d_video.c:156-160).
- **The first preferred mode is the default.** Where a panel marks more than one mode preferred, the first is taken; the C reference took the last (drm_shared.c:1357-1359). The kernel lists preferred modes first. With none preferred, the first listed mode is taken, as in the C reference (the operator's decision on vtium/vtium#5).
- **`use-original-mode` without the console's mode in the list takes the default mode.** The C reference set an empty mode (drm_shared.c:1376-1377).
- **A display whose connector has no DPMS property is treated as on.** The C reference refused every swap on it (drm_shared.c:1067-1068 with 789-790).
- **A switch key takes the VT's switching back.** A switch key on a VT another process left in `VT_AUTO`, in another process's `VT_PROCESS`, or in a mode the headers do not name sets vtium's `VT_PROCESS` before it asks for the VT, so the switch happens and its release is vtium's to answer. The C reference's key only asked for the VT (vt_real.c:484-530), which the kernel drops from a VT in `VT_AUTO` and graphics mode and hands to the other process in its `VT_PROCESS`, so the key was taken and nothing visible happened. Spec: the kernel's `set_console` (drivers/tty/vt/vt.c).
- **An acquire is not acknowledged.** The kernel needs no `VT_ACKACQ` after switching to a VT, and while a switch away is pending it takes any answer but a refusal as allowing it. The C reference acknowledged every acquire (vt_real.c:136), so with an acquire and a release pending together, as after a switch in and straight out, the signalfd's SIGUSR1 first let the switch away finish with the VT marked active, its release then dropped and the displays awake on another VT's screen. Spec: the kernel's `vt_reldisp` (drivers/tty/vt/vt_ioctl.c).
- **A keyboard left raw is restored as `K_UNICODE`.** A `K_RAW` or `K_MEDIUMRAW` keyboard found at the open, as a program that ended without its own restore leaves it, is restored as `K_UNICODE`, as a `K_OFF` one was in the C reference, so the console's keyboard works after vtium. The C reference restored a raw mode as it found it (vt_real.c:305-316). Spec: the operator's decision on vtium/vtium#19.
- **A setup that fails after the VT is opened puts it back.** The guard restores the keyboard mode, `VT_AUTO` and `KD_TEXT` on every return after the open, and a `Restore` on a panic. The C reference left the VT at `K_OFF`, `KD_GRAPHICS` and `VT_PROCESS`, with no keyboard and no text console, when the seat's setup failed after the open (aaronsb/kmscon#253). Spec: vtium/vtium#19.
- **An event node's seat is its parent input device's on every event.** The C reference read the parent's `ID_SEAT` when it added a node but the node's own at a `change` (monitor.c:442-468), which an event node rarely carries, so on any seat but `seat0` each `change` removed the node and added it again. Spec: the C reference's own add, which names the parent's seat.
- **Input is read for a bounded part of a turn.** A device is read at most `INPUT_READS_PER_TURN` times a turn, and the next turn reads on; the C reference read until a short read, so a device that kept the buffer full held the loop. Spec: `lib.rs`, a loop that never blocks.
- **The seat is checked before a device is opened.** A card or framebuffer of another seat is skipped before it is opened to read its flags; the C reference opened every seat's cards and framebuffers first (monitor.c:385-426). Spec: the C reference's own filter, which drops them either way.
- **An absolute axis whose range is one value moves nothing, and scaling cannot overflow.** The C reference divided by the axis's range, so a device reporting the same minimum and maximum stopped the console with a division by zero, and it scaled in 32 bits (input_pointer.c:123-124). Spec: `linux/input.h`, `struct input_absinfo`, which bounds no range.
- **A closed pointer holds no button.** A device closed, as the devices sleep for a VT switch, lets go of its pointer's held button, so a button released on another VT reports no drag when the pointer next moves, and a touchpad takes the finger's next position as the origin it moves from, so the pointer does not jump by the finger's travel on the other VT. The C reference kept the pressed button across its devices' sleep and wake-up (input.c:176-203), so a program tracking the pointer with mode 1002 was sent a drag at every move until the next button event. Spec: vtium/vtium#130; a key held across the switch, which a keyboard opened again also drops. Tests: `sleep_lets_go_of_a_held_button`, `a_touchpads_finger_takes_its_origin_afresh_after_a_sleep`.
- **A keyboard's frame is no pointer frame.** Only a device with a pointer ends a pointer frame with `Sync` and restarts the pointer's hide timeout, so typing does not keep the pointer from hiding. The C reference took every device's `EV_SYN`, a keyboard's after each key included, as a pointer frame (input.c:56-74, input_pointer.c:63-71), so the pointer never hid while the user typed and each key redrew every display. Spec: the operator's decision on vtium/vtium#102, the pointer hides after a time with no pointer event; tier 3's `pointer-plane` case found it. Test: `a_keyboards_frames_leave_the_pointers_hide_alone`.
- **A display's density is read, not fixed.** The owner converts point sizes to pixels at the display's density, 96 DPI where none is measured. The C reference fixed its DPI at 72, so a point size was a height in pixels (font.c:198-199, "Use a fixed DPI of 72"). Tests: `density.rs`'s, `the_density_comes_from_the_connectors_physical_size` and `the_density_comes_from_the_devices_physical_size`.
- **A display the rules leave dark is set off.** A connector the owner's predicate leaves dark is announced as no display, and a modeset turns off the CRTC another master left it on, with its planes. On a legacy card the CRTC is turned off only once `Card` implements `Kms::disable_crtc`, a follow-up vtium/vtium#156 carries; until then a warning names the connector. The C reference bound every connected connector with modes and lit it (drm_shared.c:1481-1490). Spec: ADR-1401 D3. Tests: `a_connector_the_policy_leaves_dark_is_committed_off_and_announced_as_no_display` and the dark tests beside it.
- **fbdev converts without dithering.** A layout other than XRGB8888 is converted by cutting each channel to its length. The C reference dithered, carrying the error from one drawn pixel to the next (fbdev_render.c), so a pixel's value depended on which pixels were drawn before it; the copy here gives the same pixels for the same frame. Spec: docs/architecture.md, "Determinism".

## Beyond the C reference

- **1-bit displays.** The C reference refused an fbdev device that was not true color at 32, 24 or 16 bits (fbdev_video.c:199-247) and knew nothing of a DRM panel's depth. vtium draws a 1-bit fbdev device and tells the owner which displays are 1-bit or gray, and how many levels each shows, the operator's decision on vtium/vtium#135. Tests: `a_1_bit_framebuffer_is_packed_where_damaged`, `mono01_sets_the_unlit_bits`, `reversed_pixels_put_the_first_in_the_high_bit`, `a_pixel_is_lit_by_its_luma_as_the_kernel_lights_it`, and `depth.rs`'s.
- **vtium watches the VT the kernel shows.** It follows tty0's `active` attribute, so a switch the kernel makes with no release or acquire signal still puts the displays and input to sleep or wakes them. The C reference learned of switches only from its signals and its tty. The operator's decision on vtium/vtium#19.
- **vtium keeps its VT's tty silent and re-takes the VT if anything arrives on it.** The tty is in raw mode with no echo while vtium holds it, and its line settings are put back on every exit the guard covers. The C reference left the tty's settings as it found them and flushed what it read. The operator's decision on vtium/vtium#19.

## Dependencies

- **rustix** for every syscall (vtium/vtium#6), with its `runtime` feature for the two calls it keeps there: the default signal dispositions and the empty signal mask the pty's child starts with. It is pinned at `=1.1.5`: rustix keeps that module unstable and publishes it under a name that changes between releases, so any update is made by hand, renaming the import in `src/pty.rs`.
- **calloop**, the event loop kmscon ADR-304 chose, <!-- adr-cite-ignore: kmscon's record, not one of vtium's --> pinned at 0.14.4: 0.14.5 needs Rust 1.86, and the workspace's `rust-version` is 1.85. Move to 0.14.5 or later when the workspace moves to 1.86.
- **drm-ffi** for DRM's ioctls, safe wrappers over `drm-sys`'s kernel structures (vtium/vtium#6 named the `drm` crate, whose wrappers these are). It was already in reach through `drm`, and taking it alone leaves out `drm`'s `bytemuck` derive and its proc-macro crates; it adds `drm-sys` and a second `linux-raw-sys`, 0.9.4, beside rustix's 0.12.1. Pinned at `=0.9.1`. Its `mode::get_property` given no buffers asks the kernel a second time with the property's counts and null buffers, which the kernel refuses with `EFAULT` for any property with values or enumerators, so `Card::properties` always gives it buffers; tier 3 saw every plane's type go unread without them. Its `mode::get_resources` reads twice and sets each list's length to the count the second read reports, past the list's buffer when a connector is plugged in between the reads, so `src/ioctl.rs` makes that call itself and `Card::resources` reads again while a count grows (vtium/vtium#89).
- **log** for the crate's messages, which the binary routes. It was already in the tree through calloop's `tracing`.
- **xkbcommon** for libxkbcommon, which the C reference used for keymaps, compose and key state. It is the binding Smithay's and the X.Org Rust bindings share, safe wrappers over the C library, built with its default features off, so neither its Wayland nor its X11 parts come in; it adds `xkeysym` and links `libxkbcommon.so` dynamically. Pinned at `=0.9.0`. libxkbcommon logs its own errors to stderr; the C reference routed them through its logger, which needs a C variadic callback the binding does not take.
- **udev** for libudev, which the C reference's monitor used, and which its minimal build still linked: Smithay's binding, adding `libudev-sys`, `io-lifetimes` and, to build, `pkg-config`; it links `libudev.so` dynamically, a library of its own and not libsystemd (ADR-1400's observable). Pinned at `=0.9.3`.
- **libc** for the real-time signals: `kill(2)` with systemd's power-off signal, `pidfd_send_signal(2)` through `syscall` with vtium's blank and wake signals, and libc's `SIGRTMIN`, since rustix sends a real-time signal only through an unsafe constructor whose contract forbids sending it; and the blank and wake signals' set, mask and signalfd (`sigemptyset`, `sigaddset`, `pthread_sigmask`, `signalfd`), since calloop's signal source and nix's signal set name the standard signals alone. It was already in the build through xkbcommon and nix. Pinned at `=0.2.191`.
- **libsystemd or libelogind**, with the `logind` feature only, for sd-bus: six calls declared in `src/logind.rs` and linked dynamically, with no binding crate. libelogind exports each of the six under libsystemd's symbol versions, with the same signatures and `sd_bus_error` layout in its `sd-bus.h`; `sd_bus_set_method_call_timeout` sets the floor, libsystemd 240 and libelogind 241. The minspec build has the feature off and links neither (ADR-1400). Where both libraries' `.pc` files are installed, `LIBSYSTEMD_NO_PKG_CONFIG=1` makes the build take libelogind. The build script's arrival moved that binary by a few tens of bytes with no code changed, through the crate's hash and so its layout; the figure varies with the build path (vtium/vtium#100).
- **pkg-config**, a build dependency with the `logind` feature only, which finds libsystemd or libelogind. It was already in the build through `libudev-sys`. Pinned at `=0.3.34`.
- **nix** arrives through calloop's `signals` feature, which gives the loop its signalfd. rustix has no signalfd wrapper. The loop's standard signals go through it; the real-time signals' signalfd is a libc call in `src/rt_signal.rs`, since nix's signal set names the standard signals alone. vtium-platform calls nothing in nix. Revisit when rustix wraps signalfd.

## Left to the host

- **The child's command and environment** are the host's `Command`: `TERM`, `COLORTERM`, `XDG_SEAT`, `XDG_VTNR`, `TERM_SESSION_TYPE`, the environment reset and `/bin/login` as the default (pty.c:252-302). That is the binary's (vtium/vtium#21).
- **The respawn policy**: a new session after an exit, oneshot, and the C reference's limit of 5 restarts within 2 seconds (pty.c:52-53, 737-768). That limit reads the clock, so it belongs where the binary decides what an exit means, not in the loop (vtium/vtium#21).

## Left to the seat and the binary

- **Choosing the VT backend**: libseat when configured (vtium/vtium#34), else `Tty::find` or the configured VT through `Tty::open` and `KernelVt::new`, else `FakeVt`. `KernelVt::new` takes the loop, is called before any thread starts, and its VT is stored where `access` finds it before the loop dispatches again, and dropped before the loop. With `--switchvt` the seat calls `activate`; without it, `wait`, and it scans the devices once the VT is shown. The panic hook runs a `Restore`.
- **Opening the devices** is the seat's: on `DeviceEvent::Added` it opens a card or a framebuffer through `Vt::open_device` and hands the descriptor to `Card::new` or `Fb::new`, and an event node to `Input::add`; on `Removed` it drops the device or calls `Input::remove`; on `Hotplug`, `Drm2d::poll`. Which backend a device gets, drm2d or fbdev, and skipping a framebuffer a DRM driver backs (`DeviceFlags::drm_backed`), are the seat's choices too.
- **When to sleep and wake** follows the VT's events and the hand-back (vtium/vtium#36). On `VtEvent::Acquired`: `Input::wake_up` and each device's `wake_up`. On `Release`: the terminal deactivated, `Input::sleep` and each device's `sleep`, then `acknowledge_release` once each device reported `Event::Asleep`, at once with no device. On `Hangup`: exit. On `Retaken`, which comes only while the VT is ours: each device's displays set up again, as a modeset does, and every display drawn whole. At exit: `leave`, the loop served for up to 100 ms while it is pending, then the `KernelVt` dropped. A wake-up retried past `WAKE_RETRIES` with no input or hotplug to start it, while the session is in the foreground, is vtium/vtium#36.
- **The key order**: each `InputEvent::Key` goes to `Vt::key`, then the seat's keys, then the terminal's grabs, then DECARM and the encoder (vtium/vtium#21); the screen's size to `Input::set_pointer_max`; DECLL to `Input::set_leds`. vtium-platform's `Mods` share their bits with vtium-term's.
- **What to draw** is the compositor's (vtium/vtium#16), under the frame contract above.
- **The DPMS blank's wiring**: the binary feeds the user's key presses and pointer actions (a move, a button or the wheel) and `Event::Awake` to `Blank::input`, a hotplug never. On each such press or action it calls each device's `input`. Whenever `Blank::input` returns true, for an event or for one device's `Event::Awake`, it calls every device's `unblank`, so a wake-up on one device ends the blank on all of them. An `Err` from `input` or `unblank` is logged and the loop goes on: the device retries within its bounds, and reports `WakeFailed` when they run out. Its loop wakes at `Blank::deadline`, on the binary's clock, and when `Blank::fire` says so it powers the displays down and deactivates the terminal (vtium/vtium#21).
