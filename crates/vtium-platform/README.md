# vtium-platform

vtium-platform is vtium's platform layer: everything that touches the kernel and the session. It holds the pty and the event loop, the displays (vtium/vtium#18), and the VT, the seat's devices through udev, and the keyboards and pointers (vtium/vtium#19). All of them run in one loop. Syscalls go through rustix (vtium/vtium#6), so descriptors are owned values and errors are `Result`s. DRM's ioctls go through `drm-ffi`. Keys go through libxkbcommon, and the device monitor goes through libudev. Those two are the C libraries at the crate's boundary.

The crate denies `unsafe_code`. Each site that needs unsafe code carries an `#[expect]` with its reason and a SAFETY comment. These are the sites:

- the ioctls in `src/ioctl.rs`, and the console font's ioctls in `src/vt/font.rs`;
- the mmap in `src/mmap.rs`, and its move to a render thread inside a token;
- the close-on-exec marking in `src/fds.rs`;
- the real-time signals' set, mask, signalfd and `pidfd_send_signal` in `src/rt_signal.rs`;
- power-off's kill in `src/power.rs`;
- the pty's pre-exec hook;
- with `libseat`, the close of the descriptor that libseat hands back with a device, in `src/seat/binding.rs`;
- with `images`, six sites in `src/image_helper/`. Three start and stop the image helper: its pre-exec hook, the `prctl` that empties its capability bounding set, and the `pidfd_send_signal` to its process group. Three serve it: the read-only mapping of a reply, the name service's account lookups, and sd-login's calls;
- with `gpu`, the calls to GL, EGL and GBM in `src/gpu/`, which [The GPU path](#the-gpu-path) lists.

`src/rt_signal.rs` and `src/power.rs` call libc. That is an exception to vtium/vtium#6's rule that syscalls go through rustix alone. rustix has no signalfd, and it has no safe call that masks real-time signals, sends one with kill, or sends one through a pidfd. The pty and the event loop read the child's output, so the crate also denies the determinism lints that vtium-parse denies.

## Behaviour

vtium opens the pty and starts the child as the leader of a new session, with the pty as its controlling terminal. It sets `VERASE` and the window size. It reads the child's output edge-triggered, at most `READS_PER_TURN` reads and `READ_BUDGET` bytes a turn. The consumer may take part of what is read. vtium holds the rest for later turns, so the loop serves its other sources in between. A pause stops reading and feeding until the resume. What the user sends (keys, pastes, button reports) is queued whole and never stalls the reading. A pty closed and opened again starts with nothing queued, because the queue belongs to the `Pty`, which is dropped on close. Dropping a `Pty` sends SIGHUP to the leader of the pty's session and then closes the master, which hangs up the session (see the pty's rules below). The leader is the child vtium started, until the child hands the pty on. login hands it on: its fork starts a new session on the pty.

Two calls serve the sessions that outlive vtium (ADR-1401 D13). `Pty::release` lets a pty go at vtium's exit with no SIGHUP, and it neither kills its child nor waits for it. `Pty::adopt` makes a pty of a master whose session an earlier vtium started. vtium cannot wait for a process it did not start, so an adopted pty watches the process that leads the pty's session through a pidfd. It reports that process's exit with status 0. `Pty::redraw` sets the adopted pty's size, and sends SIGWINCH itself where the size is unchanged. Tests: `a_released_pty_leaves_its_session_to_the_pty_that_adopts_it`, `a_pty_whose_session_ended_is_not_adopted`.

The child starts with every signal from 1 to the kernel's last, apart from SIGKILL and SIGSTOP, at its default disposition and none blocked. Under POSIX `exec`, an ignored signal stays ignored and the signal mask is kept. Without the reset, two kinds of signal would pass on to the session in that state (vtium/vtium#90). One kind is SIGINT and SIGQUIT, which a vtium started in the background ignores. The other is the signals the loop blocks. `tests/pty.rs` spawns a child from a parent that ignores SIGINT and blocks SIGTERM, and reads the child's `/proc/<pid>/status`.

The child is watched through a pidfd, so SIGCHLD keeps its default disposition and the loop handles no signal for it. Signals the console handles go through one signalfd in `Loop`.

vtium waits for every child it starts, so no child stays a zombie. While the pty is in the loop, its exit source waits for the child once the child's pidfd reports the exit. Each other path waits for the child too:

- If the pidfd cannot be opened after the fork, `Pty::spawn` kills the child with SIGKILL and waits for it before it returns the error.
- If `Pty::insert` cannot add its second source, it removes the first one, so the loop holds nothing of the pty and the pty is as it was before the call.
- A `Pty` dropped while it is in no loop kills its child with SIGKILL and waits for it, since no loop will. A `Pty` is in no loop when it was never inserted or its insert failed.
- A `Pty` dropped in its loop while its child runs first takes its own sources out of the loop. It then adds a source that waits for the child once the SIGHUP ends it. The sources go first because the exit source watches the same pidfd, and the loop's poller refuses a descriptor it already watches. If the loop refuses the new source anyway, the drop kills the child with SIGKILL and waits for it.

The unit tests in `src/pty.rs` make each of these steps fail on purpose and check that the test's thread has no child left that nobody waited for.

What the consumer is fed, and every reply it writes, depend only on the bytes the child sent (docs/architecture.md, "Determinism"). A stall delays them and drops nothing, as long as the consumer stops once `Replies::full` is true. `src/channel/tests.rs` holds the chunking-invariance property test, with random read sizes, random takes, a child that reads at random and pauses at random. `tests/pty.rs` repeats it through a real pty.

### The pty's rules

- **The consumer stops at the reply that fills the queue, and no reply is dropped.** `Replies::full` is true once the replies the child has not read reach `REPLY_HIGH`. The consumer then returns after the sequence whose reply reached that mark. Nothing more is read or fed until the replies drain below `REPLY_LOW`. Suppose a consumer parsed the whole read before it looked at the stall. It could pass `REPLY_MAX` and drop replies, and whether it did would depend on how reads split the stream. Spec: docs/architecture.md, "Determinism", chunking invariance. `REPLY_MAX` is a ceiling for a consumer that does not stop.
- **Closing the pty signals the session's leader before the session's input ends.** Closing a pty's master hangs up its session. But the kernel wakes a process reading the pty with EIO before it sends SIGHUP to the session's leader. A leader that reads the pty, such as a shell waiting in `read`, can see the EIO and exit in that gap. The kernel's SIGHUP then finds no process, and the leader is never told that its terminal hung up. So vtium sends SIGHUP to the session's leader before it closes the master. The signal is then pending before the leader's input ends: the leader's read is interrupted, or it returns EIO with the signal delivered at the same return.

  Under login, the session's leader and the child vtium started are different processes. util-linux login's first process ignores SIGHUP, gives the pty up and forks. The fork starts a new session on the pty, so the shell leads the session. login's first process only forwards a SIGHUP it receives, and that SIGHUP can come after the master closes. vtium therefore reads the session's id from the master (`tcgetsid`) and opens a pidfd for it. The kernel clears the pty's session when its leader exits. So vtium signals the leader only if the master still names that session once the pidfd is open. With no session on the pty, a child that still runs gets the signal. With the master closed alone, the shell of a console that vtium's heads test closed under load ran no SIGHUP trap in about one close in 300.

  The kernel's own SIGHUP still follows. A pending SIGHUP is not queued twice, so a leader that has not yet taken vtium's SIGHUP gets one SIGHUP. A leader that has taken it gets a second. Spec: ADR-1401 D5, under which the programs of a closed console get SIGHUP. Tests: `closing_the_pty_signals_its_leader_before_its_input_ends`, `closing_the_pty_signals_a_leader_its_child_handed_the_pty_to`, and the binary's `closing_a_console_hangs_up_its_session_alone`.
- **The children hold none of the descriptors vtium inherited.** vtium starts two kinds of child: the pty's child and the sysfs suspend's shell. Before it starts either, it marks every descriptor above stdio close-on-exec through `cloexec_above_stdio`. That includes the descriptors vtium's parent passed down without the flag. Each child therefore holds only its stdio. The mark is made in the parent before the fork. Where `close_range` cannot set the mark, vtium lists `/proc/self/fd`, which allocates, and the hook between fork and exec may not allocate. vtium marks and starts both children on its main thread, so nothing on that thread opens a descriptor between the mark and the fork. When a session's child must wait for its systemd scope, the fork runs on a short-lived thread, `vtium-spawn`, which the main thread starts after the mark. Without the mark, a descriptor that vtium's parent leaked would reach the login session.

  The suspend shell also starts with every signal at its default disposition and none blocked, as the pty's child does. It needs that for three reasons. The loop blocks the signals it reads. A vtium started in the background ignores SIGINT and SIGQUIT. And an exec keeps both the mask and the ignored signals. Spec: vtium/vtium#133. Tests: `the_child_holds_only_the_pty`, `the_sysfs_shell_holds_only_its_stdio_with_default_signals`.
- **Pointer motion is coalesced for a child that does not read.** From `MOTION_COALESCE_AT` bytes queued, a motion report replaces the motion reports queued after the last other write; button reports, keys and replies are queued whole and keep their order. Without it, a child that enabled any-motion tracking (mode 1003) and never read would grow the queue for as long as the pointer moved. Spec: vtium/vtium#20.
- **The child's exit reaches the consumer after the output it left, in bounded time.** vtium reports the exit once a read after it finds nothing more, or after `EXIT_FEED_MAX` bytes more than the pty holds. So a process the child started, which ignores the hangup and writes on, cannot hold the exit back. The host then closes the pty and drops the rest. Replies written after the exit are dropped and never stall the reading. Reporting the exit when the child is reaped would close the pty with the child's own output still unread. How much of that output the screen showed would then depend on timing. FIONREAD cannot count what the child left: on Linux 7.2 it reports the line discipline's 4 KiB while the flip buffers hold 16 KiB more. Spec: docs/architecture.md, "Determinism", chunking invariance.
- **A pty is read for at most `READ_BUDGET` bytes a turn.** A readiness event reads until one of three things happens: the pty has no more, `READS_PER_TURN` reads are done, or `READ_BUDGET` bytes are read. The next turn reads on. The loop therefore serves every other head between one head's turns of output. A flooding pty's reads come in about 4 KiB. So `READS_PER_TURN` alone would let a turn parse 200 KiB of one head's flood. Another head's key is two to three turns from its echo's frame, so it would wait behind up to 600 KiB of that flood.

  This was measured through the seat of `crates/vtium/tests/heads.rs`, with a 7680x2160 head flooding, in a release build on the operator's machine. With the reads bounded alone, the other head's key reached its frame in 25 to 40 ms. At 64 KiB a turn, it reached its frame in 7 to 10 ms. The flood parsed at 15.6 to 15.9 MB/s against 14 to 14.7 MB/s. Spec: ADR-1401 D2. Tests: `a_turn_reads_at_most_read_budget_bytes`, and `a_flood_in_the_large_head_leaves_the_small_head_its_keys_and_frames` and `a_flood_in_the_small_head_leaves_the_large_head_its_keys_and_frames` in `crates/vtium/tests/heads.rs`.

## Displays

A video device is one DRM card (`Drm2d`) or one fbdev node (`Fbdev`), which the seat opens and hands over. Each lends the buffer that the next frame is drawn into as a `Token` (ADR-1401 D2). A token is an owned value. It names one of the display's buffers by its index, and carries that buffer's age and its memory. It goes to the head's render thread, which draws into it as a `Canvas` in XRGB8888. `swap` takes the token back with the frame's damage and presents its buffer. The display reports through events, which the owner drains once a turn: a display added, removed or to be redrawn whole, a frame flipped, and the device awake or asleep. No display keeps a copy of its frame. Nothing copies a frame from one buffer into another, apart from fbdev's conversion copy below. Tests: `a_tokens_age_counts_the_frames_since_its_buffer_was_drawn`, `two_displays_of_one_card_keep_their_ages_apart`, `a_display_lends_no_second_token_while_one_is_out`, `one_and_three_buffers_lend_while_a_flip_is_pending`, `a_buffer_replaced_while_its_token_is_out_stays_until_the_token_returns`, `a_token_back_after_a_vt_release_or_a_blank_is_dropped`, `a_framebuffer_token_back_too_late_is_dropped`.

- **drm2d** draws into one, two or three dumb buffers a display, as the `Buffers` value given to `Drm2d::new` says. The binary gives three in a build that adds features on top of minspec, and two in the minspec build (vtium's README). The buffers take turns: each frame swapped goes to the next. With two, a frame is drawn once the flip of the frame before it has completed. With three, the third buffer is drawn into while the second flips. With one, vtium draws into the buffer being shown, so a frame can be seen half drawn, and no frame waits for a flip.

  A display is a connected connector with modes. vtium drives it through a free CRTC and, with atomic modesetting, through its primary plane and cursor plane. Its mode is the configured `mode=WxH[@rate]`. With `use-original-mode`, it is the mode the console left. Otherwise it is the default mode: the panel's first preferred mode, which the kernel lists first, or its first listed mode when none is preferred (the operator's decision on vtium/vtium#5).

  A hotplug scan reads each display's connector again. Sometimes the connector's EDID names another monitor than the one the display was bound to. That happens when a monitor is swapped for another faster than vtium sees the first one's disconnect. vtium then lets the display go and binds the connector again as the new monitor. The head file's rules, the dark rule, the density and the mode all follow the new monitor. vtium asks the dark rule again about a connector left dark, in the same way. A connector whose EDID cannot be read counts as showing the same monitor. A display may be bound while its EDID cannot be read, and its EDID may become readable later. vtium then lets that display go and binds it again, once. The owner hears that as a removal and an addition, so the head file's EDID rules can match the display.

  The same monitor may list other modes than those its mode was chosen from, as after a DisplayPort link is trained again. vtium then chooses the mode again from the new list and sets it. It chooses by the rules above with one difference: under `use-original-mode`, the mode vtium set stands in for the mode the console left. The display therefore keeps its mode where the new list still has it. The display is then asked to redraw, and the owner reads its new size and refresh rate.

  The card may refuse the new mode, and the default mode too. vtium then sets the display again at the mode it is set to, which its buffers fit, in the same modeset as the other displays. It holds the new mode back. It tries that mode again only when the card wakes or the set of displays changes, and a newer list of modes replaces it. On a legacy card, the card refuses a mode by failing the CRTC set with `EINVAL`, and vtium tries no default mode.

  The new mode may also fail for another reason, as where its buffers cannot be made. vtium then also sets the display at the mode it is set to, in the same modeset. A wake-up therefore still wakes the card and keeps DRM master, and a hotplug still sets the other displays. The wake-up retries then try the new mode again. A retry that sets it ends the retries. Once the retries run out, vtium logs a warning and holds the new mode back, as it holds back a mode the card refused. It then reports no `WakeFailed`, and the next key press tries nothing.

  A legacy display whose CRTC cannot be set, at either mode, counts as not set. vtium does not ask it to redraw until a modeset sets it. The wake-up retries try it again. Once they run out, they report `WakeFailed` with the card still awake, so the next key press tries again.

  vtium first tries a modeset of every display as a test-only commit. When the test fails, vtium tries it again with each display's default mode. When the displays online and the new ones still cannot be set together, vtium leaves the new ones off until the next hotplug, and sets the displays online alone. A modeset makes new buffers only for a display whose mode needs them, and only once the modeset takes. A display online keeps its buffers and commits the front buffer it shows.

  A swap is a non-blocking commit with a flip event. It carries the frame's damage as `FB_DAMAGE_CLIPS` where the primary plane takes them, and the hardware cursor on the cursor plane. Some devices have universal planes and no atomic commits, and some have neither. Such a device uses the legacy calls, presents whole frames, and reports no damage support (the operator's decision of 2026-10-26).
- **The card's lists.** The kernel reports each of a card's lists through an ioctl: a connector's modes and encoders, an object's properties, and the card's planes. The ioctl writes at most the room it is given and returns the list's full length. vtium reads each list in two. The first read gives the length, and the second reads the list into a buffer of exactly that length. vtium refuses a list unread when it is longer than its bound (`MODES_MAX`, `ENCODERS_MAX`, `OBJECT_PROPERTIES_MAX` or `PLANES_MAX`). It also refuses one whose length differs at the second read, as when a connector is torn down and its id given to another between the two reads.

  The scan's first read of a connector gives no room for modes. That read makes the kernel probe the connector while vtium holds DRM master. Where the first read found no modes, the second read gives room for one mode, so that it does not probe again. Sometimes vtium wants only the encoder a connector is on, as when a modeset turns off what another master left on a dark connector. vtium then reads the connector once with room for one mode, which probes nothing, and trusts none of its lists. vtium reads a property's name in one GETPROPERTY that asks for none of the property's values.

  Each refusal is a warning that names what was refused, at each scan that meets it. The scan leaves unbound a connector it cannot read, and a connector whose properties it cannot read. The next scan reads both again. A refused list of planes leaves a display without a primary plane, so the scan leaves that display unbound too, and the next scan binds it. A connector the card no longer has, as during a monitor's power cycle, is skipped with a line at debug level.
- **Flips.** A swap, a cursor commit or a modeset puts a flip in flight on the display. A swap while that flip is in flight is `Busy`. Each flip in flight is bounded by `FLIP_WAIT_MS`. When its completion event has not come by then, vtium takes the flip as done, and the display reports `Flipped`. vtium logs the first lost event at warn level, and later ones at debug level until the card delivers an event again. An event that arrives later still reports `Flipped`. drm-ffi's commit and page flip carry no user data of vtium's to tell flips apart. So an event that arrives after a newer commit was accepted is taken as that commit's event.

  The one exception is the event of a flip whose display vtium let go while the flip was in flight, as when the display is unplugged. vtium drops the next event on that CRTC within `FLIP_WAIT_MS`, the bound that flip had. That event therefore does not end the flip of a display that takes the CRTC after it (`the_event_of_a_display_let_go_ends_no_later_flip`, `a_lost_event_of_a_display_let_go_ends_no_later_flip_past_its_bound`).
- **A commit refused as busy.** The card refuses a commit with `EBUSY` while another commit is in flight on the CRTC. Sometimes vtium knows of no flip of its own in flight. That other commit is then one of two kinds. It may be a commit vtium does not track, which sends vtium no event when it lands. Or it may be a flip of vtium's that the bound took as done while the kernel still had it. The display then asks the kernel for an event at its next vblank (`WAIT_VBLANK`), and waits for it as for a flip in flight. While it waits, `is_flipping` holds, the display lends no token, and a swap is `Busy` and sends nothing. The vblank's event wakes the loop and reports nothing, since no frame flipped. The owner draws the frame again at that turn, as it does for any display that stops flipping. A card that refuses every commit therefore gets one commit from vtium at each vblank, and the loop sleeps between them.

  When the CRTC goes off during the wait, the kernel sends the vblank's event at once, so the wait ends then. The wait is bounded by `FLIP_WAIT_MS` as a flip is. Past that bound, vtium takes the wait as done, with no `Flipped`. Some cards have neither a vblank interrupt nor the kernel's software vblank timer. Such a card refuses the wait with `EOPNOTSUPP`, while its commits can still be refused as busy. Since Linux 6.19, virtio-gpu, bochs, qxl and cirrus-qemu drive their vblank from that timer and take the wait. The firmware framebuffer drivers (simpledrm, efidrm, ofdrm, vesadrm and corebootdrm), udl and gud have neither, and neither has virtio-gpu before 6.19. On those cards the display has nothing to wait for. The frame goes with the next swap the owner tries, and nothing asks for that swap until the next draw. The stall of vtium/vtium#171 therefore remains on those cards. The maintainer decided to keep that stall rather than redraw on a timer (vtium/vtium#176).
- **Sleep and wake-up.** `sleep` does four things in turn. It blanks the shown buffers, waits for any flip in flight, hides the cursor and overlay planes that show something, and drops DRM master. It then reports `Asleep`, on which the VT release is acknowledged. The wait lets the program that takes the card commit at once. The kernel refuses a commit that does not block with `EBUSY` on a CRTC whose last commit has not flipped (`stall_checks`). Where no plane shows anything, as before vtium shows a pointer, the sleep commits nothing.

  `wake_up` takes master and sets every display again. While it fails, as while another process holds master, vtium tries it again every `WAKE_RETRY_MS`, at most `WAKE_RETRIES` times. It then reports `WakeFailed`. A hotplug scan (`poll`) lets go of displays that have gone, binds new ones, and sets modes when anything is new. While the card is awake, the scan keeps DRM master even with no display at all. A display that returns, which is one bound after the card's first scan, is set from off by its first modeset.

  vtium tries a failed hotplug again in the same way, setting every display, and also a failed modeset that `unblank` runs. These retries report no `Awake`, which the owner would count as user activity. A failed retry keeps DRM master and the displays online, so the blank still powers them down. No other process drew into the buffers meanwhile. So such a retry asks a display to redraw only where it changes the display's mode or powers it on, while a wake-up asks every display to redraw. Only a failed wake-up lets master go, as another process may hold it.

  After `WakeFailed`, the card keeps trying once every `WAKE_RETRY_SLOW_MS` while its VT is shown. So the displays wake about a second after the other process lets go of master, even when nobody touches the keyboard. Each slow try is a single try. A slow try that fails starts no fast retries, and logs its failure to take master at debug level, so the slow tries do not fill the log. Asleep, the card tries the wake-up. Awake, it tries a modeset of every display. While the displays are blanked, an awake card's slow try commits nothing: the modeset is owed to `unblank`, and the slow tries stop. A VT release stops the slow tries, and a VT acquire starts the fast tries again.

  The user's next key press or pointer action (a move, a button or the wheel) also tries again at once, through `input`. Asleep in the foreground, it tries the wake-up, with every display on. Awake, it tries a modeset of every display, which is owed to `unblank` while the displays are blanked. The next hotplug tries the wake-up again too. Each of these tries is retried in the same way, fast and then slow.
- **fbdev** keeps the mode it is given, in one buffer, whatever `Buffers` a DRM display keeps. It uses 32, 24 or 16 bits of true color, or 1 bit where the device's visual is `FB_VISUAL_MONO01` or `FB_VISUAL_MONO10`. XRGB8888 is drawn straight into the framebuffer, which the token lends. Any other layout is drawn into an XRGB8888 copy, which the token lends instead. The swap then converts the copy into the framebuffer where the damage says.

  A 1-bit device is packed a whole byte at a time from the copy. A pixel is lit where its BT.601 luma is at least 128, the threshold that the kernel's `drm_fb_xrgb8888_to_mono` gives a DRM panel. The first pixel of a byte is its low bit, or its high bit on a big-endian host. Under `FB_NONSTD_REV_PIX_IN_B` the order is reversed, as the kernel's own drawing orders the pixels (`fb_draw.h`). The kernel's `FBINFO_FOREIGN_ENDIAN` is not visible to userspace, so a device that sets it packs in the wrong order. A timer at the refresh interval stands in for the flip event. fbdev has no cursor plane.
- **A 1-bit or gray display** is reported by `levels`. An fbdev device at 1 bit has 2 levels. For a card whose driver `src/display/depth.rs` lists, `levels` gives its panel's levels, 2, 4 or 16 (vtium/vtium#135). vtium finds the driver by the name `DRM_IOCTL_VERSION` gives and the device-tree node that sysfs shows. No connector or plane property says that a DRM panel is 1-bit or gray. The panel's driver offers XRGB8888 and turns each frame into the panel's levels itself. So the owner draws a 1-bit display in black and white, and a gray one in color, which the panel converts. vtium does not read fbdev's gray visuals: it refuses a gray framebuffer, as before.
- **Density** is reported by `density`, in dots per inch across and down. vtium computes it from the display's mode and its physical size. On DRM, the physical size is the connector's `mm_width` and `mm_height`, which the kernel reads from the EDID. Where a side of those is 0, vtium uses the image size in centimetres that the base block of the connector's EDID in sysfs states. That EDID is `card<N>-<connector>/edid` under the card's directory, and vtium reads it with no card node opened. On fbdev, the physical size is `width` and `height` in millimetres.

  Some values give no physical size: a side of 0, and fbdev's all ones. A density outside `DPI_MIN` to `DPI_MAX`, 20 to 1,000, gives none either, as a projector that reports 1 mm gives. The density is then `Density::ASSUMED`, 96 DPI. It is marked as not measured, so the owner can say so once per display (the operator's decision on vtium/vtium#145). Neither read needs DRM master (ADR-1301 D3).
- **Identity.** `identity` names each display as the head file's rules match it (ADR-1401 D4). It gives five things:

  - its connector's name (`DP-8`);
  - its card's udev `ID_PATH`, as the owner gives it through `set_card_path`;
  - the monitor its EDID names (`EdidId`, `MAKER:PRODUCT:SERIAL`);
  - its connector's `PATH`, where it has one;
  - whether its connector was visible when the card was first scanned, which is the mark of the boot display (D9). A connector is visible when its CRTC shows a mode and is on, by `ACTIVE` on an atomic card and by the connector's DPMS on a legacy one.

  The EDID is the base block that the density reads, read once. vtium reads three fields from it:

  - its PNP maker id, three letters of five bits each. A value past `Z` is read as the character it lands on, as the kernel reads it.
  - its product code, shown as four hex digits.
  - its serial string, display descriptor `0xff`'s 13 bytes up to its line feed, without the spaces that pad it. Each byte that is not printable ASCII is read as `_`, and so is a space within the string, and so is `#`, which starts the head file's comments. Where there is no serial string, vtium uses the serial number where that is not 0, shown as eight hex digits. Otherwise the serial is empty.

  A connector with no EDID to read has no EDID id.

  A driver gives each port of a DisplayPort multi-stream (MST) hub a `PATH` property when it creates the port's connector. The property's value names an immutable blob. The blob holds `mst:` and then the object id of the card's connector that the hub is plugged into. The port number at each hub on the way follows, joined by `-`, and then a NUL. For example, `mst:213-1`, or `mst:213-2-1` behind a second hub. Every other connector has no `PATH`.

  The scan reads the blob in two GETPROPBLOB calls, which need no DRM master. The first asks for its length. The second reads it into a buffer of exactly that length, since the kernel copies a blob only into a buffer of its own length. vtium refuses a blob unread when it is longer than `BLOB_MAX`, 4096 bytes. It also refuses one whose length differs at the second call, as when the blob was freed between the calls and its id given to another. At each refusal, a warning names the connector. Its display is bound without a path, and keeps none while the connector stays connected.

  The identity carries the blob's text: the bytes before the first NUL. Each byte that is not printable ASCII, a space included, is read as `_`, and so is `#`. The text is therefore always one token of the head file. A `PATH` whose value is 0 gives no path and logs nothing. A `PATH` whose blob the card cannot give is logged as a warning. A `PATH` whose text is empty gives no path. In each case the display is bound as any other. The driver's name for a hub port's connector (`DP-8`) can change after a replug or a resume, while its `PATH` stays the same.
- **Dark.** `set_dark` gives the predicate that leaves a display dark (ADR-1401 D3). vtium asks it about each connector's identity when the scan first finds the connector connected. While no predicate is set, every display is lit. A connector left dark is announced as no display. `dark` lists it, in the card's connector order, until it is unplugged or let go by `release_dark`, which the head file's display guard asks for (D9). Once it is plugged back or let go, vtium asks the predicate again at the next scan.

  Another master may have left a dark connector on a CRTC. Each modeset sets that connector off, a wake-up's modeset included, so a frame that master left goes dark. The connector leaves its CRTC. A CRTC that no display takes is turned off with every plane on it, and every connector on it is taken off it, an unplugged one included. A CRTC that is not active shows nothing and stays out of the commit, which asks no flip event of it. The card may refuse a modeset with the dark connectors set off. vtium then tries it again without them, so a dark connector never costs the lit displays. The first such refusal is a warning that names them, and later ones are logged at debug level. With every display dark, the scan sets them off and keeps DRM master, as with no display.

  The legacy calls turn the CRTC off through `Kms::disable_crtc`. Its default, which `Card` keeps, says `Unsupported`, and vtium logs that as a warning. So a legacy card shows what another master left on a dark connector until `Card` implements the call, a follow-up that vtium/vtium#156 carries.
- **DPMS** is set through the connector's DPMS property or `FBIOBLANK`. On an atomic device it is the CRTC's `ACTIVE`, so every commit's `ACTIVE` follows the display's recorded DPMS. A wake-up, which is the VT acquired, counts as user activity (the operator's decision on vtium/vtium#18). Its modeset's `ACTIVE=1` brings every display on, blanked ones included. Each display is recorded on and drawn whole, and fbdev unblanks. Where fbdev's `FBIOBLANK` fails to unblank, in `unblank` or a wake-up, vtium tries it again every `WAKE_RETRY_MS`, at most `WAKE_RETRIES` times. fbdev then reports `WakeFailed`, and its `input` tries the unblank again at the next key press or pointer action.

  A hotplug while the displays are on sets modes as above. A hotplug while they are blanked rescans the connectors and records the change. It commits nothing and leaves a modeset owed. Every display stays dark, one just plugged in included, until the input that ends the blank. That input's `unblank` runs the owed modeset with every display on. When the blank comes first, a hotplug's modeset that waits on a flip in flight is owed in the same way, and so are the retries of a failed hotplug. An owed modeset that fails is retried as a failed hotplug is. The blank stays ended, since no later input comes to `unblank`. A display whose connector has no DPMS property stays lit through the blank. It still counts as blanked, so it lets no hotplug commit.

  Without a modeset owed, `unblank` powers each display on through its DPMS. Where any display refuses, vtium still powers the rest on, and a modeset is owed, retried in the same way, with every display on. While asleep, `unblank` does nothing, as the wake-up brings every display on. vtium therefore commits no modeset while the displays are blanked, apart from a wake-up's, which ends the blank. The recorded DPMS always matches what the hardware shows. A display just bound is set on.

  `Blank` powers the displays down after `dpms-timeout` with no key press or pointer action (600 s by default, `DPMS_TIMEOUT`), and up at the next one. The owner wires input and wake-ups to it, and also a program's timeout and wake (vtium/vtium#79). A timeout change restarts the countdown and keeps a blank in force. `Blank::deadline` follows the countdown, and is none while blanking is off.

### The frame contract

The displays and the renderer (vtium-render, vtium/vtium#16) share one contract, which the steering session set on 2026-10-27. vtium-render's README states its side in the same terms.

1. **A frame.** At the prepare step the owner takes the display's `Token` (`lend`). The token names the buffer that the frame is drawn into, and carries its age, counted in the display's own frames. The age is 1 when the buffer holds the frame just before this one, and n when it holds the frame n frames back. It is 0 when nobody knows what the buffer holds. A frame counts whether its swap succeeded or failed, because either way the buffer holds it. Each display counts its own frames, so two displays of one card keep their ages apart. The renderer draws into the token's buffer, writing only. It draws every cell changed in the last `age` frames, or the whole buffer at age 0, and returns the frame's damage. At the swap the owner gives the token back with that damage (`swap`), or unused (`give_back`). A token dropped instead leaves every buffer of its display at age 0. While a token is out the display lends no other, so a display has one frame on its way at a time.
2. **The renderer's history** is its own per-cell stamps. The display never assumes the renderer counts its buffers.
3. **Empty damage means nothing changed.** The owner does not swap a frame whose damage is empty. The whole screen is one rectangle covering it, sent with no clips. A swap may have nothing to send: no damage, none kept from a failed swap, and no cursor change. Such a swap commits nothing and returns `Swapped::Nothing`. A cursor change is a change from what the cursor plane shows, so a change undone before the swap is none. `need_commit` says whether a swap with no new damage has something to send. A token given back unused presents nothing. `commit` sends what a display holds with no new frame: a frame kept from a failed swap, or the cursor plane alone. While a token is out, `commit` sends the cursor plane alone, and the kept frame goes with the token's own swap.
4. **The pointer.** `hardware_cursor` says whether a cursor plane carries the pointer. It is true once `setup_cursor` put an image on the plane. It is false on fbdev, on the legacy calls, and where the plane refused the image. It also turns false once a commit with the cursor plane failed and one without it took. Where it is true, `show_cursor`, `hide_cursor` and `set_cursor_offset` are followed by `commit(id)`, or by the swap of a token with no damage. That swap is a cursor-only commit, which leaves the primary plane and the front buffer as they are and returns `Swapped::Cursor`. Elsewhere the renderer draws the pointer in software.
5. **A failed swap** (`Busy`, `Failed` or `Off`) keeps the frame's damage and the cursor's state for the next swap that succeeds, which may have no new damage. The buffer the frame was drawn into holds it, so when that buffer is lent again it is age 1. With one or three buffers, the display lends no token while that frame waits for a flip. When the flip ends, vtium sends that frame as it is with `commit`, unless the screen changed meanwhile. In that case vtium draws a new frame over it, and that frame's swap carries the damage of both. Each frame's damage therefore goes out once (vtium/vtium#246; test `a_kept_frames_damage_goes_out_once`).
6. **Display power.** While a display is not on (DPMS standby, suspend or off) it is not ready for a frame (rule 7) and lends no token, and a swap is `Off`. Powered on, by `set_dpms`, by `unblank` or by a wake-up's modeset, it reports every buffer at age 0, sends its next frame whole and emits `Redraw`.
7. **Ready for a frame.** `ready_for_frame` says whether a display takes a frame now, whatever form the frame takes. On the CPU path a frame is the buffer a token lends, which the renderer draws into. A DRM display is ready when its card is awake and waits for no modeset or sleep. The display must also be online and powered on, have no token out, and have a buffer free to draw into. With two buffers, a free buffer means that the display waits for no flip or vblank. With one or three, the display lends during a flip, unless a frame kept from a failed swap waits for that flip. A DRM display lends a token exactly while it is ready. A framebuffer is ready while it is awake and powered on, has no token out, and its stand-in flip event is not pending. It also lends its token during that flip.

   On `Event::Flipped` the owner may draw again. It may also draw once `ready_for_frame` holds after a wait that reports no event, such as a commit refused as busy. With nothing changed, rule 3 means no swap. `Redraw` follows a modeset that gave a display new buffers, a wake-up, and a power-on. A display kept through a hotplug keeps its buffers' ages and gets no `Redraw`.
8. **A token that comes back too late.** A token may come back to a display that has gone, or that was given new buffers, as by a modeset after a hotplug. It may also come back to a display that was let go for a VT release (`sleep`), or powered down, while the frame was drawn. Such a token is dropped with `SwapError::Dropped`. The frame is not shown, and every buffer of the display is age 0, so the next frame is drawn whole.

   The display may replace or let go of a buffer while its token is out. That buffer stays allocated and mapped until the token comes back, so a modeset never waits for the render thread. The display frees the buffer when the token comes back. For a token the owner dropped, the display frees it at its next lend, swap, hotplug scan, sleep or wake-up. A card whose last display has gone frees it at its next hotplug scan. A DRM display's sleep may find lent the buffer it clears, as with one buffer. It then clears the token's memory when the token comes back.

   A framebuffer's sleep does three things in turn. It revokes the token lent, then clears the framebuffer, then reports `Event::Asleep`. A revoked token draws nothing, so nothing the token draws after the sleep reaches the next VT's screen. The display drops its own mapping of the framebuffer at the sleep. The revoked token holds the same mapping, and the kernel unmaps the framebuffer once that token is dropped too. Revoking a token takes the lock of the memory it lent, which a draw in progress holds. So where the render thread is drawing when the sleep comes, the sleep waits, with the loop running, until the token comes back, or for `FLIP_WAIT_MS`. Past that bound the loop waits for the draw to end, and revokes the token then.

   A framebuffer may be set up again with no sleep before, as at a retake. Both set-ups map the same memory. So the new set-up revokes the earlier set-up's token where no frame is drawing into it. Where a frame is drawing into it, the next sleep revokes it under that token's own lock.

### What drm2d and fbdev do, and why

- **The wake-up tries outlast a flip wait.** vtium tries a DRM card that fails to wake `WAKE_RETRIES` times, `WAKE_RETRY_MS` apart, which is twice `FLIP_WAIT_MS`. A switch with no handshake between two vtium processes leaves the one switched away from up to `FLIP_WAIT_MS` to sleep and drop DRM master. The one switched to waits that out instead of reporting `Event::WakeFailed`. Spec: vtium/vtium#19.
- **The wake-up tries go on once a second while the VT is shown.** Once the fast tries run out, a card tries again every `WAKE_RETRY_SLOW_MS` for as long as its VT is shown. A display with no keyboard, such as a kiosk's, then wakes on its own after another program lets go of DRM master. Spec: the operator's decision on vtium/vtium#36, recorded in ADR-1000's "Replies and policy". Tests: `a_slow_retry_wakes_the_card_once_master_is_let_go`, `the_loop_wakes_the_card_at_a_slow_retry`, `a_vt_release_stops_the_slow_retries`, `input_tries_at_once_while_the_slow_retries_run`, `a_slow_retry_sets_the_modes_unless_blanked`.
- **A card opened while its VT is not shown lets DRM master go.** vtium opens the seat's cards at start and on hotplug, whichever VT is shown. The kernel makes the first process to open a card that no process holds master of the card's master. The seat puts a card to sleep when it was opened while its VT is not shown, or not yet acquired. A card's sleep drops master whether or not the card woke, so the vtium of the VT shown takes master, and the acquire takes it back. A card opened for the VT shown is woken at once and keeps the master that its open gave it. Spec: vtium/vtium#139, the kernel's DRM master rules (`drm_auth.c`). Tests: `a_card_opened_while_the_vt_is_not_shown_lets_master_go_until_the_acquire`, `a_device_added_while_the_vt_is_not_shown_sleeps_until_the_acquire`.
- **Nothing waits by blocking the loop, and every flip in flight is bounded.** A modeset or a sleep may find a page flip in flight. It then waits for the flip's completion event with the loop running. The VT release is acknowledged on `Asleep`. Modesets are committed non-blocking with a flip event. Every flip in flight, a swap's included, is taken as done after `FLIP_WAIT_MS` without its event. So a lost event never leaves a display swapping for good, and no wait stops the loop. Spec: the operator's decision on vtium/vtium#18, 1 s for flip waits; `lib.rs`, a loop that never blocks.
- **The sleep hides only what shows, and a flip slow at a VT release is no warning.** The sleep waits for each flip in flight, at most `FLIP_WAIT_MS`. It then hides the cursor and overlay planes that show something, with a commit that does not block. Where no plane shows anything, as before vtium shows a pointer, it commits nothing. A flip whose bound runs out during the sleep is logged at debug level, since the program taking the card may hold it up. That happens when another master starts on the card while vtium's first modeset is in flight.

  Why: this was seen on the maintainer's laptop, padnoir, where X took VT 1 before vtium's first frame (vtium/vtium#280). The sleep logged that the first modeset's flip did not come within 1000 ms. It also logged that the card refused with `EBUSY` the commit that hid the planes, which then hid nothing, as that modeset had not flipped. Because the sleep waits for each flip, the program that takes the card is not refused as busy on a CRTC vtium flipped. Tests: `a_sleep_waits_for_the_pending_flip`, `a_sleep_waits_for_a_frames_flip_and_commits_nothing_to_hide`, `a_sleep_over_the_first_modesets_flip_warns_of_nothing`, `a_sleep_goes_ahead_when_the_flip_never_completes`.
- **A failed swap is not a presented frame.** Its error reaches the caller, with `EBUSY` as `SwapError::Busy`, and the buffers stay as they were. The frame's damage and the cursor's state go with the next swap that succeeds, bounded by `DAMAGE_CLIPS_MAX`. The buffer's age tells the compositor that the back buffer holds the frame that failed. Spec: the kernel's `FB_DAMAGE_CLIPS`, damage since the plane's last update.
- **A frame the card refuses as busy is sent at the display's next vblank.** Sometimes the card refuses a commit with `EBUSY` while no flip of vtium's is in flight. The display then keeps the frame, and waits for the event of its next vblank as for a flip in flight. The owner sends the kept frame with `commit` at the turn that event wakes. So the frame reaches the screen with no other event, no timer and no blocking commit. Without that wait, an idle session would show the frame before until output, a blink or a key draws again. Spec: vtium/vtium#171. Tests: `a_frame_refused_as_busy_with_no_flip_in_flight_reaches_the_screen`, `a_card_refusing_every_commit_is_sent_one_a_vblank`, `a_lost_vblank_event_is_taken_as_done_after_the_bound`, `a_display_powered_down_during_a_vblank_wait_ends_it_at_once`, `a_vblank_event_left_from_before_a_sleep_ends_no_later_flip`. On a card with no vblank interrupt the frame still waits for the next draw (vtium/vtium#176; test `a_frame_refused_on_a_card_without_vblank_events_goes_with_the_next_swap`).
- **A commit the cursor plane fails gives the plane up.** A commit that shows the cursor may fail for a reason other than busy. vtium then sends it again without the cursor. When that takes, vtium gives the cursor plane up and `hardware_cursor` turns false, so the owner draws the pointer. When it fails too, vtium keeps the cursor. Otherwise the next frame would commit the same cursor state, and a plane that refused it would keep the display frozen. No specification covers it. Tests: `a_commit_the_cursor_plane_fails_is_sent_without_it`, `a_cursor_only_commit_that_fails_gives_the_plane_up`, `a_commit_that_fails_either_way_keeps_the_cursor`.
- **An image larger than the cursor plane is refused, not cut.** `setup_cursor` refuses an image wider or taller than the plane, at most `CURSOR_MAX` a side, as `Unsupported`, so the owner draws it whole in software. No specification covers it. Test: `an_image_larger_than_the_plane_is_refused`.
- **Each of the card's lists is bounded, and one that changes between its two reads is refused.** vtium reads each list into a buffer of the length that the first read gave: a connector's modes and encoders, an object's properties, and the card's planes. Each is at most `MODES_MAX`, `ENCODERS_MAX`, `OBJECT_PROPERTIES_MAX` and `PLANES_MAX` in turn. A length that differs at the second read refuses the list until the next scan.
- **A monitor's power cycle skips the gone connector.** A connector the kernel lists but no longer answers for is skipped. An `EACCES` from the test-only commit, as while another process holds master, is expected, so it is logged at debug level and not tried again with default modes.
- **A card keeps DRM master with no display, and a display that returns is set from off.** While its VT is shown, an awake card keeps DRM master when its last display goes and when every display is dark. The kernel's console (its fbdev emulation, `drm_fb_helper_hotplug_event`) lights a display at a hotplug only while no process holds master. So it never takes a display plugged in meanwhile.

  A display that returns is one vtium finds after the card's first scan, plugged in or plugged back. Before its first modeset, vtium sends a blocking commit. That commit turns off the CRTC its connector is on. It also turns off the CRTC the display is about to take, if that one shows a mode. Both go off with their planes and every connector on them. The modeset after it then sets the display's mode, CRTC and connector anew. The kernel counts that as a full modeset (`drm_atomic_crtc_needs_modeset`), whatever mode another master or the console left there. A display on no CRTC, about to take a CRTC with no mode, needs no such commit, since its modeset starts from off already. A CRTC that a display online also drives, and keeps, is never turned off; the returning display only leaves it.

  A hotplug may move a lit display off its CRTC so that the returning display can take that CRTC. The commit then turns the CRTC off with the lit display's connector on it. The moved display stays dark until the modeset lights it on its new CRTC (the entry on hotplug moves below). With the legacy calls, vtium turns the connector's CRTC off through `Kms::disable_crtc`, which `Card` does not implement yet (vtium/vtium#156).

  Why: this was seen on this machine's amdgpu cards, in the operator's VT pass of 2026-10-30, read from `/sys/kernel/debug/dri/0/state`. The small panel was unplugged and plugged back while its card had let master go. The console lit it with the console's own framebuffer. It then stayed dark under vtium's frames, committed on the console's mode, until a VT switch set its mode again. The kernel does not state that a plane-only commit on a mode the console set leaves a display dark. That was observed here, and the full modeset is the defensive answer. Spec: the kernel's DRM master rules (`drm_auth.c`) and its full modeset rule. Tests: `a_display_plugged_back_in_shows_its_first_frame`, `a_display_plugged_back_in_on_a_crtc_left_on_shows_its_first_frame`, `a_display_a_console_lit_while_away_is_set_anew_and_shows`, `a_display_returning_on_a_crtc_another_display_drives_leaves_it_on`, `a_legacy_display_returning_turns_off_only_a_crtc_no_display_drives`, `the_first_scans_displays_are_set_without_an_off_commit`, `a_returned_display_is_set_from_off_only_once`, `every_display_dark_is_set_off_and_master_kept`.
- **A wake-up sets each display on the CRTC its connector is on.** The program that held the card while vtium's VT was away may have moved a display's connector to another CRTC, or turned the display off. A wake-up after the card's first scan reads which CRTC each connector is on. It then gives each display bound before one CRTC. Each display wants one CRTC: the CRTC its connector is on. Where its connector is on no CRTC, or the display returns, it wants the CRTC it had. A display that returns has its first modeset turn off the CRTC its connector is on.

  The wake-up places the displays in three groups. First come the displays whose connector is on a CRTC, then those whose connector is on none, then those that return. Within each group the displays bound before come first, then the displays new at that scan, each in the card's connector order. A display takes the CRTC it wants where no display took it. Otherwise it takes the first free CRTC that an encoder of its connector can drive. Where none is free, a matching step moves displays placed before it to other CRTCs they can drive. The step moves a display whose connector is on a CRTC only where nothing else frees one, and then moves as few such displays as it can. The CRTCs the displays held before are themselves a placement of every display, so the matching always finds one. A display it could not place would stay off, with a warning, and the owner would hear it removed.

  A display that moves takes the new CRTC's primary and cursor planes, so its pointer moves with it. Where the new CRTC has no cursor plane, the display gives its pointer image up and the owner draws the pointer. Where the new CRTC has no primary plane, the display stays off, with a warning, until a later scan finds it a CRTC. The owner hears it removed.

  Before every atomic modeset, vtium also turns off each CRTC that no display takes and that only the displays' connectors are on. It turns the CRTC off with its planes, and takes those connectors off it. vtium reads which CRTC each connector is on from the connector's `CRTC_ID`. It therefore finds such a CRTC even where it cannot read which CRTC an encoder drives. The kernel puts a connector on a CRTC only while that CRTC is enabled, so such a CRTC shows a mode. But it may be inactive (`ACTIVE` at 0), as a desktop's DPMS off leaves it. vtium turns these CRTCs off in the blocking commit it sends before the modeset, which asks for no flip event. The modeset asks a flip event of every CRTC in its commit. The kernel refuses a flip event on a CRTC that is off and stays off (`drm_atomic_crtc_check`). A CRTC that a connector no display drives is on stays as it is.

  vtium sees only the connectors that the card lists to it. vtium asks for no writeback connectors (`DRM_CLIENT_CAP_WRITEBACK_CONNECTORS`), so the card never lists a writeback connector to it. A writeback connector may share a CRTC with a display's connector. vtium then tries to turn that CRTC off with the writeback connector still on it, and the kernel refuses the commit (vtium/vtium#257). Sometimes vtium cannot read which CRTC one of the listed connectors is on. It then turns no CRTC off before that modeset, since that connector might share a CRTC with a display's connector.

  Why: this was seen on the maintainer's machine on 2026-11-03 (vtium/vtium#249). KWin left DP-8 on crtc-0, which was another CRTC than the one a vtium that had run all day had set it on. That vtium's wake-up put the connector back on its own CRTC, and left crtc-0 lit with no connector. The kernel refuses that with `EINVAL`, whatever mode the commit sets. So the retry with the default mode failed too, and no wake-up drew again. A vtium started fresh took the CRTC the connector was on and drew at once. Spec: the kernel's atomic check, which refuses a CRTC that stays enabled with no connector on it (`drm_atomic_helper_check_modeset`). Tests: `a_wake_up_follows_the_display_to_the_crtc_the_desktop_left_it_on`, `a_display_plugged_in_while_away_leaves_the_crtc_a_display_follows_to`, `a_display_the_desktop_turned_off_keeps_its_crtc`, `a_display_the_desktop_left_lit_keeps_its_crtc`, `a_display_on_no_crtc_keeps_its_own_where_it_is_free`, `a_wake_up_moves_a_display_off_the_only_crtc_another_can_take`, `a_wake_up_moves_a_display_left_off_before_one_left_lit`, `a_modeset_turns_off_a_lit_crtc_its_displays_connectors_leave`, `a_wake_up_that_cannot_read_the_encoder_turns_the_desktops_crtc_off`, `a_wake_up_turns_off_a_crtc_the_desktop_left_with_a_mode_but_inactive`, `a_crtc_a_displays_connector_shares_with_another_stays_on`, `a_connector_whose_crtc_cannot_be_read_keeps_every_crtc_on`, `a_display_moved_to_a_crtc_with_no_cursor_plane_gives_the_cursor_up`.
- **A display's modeset takes every other connector off its CRTC.** The placement knows only the connectors vtium binds. So a display can take a CRTC that another connector is still on. That connector may be a third monitor on a card with two CRTCs, for which the placement found no CRTC. It may also be a connector left dark, whose own commit the card refused. Two connectors on one CRTC are a clone. The kernel refuses a clone for encoders that cannot clone each other (`drm_atomic_check_valid_clones`), so the wake-up would fail. Each atomic modeset therefore reads every listed connector's `CRTC_ID`. It takes each connector vtium does not bind off a CRTC that a display takes. The legacy CRTC set does the same itself (`update_output_state`).

  vtium does not read which encoders can clone. So it also takes off a connector that could have stayed cloned with a display's connector. A mirror that a desktop left, such as two monitors on one CRTC, then ends, and that connector stays dark. vtium leaves as it is a connector whose properties it cannot read. It warns where that connector's encoder shows it on a CRTC a display takes, and the card then refuses the commit. Limit: the card never lists a writeback connector to vtium (see the wake-up entry above). A writeback connector therefore stays on a CRTC a display takes, and the card may then refuse the commit. This is rare (vtium/vtium#257). Why: a probe of the wake-up placement ended with two connectors bound to one CRTC (vtium/vtium#257). Spec: the kernel's atomic check, `drm_atomic_check_valid_clones`, called for each CRTC a commit sets anew (`drm_atomic_helper_check_modeset`). Tests: `a_display_takes_its_crtc_from_a_connector_vtium_does_not_bind`, `a_connector_whose_crtc_cannot_be_read_keeps_every_crtc_on`, `an_unreadable_connector_on_a_displays_crtc_is_warned_of`, and in `src/display/fake.rs` `a_commit_that_clones_encoders_that_cannot_clone_is_refused`.
- **A scan lets go of the displays that are gone before it binds new connectors, and the first scan places its displays together.** A scan first lets go of each display whose connector is gone. It then places the displays of the new connectors on the CRTCs left. A new display wants the CRTC that a display gone in the same scan held. It wants it where the two show the same monitor and no other new display took that CRTC back first. Where both connectors have a `PATH`, which a DisplayPort multi-stream port keeps, the same monitor means the same `PATH`. Two monitors of one model with no serial number read the same EDID, which is why the `PATH` decides. Where neither connector has a `PATH`, the same monitor means the same EDID. So a monitor that drops for a moment and comes back under a new connector id takes back the CRTC its old display held, whichever CRTC that is. On a card with one CRTC, it is bound at that scan.

  A hotplug's scan keeps each lit display on its CRTC unless moving it is the only way to light a new display (the next entry). The first scan places every display the way a wake-up does (the entry above). Where the card has a CRTC for each display, the first scan therefore places every display. That holds even where a desktop left one display lit on the only CRTC another display can drive.

  A display gone lets its CRTC go only by freeing its framebuffers. On amdgpu, removing the framebuffer a CRTC shows makes the kernel turn that CRTC off and take its connectors off it (`atomic_remove_fb`, drm_framebuffer.c). That releases a multi-stream connector's share of the link (`drm_dp_atomic_release_time_slots`). Some drivers, such as i915, keep the CRTC on when its framebuffer goes. There the CRTC stays lit with the gone connector on it, holding that share of the link, until a commit takes the connector off. vtium makes that commit only when it can still read the connector, at the modeset that puts a display on that CRTC. vtium/vtium#156 carries turning off a CRTC through the legacy calls, which take every connector off it.

  Why: this was seen on the maintainer's machine on 2026-11-03 (vtium/vtium#252). DP-8 dropped and came back under a new connector id. The old display still held the desktop's CRTC when the new one was bound, so the new one went onto another CRTC. That led to the failed wake-ups of vtium/vtium#249. Spec: vtium/vtium#252. Tests: `a_connector_back_under_a_new_id_keeps_its_crtc`, `a_connector_back_under_a_new_id_on_a_one_crtc_card_binds_at_once`, `a_connector_back_under_a_new_id_takes_back_a_crtc_that_is_not_the_first`, `a_monitor_back_under_a_new_id_with_its_edid_takes_back_its_crtc`, `monitors_of_one_model_back_on_their_ports_take_back_their_own_crtcs`, `a_fresh_start_on_the_desktops_leftovers_places_every_display`.
- **A hotplug moves a lit display to another CRTC only where that is the only way to light a new one.** At a hotplug, and at the retry of a failed one, vtium places every display together, as a wake-up does. Each display bound before wants the CRTC it has. A lit display stays on its CRTC whenever every new display can light without it moving. A new display takes a free CRTC where its connector's encoders drive one. Otherwise vtium moves a display that is not lit yet, such as another one plugged in at the same hotplug.

  Sometimes the only CRTCs a new display can drive are held by lit displays. If a lit display can move to another CRTC that one of its connector's encoders drives, vtium moves that display, and the new display takes the CRTC it left. Of the placements that light the new displays, vtium takes one that moves the fewest lit displays. Of those, it takes one that leaves the fewest other displays off the CRTC they want, such as a monitor that returns wanting the CRTC it held. vtium places the displays one at a time. Each goes along the cheapest chain of moves that frees a CRTC for it, which is the step of the Hungarian method. So the placement stays the cheapest for the displays placed so far, however many are plugged in at one hotplug. The modeset after the hotplug sets the moved display's mode on its new CRTC anew. The moved display therefore blanks once, and then lights beside the new one, showing what it showed before.

  The card may refuse that modeset and every other placement of the displays. A moved display then goes back to the CRTC it was moved off, and lights there. The new display stays off with a warning. The entry on a display that cannot be set, below, says which placements vtium tries and which displays go back. Suppose the card refused a display's new CRTC, and not for a reason that may pass. And suppose a new display sat on the CRTC that this display was lit on and goes back to. vtium then records that new display's connector. A new display left off only because another set failed for a reason that may pass is not recorded. With atomic commits, the card refuses with `EINVAL` or `ENOSPC` from the commit. With the legacy calls, which set each display on its own, it refuses with `EINVAL` from a moved display's CRTC set, and any other error may pass.

  Until that connector is unplugged, or a modeset lights its display, a hotplug moves no lit display for that display. vtium places the other displays first, moving lit displays for them as it would. It then gives the refused display a CRTC only where one is left free. So the lit displays do not blank at every hotplug for a display that the card cannot light beside them, and another display plugged in later still lights. A move whose new display is unplugged before the move's modeset runs is undone. The display goes back to the CRTC vtium last set it on, and is not blanked. Where no placement gives the new display a CRTC, nothing moves and nothing is committed. The new display stays dark, with an error in the log, and each later hotplug tries again.

  While the displays are blanked, a hotplug moves no lit display. Its modeset waits for the input that ends the blank. A display with no DPMS property keeps drawing while blanked, and must keep flipping on the CRTC its connector is on. A new display that can light only by moving a lit one then waits for that input. The scan at that input moves the lit display and lights both. A display that must move while a flip of its own is in flight moves once that flip is done. The flip's event comes for the CRTC the display left, which the new display takes, and vtium gives the event to the display whose flip it is.

  Why: the maintainer decided that a hotplug may move a lit display so that a new one lights, as a desktop such as KWin does at a hotplug (vtium/vtium#268). Until then a hotplug kept each lit display where it was. So a new display that could drive only a lit display's CRTC stayed dark at every hotplug until the next wake-up. Spec: vtium/vtium#268. Tests: `a_hotplug_moves_a_lit_display_when_that_is_the_only_way_to_light_the_new_one`, `a_hotplug_moves_no_lit_display_where_the_new_ones_light_without_it`, `a_hotplug_moves_as_few_lit_displays_as_it_can`, `a_hotplug_that_cannot_light_the_new_display_moves_nothing`, `a_hotplug_moves_a_lit_display_once_its_flip_is_in`, `a_lit_display_the_card_refuses_on_its_new_crtc_goes_back`, `a_chain_of_lit_displays_the_card_refuses_goes_back`, `a_refused_connector_leaves_later_hotplugs_free_to_move_for_others`, `a_refused_connector_unplugged_and_back_moves_a_lit_display_again`, `a_moved_display_set_on_its_new_crtc_stays_there`, `a_move_whose_new_display_leaves_before_its_modeset_is_undone`, `a_move_undone_while_the_encoders_cannot_be_read_puts_the_display_back`, `a_modeset_that_fails_for_a_passing_reason_records_no_refusal`, `a_legacy_set_that_fails_for_a_passing_reason_records_no_refusal`, `a_refused_display_takes_the_first_free_crtc_it_can_drive`, `a_hotplug_while_blanked_moves_no_lit_display_until_input`, and in `src/display/drm2d/matching.rs` `the_matching_places_three_displays_on_three_crtcs_at_the_least_cost`, `the_matching_places_four_or_five_displays_on_four_crtcs_at_the_least_cost`, `two_displays_plugged_in_at_once_move_as_few_lit_displays_as_they_can`, `a_returning_monitor_keeps_the_crtc_it_wants_from_a_lit_display_moved_there`.
- **Each modeset sets the connector's color state for the 8-bit sRGB vtium draws.** At each modeset vtium sets three properties of the connector, each only where the connector has it. It sets `Colorspace` to `Default` and `HDR_OUTPUT_METADATA` to no blob. It sets `max bpc` to the bits per color of the format vtium's buffers are made in, which is 8 for XRGB8888. Where the connector's range for `max bpc` does not hold 8, vtium sets the value of the range nearest 8. Where it cannot read the range, it sets 8. The atomic modeset sets the three properties in its commit, so its test-only commit checks them with the mode. The legacy calls set each on its own before they set the CRTC, as they set DPMS.

  The test-only commit may fail with every connector's properties and pass without them. vtium then adds each connector's properties back in connector order, as many as the card takes beside those already added: all three, else all but `max bpc`, else none. So a connector whose properties the card refuses costs no other connector its reset. A property that the legacy calls cannot set is left as it is. Either way the display is set, and a property vtium could not set stays as vtium found it. vtium logs a warning that names the connector that refused, and says which of its properties stay as vtium found them. It logs that warning once for each connector, and a debug line after that. vtium restores nothing when it gives the card up at a VT switch. The next program to hold the card sets what it uses.

  Why: a desktop with HDR on leaves its connector set for HDR. The monitor then reads vtium's 8-bit sRGB frames as HDR content, and may show them washed out or too bright. On the maintainer's machine, KWin left DP-8 at `Colorspace=BT2020_RGB`, with HDR metadata set and 16 bits per color asked for. The kernel logged `HDR SB:` lines at vtium's own modesets (vtium/vtium#253).

  Spec: ADR-1401 D14, from the maintainer's decision on vtium/vtium#253 of 2026-11-03. The kernel documents the connector properties in `drivers/gpu/drm/drm_connector.c`. Under that documentation, a client sets `HDR_OUTPUT_METADATA` when it composes HDR content for a sink it has found HDR-capable. `Colorspace` tells the driver which color encoding the client produces, and `Default` leaves the encoding to the driver. `max bpc` limits the bits per color that the driver sends. The kernel's rule for new properties (`Documentation/gpu/drm-kms.rst`) says that a property's initial state matches the behaviour from before the property existed. Tests: `each_modeset_sets_the_color_state_a_desktop_left_back_to_8_bit_srgb`, `a_connector_without_the_color_properties_sets_its_mode`, `a_color_state_the_driver_refuses_leaves_the_display_set`, `a_color_property_one_connector_refuses_costs_no_other_its_reset`, `each_connector_that_refuses_is_warned_of_once`, `a_max_bpc_range_without_8_takes_its_nearest_value`, `a_legacy_modeset_sets_the_color_state_back_to_8_bit_srgb`.
- **A display that cannot be set beside the others is left off, and the others keep running.** Before vtium leaves any display off, it tries other placements of all the displays. Each placement puts each display on a CRTC its connector's encoders drive, with a lit display moved where that helps. vtium keeps the first placement that the card takes. It tries each placement in one test-only commit. That commit also carries what the modeset would turn off first, so the search turns no lit display off. Only the placement that the test takes is set. vtium first tries the placements that put no lit display off the CRTC it wants, then those that put one off. Within each group it tries first the placements that put the fewest other displays off their CRTCs.

  The search is bounded. It considers only placements that move at most one lit display. It lists them in at most 4096 steps, where a step puts one display on one CRTC. It tries at most 16, each once, with no retry and no delay. A failure that may pass ends the search. Eight displays that can each drive eight CRTCs have 40320 placements, so the bound keeps a refused hotplug from stalling the loop. For example, suppose B is lit on CRTC 100 and can also drive 102, and A can drive 100 and 101 but the card refuses 101. vtium then moves B to 102 and lights A on 100, at a wake-up or at the hotplug that plugs A in.

  The card may refuse every placement. Each display then goes back where the placement put it. A new display that a placement tried on another CRTC is recorded as refused, as the entry above describes, so later hotplugs do not search for it again. The rest of this entry says what happens then.

  The legacy calls have no test-only commit, so there vtium tries a placement by setting it. It first sets the lit display that the placement moves, alone, on its new CRTC. The kernel sets a CRTC all or nothing. `drm_atomic_helper_set_config` checks the new state before it commits it, and `drm_crtc_helper_set_config` puts the old state back where a set fails. So where the card refuses that set, the lit display stays lit where it was, and vtium gives the placement up. Where the card takes it, the display lights on its new CRTC and stays there, whether or not the display it moved for then lights. vtium then sets each display still unset on the CRTC the placement gives it.

  The legacy search runs only where the card refused, with `EINVAL`, every display the modeset could not set. It does not run where each of those displays is recorded as refused. It skips three kinds of placement. It skips one that moves more than one lit display, and one that moves a lit display it already moved. It also skips one that sets a display on a CRTC the card refused it in that search. So a lit display blanks at most once in a search, as at a hotplug that moves it. It never ends dark, even where a second move would light another display. The step after the search sends a moved display back to the CRTC it was moved off. That step may still try a display where the search found the card refusing it.

  Where no placement lights every display, each display still unset takes back the CRTC that the placement gave it. Otherwise it takes a CRTC that no other display holds. Each one that a placement tried on another CRTC is recorded as refused. A modeset that lights a display drops its record. The legacy calls say which display the card refused. So once these steps are done, each display the card still refuses is left off, present but dark, whether or not vtium moved it. The others light with no retry. Where the card refuses every display, they stay bound and unset. The retries try them again, as they try an atomic modeset that the card refuses whole. So does a display whose set failed for a reason that may pass. A card that the kernel removes during the search ends the search at once. Why: a display left off stayed dark for good where the only free CRTC for it was one the card refused. The placement had kept the lit displays where they were (vtium/vtium#298).

  The modeset of the displays online and the new ones may fail with the configured and the default modes. The new ones are then left off until the next hotplug, with a warning, and the displays online are set alone. The hotplug's or the wake-up's placement may have put a display bound before off the CRTC it wanted. That display goes back to that CRTC before the others are set. A display new to that modeset never goes back, and that includes one bound before but never set. At a wake-up, the CRTC a display wants is the one its connector is on. Where its connector is on none, it is the display's own CRTC. A display does not go back where a display that stays where it is placed holds that CRTC.

  Several displays may want to go back to one CRTC. Then one goes: the one that was lit there. Where both or neither were lit there, the first in the card's connector order goes. The lit display goes first because of wake-ups. At a wake-up, the display lit on a CRTC is the one that the program that held the card left on it, so that CRTC is known to drive it. At a hotplug every display that can go back was online, and so lit, and connector order decides. A display that cannot go back stays on its new CRTC and holds it in turn. So a display that would go back to that CRTC stays too, and vtium repeats the test until no more displays stay. No two displays therefore end on one CRTC.

  A failed atomic commit does not say which CRTC the card refused. So where the card refuses that choice too, each display that went back in it loses the tie, and the other goes back instead. vtium tries each choice once. Where the card refuses every choice, vtium first sets the displays without one display that stays on its new CRTC, the last in connector order first. It then sets them without all such displays, and leaves those off, so the others light. Once a set lights, no retry runs. The same holds for a wake-up that moved a display with no new display among them. A new display left off is recorded as refused only once a choice without it lights. vtium leaves off only displays that the placement moved. The card may refuse even the displays it did not move, as when it refuses every CRTC. The modeset then fails, and the wake-up retries try the choices again until `WakeFailed`. A display left off is placed again at each later scan. It stays present but dark meanwhile, as the next entry says.

  With the legacy calls, which set each display on its own, vtium knows which sets failed. A moved display that cannot be set on its new CRTC goes back. So does each moved display on the CRTC that it goes back to, which must leave that CRTC. A moved display set on its new CRTC, in no one's way, stays there. Only a display new to that modeset is left off, where it sits on the CRTC that a moved display goes back to. vtium sets the displays again only where a display goes back. ## The GPU path

The `gpu` feature adds the GPU path's platform half (ADR-1303 D7, D8). The minspec and default builds compile none of it and link no GL library. vtium-render draws through its `Gl` trait, vtium-platform holds the context, and the binary joins the two with an adapter (`crates/vtium/src/gpu.rs`), so this crate builds without vtium-render. `cargo test -p vtium-platform --features gpu` runs its tests.

`Context` is a GL context and the calls vtium's renderer needs of it, each a safe method. It needs OpenGL ES 3.0 or later, with `highp` floats and integers in fragment shaders. It makes its context with no surface, current on the thread that made it. It is not `Send`, so it stays on that thread. Every method makes its own context current first. A call therefore never reaches another context that the thread made current since.

- **The set-up** of a head's context, `Context::on_render_node`, runs the steps that ADR-1303 D9 orders. It stops at the first that fails, which `SetupError` names. The steps are these: the render node opened, libgbm opened and a GBM device made over the node, libEGL opened, and the EGL display on GBM's platform initialized. Then come its extensions (`EGL_KHR_surfaceless_context`), the context, its version, and `highp`. glow panics on a `GL_VERSION` it cannot read. So vtium reads the version through a `glGetString` of its own before glow loads its function table. A version that does not read as OpenGL ES 3.0 or later fails the version's step. Dithering is turned off.

  A render node needs no DRM master. `render_node` finds a card's render node from sysfs, by the card's device number, without opening the card. The set-up refuses, at the display's step, a render node whose EGL device is Mesa's software renderer. Mesa's GBM falls back to llvmpipe on a device whose driver it lacks, as `/dev/null` shows in the tests. A head drawn through llvmpipe would gain nothing over the CPU path.
- **No library is linked.** libEGL is opened as `libEGL.so.1` through khronos-egl's `dynamic` feature, which needs EGL 1.5. It is never opened as the unversioned `libEGL.so` that a development package installs. libgbm is opened through `src/gpu/gbm.rs`, which takes the eleven calls vtium makes by name. A `gpu` build therefore starts on a machine without either library, and its set-up fails at the step that needs it.
- **The tests' context,** `Context::software`, is Mesa's llvmpipe through EGL's device platform, with no GBM and no dma-buf. Its device is the one whose extensions name `EGL_MESA_device_software`. Mesa's surfaceless platform picks the machine's GPU where it has one, as it does on this project's development machines. So only the software device draws the same pixels everywhere.
- **Textures** are made with `glTexStorage2D`: one level of `GL_R8` or `GL_RGBA8`, with nearest filters, clamped at the edges. A texture is therefore complete whatever samples it. The context keeps each texture's format and size. It records them even where GL refused the storage, since an upload into such a texture only raises `GL_INVALID_OPERATION` and reads nothing.

  The context checks an upload against them before GL sees it:

  - the texture is one the context made and has not freed;
  - the rectangle lies inside it;
  - the format gives the bytes a pixel;
  - the stride holds whole pixels and a whole row;
  - the slice holds every row, `(h - 1) * stride + w * bpp` bytes.

  It then sets `GL_UNPACK_ALIGNMENT` to 1, `GL_UNPACK_ROW_LENGTH` to the stride in pixels, and no skipped pixels or rows, with no buffer bound for unpacking. So GL reads exactly those bytes.
- **An upright frame** is an RGBA8 texture with a framebuffer over it. **An imported buffer** is a dma-buf made into an EGL image (`EGL_EXT_image_dma_buf_import`). Where the buffer names a modifier, the import passes it through `EGL_EXT_image_dma_buf_import_modifiers`. The image is bound as the storage of a renderbuffer (`GL_OES_EGL_image`) and attached to a framebuffer. The context refuses a framebuffer that GL calls incomplete. For an import, the refusal is an error of kind `Import`, which ADR-1303 D9 answers with a staging copy.

  The context reads at once the flags that GL raises while it binds the image, so the next check does not report them against a frame. A lost context and out of memory keep their kinds, which leave the GPU path. Any other error fails the import. The context first sets aside, for the next check, the flags an earlier call left, so they fail no import. A program's shaders that do not compile give both logs. A uniform's name with a NUL in it is refused.
- **A draw** is one instanced draw of a triangle strip of four vertices for each quad. Each quad's `dst` is at attribute location 0 as an `ivec4`, through `glVertexAttribIPointer`, and its `src` and `params` at 1 and 2 as `uvec4`s, each with a divisor of 1. The draw uploads its quads into the instance buffer first, so the buffer holds exactly the instances drawn and no attribute reads past it. Textures take units in the order the draw lists them, each sampled through a sampler object of the draw's filter; a draw of more textures than `GL_MAX_TEXTURE_IMAGE_UNITS` is refused. A uniform the driver dropped has no location and is not set. `Replace` turns blending off, and `Over` blends with `GL_ONE`, `GL_ONE_MINUS_SRC_ALPHA`. The viewport is the target's size, and the scissor is set where the draw has one. A clear sets a scissor to its rectangle and clears to its color, opaque.
- **Submit and wait** end a frame. `Context::submit` makes an EGL fence sync after the frame's commands (`eglCreateSync` with `EGL_SYNC_FENCE`). `Context::wait` waits on it with `eglClientWaitSync` before the render thread gives the buffer back to the display. The kernel waits on a dma-buf's implicit fence only once the driver has sent it the frame's work. Mesa's glthread may return from `glFlush` before then, so a flip right after a flush could show an unfinished frame.

  Passing the fence's descriptor with an atomic commit as `IN_FENCE_FD` would let the kernel wait instead. But a card without atomic commits flips through the legacy page flip, which takes no fence. The wait on the render thread serves both kinds of card, and keeps the descriptor out of the token. Some displays lack `EGL_KHR_fence_sync` at EGL 1.5, and some GLs lack `GL_OES_EGL_sync`. There the frame ends with `glFinish` in place of the fence (`Context::fence_syncs`). Tests: `a_frame_ends_with_a_fence_sync_that_a_wait_takes_once`.
- **Check** reads every error flag that GL holds, at most 16. It also reads the reset status, where the driver reports one (`GL_EXT_robustness`, `GL_KHR_robustness` or OpenGL ES 3.2). It sorts them in this order: a lost context first, as `GL_CONTEXT_LOST` or a reset, then `GL_OUT_OF_MEMORY`, and then the first other error with its code. The first two are the errors that leave the GPU path. The context asks for a reset to be reported where EGL offers it (`EGL_EXT_create_context_robustness`).

  `raise` is hidden from the documentation and is for tests alone. It makes the next call that reaches GL fail with an error of a kind the test chooses, and that includes a check. It touches no GL, as vtium-render's stand-in GL does, since no real driver runs out of memory or loses its context on demand.
- **A read** checks that its slice is exactly the rectangle in RGBA8 and sets `GL_PACK_ALIGNMENT` to 1 and no row length, with no buffer bound for packing.
- **A buffer to import** comes from a `Gbm` device. A display allocates each buffer it shows as a `Bo`, and exports it as a `DmaBuf`, which the GPU path's `Token` carries (`Token::new_dma_buf`, `Token::dma_buf`). No display allocates one yet. drm2d's GBM buffers come with the work that chooses the path (ADR-1303 D11, part 6).

The unsafe sites, each with an `#[expect(unsafe_code)]` and its reason:

- `src/gpu/gbm.rs`, in four groups:
  - opening libgbm and taking its calls by name;

## The VT

A seat has one `Vt` backend. With the `libseat` feature, the backend is the seat manager's (`LibseatVt`, see [The seat through libseat](#the-seat-through-libseat)) when one answers. Otherwise it is the kernel's VT (`KernelVt`), or the fake VT (`FakeVt`) on a system without `/dev/tty0`. Each reports `VtEvent`s, which the owner drains once a turn.

- **Opening.** The VT is the configured one, or the one stderr is, or the first free one (`VT_OPENQRY`). vtium opens it read-write, and never as the controlling terminal. Its number is the device's minor, logged as `vt: using tty /dev/ttyN`. `KernelVt::new` first adds SIGUSR1 and SIGUSR2 to the loop's signalfd, which blocks them. So a switch before the loop runs waits there and ends nothing. `KernelVt::new` then puts the VT in graphics mode, and takes its switching with `VT_PROCESS` (SIGUSR1 to acquire, SIGUSR2 to release).

  It saves the keyboard mode. An earlier program may have left `K_OFF`, `K_RAW` or `K_MEDIUMRAW`, as a program that ended without its own restore leaves it. vtium then saves `K_UNICODE` in its place, so the console's keyboard works after vtium (the operator's decision on vtium/vtium#19). It sets `K_RAW`, then `K_OFF`, so keys reach only evdev. A refused `K_OFF` leaves `K_RAW`, with a warning. It saves the tty's line settings, and puts the tty in raw mode with no echo. A refusal leaves the tty as it was, with a warning. A VT shown at the open reports `Acquired` at the first drain.
- **The guard.** `KernelVt` owns the VT's state. Dropping it puts back the tty's saved line settings, the saved keyboard mode, `VT_AUTO` and `KD_TEXT`, each only where it set them. So an open that fails half way, a setup that fails after the open, and a return from the loop all restore them. Once the VT is put back, its drop sets SIGUSR1 and SIGUSR2 to be ignored. The loop's signalfd unblocks them when it drops, and the ignored dispositions mean that no pending release ends the process then. The VT therefore drops before the `Loop`.

  The release and minspec profiles abort on a panic without running `Drop`. `KernelVt::restorer` gives a `Restore`, which holds its own handle on the VT and restores once however often it runs. A run in a second thread waits for the first to finish before its hook returns and the process aborts. The panic hook that runs it, and the SIGTERM and SIGINT handling that ends the loop, are the binary's (vtium/vtium#21). Nothing in the process runs at SIGKILL, the OOM killer or a stack overflow. At the next switch away, the kernel finds the VT's `VT_PROCESS` owner gone. It then puts the VT back to text mode, its default keyboard mode and `VT_AUTO` (vt_ioctl.c, `reset_vc`). A getty started on the VT with `TTYReset` does the same.
- **Release and acquire.** SIGUSR2 for this VT, while the VT is ours, reports `Release` and answers nothing. The owner puts the input and the displays to sleep. It then calls `acknowledge_release` once every display reported `Event::Asleep`, or `refuse_release` to stay. A pending flip delays the `Asleep` by at most `FLIP_WAIT_MS`, with the loop running. The kernel may refuse the answer, as after another process set the VT's switching while the release waited. The release is then gone, and vtium takes the VT again, `VT_PROCESS` included, so the next switch to it signals the acquire.

  On a VT still shown, an acknowledgement reports `Acquired` and a refusal reports `Retaken`. On a VT no longer shown, an acknowledgement reports nothing and a refusal reports `Release`. The acknowledgement of that `Release` then decides in the same way. So a switch back while the owner sleeps reports `Acquired` only at that acknowledgement. Either answer fails only when `VT_GETSTATE` fails too. A release while the VT is not ours is allowed at once.

  SIGUSR1 for this VT reports `Acquired` and answers nothing, while `VT_GETSTATE` shows the VT. While a release waits, SIGUSR1 is ignored. The kernel needs no `VT_ACKACQ` after switching to a VT. While a switch away is pending, it takes any answer but a refusal as allowing it (`vt_reldisp`, drivers/tty/vt/vt_ioctl.c). So an acknowledged acquire with a release pending, as after a switch in and straight out, would let the switch away finish. The VT would then be marked active, with the displays awake on another VT's screen. On an acquire the owner wakes the input and the displays, and the wake-up counts as user activity. Signals for another VT are ignored.
- **Keys.** First in the key order, `Vt::key` takes Ctrl+Alt+F1 to F12 and `XF86Switch_VT_1` to 12, while the VT is ours and shown, and asks the kernel for that VT. Unless `VT_GETMODE` shows vtium's own `VT_PROCESS` (SIGUSR2 to release, SIGUSR1 to acquire), the key sets it first. The kernel drops a switch from a VT that another process left in `VT_AUTO` and graphics mode. It sends the release to another process's `VT_PROCESS` (`set_console`, drivers/tty/vt/vt.c). So without the key's own `VT_PROCESS`, the key would be taken and nothing visible would happen. vtium's own `VT_PROCESS` is left as it is, so a release not yet read still completes. vtium cannot tell itself from another process that set `VT_PROCESS` with vtium's signals.

  This VT's own key is taken and does nothing. While the VT is ours and shown, `Vt::is_own_key` says that a key is this VT's own, for the rescue (ADR-1401 D9), and switches nothing. The fake VT takes Ctrl+Super+F12, which gives it up or takes it back. It reports no VT number and has no own key. A fake VT that a test numbers (`FakeVt::with_number`) has the kernel VT's own key for that number while it is ours.
- **The tty.** vtium keeps its VT's tty silent, and re-takes the VT if anything arrives on it. A re-take sets graphics mode, `VT_PROCESS`, the keyboard off, and the tty silent again. vtium tries each on its own, so one refusal leaves the rest set. The settings found at the open stay the ones that a drop puts back. vtium sets `VT_PROCESS` only where it is not vtium's own, since `VT_SETMODE` would cancel a switch the kernel waits on. The kernel waits on vtium only in vtium's own `VT_PROCESS`. A tty that the open could not silence is left as it is, then and at every exit. What arrived is dropped (`TCIFLUSH`).

  A re-take while the VT is ours reports `VtEvent::Retaken`. A re-take while a release waits reports it if the owner refuses the release. A re-take while the VT is not ours reports nothing, since the next `Acquired` sets everything up. The exception is a VT that is shown with no acquire signal, which the re-take then reports. vtium logs the first re-take as a warning, and also the first after `RETAKE_QUIET_MS` with none. It counts the others at debug level. `Vt::restore` sets the same modes in the same way. A hangup, or a flush that fails as on a hung-up tty, reports `Hangup` once, and every later switch fails with `EPIPE`.
- **The VT shown.** `KernelVt::new` watches `/sys/class/tty/tty0/active`. That file names the VT the kernel shows, and notifies on every switch. vtium reads it from its start after each notification. The read is edge-triggered, because sysfs reports the attribute readable at every poll. The kernel notifies before it signals an acquire, and whichever of the two the loop reads second changes nothing.

  This VT may be shown while it is not ours, as when another process set `VT_AUTO` while it was not ours. vtium then takes it again and reports `Acquired`, with no input on the tty needed. Another VT may be shown while this one is ours: a switch the kernel made with no release, as under `VT_AUTO`. vtium reports that as `Release`. The owner sleeps and acknowledges, and that acknowledgement decides as for any release the kernel no longer waits on. So the VT is taken again in the background, and a switch back while the owner sleeps is acquired only at the acknowledgement. While a release waits, the answer decides.

  Several vtium processes, one per VT, each read the same attribute, and each acts only for its own VT. Several sessions in one process are vtium/vtium#82. The attribute exists only on the seat with kernel VTs, and the fake VT does not open it. If vtium cannot open it, vtium logs a warning once and runs without it. A switch made with no signal then goes unseen until the next signal, key or input on the tty.
- **Switching.** `activate` asks for this VT unless it is shown. `leave`, at exit, asks for the VT that was shown when `activate` asked for this one. It does not ask when this VT is not shown, or was that VT. While the request is pending, the loop serves SIGUSR2. A vtium that never asked for its VT switches nothing at exit, so stopping or restarting it on the VT shown leaves that VT shown. A vtium started with `--no-switchvt` is one that never asks: it waits for its VT instead. `wait` says whether the VT is shown, and an `Acquired` or a `Hangup` ends the wait with the loop running.

## The seat through libseat

With the `libseat` feature, vtium can be a client of a seat manager, seatd or logind, which owns the seat's display devices and its VT switching (ADR-1400's L2 layer, vtium/vtium#34). `LibseatVt` asks it through libseat. Without the feature, or when no seat manager answers, vtium runs on the kernel's VT as above.

- **Choosing it.** `LibseatVt::start` looks once at whether seatd listens: whether a connection to seatd's socket (`SEATD_SOCK`, else `/run/seatd.sock`) is accepted. The connection opens no seat in seatd. Then `start` tries to open the seat. An answer that says the seat is busy keeps the seat, pending (see Retries). Any other error means no seat manager answered: `start` hands the VT back, and the binary uses the kernel's VT or the fake VT.
- **The VT shown.** seatd gives a new client the VT shown when the client opens the seat. So vtium sometimes waits before it opens the seat: when vtium has a VT, the VT is not shown, and seatd listens. vtium then opens the seat only once its VT is shown. It watches tty0's `active` attribute, which the kernel notifies at every switch. The kernel's VT backend reads the same attribute (see [The VT](#the-vt)). vtium opens the seat at the loop's turn that reads its own VT there, and no timer runs while it waits. `activate` asks the kernel to show the VT meanwhile. If vtium cannot watch the attribute, it cannot learn when its VT is shown. Opening the seat anyway would give vtium the session of the VT shown, so vtium logs an error and reports `Hangup`, which ends it. The kernel's VT backend runs on with a warning in that case, because the kernel still signals each switch to it. The seat backend gets no such signal, and looking at the VT on a timer would bring the timer back. logind takes the session from vtium's login session instead, so without seatd the seat opens at once. Tests: `with_seatd_the_seat_opens_at_the_turn_that_reads_its_vt_shown`, `with_seatd_a_vt_shown_that_cannot_be_watched_hangs_up`.
- **Retries.** No retry waits on the loop. While libseat's answer says that the seat is still held by its previous client, the seat stays pending. Those answers are `EBUSY` and `EBADF`, and also `ENOSYS`, `EPIPE` and `EIO` while seatd listens. seatd 0.9.3 refuses a client while another holds the seat by hanging up without an error. libseat answers that hang-up `EPIPE` when `LIBSEAT_BACKEND` names seatd. Otherwise it answers `ENOSYS`, once its other backends have failed too. seatd refuses the new client while another client is active on the seat. The refusal ends when seatd removes that client, at the client's hang-up or close of the seat. No event says when that happens, because seatd sends nothing to a process that is not its client. So vtium tries a pending seat again from a loop timer every `SEAT_RETRY_MS`, at most `SEAT_OPENS_MAX` times. With seatd, vtium tries the seat only while its VT is shown, since seatd gives a new client the session of the VT shown. When another VT is shown at a try, vtium makes no try, and waits for its VT to be shown again as above. Test: `a_busy_seat_is_tried_again_only_while_its_vt_is_shown`. Past that, or on any other error once vtium has chosen the seat, vtium logs an error and reports `Hangup`, which ends it.

  logind answers `EBUSY` for a card while another process holds the card's DRM master (`session_device_open`). The kernel sends no event when a DRM master is dropped. So vtium tries a device that the seat manager answers `EBUSY` for in the same way, at most `DEVICE_OPENS_MAX` times. `ENODEV` and `EACCES` are final. A card that the kernel is still adding answers `ENODEV`, but the device monitor reports a card only at the event that finds the card open (see [Devices](#devices)). Both seat managers open a device as root, so neither answers `EACCES` for a while and then opens. seatd 0.9.3 opens no framebuffer, and answers `ENOENT` for one. logind answers `ENODEV`. So a framebuffer through the seat fails at the first ask. `Vt::open_device` answers `WouldBlock` meanwhile. `Vt::settled` names the device once it opened or failed, so the owner asks again. vtium does not ask the seat manager for a device before the seat is enabled, since the seat manager refuses it then. It asks at the enable. seatd answers `EPERM` once it has begun to disable vtium and before vtium has read the disable. A device that the seat manager answers `EPERM` for waits for the next enable in the same way.
- **Enable and disable.** The seat's descriptor is in the loop, and libseat dispatches it when it is readable. An enable reports `Acquired`. It puts the VT in graphics mode with the keyboard off, which logind leaves on. A disable reports `Release`. vtium acknowledges the disable to the seat manager once the owner calls `acknowledge_release`, after its displays sleep. It then puts the VT back in text mode, with the keyboard mode found at the start (`K_UNICODE` in place of `K_OFF`, `K_RAW` or `K_MEDIUMRAW`). libseat has no refusal, so `refuse_release` acknowledges too. libseat's logind backend can send an enable while the acknowledgement waits. vtium reports that enable after the acknowledgement, so the owner always sees its release finish before the next acquire. A failed dispatch, as when the seat manager closes the connection, reports `Hangup`. Without that, libseat's level-triggered descriptor would wake the loop without end.
- **Keys and switching.** While the seat is vtium's, Ctrl+Alt+F1 to F12 and `XF86Switch_VT_1` to 12 ask the seat manager for that session (`libseat_switch_session`). This VT's own key is taken and does nothing, and `is_own_key` names it as the kernel VT does. `leave` asks the seat manager for the VT shown when `activate` asked for this one. It asks for nothing when vtium never asked, as under `--no-switchvt`. `wait` is `Done` once the seat is enabled.
- **Devices.** A card or framebuffer opened through the seat comes with a second descriptor for the owner. That descriptor shares the open file, so the seat manager's revoke and its DRM master reach both. `Vt::close_device` closes it through the seat manager when the owner removes it. The keyboards and pointers open through the seat too, once vtium calls `Input::open_through` with `LibseatVt::input` (ADR-1400's L2 layer); see [Input](#input) for how they open and close.
- **The drop** closes the devices through the seat manager, puts a VT that was vtium's back in text mode with the keyboard mode found, and closes the seat. A panic aborts without the drop; the seat manager then finds its client gone and resets the VT itself.

## Devices

`Udev` enumerates the `drm`, `graphics` and `input` subsystems at `scan`, and then reads udev's monitor socket from the loop. It keeps a card named `cardN`, a framebuffer `fbN`, and an `eventN` node with an input parent when their `ID_SEAT` is the seat's, which is `seat0` when unset. It reports each as `DeviceEvent::Added`. An event node's seat is its parent input device's, at an add and at every `change`, since an event node rarely carries an `ID_SEAT` of its own. Reading the node's own at a `change` would remove and add the node again at each change, on any seat but `seat0`. A card or framebuffer of another seat is skipped before it is opened to read its flags.

While a udev daemon runs (`/run/udev/control` is present), the scan skips a device that udev has not set up yet. An example is a card whose driver is still loading when vtium starts. Its database entry, which holds `ID_PATH` and `ID_SEAT`, is not written yet, and udev's `add` event reports the device once it is. A node added twice is reported once. Another device may take over a node, as a GPU's driver takes over the boot framebuffer's card node. Its `add` event then reports the old device removed and the new one added, with the new card's own `ID_PATH`.

The kernel sends a card's `add` event before an open of the card can succeed. `drm_minor_register` (`drivers/gpu/drm/drm_drv.c`) calls `device_add`, which sends the event. Only after that does it store the minor where `drm_minor_acquire` finds it, so an open in between answers `ENODEV`. udev and vtium can handle the event in that gap. The monitor then keeps the card as known, but does not report it. `drm_dev_register` goes on to register the card's connectors, and `drm_connector_register` (`drivers/gpu/drm/drm_connector.c`) sends a `change` event on the card, with `HOTPLUG=1`, for each connector. At the card's next event the monitor opens the card again, and reports the card once the open succeeds. A card removed before then was never reported, so its removal reports nothing. Why: in tier 3, a card plugged back in answered `ENODEV` at its `add` event in 2 of 42 runs, and vtium never set the card up (vtium/vtium#35). Tests: `a_card_the_kernel_is_still_adding_is_reported_at_its_next_change`, `a_card_removed_while_the_kernel_is_still_adding_it_reports_nothing`.

A framebuffer's `add` event can also come before the framebuffer is registered. `do_register_framebuffer` (`drivers/video/fbdev/core/fbmem.c`) creates the device, which sends the event, before it stores the framebuffer where `fb_open` finds it. But `register_framebuffer` holds `registration_lock` the whole time, and `fb_open` takes the same lock to find the framebuffer (`get_fb_info`). So an open in between waits until the registration is done, and does not answer `ENODEV`. The monitor reports a framebuffer at its `add` event.

A card is primary when its PCI parent has `boot_vga` set, or when it is a platform display controller not on USB. It is auxiliary on the `udl` driver. A framebuffer is DRM-backed unless its id names no DRM driver. It is auxiliary as `udlfb`, and primary as `VESA VGA`. A `change` carrying `HOTPLUG=1` on a known card reports `Hotplug`, for `Drm2d::poll`. A known device whose `ID_SEAT` changes is removed and added again, and a `change` on an unknown one may add it. Without a udev daemon, the scan still reads sysfs, every device is `seat0`'s, and no hotplug arrives.

An event node's `Device` carries its `InputIdentity`, which is what a head file's rules match it by (ADR-1401 D4). vtium reads it without opening the node. It holds the node's classes and its `ID_PATH`. It holds its `ID_VENDOR_ID`, `ID_MODEL_ID` and `ID_SERIAL_SHORT`. It holds the unique id, vendor, product and name that its parent input device shows in sysfs. It also holds its udev properties, for `udev:`. The properties that the identity reads by name come first, and then the rest in udev's order. There are at most `UDEV_PROPERTIES_MAX` of them, each at most `UDEV_PROPERTY_MAX` bytes. A property past either bound is left out with a warning, and never cut short.

The classes are udev's `ID_INPUT_*` tags, each counting at the value `1`. So a hwdb entry that sets one to `0` takes it away, wherever udev's `input_id` ran (`ID_INPUT=1`). Where `input_id` did not run, vtium applies udev's rule to the capability bitmaps that sysfs shows (`capabilities/ev`, `key`, `rel`, `abs` and `properties`):

- `switch` for `EV_SW`;
- `keyboard` for every key code 1 to 31, Escape through S;
- `key` for any key code below the buttons, or in the key ranges above them;
- at most one kind of pointer, the first of these that matches:
  - `accelerometer` by its property;
  - `tablet` by a pen;
  - `touchpad` by a finger tool without `INPUT_PROP_DIRECT`;
  - `mouse` by its buttons with relative axes or none, or with absolute axes and no touch;
  - `touchscreen` by touch or `INPUT_PROP_DIRECT`;
  - `joystick` by its buttons or axes;
- `pointingstick` by its property;
- `tablet-pad` by its numbered buttons.

`trackball` comes only from udev's hwdb. The rule is written from ADR-1401's account of udev's and the kernel's device types. It is checked against udev's tags on every event node of the operator's machine (`the_evidence_devices_get_the_same_class_from_udev_tags_and_from_their_bits`). The machine has no tablet, tablet pad, joystick, touchscreen or accelerometer. So `tablet`, `tablet-pad`, `joystick`, `touchscreen` and `accelerometer` stay unchecked until tier 3 compares the guest's emulated devices with `udevadm`. vtium reads the bitmaps in the reading process's word width, as the kernel prints them to it: 32-bit words to a 32-bit vtium.

`listing::list` lists the seat's devices as a head file's rules see them, for `vtium check-config` (ADR-1401 D7). It reads them from a sysfs root and a udev database root, which are `/sys` and `/run/udev/data` on the machine, and opens nothing under `/dev`. It keeps what the monitor keeps, by the same seat rule. An event node's identity is the monitor's `InputIdentity`. It comes from the node's parent input device's sysfs attributes and its udev database entry (`c13:N`). It uses udev's classes where the entry has them, and else the bitmaps' rule.

The node's properties are the ones that sd-device, behind libudev, gives the start:

- `DEVPATH`;
- the `uevent`'s properties, with `DEVNAME` under `/dev/`;
- `SUBSYSTEM` and `DRIVER`;
- from the entry: `USEC_INITIALIZED` (`I:`), and the `E:` properties, each replacing one of the same name;
- also from the entry: `DEVLINKS` (`S:`, each under `/dev/`, joined by spaces), and `TAGS` (`G:`) and `CURRENT_TAGS` (`Q:`) as `:a:b:`.

sd-device joins the links and tags in a hash order that changes from process to process. So the start and the listing both present `DEVLINKS`, `TAGS` and `CURRENT_TAGS` sorted, and a glob over them matches in the same way on every run.

A card carries its entry's `ID_PATH`. It is primary by the monitor's rule, read from the `boot_vga`, PCI, USB and platform parents that sysfs shows. Its displays are its connectors whose `status` is `connected` and whose `modes` lists a mode. They are ordered by `connector_id`, then by name in natural order (`DP-2` before `DP-10`), each with the monitor its `edid` names. The start registers a card's displays in its own order, which can differ. A listed display is never lit at start and carries no `PATH`, since both take opening the card. A framebuffer that no DRM driver backs is listed by the monitor's rule, as the start's one display `fb`. The rule reads the id that its sysfs `name` shows (`VESA VGA` is primary). A database entry or `uevent` is read to `UDEV_PROPERTIES_MAX` times `UDEV_PROPERTY_MAX` bytes, and its lines past that are left out whole.

## Input

`Input` holds every keyboard and pointer of the seat. A device that udev adds is opened and probed at once while awake, and else at the next wake-up. It is a keyboard when it has any key from Escape to Mute. It is a pointer when it has relative axes and a left button, or absolute axes with touch or a left button. It is taken as a pointer only with `mouse` on. Anything else is dropped.

A device may be added with a disposition (`add_with`, `set_disposition`), as a head file's rules place it. It is then taken by that disposition instead:

- `Deliver` keeps it whatever it reports, and counts it as a keyboard for any key.
- `Rescue` opens it to watch for the VT keys alone. While the VT is ours, `device_events` keeps only its presses of `Ctrl-Alt-F<n>` and `XF86Switch_VT_<n>`, the VT's own key (the rescue key) among them, marked `rescue`. It drops the device's other keys, its repeats and its pointer events, an ignored OTP token's characters included. The device's modifiers count in no one else's.
- `Closed` never opens it.

`events` drains every event but a watched device's. `device_events`, given the VT, drains each event with the device it came from. Wake-ups and sleeps are counted. The devices open at the first wake-up and close at the matching sleep, which stops the repeat. A device that ends, fails a read or hangs up is closed and kept, since a seat manager revokes devices on a switch. It is opened again at the next wake-up. udev reports one that is unplugged. Each readable device is read 16 events at a time until it has none, at most `INPUT_READS_PER_TURN` reads a turn. Its events become `InputEvent`s, which the owner drains in order.

- **Through the seat.** With the `libseat` feature, `Input::open_through` hands `Input` a `SeatInput`, which `LibseatVt::input` makes. From then on each device opens through the seat manager, which gives vtium a descriptor of it, and no device under `/dev/input` is opened directly. vtium makes that descriptor non-blocking. It shares its open file with the seat manager's descriptor, so the seat manager's revoke at a switch reaches it. A sleep closes each device through the seat manager, and the next wake-up opens it through the seat manager again, as libseat's clients do. The seat manager may refuse a device with `EBUSY`. vtium keeps that device, and tries it again from `Input`'s own loop timer every `SEAT_RETRY_MS`, up to `DEVICE_OPENS_MAX` tries. vtium drops the device with a warning once those tries run out. It also drops a device at once, with a warning, when the seat manager refuses it with any other error, `ENODEV` and `EACCES` among them. A device that vtium cannot open directly is dropped in the same way. A device asked for while the seat is not vtium's is kept and tried again at the next wake-up. So is a device refused `EPERM` while the seat manager takes the seat away. Dropping the `LibseatVt` closes the devices still open through the seat manager before it closes the seat. A later close of one of them closes only its own descriptor.

- **Keys.** The keymap is the `xkb-keymap` file, else the rules' names (`xkb-model`, `xkb-layout`, `xkb-variant`, `xkb-options`), else the system's default names, else the built-in keymap. `input::system_layout` gives the system's layout as the rules' names, in every build. It reads `KEYMAP=` from `/etc/vconsole.conf`, which is the console keymap. It translates the keymap through systemd's `/usr/share/systemd/kbd-model-map`, the table that localed uses for the same translation. The first row whose first column is the keymap gives the layout, model, variant and options, with `-` for an empty column. Reading the two files needs no bus.

  vconsole.conf may set no `KEYMAP=`, or the table may have no row for it. A build with the `logind` feature then reads localed's layout instead (`logind::layout`). That layout is the properties `X11Layout`, `X11Model`, `X11Variant` and `X11Options` of `org.freedesktop.locale1`. Each call is bounded by 2 s. The first property that cannot be read stops the rest, and vtium logs the reason. Any other build, minspec among them, uses xkb's default names. vtium names in one log line a keymap that the table cannot translate. `KeymapConfig::follow` then fills each `xkb-*` key that vtium.conf leaves unset from those names, so a key that vtium.conf sets wins. The system's variant comes only with the system's layout, since a variant names the variant of each layout in turn. vtium reads the system's layout once, at the start.

  The compose table is `xkb-compose-file`, else the locale's, found as locale(7) says without setting it. Otherwise compose is off. Each keyboard keeps its own xkb state and compose state. vtium reads a key's keysyms, feeds a press to compose, and updates the state. The keyboard's LEDs follow the state. Three kinds of press send nothing: a press that composes nothing yet, one that cancels a sequence, and one that completes a sequence that makes a string and no keysym. A completed sequence sends its keysym alone. Only presses are sent. Each carries the xkb key code, the ASCII keysym for shortcuts, Shift, Lock, Control, Alt and Super, and the keysyms and their characters.
- **Repeat.** The kernel's repeat is ignored. A key that the keymap marks as repeating repeats from a loop timer, marked as a repeat. It repeats after `xkb-repeat-delay`, and then every `xkb-repeat-rate` milliseconds (250 and 50 by default, 999 at most). It stops at its release or at another repeating press. A modifier pressed or released while a key repeats changes what repeats.
- **Releases.** A keyboard reports `InputEvent::Release` for every key whose press it reported as `InputEvent::Key`, once, when the key is let go. The release carries the keysyms its press carried and the modifiers after it, so the release of Shift holds no Shift. A key whose press went into a compose sequence reports no release. Neither does a key held across a sleep, since its press belongs to the state before the wake-up. A key held across a layout change still reports its release, because the kernel's key codes do not change with the layout. A device watched for the rescue key sends no release. The kitty keyboard protocol reports releases to a program that asks for them (ADR-1005 D8). Tests: `a_release_is_reported_for_every_reported_press`, `a_key_that_went_into_a_compose_sequence_reports_no_release`, `a_key_held_across_a_sleep_reports_no_release`, `a_key_held_across_a_layout_change_reports_its_release`.
- **NumLock, Hyper and Meta.** A key's modifiers hold Shift, Lock, Ctrl, Alt and Super. They also hold `Mods::NUM` while NumLock is on. They hold `Mods::HYPER` and `Mods::META` while a Hyper or Meta key is held, where the keymap gives that modifier a real modifier of its own. Its own means that neither the Alt keys nor the Super keys set it, and that it is neither Mod1 nor Mod4, which vtium reads as Alt and Super.

  vtium finds each real modifier on a scratch xkb state. It presses the keys that make Num_Lock, Hyper_L or Hyper_R, Meta_L or Meta_R, Alt_L or Alt_R, and Super_L or Super_R, at the level that makes them. It then reads what the keymap's actions set. Every libxkbcommon applies those actions. Asking an xkb state for a virtual modifier by its name works only from libxkbcommon 1.8. In xkeyboard-config's default keymaps, as in the built-in one, Meta shares Alt's modifier and Hyper shares Super's. So a key there reports Alt and Super alone, and never Meta beside Alt. The bits are vtium-term's own, so mode 1035 and the kitty keyboard protocol read them as the platform reports them. Spec: ADR-1005 D8; xkeyboard-config 2.49's `symbols/pc`, where Meta_L is on Mod1 and Hyper_L is on no modifier of its own. Tests: `num_lock_is_a_modifier_while_it_is_on`, `the_default_keymap_reports_neither_hyper_nor_meta`, `a_keymap_that_gives_hyper_a_modifier_of_its_own_reports_it`, `a_keymap_that_gives_meta_a_modifier_of_its_own_reports_it`.
- **Waking.** A keyboard opened again starts a fresh xkb state. The state keeps the locked modifiers and layout, and resets compose, so a key held across a VT switch does not stick. The keyboard's LEDs are written again.
- **Pointer.** Relative motion moves the pointer within the screen the owner sets (`set_pointer_max`), or within its own head's screen (`set_pointer_max_of`). A touchscreen or a virtual machine's tablet puts the pointer where its axes say, scaled to the screen. A touchpad moves it by the finger's motion from where it is, held at the edges. Buttons report press and release. The left button reports a double click within 500 ms of the last. The wheel reports its steps. Each evdev frame of a pointer device ends with `Sync`. The hide timeout reports `Hide` when no frame comes. It is `POINTER_HIDE` unless the owner sets another or none (`InputConfig::with_pointer_hide`).
- **Modifiers and LEDs.** `mods` reads the modifiers held on every keyboard but those watched for the rescue key. `mods_of` reads those of a set of devices, such as a head's. A keyboard whose effective modifiers change reports `InputEvent::Mods`, with the modifiers that this one keyboard holds after the change, never the seat's. It reports them at a modifier key's press, after the press's own `Key`. It reports them at the key's release, which sends no `Key`, and at Caps Lock's toggle.

  A closed keyboard holds no modifiers. So a keyboard that held a modifier reports none as it closes: at a sleep, when udev removes it, when the rules close it, or when it ends. It reports what it holds as it opens again, which is a lock it kept and no key held before its sleep. Neither the kernel's repeat nor vtium's own repeats a report. An owner that follows a modifier across a head's keyboards combines them through `mods_of`. The binary does that when it hides the address of a link once Ctrl is let go on every keyboard of the head (ADR-1002). A device watched for the rescue key holds no modifiers in its owner's view, as `mods` counts none there. So a keyboard moved to the rescue reports none, and one moved back to delivery reports what it holds. A program's DECLL reaches every keyboard but those watched for the rescue key through `set_leds`, and a set of devices through `set_leds_of`.

### What the input devices do, and why

- **An absolute axis whose range is one value moves nothing, and scaling cannot overflow.** A device that reports the same minimum and maximum would otherwise stop the console with a division by zero. Spec: `linux/input.h`, `struct input_absinfo`, which bounds no range.
- **A closed pointer holds no button.** A device may close, as the devices sleep for a VT switch. It then lets go of its pointer's held button, so a button released on another VT reports no drag when the pointer next moves. A touchpad takes the finger's next position as the origin it moves from, so the pointer does not jump by the finger's travel on the other VT. Otherwise a program that tracks the pointer with mode 1002 would be sent a drag at every move until the next button event. Spec: vtium/vtium#130; a key held across the switch, which a keyboard opened again also drops. Tests: `sleep_lets_go_of_a_held_button`, `a_touchpads_finger_takes_its_origin_afresh_after_a_sleep`.
- **A keyboard's frame is no pointer frame.** Only a device with a pointer ends a pointer frame with `Sync` and restarts the pointer's hide timeout. So typing neither keeps the pointer from hiding nor redraws every display at each key. Spec: the operator's decision on vtium/vtium#102, that the pointer hides after a time with no pointer event; tier 3's `pointer-plane` case found it. Test: `a_keyboards_frames_leave_the_pointers_hide_alone`.

### What only tier 3 and the VT review prove

The stand-ins follow the kernel where the VT and the input depend on it, and only a real VT shows that they do. Tier 3 (vtium/vtium#22) proves the VT ioctls and the switch between two vtium VTs: the release waiting for `Asleep`, the acquire's wake-up, and the log lines it reads. The operator's VT review proves what needs a person at the keyboard, in its VT steps in `make vt-check GUIDE=vt`:

- keys reaching vtium and not the kernel console in `K_OFF`;
- Ctrl+Alt+Fn, and the switch back at exit;
- the VT left in text mode with a working keyboard after vtium exits, a kill included;
- the keyboard LEDs;
- the pointer on a real mouse and touchpad;
- compose with the locale's table;
- a USB keyboard plugged in and out while vtium runs.

Here is a known limit for tier 3 to look at, read from the kernel's source and not observed. vtium's VT may be left in `VT_AUTO` and text mode. The kernel may then switch from it to another VT in text mode with no release. The VT switched to can then stay black until the next switch. Neither VT is in graphics mode, so the kernel's fbdev emulation does not redraw it while vtium still holds DRM master. vtium's sleep, on the `Release` that the switch reports, blanks its buffers and drops master without restoring the console.

## Dependencies

- **rustix** for every syscall (vtium/vtium#6). Its `runtime` feature gives the two calls that rustix keeps there: the default signal dispositions and the empty signal mask that the pty's child starts with. It is pinned at `=1.1.6`. rustix keeps that module unstable, and publishes it under a name that changes between releases. So any update is made by hand, renaming the import in `src/pty.rs`.
- **calloop** for the event loop, pinned at 0.14.4: 0.14.5 needs Rust 1.86, which the workspace's `rust-version`, 1.88, now meets. The move to 0.14.5 or later is still to be made.
- **drm-ffi** for DRM's ioctls: safe wrappers over `drm-sys`'s kernel structures. vtium/vtium#6 named the `drm` crate, whose wrappers these are. It was already in reach through `drm`, and taking it alone leaves out `drm`'s `bytemuck` derive and its proc-macro crates. It adds `drm-sys` and a second `linux-raw-sys`, 0.9.4, beside rustix's 0.12.1. Pinned at `=0.9.1`.

  Its `mode::get_connector`, `get_properties`, `get_property` and `get_plane_resources` read twice. They set each list's length to the count that the second read reports, which runs past the list's buffer when the object changes between the reads. So `src/ioctl.rs` makes GETCONNECTOR, OBJ_GETPROPERTIES, GETPROPERTY and GETPLANERESOURCES itself. `Kms` reads each list into a buffer of the length it first asked (vtium/vtium#192). vtium reads a property's name in one GETPROPERTY that asks for none of its lists. drm-ffi's `get_property`, given no buffers, asked a second time with null buffers, which the kernel refused with `EFAULT`.

  Its `mode::get_resources` also reads twice, and sets each list's length to the count that the second read reports. That runs past the list's buffer when a connector is plugged in between the reads. So `src/ioctl.rs` makes that call itself, and `Card::resources` reads again while a count grows (vtium/vtium#89). Its `mode::get_property_blob` does the same with a blob's length. So `src/ioctl.rs` makes GETPROPBLOB itself too, and `Kms::blob` reads a blob into a buffer of the length it first asked.
- **log** for the crate's messages, which the binary routes. It was already in the tree through calloop's `tracing`.
- **xkbcommon** for libxkbcommon's keymaps, compose and key state. It is the binding that Smithay's and the X.Org Rust bindings share: safe wrappers over the C library. It is built with its default features off, so neither its Wayland nor its X11 parts come in. It adds `xkeysym` and links `libxkbcommon.so` dynamically. Pinned at `=0.9.0`. libxkbcommon logs its own errors to stderr. Routing them through vtium's logger would need a C variadic callback, which the binding does not take.
- **udev** for libudev, which vtium's minimal build links too. It is Smithay's binding, and adds `libudev-sys`, `io-lifetimes` and, to build, `pkg-config`. It links `libudev.so` dynamically, which is a library of its own and not libsystemd (ADR-1400's observable). Pinned at `=0.9.3`.
- **libc** for the real-time signals. vtium calls `kill(2)` with systemd's power-off signal, and `pidfd_send_signal(2)` through `syscall` with vtium's blank and wake signals. It uses libc's `SIGRTMIN`, since rustix sends a real-time signal only through an unsafe constructor whose contract forbids sending it. It also uses libc for the blank and wake signals' set, mask and signalfd (`sigemptyset`, `sigaddset`, `pthread_sigmask`, `signalfd`). calloop's signal source and nix's signal set name the standard signals alone. libc was already in the build through xkbcommon and nix. Pinned at `=0.2.191`.
- **libsystemd or libelogind**, with the `logind` feature only, for sd-bus. vtium declares seven calls in `src/logind.rs` and `src/logind/localed.rs` and links them dynamically, with no binding crate. libelogind exports each of the seven under libsystemd's symbol versions. Its `sd-bus.h` gives the same signatures and `sd_bus_error` layout. `sd_bus_set_method_call_timeout` sets the floor: libsystemd 240 and libelogind 241. The minspec build has the feature off and links neither (ADR-1400). Where both libraries' `.pc` files are installed, `LIBSYSTEMD_NO_PKG_CONFIG=1` makes the build take libelogind. The build script's arrival moved that binary by a few tens of bytes with no code changed. It did so through the crate's hash, and so its layout, and the figure varies with the build path (vtium/vtium#100).
- **libseat**, with the `libseat` feature only, for the seat through a seat manager. This is the `libseat` crate, Smithay's safe binding, MIT. It sits over `libseat-sys`, which finds libseat through pkg-config, and fails the build naming libseat when it is missing. Its default `custom_logger` feature is off. That feature compiles a C file to route libseat's log into the `log` crate, which would need a C compiler for every target, the ARM ones included.

  vtium sets libseat's log level to errors, and gives libseat a log handler of its own, written in Rust in `src/seat/binding.rs`. That file declares `libseat_set_log_handler` itself, because `libseat-sys` leaves it out. The handler hands libseat's `va_list` on to C's `vsnprintf` unchanged. Each Linux target that vtium builds for passes a `va_list` as one pointer. A test that runs libseat itself checks that on each target the tests run on. vtium holds back the errors that libseat reports while vtium opens the seat. When the open ends as vtium expects, vtium logs them at the debug level. On a machine without seatd, for example, libseat reports that seatd's socket is missing, and vtium then says that it uses the kernel's VT. Otherwise vtium logs each one as a warning, as it does every error libseat reports later. The crate adds `libseat-sys` and `errno`. It panics on a device path that is not UTF-8 or that holds a NUL, so `src/seat/binding.rs` refuses those first. Pinned at `=0.2.4`. The minspec and default builds have the feature off and link no libseat (ADR-1400).
- **pkg-config**, a build dependency with the `logind` feature only, which finds libsystemd or libelogind. It was already in the build through `libudev-sys`. Pinned at `=0.3.34`.
- **glow**, with `gpu` only, for GL's function table, which it loads through `eglGetProcAddress`; ADR-1303 D8 names it. It has no native dependency on Linux, and none of its features is on. Two calls it lacks, `glEGLImageTargetRenderbufferStorageOES` and the reset status, are loaded by name in `src/gpu/context.rs`. Pinned at `=0.18.0`.
- **khronos-egl**, with `gpu` only, for EGL, through its `dynamic` feature, which opens libEGL at run time, and its `1_5` feature, since vtium needs `eglGetPlatformDisplay` and `eglCreateImage`; ADR-1303 D8 names it. Pinned at `=6.0.0`.
- **libloading**, with `gpu` only, which opens libgbm at run time; khronos-egl's `dynamic` feature already builds it. Pinned at `=0.8.9`.
- **libEGL and libgbm**, with `gpu` only. vtium opens them when it makes a context or a GBM device, and never links them. A machine without them builds and starts, and its GPU path's set-up fails at the step that needs them. The tests need Mesa's EGL and llvmpipe: `libegl1`, `libegl-mesa0` and `libgl1-mesa-dri` on Debian and Ubuntu, `mesa` on Arch.
- **nix** arrives through calloop's `signals` feature, which gives the loop its signalfd. rustix has no signalfd wrapper. The loop's standard signals go through it; the real-time signals' signalfd is a libc call in `src/rt_signal.rs`, since nix's signal set names the standard signals alone. vtium-platform calls nothing in nix. ## Left to the host

- **The child's command and environment** are the host's `Command`: `TERM`, `COLORTERM`, `XDG_SEAT`, `XDG_VTNR`, `TERM_SESSION_TYPE`, the environment reset and `/bin/login` as the default. That is the binary's (vtium/vtium#21).
- **The respawn policy**: a new session after an exit, oneshot, and a limit of 5 restarts within 2 seconds. That limit reads the clock, so it belongs where the binary decides what an exit means, not in the loop (vtium/vtium#21).

## Left to the seat and the binary

- **Choosing the VT backend**: with `libseat`, `LibseatVt::start` when a seat manager answers, else `Tty::find` or the configured VT through `Tty::open` and `KernelVt::new`, else `FakeVt`. `KernelVt::new` takes the loop, is called before any thread starts, and its VT is stored where `access` finds it before the loop dispatches again, and dropped before the loop. With `--switchvt` the seat calls `activate`; without it, `wait`, and it scans the devices once the VT is shown. The panic hook runs a `Restore`.
- **Opening the devices** is the seat's job. On `DeviceEvent::Added`, the seat opens a card or a framebuffer through `Vt::open_device`, and hands the descriptor to `Card::new` or `Fb::new`. It hands an event node to `Input::add`, which opens it through the seat once `Input::open_through` has given it one. On `Removed`, the seat drops the device and calls `Vt::close_device`, or calls `Input::remove`. On `Hotplug`, it calls `Drm2d::poll`. A card or framebuffer whose open answers `WouldBlock` waits, and the seat opens it again once `Vt::settled` names it. The seat also chooses which backend a device gets, drm2d or fbdev, and skips a framebuffer that a DRM driver backs (`DeviceFlags::drm_backed`).
- **When to sleep and wake** follows the VT's events and the hand-back (vtium/vtium#36). On `VtEvent::Acquired`: `Input::wake_up` and each device's `wake_up`. On `Release`: the terminal deactivated, `Input::sleep` and each device's `sleep`, then `acknowledge_release` once each device reported `Event::Asleep`, at once with no device. On `Hangup`: exit. On `Retaken`, which comes only while the VT is ours: each device's displays set up again, as a modeset does, and every display drawn whole. At exit: `leave`, the loop served for up to 100 ms while it is pending, then the `KernelVt` dropped. Once its `WAKE_RETRIES` fast tries run out, each card goes on trying every `WAKE_RETRY_SLOW_MS` by itself while the VT is shown, so the seat starts no retry of its own. The rest of the hand-back, setBackground, setForeground and the focus reports, is vtium/vtium#36.
- **The key order**: each `InputEvent::Key` goes to `Vt::key`, then the seat's keys, then the terminal's grabs, then DECARM and the encoder (vtium/vtium#21); the screen's size to `Input::set_pointer_max`; DECLL to `Input::set_leds`. vtium-platform's `Mods` share their bits with vtium-term's.
- **What to draw** is the compositor's (vtium/vtium#16), under the frame contract above.
- **The DPMS blank's wiring**: the binary feeds the user's key presses and pointer actions (a move, a button or the wheel) and `Event::Awake` to `Blank::input`, a hotplug never. On each such press or action it calls each device's `input`. Whenever `Blank::input` returns true, for an event or for one device's `Event::Awake`, it calls every device's `unblank`, so a wake-up on one device ends the blank on all of them. An `Err` from `input` or `unblank` is logged and the loop goes on: the device retries within its bounds, and reports `WakeFailed` when they run out. Its loop wakes at `Blank::deadline`, on the binary's clock, and when `Blank::fire` says so it powers the displays down and deactivates the terminal (vtium/vtium#21).
